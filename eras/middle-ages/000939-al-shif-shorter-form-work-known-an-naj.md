# Al-Shif[=a]'

The *Al-Shif[=a]'* (translated as *Sanatio*) is one of two encyclopaedic treatises dealing with philosophy attributed to Avicenna. It is the larger of the two philosophical works and exists in manuscript form, nearly complete, in the Bodleian library and other locations.

## Content and Influence
The *Al-Shif[=a]'* served as a primary source for the account of Avicenna's philosophy provided by Shahrastani, which appears to be largely a reproduction and analysis of the work. Additionally, a portion of the *Al-Shif[=a]'* concerning the *De Anima* was published at Pavia in 1490 under the title *Liber Sextus Naturalium*.

## Related Works
A shorter version of the *Al-Shif[=a]'* is known as the *An-naj[=a]t* (*Liberatio*). Both works have seen Latin editions, though these were modified by a monkish editor who provided corrections.

The *Al-Shif[=a]'* is part of a vast body of work ascribed to Avicenna, which totals approximately 100 treatises. These works vary significantly in length, ranging from short tracts of a few pages to multi-volume sets. Avicenna's intellectual reach extended across many disciplines within the Arabian circle of sciences, including physics, mathematics, astronomy, music, philology, and theology. Other notable works by Avicenna include the *Canon of Medicine*, which established his reputation in Europe, as well as treatises on *Logic*, *Metaphysics*, *Physics*, and *De Caelo*, which provide synoptic views of Aristotelian doctrine.
