# Civic Halls, Royal Palaces, and Government Offices

Across various global cities and historical periods, public architecture has served as a primary symbol of administrative power, civic pride, and royal authority. These structures range from the Gothic town halls of Europe to the sprawling administrative fortresses of the Middle East and the colonial government houses of India.

## Civic Halls and Town Halls
Civic halls, often referred to as town halls, frequently serve as the center of municipal administration and community identity. In Breslau, the town hall is a fine Gothic building begun in the middle of the 14th century and completed in the 16th century. It is located in the Ring, the city's market square, and contains the Fürstensaal, where the diets of Silesia were formerly held. Beneath the building is the Schweidnitzer Keller, a beer and wine house used continuously since 1355. Similarly, Danzig preserves a beautiful Gothic town hall characterized by a graceful spire.

In Belgium, the town halls of the Middle Ages—specifically those in Bruges and Ypres—symbolize the strength, pride, and glory of the Belgian cities. These civic monuments are noted for their vast proportions, wide rooms, and wonderful belfries. In Scotland, the town hall of Dundee, designed by Robert Adam and built in 1734, features a 140 ft. high steeple with a peal of bells and a piazza. In Dumfries, the old town hall, known as the Mid Steeple, was built in 1708 by Tobias Bachup of Alloa and is identified by its red tower and cupola. In Spain, the city hall of Cordova is listed among the city's most important public buildings.

## Royal Palaces and Residences
Royal palaces serve as the residences of sovereigns and the centers of court life. In Cordova, the Alcazar (royal palace) was built by the caliph Abd-ar-Rahman III (912-961) and is situated amid gardens. While its older parts are in ruins, the "New Alcazar," built by Alphonso XI of Castile in 1328, was long used as the offices of the Holy Inquisition and currently has one wing in good repair that serves as a prison.

In Calcutta, Government House serves as the residence of the viceroy. Built by Lord Wellesley in 1799, it is a fine pile modeled after Kedleston Hall in Derbyshire and is situated on grounds covering six acres. Additionally, Belvedere House in the southern suburb of Alipur serves as the official residence of the lieutenant-governor of Bengal.

In England, the royal palace is the domain of the Lord Chamberlain, the second dignitary of the court. The Lord Chamberlain's department includes the presence chamber, privy chamber, bedchamber, the guardroom, the wardrobe, the chapels royal, and the housekeeper's room. He carries a jewelled or golden key as a symbol of his charge over the palace.

## Government Offices and Administrative Centers
Government offices vary from specialized modern buildings to integrated administrative complexes. In Breslau, the government offices are described as a handsome pile erected in 1887. Danzig also features modern government offices and a house of the provincial diet. In Scotland, the county buildings in Buccleuch Street are noted as an imposing example of the Scots Baronial style.

Historically, the capital of the empire in Bagdad was a mercantile city surrounding a huge administrative fortress. Within this fortress, every department of state had a well-ordered public office. The administration utilized a complicated system of Divans, influenced by Roman and Persian systems. Separate bureaux handled Military affairs, Justice, Crown Lands, Privy Seal, Finance, and Posts, all managed by ministers and an army of scribes, accountants, and clerks.
