# Bolton-le-Moors

Bolton-le-Moors is a municipal, county, and parliamentary borough located in Lancashire, England. Situated 11 miles northwest of Manchester and 196 miles northwest by north from London, the borough covers an area of 15,279 acres. The town is divided into Great and Little Bolton by the Croal, which is a small tributary of the Irwell. As its name suggests, the area is surrounded by high moorland.

## Etymology and Early History
The name of the town has appeared in several forms over time. The earliest recorded versions are Botheltun or Bodleton. Later variations include Boltune, Bowelton, Bodeltown, Bolton-super-Moras, Bolton-in-ye-Moors, and Botheltun-le-Moors.

The manor was originally granted by William I to Roger de Poictou. Ownership later passed through the Pilkington and Ferrers families before being held by the Harringtons of Hornby Castle. The Harringtons eventually lost the manor, along with their other estates, due to their adherence to Richard III. In 1485, Henry VII granted the manor to the first earl of Derby. While the manor is currently held by various lords, the earls of Derby continue to hold a fourth part. Additionally, the manor of Little Bolton appears to have been distinct from Great Bolton since at least the reign of Henry III, remaining under the ownership of the Boltons or Botheltons until the 17th century.

## Industrial Development
Bolton established a reputation for woollen manufactures in its early history. During the reign of Richard I, the town was already a center for the woollen cloth trade, evidenced by the appointment of an aulneger to stamp and measure bundles of woollen goods. The industry grew further in 1337 following the settlement of Flemish clothiers. By 1566, an act of parliament appointed deputies to assist aulnegers in Bolton. In 1558, Leland noted in his *Itinerary* that Bolton produced "cottons," though these were actually woollen goods.

Actual cotton manufacturing did not begin in Lancashire until 1641, at which time Bolton was the primary center for the production of dimities, vermilions, and fustians. This industry was later stimulated by the arrival of French refugees following the revocation of the edict of Nantes. Notable milestones in textile production include the first manufacture of muslins and cotton quiltings in 1763, and velvets around 1756, produced by Jeremiah Clarke.

The cotton trade experienced significant growth due to the inventions of Samuel Crompton (1780) and Sir Richard Arkwright (1770), both of whom were born in the parish. The first spinning factory in Bolton was established in 1780. By 1851, the town housed 66 cotton mills operating 860,000 throstle spindles. The town's damp climate specifically assists its specialty in fine spinning. Other significant industries include iron and steel works, collieries, and the printing, dyeing, and bleaching of calico and cotton; bleaching has been practiced since the early 18th century. In 1791, a canal was built connecting Bolton to Manchester, and Bolton Moor was enclosed by an act of parliament in 1792.

## Urban Features and Institutions
Despite its early origins, the town's appearance is described as wholly modern. The few remaining remnants of antiquity are two 16th-century houses in Little Bolton, one of which is a notable example of Tudor architecture. The site of the church of St Peter has hosted a parish church since the 12th century or earlier, though the current building dates to 1870.

The borough features a variety of civic and cultural institutions, including:
* A town hall with a tower and classical facade.
* A market hall and an exchange.
* Assembly rooms and various benevolent institutions.
* Museums of art and industry, as well as natural history.
* Several free libraries and municipal technical schools.

Education is represented by Lever's grammar school, founded in 1641, which once employed John Lempriere (author of the classical dictionary) and Robert Ainsworth (the Latin lexicographer) as masters.

The town also provides public recreational spaces. A large public park was opened in 1866 as relief work for unemployed operatives during the cotton famine of the early 1860s. Another public park is located on the north-west moors and includes Rivington Pike, which reaches 1,192 feet.

## Administration and Demographics
Bolton was created as a parliamentary borough in 1832, returning two members. According to census data, the population was 146,487 in 1891 and grew to 168,215 by 1901. The town is served by the Lancashire & Yorkshire and London & North-Western railways, with running powers provided for the Midland railway.
