# Minoritenkirche and Herz Jesu-kirche

The Minoritenkirche and the Herz Jesu-kirche are two of the religious edifices located in the town of Bonn, Germany. Bonn is situated in the Prussian Rhine province on the left bank of the Rhine, approximately 15 miles south by east from Cologne.

## Religious Architecture in Bonn
The town of Bonn features a variety of religious buildings, including two Protestant churches and six Roman Catholic churches. The most prominent of these is the Munster, a grey stone edifice built in the Transition and Romanesque styles between the 11th and 13th centuries. The Munster is characterized by five towers, with a central tower reaching a height of 315 feet.

Other notable churches in the town include:
*   The Stiftskirche (monasterial church), which underwent rebuilding between 1879 and 1884.
*   The Jesuitenkirche, dating from 1693.
*   The Marienkirche, dating from 1892.

## The Minoritenkirche and Herz Jesu-kirche
Among the Roman Catholic churches in Bonn is the Minoritenkirche, which dates from the period between 1278 and 1318. Additionally, the town contains the Herz Jesu-kirche, which dates from 1862.

Beyond these Catholic structures, the town's religious landscape includes a synagogue and a university chapel that functions as an English church.

## Urban Context
These churches are part of a town that, as of 1905, had a population of 81,997 (increasing from 35,989 in 1885). The central area of Bonn is characterized by narrow streets, while the outskirts are home to numerous fine buildings. The town is also noted for its attractive appearance from the river, featuring a bridge built between 1896 and 1898 that is 1417 feet long. This bridge is flanked by a two-mile embankment and the Coblenzer-strasse, an area featuring pretty gardens and beautiful villas. Other civic landmarks in the town include a theatre, several high-grade schools, a block of law-court buildings, and a town hall on the market square dating from 1737.
