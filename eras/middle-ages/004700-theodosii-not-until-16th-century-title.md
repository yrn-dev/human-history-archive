# Breviary of Alaric

The Breviary of Alaric, also known as the *Breviarium Alaricanum*, is a collection of Roman law compiled in A.D. 506. The work was produced during the twenty-second year of the reign of Alaric II, the king of the Visigoths, who ordered its compilation with the assistance of his nobles and bishops.

## Composition and Contents
The Breviary is distinguished from the code of Justinian by the fact that it includes both juridical treatises (*jura*) and imperial constitutions (*leges*), whereas the latter excluded the writings of jurists. The collection is composed of the following materials:
* Sixteen books of the Theodosian code.
* The Novels of Severus, Majorianus, Marcian, Valentinian III, and Theodosius II.
* The Institutes of Gaius.
* Five books of the *Sententiae Receptae* of Julius Paulus.
* Two titles from the Hermogenian code.
* Thirteen titles from the Gregorian code.
* A fragment from the first book of the *Responsa Papiniani*.

The primary value of this Visigothic code is that it serves as the only collection of Roman Law containing the first five books of the Theodosian code and five books of the *Sententiae Receptae*.

## Legal Status and Nomenclature
The collection was intended to be the exclusive law throughout the kingdom of the Visigoths. This was established via a royal rescript (*commonitorium*) which mandated that copies of the code, certified by the king's referendary, Anianus, be received as the sole law. Due to this certification, many writers have attributed the compilation to Anianus, frequently referring to the work as the Breviary of Anianus (*Breviarium Aniani*). In the certificate of Anianus, the work is termed a code (*codex*).

Among the Visigoths, the work was known by the titles "Lex Theodosii" or "Lex Romana." The specific title "Breviarium" was not introduced until the 16th century. This later terminology was adopted to distinguish the original work from a recast of the code that had been introduced into northern Italy during the 9th century for the use of Romans in Lombardy.

## Preservation and Recasts
The Lombard recast of the Visigothic code was preserved in a manuscript known as the Codex Utinensis. This manuscript was previously held in the archives of the cathedral of Udine, though it is now lost. It was first published in the 18th century by P. Canciani within a collection of ancient laws titled *Barbarorum Leges Antiquae*. Additionally, Hänel discovered another manuscript of this Lombard recast in the library of St Gall.
