# Charles V

## Overview and Political Context
Charles V presided over a vast family compact that surrounded France. The emergence of this geopolitical situation was an indirect result of the failures of Louis XI in Spain. Louis XI's interference in Spanish affairs had created enmity with Henry IV of Castile and John II of Aragon, which prevented the French king from stopping the marriage of their heirs, Ferdinand and Isabella.

## Court and Associates
The court of Charles V attracted various figures of distinction. One such individual was Francis Borgia (1510–1572), a Roman Catholic saint and the general of the order of Jesuits. Borgia was sent to the court of Charles V in 1528 by his father. While at court, Borgia distinguished himself and was created marquis of Lombay following his marriage to a high-ranking Portuguese lady, Eleanor de Castro. During his tenure at court, Borgia served as the master of the horse to the empress.

## Military Expeditions and Official Duties
Charles V led several military campaigns. In 1535, he conducted an African expedition, which Francis Borgia accompanied. The following year, in 1536, Charles V traveled into Provence, again with Borgia in his company. 

The relationship between the emperor and his court officials often extended to sensitive diplomatic and personal duties. For example, upon the death of the empress in 1539, Francis Borgia was deputed to convoy her body to the burial-place located in Granada.
