# The Chiltern District and St Albans

## History and Geography of Buckinghamshire
The district that eventually became Buckinghamshire was first reached by the West Saxons in 571. This expansion occurred as they pushed northward along the Thames valley through a series of victories. Over time, settlements were grouped into kingdoms, and Buckinghamshire was incorporated into Mercia during the consolidation of that kingdom under Offa. Following the submission of Mercia to the Northmen, the area became part of the Danelaw.

During the 10th century, the region suffered frequent ravages by the Danes. Evidence of the struggles against these invaders remains today in the form of numerous earthworks and barrows. These relics are particularly abundant in the vale of Aylesbury, which was likely one of the best protected and richest Saxon settlements during that period.

## The Chiltern Forest and Leofstan
In contrast to the vale of Aylesbury, the Chiltern district was described as an impassable forest. This area was infested by wild beasts and hordes of robbers. During the reign of Edward the Confessor, Leofstan, who served as the 12th abbot of St Albans, cut down large tracts of wood within this district. 

To address the issue of robbery, Leofstan granted the manor of Hamstead in Hertfordshire to two fellow-soldiers and a valiant knight, stipulating that they must check the depredations of the robbers. This necessity for security led to the early appointment of a steward of the Chiltern Hundreds. Although the need for such an office eventually vanished, the position continued to exist, gradually evolving into a sinecure. The district was not finally disforested until the reign of James I.

## Barnet and the Abbots of St Albans
Barnet is a residential district located in the mid or St Albans parliamentary division of Hertfordshire, situated 10 miles north of London. It is divided into three chief sections: New Barnet, East Barnet, and Chipping (or High) Barnet.

Chipping Barnet is a market town and urban district. The name "Chipping" is derived from a market that was granted to the abbots of St Albans by Henry II to be held there. The town is located on the Great North Road and previously served as an important coaching station; it continues to host a large annual cattle and horse fair.

## Military History of the Region
The area near Chipping Barnet was the site of a decisive battle in 1471 between the houses of Lancaster and York. The battle took place near a point marked by an obelisk. The Lancastrians were totally defeated in this engagement, and the earl of Warwick fell during the conflict.
