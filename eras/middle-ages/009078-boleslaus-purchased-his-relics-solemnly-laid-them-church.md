# Boleslaus I and the Relics of Adalbert

## Early Life and Accession
Boleslaus I, known as "The Great," was the king of Poland who died in 1025. He was the son of the Bohemian princess Dobrawa (also called Bona) and Mieszko, who was the first Christian prince of Poland. The court's transition from paganism to Catholicism was facilitated by Dobrawa's chaplain, Jordan. Boleslaus succeeded his father in 992.

## Expansion of the Polish State
A born warrior, Boleslaus I transformed the struggling Polish principality on the Vistula into a great power. His territorial expansions included the seizure of Pomerania in 996 to secure a seaboard. He also occupied Cracow, which had previously been a Czech city, by taking advantage of instability in Bohemia.

Later in his career, following the death of Otto III, Boleslaus invaded Germany. He penetrated to the Elbe, occupying Meissen and Stralsund, and extended his territories to the Saale and the Elster. He also occupied Bohemia, though he was driven out by Emperor Henry IV in 1004. These conflicts ended in 1018 with the peace of Bautzen, which allowed Boleslaus to retain Lusatia. Additionally, he routed Jaroslav, the grand duke of Kiev, on the banks of the Bug, which served as the boundary between Poland and Russia at the time.

## Religious Patronage and the Relics of Adalbert
Boleslaus I recognized the essential superiority of Christianity over other religions and deliberately associated himself with the faith. This insight was evidenced by his sagacity in receiving Adalbert, the bishop of Prague, who was a fugitive. Following Adalbert's martyrdom at the hands of pagan Slavs on April 23, 997, Boleslaus purchased the saint's relics. He solemnly laid these relics in the church of Gnesen, a foundation established by his father. Consequently, Gnesen became the metropolitan see of Poland.

## Royal Coronation
In the year 1000, Boleslaus hosted Emperor Otto III at Gnesen with such magnificence that the emperor declared him too worthy to be merely a *princeps*. As a result, Otto III conferred the royal crown upon him. Despite this, Boleslaus felt it necessary to crown himself king a second time in the final year of his life, twenty-five years after the first coronation.
