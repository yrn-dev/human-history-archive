# Premysl Ottakar II

Premysl Ottakar II was a prominent king of Bohemia and one of the nation's greatest sovereigns. He was the son of Wenceslas I, whom he succeeded as king in 1253. Prior to his accession to the Bohemian throne, Ottakar had ruled Moravia under his father's sovereignty. Although he had once served as the chief leader of the malcontents during a period of internal discord in the latter years of Wenceslas I's reign, a reconciliation between father and son occurred before the king's death.

## Territorial Expansion and Military Power
Ottakar II significantly expanded his dominions, acquiring the archduchies of Austria and Styria. The estates of Austria, weary of disorder and the failure of central authority following the deaths of Emperor Frederick II and Hermann VI in 1250, chose Ottakar as their duke at Truebensee in 1251. He strengthened this position by marrying Margaret, a sister of Duke Frederick II. 

His acquisition of Styria involved conflict with Bela, the king of Hungary. While a 1254 treaty had confirmed Ottakar's possession of the land, he later took advantage of Styrian resentment toward Hungarian rule to interfere in the duchy's affairs. This led to further war, resulting in a victory for Ottakar and a treaty in March 1261 that recognized him as the duke of Styria. In 1260, Ottakar had also decisively defeated King Bela in the great battle of Kressenbrunn.

Following these successes, Ottakar's power reached its peak. He obtained possession of Istria, Carinthia (the latter inherited in 1269 upon the death of Duke Ulrich III), and parts of northern Italy. At the height of his influence, his possessions stretched from the Adriatic to the Giant Mountains in Bohemia, encompassing nearly all areas west of the Leitha that would later comprise the Habsburg empire.

## Reputation and Administration
Due to his immense wealth, contemporaries referred to Ottakar as "the man of gold." Conversely, he was called "the man of iron" in reference to his military power. 

As a ruler, Ottakar worked to improve the condition of his duchies by restoring order and benefiting the inhabitants of towns. He encouraged the immigration of Germans into his territories, introducing German colonists into eastern districts. This policy was driven by political rather than racial motivations; Ottakar sought to use German townsmen as a counterpoise to the overwhelming power held by the Bohemian nobility.

## Political Aspirations and Succession
Given the scale of his power, Ottakar began to aspire to the German throne. It is considered very probable that the German crown was offered to him, though he refused it; factors contributing to this may have included his Slavic nationality. In 1273, the position of king of the Romans was instead filled by Rudolph, count of Habsburg.

Upon the death of Premysl Ottakar II, he was succeeded by his son, Wenceslas II, who was only seven years old. Because of the sovereign's youth, Otto of Brandenburg served as guardian and governor of Bohemia. This period was marked by unpopularity and anarchy until the intervention of Rudolph of Habsburg. Wenceslas II eventually assumed nominal rule at age twelve, though actual power was held by the noble Zavis of Falkenstein until 1290, when Wenceslas ordered Zavis's beheading and began governing the country himself.
