# Rupert and the Great Schism

## The Great Schism
The Great Schism was a split in the papacy that began in 1378. Following the death of Pope Gregory XI, who had returned the papal court to Rome from Avignon in 1377, an Italian named Urban VI was elected. However, several cardinals of French origin declared this election invalid and elected a rival, the anti-Pope Clement VII. 

This division resulted in two competing papal lines: those in Rome and those in Avignon. The Roman popes were supported by the Emperor, the King of England, Poland, Hungary, and the North of Europe. Conversely, the anti-popes in Avignon were supported by the King of France, the King of Scotland, Spain, Portugal, and various German princes. During this period, which lasted from 1378 to 1417, each pope excommunicated the followers of his rival. The schism was eventually healed at the Council of Constance in 1417, where the rival claimants resigned or were removed, leading to the election of Martin V as the sole pope.

## Boniface IX and Rupert
Piero Tomacelli, who reigned as Pope Boniface IX from 1389 to 1404, was elected as the successor to Urban VI. During his pontificate, Boniface IX worked to restore Roman authority within the majority of the papal states and ended the republican liberties of the city of Rome in 1398. He also successfully brought Naples, which had previously owed spiritual allegiance to the Avignon anti-popes Benedict XIII and Clement VII, into the Roman obedience.

In 1403, Boniface IX confirmed the election of Rupert and the deposition of Emperor Wenceslaus. During this time, negotiations were undertaken to heal the Great Schism, but these efforts were without result.

## Gregory XII and the King of the Romans
Pope Gregory XII (Angelo Coriaro or Correr) succeeded Innocent VII on November 30, 1406. His election was conducted under the specific condition that he would renounce his claim to the papacy if the Avignon anti-pope, Benedict XIII, did the same, in order to terminate the long schism. 

Gregory XII attempted to resolve the conflict by concluding a treaty with his rival at Marseilles to hold a general council at Savona in September 1408. However, King Ladislaus of Naples opposed this plan for political reasons, seized Rome, and brought the negotiations to nought. Subsequently, Gregory XII's own cardinals deserted him in 1408 after he broke a promise not to create new cardinals. These defectors joined the Avignon cardinals to convoke the Council of Pisa, which deposed both existing popes in June 1409 and elected Alexander V.

Despite the Council of Pisa, Gregory XII continued to receive support from Hungary, Bavaria, Naples, and Rupert, the king of the Romans. Under the protection of Ladislaus, Gregory XII held a synod at Cividale del Friuli, where he banned Alexander and Benedict as scandalous, perjured, and schismatical. Gregory XII eventually presented his resignation to the Council of Constance on July 4, 1415.
