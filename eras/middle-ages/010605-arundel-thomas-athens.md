# Arundel, Thomas to Athens

## Thomas Arundel (1353-1414)
Thomas Arundel was the archbishop of Canterbury and the third son of Eleanor (daughter of Henry Plantagenet, earl of Lancaster) and Richard Fitzalan, earl of Arundel and Warenne. Born into an influential family, Arundel's ascent within the church was rapid; he became the archdeacon of Taunton in 1373 and was consecrated as the bishop of Ely in April 1374.

During the early reign of King Richard II, Arundel aligned himself with a political party consisting of his brother Richard (earl of Arundel), Henry (earl of Derby, later King Henry IV), and Thomas, duke of Gloucester. In 1386, Arundel and Gloucester were sent to Eltham to successfully persuade the king to return to parliament. Following this, Arundel served as lord chancellor, replacing Michael de la Pole, duke of Suffolk, and helped mediate peace between the king and the supporters of the commission of regency.

Arundel's ecclesiastical and political career continued with his appointment as archbishop of York in April 1388. He resigned the chancellorship in 1389 when Richard II declared himself of age, though he returned to the office in 1391. During this second term, he briefly moved the courts of justice from London to York. In September 1396, he was translated to Canterbury, resigning the chancellorship once more. As archbishop of Canterbury, he asserted his rights vigorously, conducting a thorough visitation of his province and warning London citizens against withholding tithes.

Arundel's political standing collapsed following the king's attack on the lords appellant in 1397. Despite initial hopes of safety offered by Richard II, Arundel was arrested alongside Warwick and Gloucester. He was impeached by the Commons for assisting in the procurement of the commission of regency to the derogation of royal authority. He was sentenced to banishment and given forty days to leave the realm.

## The Town of Arundel
Arundel is a municipal borough and market town in Sussex, England, located in the Chichester parliamentary division. Situated on a hill above the river Arun—which is navigable for small vessels to Littlehampton—the town had a population of 2,739 in 1901. According to the corporation seal, the town's name is derived from *hirondelle*, meaning a swallow.

The town is dominated by Arundel Castle, which historically guarded the river passage. A castle existed during the time of King Alfred and was rebuilt by Roger de Montgomerie during the Conquest, though it was later taken from his son by King Henry I. In 1397, the castle was the site of a discovered conspiracy by the duke of Gloucester, the archbishop of Canterbury, and the earl of Arundel to murder the king's council lords and dethrone Richard II. The stronghold suffered significant damage during the 17th-century civil wars but was restored by Charles, 11th duke of Norfolk (who died in 1815). Further reconstruction in the 13th-century style occurred toward the end of the 19th century.

Other notable town features include the castellated town hall, provided by the duke of Norfolk, and the church of St Nicholas (founded c. 1375), known for its Perpendicular style, iron work, and reredos. The duke of Norfolk also built the Roman Catholic church of St Philip Neri in 1873.

## The Arundells of Wardour
The Arundells of Wardour were a branch of the ancient Arundells of Lanherne in Cornwall. Thomas Arundell, 1st Baron (c. 1562-1639), was the son of Margaret and Sir Mathew Arundell. Recommended by Queen Elizabeth to Emperor Rudolph II in 1579, Thomas served with imperial troops against the Turks in Hungary. After capturing an enemy banner at the siege of Gran or Esztergom on August 13, 1595, he was created a count of the Holy Roman Empire.

Upon returning to England in 1596, his foreign title caused jealousy among English peers and resentment from his father, who disinherited him. Queen Elizabeth expressed her displeasure, stating that faithful subjects should not "gaze upon foreign crowns," and committed him to the Fleet. After various arrests and accusations of conspiracy against the government, he was eventually created Baron Arundell of Wardour by James I on May 4, 1605.

His son, Henry Arundell, 3rd Baron (c. 1607-1694), was a Roman Catholic and strong royalist. He recaptured Wardour Castle from parliamentarians in 1644. Following the Restoration, he became master of the horse to Henrietta Maria and was involved in secret negotiations with Louis XIV regarding the treaties of Dover in 1670. In 1678, Titus Oates accused him of participating in the popish plot. He was imprisoned in the Tower, found guilty of high treason by the Middlesex grand jury, and impeached. He was eventually released on bail in 1684, and the charges were annulled after the accession of James II on May 22, 1685.
