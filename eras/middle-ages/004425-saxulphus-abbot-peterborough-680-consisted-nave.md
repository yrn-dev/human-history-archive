# Saxulphus and the Basilica of Brixworth

## Overview of Saxulphus
Saxulphus was the abbot of Peterborough around A.D. 680. He is associated with the erection of the basilica of Brixworth, located in Northamptonshire, which serves as an example of an early Christian basilica existing in England.

## Architectural Features of Brixworth
The basilica at Brixworth is characterized by several specific structural elements:

*   **The Nave and Aisles:** The building featured a nave that was separated from its aisles by quadrangular piers. These piers supported arches constructed from Roman brick.
*   **Lighting and Chancel:** Above the nave were clerestory windows. The structure included a short chancel that terminated in an apse.
*   **The Crypt:** Similar to the design of St Peter's at Rome, a circumscribing crypt ran outside the apse. This crypt was accessed via steps leading from the chancel.
*   **The West End:** A square porch was located at the west end of the building. In later periods, the walls of this porch were extended upward to form a tower.

## Context of Early Christian Architecture in England
The archive notes that England possesses more early Christian survivals than Germany or France. The Brixworth basilica is one such survival. Other early Christian structures in England include a small basilica discovered in 1892 during the excavation of the Roman city of Silchester. 

The Silchester basilica, dating from the 4th century, followed a "basilican" type plan consisting of a nave and two aisles. In that specific instance, the building ended in a porch (narthex) at the east and an apse with two flanking chambers at the west. These flanking chambers, known as the *prothesis* and *diaconicon*, functioned as sacristies. While these features are rare in Italy, they are described as being almost universal in the churches of Syria and North Africa.
