# St Gallen Manuscript of 1416

## Discovery and Contents
In 1416, Poggio discovered a manuscript (MS.) at St Gallen. This manuscript contained the works of Quintus Asconius Pedianus, a Roman historian and grammarian who lived between 9 B.C. and A.D. 76 (or A.D. 3-88). Asconius, likely a native of Patavium (Padua) who later resided and died in Rome at the age of eighty-five, had compiled historical commentaries on the speeches of Cicero for his sons. 

These commentaries were compiled during the reigns of Nero and Claudius, utilizing various sources including the *Acta Publica* (Gazette), *commentarii* (shorthand reports or "skeletons") of unpublished speeches by Cicero, the life of Cicero written by Tiro, and the writings of Cicero's contemporaries. Other sources used by Asconius included historical writers such as Antias, Tuditanus, Atticus, Varro, and Fenestella, the latter of whom was a contemporary of Livy and a frequent subject of Asconius's criticism.

The specific works found in the St Gallen manuscript included five commentaries on Cicero's speeches: *in Pisonem*, *pro Scauro*, *pro Milone*, *pro Cornelio*, and *in toga Candida*. These notes, written in good Latin, focus primarily on antiquarian, historical, and legal matters. Additionally, the manuscript contained a commentary on Cicero's Verrine orations; however, this work is universally regarded as spurious due to its grammatical character and inferior Latinity.

## Transcripts and Preservation
The original manuscript found at St Gallen in 1416 is now lost. However, three transcripts were produced from it by the following individuals:
*   **Poggio**: His transcript is currently located in Madrid (Matritensis x. 81). This copy served as the source for later manuscripts.
*   **Zomini (Sozomenus) of Pistoia**: His transcript is held in the Forteguerri library at Pistoia (No. 37).
*   **Bartolommeo da Montpulciano**: A copy of his transcript is preserved in Florence (Laur. liv. 5).

## Context of Asconius Pedianus
The value of the notes found in the manuscript is highlighted by their chronological markers. In a note regarding the speech *pro Scauro*, Asconius refers to Longus Caecina (who died in A.D. 57) as still living, while his phrasing implies that Claudius (who died in A.D. 54) was no longer alive. Consequently, this specific statement must have been written between A.D. 54 and A.D. 57. 

Beyond the commentaries found in the St Gallen MS., other works have been attributed to Asconius, including a treatise on long life and health (possibly a symposium imitating Plato), a defence of Virgil against detractors, and a life of Sallust.
