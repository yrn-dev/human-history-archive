# Boniface IX

## Early Life and Elevation
Boniface IX, born Piero Tomacelli, was a native of Naples. He came from a family described as ancient but poor. His ascent within the ecclesiastical hierarchy included being created a cardinal by Pope Urban VI. Following the tenure of Urban VI, Tomacelli was elected as his successor on November 2, 1389.

## Papal Administration and Territorial Authority
During his pontificate, which lasted from 1389 to 1404, Boniface IX worked to restore Roman authority across the majority of the papal states. In 1398, he successfully ended the republican liberties of the city of Rome. His efforts also extended to Naples; he managed to bring the city back to Roman obedience, as it had previously owed spiritual allegiance to the antipopes Benedict XIII and Clement VII of Avignon.

In terms of ecclesiastical matters, Boniface IX canonized Birgitta of Sweden in 1391. He also observed the regular jubilee in 1390 and held a more informal jubilee in 1400, the latter of which proved to be more profitable.

## Political Relations and the Great Schism
Boniface IX's reign was marked by the ongoing challenges of the Great Schism. He engaged in negotiations to heal the division, though these efforts yielded no results. In 1403, he took the step of confirming the election of Rupert and the deposition of Emperor Wenceslaus.

## Character and Financial Practices
Contemporaries of Boniface IX trusted his moral character and his prudence, despite noting that he possessed an inferior education. However, his administration was not without criticism. He was justly accused of nepotism. Furthermore, when facing financial difficulties, he resorted to the sale of offices. In 1399, he transformed annates into a permanent tax. Despite these actions, sources suggest he was probably not personally avaricious.

## Death
Boniface IX died on October 1, 1404. At the time of his death, he was still under sixty years of age.
