# Ak-Kuyunli ("White Sheep") Mongols

The Ak-Kuyunli, known as the "White Sheep" Mongols, were a political entity that established control over Bagdad during the 15th and early 16th centuries. Their rise to power was characterized by the displacement of previous regional rulers in the area.

## Rise to Power and Rule in Bagdad
The Ak-Kuyunli came to prominence under the leadership of Uzun Hasan, who is also referred to as Cassim. Around the year 1468, Uzun Hasan and the Ak-Kuyunli drove out the descendants of Kara Yusuf. Kara Yusuf had been the leader of the Kara-Kuyunli, or "Black Sheep" Mongols, who had previously seized Bagdad in 1417 after expelling Sultan Ahmed Ben Avis.

Following their victory over the Kara-Kuyunli, Uzun Hasan and his descendants maintained their reign in Bagdad for several decades.

## End of Ak-Kuyunli Dominion
The rule of the Ak-Kuyunli in Bagdad concluded when the city was captured by Shah Ismail I. Shah Ismail I was the founder of the Safawid royal house of Persia, and he became master of the city approximately between 1502 and 1508.

## Subsequent History of Bagdad
After the fall of the Ak-Kuyunli, Bagdad became a long-term object of contention between the Persians and the Turks. The city was taken by Suleiman I the Magnificent and later retaken by Shah Abbas the Great in 1620. In 1638, Sultan Murad IV besieged the city with an army consisting of 300,000 men. Despite obstinate resistance, the city was forced to surrender, resulting in the massacre of most of the inhabitants in defiance of the terms of capitulation.
