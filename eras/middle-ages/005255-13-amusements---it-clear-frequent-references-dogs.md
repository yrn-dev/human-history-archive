# Amusements and Leisure Activities

The nature of amusements and leisure varies significantly across different cultures and historical periods, ranging from the pursuit of sport and the arts to the highly stylized treatment of animals.

## Hunting, Games, and the Arts
In certain historical contexts, such as those involving kings and their retinues, hunting and falconry were keenly pursued, as evidenced by frequent references to hawks and dogs in charters. While indoor and outdoor games are mentioned less frequently, the use of dice, known as *taefl*, was widespread. 

Courtly life also placed a high value on the arts. Much time was dedicated to poetic recitation, which was often accompanied by music. Accomplished poets were granted liberal rewards for their work. The harp (*hearpe*) served as the chief musical instrument, though the flute (*pipe*) and later the fiddle (*fiðele*) were also used. Trumpets, including the *horn*, *swegelhorn*, and *byme*, were primarily utilized as signals.

## Performance and Spectacle in Kamakura
In Kyoto, the Dengaku mime became popular among all social classes. This form of entertainment was brought to Kamakura by the regent Takatoki, who summoned rival troupes of Dengaku players and watched their performances regardless of the time. Takatoki distributed these performers among noble families related to the Hojo, who then competed to provide the actors with magnificent costumes. During banquets featuring Dengaku mimes, the regent and his guests would compete by throwing their robes into a heap, which were later redeemed for heavy sums of money given to the actors.

## Animal-Based Amusements
The use of dogs for entertainment reached an extreme level under the regent Takatoki in Kamakura. After being amused by a spontaneous dog fight in his garden, Takatoki ordered that dogs be collected as taxes. This led provincial residents to breed dogs in large numbers to present them to Kamakura. These animals were treated with extreme luxury: they were fed on fowl and fish, kept in kennels adorned with silver and gold, and transported in palanquins to take the air. 

The city of Kamakura eventually housed between four and five thousand of these dogs, which were dressed in brocades and tinsel. When these dogs were carried through public streets, farmers were required to act as bearers for the sedan-chairs, and people on business had to kneel in obeisance and dismount. Dog-fights were held twelve days every month, attended by the regent, nobles, and the general public.

## Canine Breeds and Utility
Beyond organized spectacle, dogs have been bred for specific utilities and companionship. Mastiffs, which are powerful and heavily built, have been employed as watchdogs or for fighting. Spaniels, characterized by high intelligence and wide skulls, include various breeds:
* **Field Spaniels:** Used as shooting dogs to signal the proximity of game, including the Clumber (silent when hunting), the Sussex (noisier with a golden coat), and the Cocker.
* **Water Spaniels:** Notably the Irish breeds, which possess oily coats and broad splay feet.
* **Pet Spaniels:** Including the Blenheim and King Charles.

Other specialized breeds include the Dalmatian (or coach dog), a lightly built pointer; the bull-terrier, a pugnacious cross between a bulldog and a smooth terrier; and the pugdog, a dwarf race kept solely as a pet. Terriers are noted for their high educability and agile build, with types ranging from the compact smooth fox-terrier to the long-haired Skye terrier.
