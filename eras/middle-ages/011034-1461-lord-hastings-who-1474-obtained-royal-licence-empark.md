# Lord Hastings and Ashby-de-la-Zouch

## Acquisition of the Manor
In the 15th century, the manor of Ashby-de-la-Zouch was held by James Butler, the earl of Ormond. Following the attainder of James Butler, the manor was granted in 1461 to Lord Hastings. 

## Development and Fortification
In 1474, Lord Hastings obtained a royal licence that permitted him to build and fortify a castle. Additionally, this licence granted him the authority to empark 3000 acres of land.

## History of the Castle
The castle constructed by Lord Hastings served various roles over the following centuries:
*   **Royal Detention:** In 1569, Mary queen of Scots was detained at the castle under the custody of the earls of Shrewsbury and Huntingdon.
*   **The Civil War:** During the Civil War, the castle was held for the king and fortified by Colonel Henry Hastings. King Charles visited the site in 1645.
*   **Dismantling:** Following the conclusion of the war in 1648, the castle was dismantled by order of parliament.

The castle is also noted for playing a great part in the work *Ivanhoe* by Sir Walter Scott.

## History of Ashby-de-la-Zouch
The settlement was previously known as Essebi or Ashby before the family of la Zouch, from whom the estate passed via female descent after being held by Robert Beaumeis, added the adjunct to its name. At the time of the Domesday survey, the area formed part of the estates of Hugh de Grentmaisnel.

Market rights for the area were recorded as early as 1219, when Roger la Zouch was granted a two days' fair at the feast of St Helen and a weekly market in exchange for a fine of one palfrey. By the 18th century, Ashby had become celebrated as one of the best markets for horses in England. The town also hosted prosperous factories producing hats as well as cotton and woollen stockings.
