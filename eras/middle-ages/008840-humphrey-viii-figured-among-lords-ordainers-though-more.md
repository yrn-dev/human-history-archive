## The Bohun Family
The Bohuns were a family of significance in English history during the 13th and 14th centuries, originating from a village in the Cotentin located between the estuary of the Vire and Coutances. They arrived in England at or shortly after the Norman Conquest.

Humphrey III is credited as the founder of the family's greatness. He served as a *dapifer* (steward) in the royal household during the later years of Henry I. During the reign of Stephen, Humphrey III held an ambiguous position, initially siding with the king before shifting his support to the empress. He remained loyal to Henry II and fought against the rebels of East Anglia at Farnham in 1173. He lived until 1187.

Humphrey III was succeeded in the family estates by his grandson, Henry, as Humphrey had outlived his eldest son, Humphrey IV. Henry obtained the earldom of Hereford from John in 1199, aided by his mother Margaret's connection to the royal house of Scotland (she was the sister of William the Lion). Henry de Bohun was one of twenty-five barons elected to enforce the terms of the Great Charter. During a subsequent civil war, he fought for Louis and was captured at the battle of Lincoln in 1217; he died on a pilgrimage on June 1, 1220.

Humphrey V, the son and heir of Henry, returned to royal loyalty and inherited the earldom of Essex from his maternal uncle, William de Mandeville, before 1239. However, by 1258, Humphrey V shifted his allegiance to the baronial cause. He served as a nominee of the opposition on a committee of twenty-four appointed during the Oxford parliament of 1258 to reform the administration. He eventually returned to royal allegiance following the alliance between Montfort and Llewelyn of North Wales.

## The Earldom of Buckingham
The earldom of Buckingham has an obscure origin, though some charter evidence suggests it existed under William Rufus. Walter Giffard, lord of Longueville in Normandy, is regarded as the first earl, described as "Comes Bucchingehamensis" in 1097 and 1102. After the death of the 2nd earl, Walter Giffard, in 1164, Richard de Clare (earl of Pembroke) assumed the title through his wife, Rohais, but the title died with him in 1176.

The title was revived in 1377 when Thomas of "Woodstock," the duke of Gloucester, was created earl of Buckingham during the coronation of Richard II. After Thomas's death, the title of Gloucester went to Thomas le Despenser, while his son, Humphrey, held only the title of earl of Buckingham. Following Humphrey's death, his sister Anne became countess of Buckingham. She married Edmund Stafford, earl of Stafford. Upon Anne's death in 1438, the title passed to her son, Humphrey Stafford, who was created duke of Buckingham in 1444. This title remained with the Stafford family until 1521, when Edward, the 3rd duke, was executed and attainted.
