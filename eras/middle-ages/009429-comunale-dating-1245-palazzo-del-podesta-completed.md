# Palazzo del Podestà and Communal Buildings

The archive contains records of various communal and administrative palaces across several Italian cities, including structures designated as the Palazzo del Podestà and the Palazzo Comunale. These buildings served as seats of local government, judicial courts, or residences for officials.

## Palazzo del Podestà in Florence
In Florence, the Palazzo del Podestà is noted for its association with the painter Andrea del Castagno. In 1435, Castagno was commissioned to paint the fallen leaders of the Albizzi and Peruzzi within this building. Due to this specific commission, Castagno became commonly known as "Andrea (or Andreino) degl' Impiccati," meaning "of the Hanged Men." While some accounts have incorrectly attributed these paintings to the men of the Pazzi conspiracy, that event did not occur until 1478, which was long after the painter's death in 1457.

## Communal Palaces in Other Regions
Various other cities maintained communal palaces for administrative and civic functions:

*   **Corneto Tarquinia:** In this town, which was an independent commune for a period, the oldest sections of the Palazzo Comunale date from approximately 1000.
*   **Ascoli Piceno:** The original Palazzo del Comune is located in a picturesque colonnaded market-place. This building is Gothic in style with Renaissance additions and currently serves as the prefecture.
*   **Imola:** The Palazzo del Comune in Imola houses works of art, including two pieces by the Bolognese school painter Innocenzo Francucci (also known as Innocenzo da Imola), who lived from 1494 to 1549.
*   **Genoa:** Records mention the Palazzo del Capitan del Popolo, which later became the Palazzo del Comune al Mare. This site served as the office for the Customs (Dogana) and, from the 15th century, was known as the Palazzo di S. Giorgio or Casa di S. Giorgio.

## Related Administrative Structures
Beyond the communal palaces, other secular buildings served similar official capacities. In one unnamed city, the early Renaissance Palazzo Pretorio served as the former residence of the podestà and currently functions as the seat of the correctional and civil courts. Additionally, the Palazzo del Tribunale in Cosenza serves as the location for the law courts.
