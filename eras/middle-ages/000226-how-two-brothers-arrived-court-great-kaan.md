# The Journey of the Two Brothers to the Court of the Great Kaan

The journey of the two brothers, Nicolo and Maffeo Polo, to the court of the Great Kaan was a protracted expedition involving trade, diplomatic encounters, and long periods of residence in foreign cities. Starting from Constantinople in 1260, the brothers embarked on a trading venture to the Crimea. Their travels subsequently led them north along the Volga.

## Arrival in Bokara
After passing through a desert, the brothers reached Bokara, described as the best city in all Persia. The city belonged to a king named Barac. Upon arriving, the brothers found themselves unable to proceed further or return, leading them to reside in Bokara for three years.

During their stay, they encountered envoys from Alau, Lord of the Levant, who were traveling to the court of the Great Kaan, the Lord of all the Tartars in the world. The envoys were amazed to see Latins in that region, as the Great Kaan had never seen any. The envoys advised the brothers that accompanying them would bring great honor and profit, promising that the Great Kaan would treat them with liberality and that they would travel with perfect security.

## Travel to the Court
Following the counsel of the envoys, the two brothers made their arrangements and set out. They journeyed for a full year, traveling in a northward and north-eastward direction before reaching the court of the Prince. During this year of travel, they witnessed many marvels of various kinds.

## Reception by the Great Kaan
Upon their arrival, the Great Kaan received the two brothers with great hospitality and honor. He expressed significant pleasure at their visit and engaged them in an extensive series of inquiries. The Kaan first questioned them regarding the emperors of their lands, specifically asking how they administered justice, maintained their dignity, and conducted themselves in battle. He sought similar information regarding other kings, princes, and potentates.

The Kaan further inquired about the customs of the Latins, the Church, the Pope of Rome, and the activities occurring in Rome. Because the two brothers were well-versed in the Tartar language, they were able to provide the Kaan with truthful and orderly accounts of these matters.

## Subsequent Missions and Return
Following their initial visit, the Great Kaan sent the two brothers as his envoys to the Pope, providing them with a tablet of gold bearing his orders on their behalf. Their return journey took them to the city of Acre and eventually back to Venice.

The brothers later departed Venice once more to return to the Great Kaan, this time accompanied by Nicolo's son, Mark. Upon arriving at the Imperial Palace, they prostrated themselves before the Sovereign. The Kaan welcomed them with great rejoicing and honor. The party presented the Kaan with letters and credentials from the Pope, as well as Oil from the Sepulchre, which the Kaan valued highly. During this meeting, Nicolo introduced his son Mark to the Emperor, who welcomed him as a liegeman.

## Departure from the Kaan
When the time came for the party to return to their own country, the Great Kaan provided Nicolo, Maffeo, and Mark with two golden Tablets of Authority. These tablets ensured liberty of passage throughout his dominions and guaranteed that all necessary provisions would be supplied for them and their company. Additionally, the Kaan entrusted them with messages for the kings of Christendom, including the kings of England, France, and Spain. To facilitate their return, the Kaan equipped thirteen ships, each featuring four masts and often spreading twelve sails.
