# Ashburton

## Overview and Governance
Ashburton, also known as Essebretona, Asperton, or Ashperton, is an ancient stannary town and a borough by prescription. Historically, the town was governed by a bailiff and a portreeve, both of whom were elected on an annual basis at the court leet conducted by the lord of the manor.

## Land Ownership and Manorial History
According to the Domesday survey, Ashburton was held in chief by Osbern, the bishop of Exeter, and rendered geld for six hides. In 1552, the bishop sold the two manors of Ashburton Foreign and Ashburton Borough, after which they became property of the crown. Eventually, the manors were acquired in moieties by the Clinton family. In the present time, Sir Robert Jardine and Lord Clinton serve as joint lords of the manor.

## Parliamentary Representation
Ashburton's history as a parliamentary borough saw fluctuating representation. The town returned two members in 1298 and 1407. From 1407 until 1640, it returned only one member, before returning to two members. This representation was reduced by the Reform Act of 1832, which deprived the town of one member, and subsequently by the Reform Act of 1885, which deprived it of the other.

## Markets and Fairs
The town's commercial activity was expanded through various grants. During the reign of Edward II, Bishop Stapledon obtained two annual fairs lasting three days—one at the feast of St Martin in winter (November 11) and another at the feast of St Laurence (August 10)—as well as a Saturday market. 

Later, in 1672, a Tuesday market was granted to John Ford specifically for the sale of woollen goods and wool made from English yarn. Additionally, Andrew Quicke obtained two annual fairs in 1705 for the sale of merchandise, corn, and cattle, held on the first Thursdays of June and March.
