# Chronica gentis Scotorum

The *Chronica gentis Scotorum* is a historical work authored by John of Fordun. A notable edition of this text was edited by W. F. Skene and published in Edinburgh between 1871 and 1872.

## Historical Content and Traditions
The *Chronica gentis Scotorum* is cited as a source regarding the history of the Baliol family. According to a tradition described as late and dubious, the family name was abandoned by its owners in favor of the form "Baillie." This change was allegedly prompted by the name becoming discredited due to the pusillanimous conduct of Edward Baliol and John Baliol.

## Related Historical Works and Authors
The *Chronica gentis Scotorum* exists within a broader context of Scottish historical writing. Other works concerning the history of the Scots include:

*   **The Orygynale Cronykil of Scotland**: Written by Andrew of Wyntoun and edited by David Laing (1872-1879).
*   **Historia ecclesiastica gentis Scotorum**: Published in Bologna in 1627 by Dempster. In this work, Dempster utilized his versatility in biography, history, law, criticism, and philology to argue that several figures—including Boniface, Alcuin, Bernard (Sapiens), and Joannes Scotus Erigena—were Scots. Dempster further claimed that Boadicea was a Scottish author, though these assertions have been criticized as absurd exaggerations resulting from personal and patriotic vanity.

## Associated Scholars and Editors
W. F. Skene, who edited the *Chronica gentis Scotorum*, was also involved in other significant works of Scottish and Celtic history. His contributions include the editing of the *Chronicles of the Picts and Scots* (Edinburgh, 1867, edited 1876) and the authorship of *Celtic Scotland* (Edinburgh, 1877). Additionally, Skene edited the *Four Ancient Books of Wales* (Edinburgh, 1868).
