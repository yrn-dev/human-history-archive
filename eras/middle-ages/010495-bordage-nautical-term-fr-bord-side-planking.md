# BORDAGE

The term bordage possesses two distinct meanings within the archive's records: one pertaining to nautical construction and the other to feudal land tenure.

## Nautical Definition
In a nautical context, bordage refers to the planking located on the side of a ship. The term is derived from the French word *bord*, meaning "side."

The importance of ship planking is illustrated in the *Heimskringla Chronicle of the Kings of Norway*. In one account, a ship of war was being planked on both sides. During the construction process, an individual named Thorberg absented himself for a period of time. Upon his return, the vessel was found to be destroyed because someone had cut a series of deep notches from stem to stern down one side of the planking. 

The king, outraged by this act of envy, threatened the perpetrator with death but offered a great reward to whoever could identify the culprit. Thorberg confessed to the act himself. As punishment, the king commanded Thorberg to restore the planking to its original condition under threat of death. Thorberg complied by chipping the planks until the deep notches were smoothed and made even with the rest of the hull. The king and those present determined that the ship was actually more handsome on the side Thorberg had chipped, and the king subsequently ordered the other side to be shaped in the same manner. Following this, Thorberg served as the master builder until the completion of the vessel.

This specific ship, named *Serpent the Long*, was a dragon ship built to be larger and more carefully constructed than a previous ship captured in Halogaland, which the king called *Serpent the Short*. The *Serpent the Long* featured thirty-four benches for rowers, gilt bulwarks as high as those on sea-going ships, and a gilt head and arched tail. It was regarded as the most costly and best ship ever produced in Norway.

## Feudal Definition
In a feudal context, bordage refers to a specific form of tenure. The term is derived from the Latin word *borda*, meaning "a cottage." This tenure was utilized by a certain class of villeins to hold their cottages.

A "bordar" (referred to in Medieval Latin as *bardarius*) was a villein who received a cottage from his lord. In exchange for this cottage, the bordar was required to provide menial services. The term bordage thus encompasses both the system of tenure itself and the specific services that were due from these bordars.
