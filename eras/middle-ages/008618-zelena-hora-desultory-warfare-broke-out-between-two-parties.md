# Zelena Hora

Zelena Hora, also known as Grunberg, is a location associated with significant political and literary events in Bohemian history. It served as the site of a major noble confederacy during the 15th century and is linked to disputed early records of the Bohemian language.

## The League of Zelena Hora
In 1465, Zelena Hora was the site of a meeting where many nobles of the Roman religion formed a confederacy against King George of Podebrad. This opposition arose after a period of initial prosperity in George's reign, during which he had been supported by the towns and the smaller nobility. However, Romanist nobles eventually became hostile toward the king, contributing to a protracted struggle between George and the papal see.

The tension escalated after George assembled the estates at Prague in 1452, where he declared his commitment to the communion in both kinds and his willingness to risk his crown and life to defend his faith. Following the formation of the league at Zelena Hora, Pope Paul II provided moral support to the confederates in 1466 by excommunicating George of Podebrad and releasing all Bohemians from their oaths of allegiance to him.

## Conflict and the Hungarian Intervention
The struggle involving the league of Zelena Hora was further complicated by the intervention of King Matthias of Hungary. Although Matthias had previously been a firm ally of King George—having influenced George's coronation via the Romanist bishop of Waitzen—he deserted this alliance under papal influence to support the lords of the league.

This alignment led to a period of desultory warfare between the two parties. While George was initially successful in these conflicts, the tide turned when King Matthias invaded Moravia and took possession of its capital, Brunn. On May 3, 1469, at a meeting of Catholic nobles in Olmutz, Moravia, Matthias was proclaimed king of Bohemia. Although George achieved some further successes against his rival, the war was temporarily halted by George's death in 1471.

## Literary Records
Zelena Hora is also mentioned in the context of the Bohemian or Czech language. The archive contains references to the so-called MSS. (manuscripts) of Zelena Hora (Grunberg). However, these records are noted to be almost certainly forgeries created during the early part of the 19th century.
