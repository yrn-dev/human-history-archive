# Al-Bukhari

Mahommed ibn Isma'il al-Bukhari (810-872) was an Arabic author recognized for producing the most generally accepted collection of traditions (*hadith*) from Mahomet. Born in A.H. 194 (A.D. 810) in Bokhara to an Iranian family, he became a prominent authority on the learning of traditions.

## Early Life and Education
Al-Bukhari distinguished himself early in his life through his ability to learn traditions by heart. At the age of sixteen, while accompanying his family on a pilgrimage to Mecca, he expanded his knowledge by gathering additions from authorities encountered along the route. By the age of eighteen, he had dedicated himself to the systematic process of arranging, testing, sifting, and collecting traditions.

To further his work, al-Bukhari traveled extensively throughout the Moslem world, visiting regions ranging from Egypt to Samarkand. According to accounts, he learned three hundred thousand traditions—both true and false—from more than a thousand men. His contemporaries regarded his speed and power of memory as miraculous.

## Theological and Legal Views
In terms of his theological position, al-Bukhari was anti-rationalistic and conservative. He maintained a friendship with and earned the respect of Ahmad Ibn Hanbal. Regarding matters of law, he appears to have been a Shafi'ite.

## The Sahih
After spending sixteen years away from his birthplace, al-Bukhari returned to Bokhara to compile his *Sahih*. This work is a collection of 7,275 tested traditions. He arranged the material into chapters to provide a foundation for a complete system of jurisprudence that did not rely on speculative law. This was the first book of its kind.

The *Sahih* has attained a status of quasi-canonicity within Islam and is treated almost like the Koran. The influence of the work persisted long after al-Bukhari's death; for example, in 1628, the Arabian historian Maqqari delivered a course of lectures on Bukhari's collection of traditions while in Damascus.

## Death and Legacy
Al-Bukhari died in A.H. 256 while in banishment at Kartank, which is a suburb of Samarkand. His legacy continues through his writings and the veneration of his burial site, where solemn pilgrimages are made and prayers are believed to be heard.
