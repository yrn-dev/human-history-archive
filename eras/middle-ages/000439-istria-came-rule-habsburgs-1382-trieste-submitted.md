# Istria and Trieste under Habsburg Rule

## Early Transitions of Power
Before the establishment of Habsburg dominance in the region, Istria was among the possessions of Premysl Ottakar II, the king of Bohemia. During his reign, Ottakar's power reached its peak, and his dominions extended from the Giant Mountains in Bohemia to the Adriatic, encompassing Istria, Carinthia, and portions of northern Italy. 

The transition to Habsburg control began with the election of Rudolph, count of Habsburg, as king of the Romans (and German king) in 1273. Rudolph challenged Ottakar's titles to several duchies and sought to recover imperial lands. Following a period of conflict, Ottakar submitted to Rudolph in November 1276, renouncing the duchies of Styria, Carinthia, and Austria. While Rudolph initially administered these lands as head of the Empire, he worked to make them hereditary possessions for his family, moving the Habsburg headquarters from the Rhine to the Danube. On December 27, 1282, Rudolph invested his sons, Albert and Rudolph, with the duchies of Styria and Austria.

## Administrative Structure and the Küstenland
In later periods of the Austrian empire, Istria and Trieste were integrated into a specific administrative grouping known as the *Küstenland* (coast-land or littoral). This grouping consisted of three crown-lands: Istria, Trieste, and Görz and Gradisca. By 1900, the combined population of these three lands was 755,183, covering an area of 3,084 square miles. 

For administrative purposes, these territories were united under the governor of Trieste. The financial and legal authorities based in Trieste exercised jurisdiction over the entire littoral region. In the allotment of political constituencies, these areas were represented separately; for example, Istria and Trieste (and territory) were listed as distinct entities in the empire's administrative records.

## The Illyrian Provinces and the Kingdom of Illyria
The political status of the region shifted significantly during the Napoleonic era. In 1809, the name "Illyrian Provinces" was revived to describe a territory that included Istria, Trieste, Fiume, Görz and Gradisca, Carniola, Dalmatia, and parts of Croatia and Carinthia. These lands were ceded by Austria to Italy on October 14, 1809, through the peace of Schönbrunn. Under this arrangement, the provinces were governed in the interest of Napoleon and occupied by French troops. The republic of Ragusa was later annexed to these provinces in 1811.

French occupation ceased to be effective around the end of 1813, and the provinces reverted to Austrian control. In 1816, the Kingdom of Illyria was constituted. This kingdom, which remained a part of the Austrian crown until 1849, was formed from the crown-lands of Trieste, Istria, Carniola, Görz and Gradisca, and Carinthia.

## Nationalist Movements and Irredentism
In the later years of Austrian rule, the Italian-speaking populations of Istria and Trieste became centers of nationalist sentiment. A group known as the *Italianissimi* specifically desired separation from Austria with the goal of annexation to Italy. This sentiment was more pronounced in Istria and Trieste than in other Italian-speaking regions like Tirol.

These desires were championed by a political party in Italy called the "Irredentists," who demanded that Trieste, Istria, and South Tirol be joined to Italy. This movement led to significant unrest, including public meetings and riots within Italy, where the Austrian embassy in Rome was insulted and the Austrian flag was torn down from the consulate in Venice. The instability spread across the border, resulting in riots in Trieste. In response, the Austrian government moved troops into Tirol to signal its determination not to surrender any territory. For a period, there was concern that the Italian government might attempt to realize these territorial goals through an alliance with Russia, though this danger eventually passed.
