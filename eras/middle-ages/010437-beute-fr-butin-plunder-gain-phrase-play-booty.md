# Booty

## Etymology and Definition
The term "booty" refers to gain or plunder. It is apparently influenced by the Old English word *bot*, meaning profit or advantage, and is an adaptation from an earlier form cognate with the French *butin* and German *Beute*. 

In a specific idiomatic sense dating from the 16th century, the phrase "to play booty" describes the act of playing intentionally poorly at the start of a contest to deceive an opponent, or playing into the hands of a confederate.

## Military Conquest and Plunder
Throughout history, the acquisition of booty has been a frequent outcome of military engagements, raids, and the capturing of settlements.

### Norse Expeditions
Norse leaders frequently engaged in campaigns to secure booty across various regions:
*   **Spain and Africa:** King Sigurd fought multiple battles in Spain. He captured a castle called Sintre, occupied by heathens who plundered Christians; after killing the inhabitants who refused baptism, he obtained an "immense booty." He later won a third battle at the city of Lisbon and a fourth at a town called Alkasse, where he killed so many people the town was left empty, resulting in further immense booty. Additionally, Harald, leading Varings and Latin men, traveled to Africa (known to the Varings as Serkland), where he took eighty castles and consistently gained victories and booty.
*   **Europe and the Baltic:** King Olaf plundered and burnt the merchant town of Varrande in Poitou and operated along the Loire, where the town of Partheney was left in ashes. Earl Hakon sailed into the Baltic to plunder during the summer months.
*   **Other Regions:** Olaf gained a large booty after victory over country people on the island of Borgundarholm (Bornholm). Harald Grayskin forayed into Bjarmaland, fighting a battle on the banks of the Vina (Dwina), where he wasted and burned the land to make "enormous booty."

### Classical Antiquity
In Greek and Carthaginian conflicts, booty was often seized from the defeated:
*   **Athenian Victories:** During a battle against the Persian army, Athenian heavy-armed troops defeated the Persians, gaining an "immense booty" by plundering the bodies of the slain and the Persian tents.
*   **Selinuntine Conflict:** The Selinuntines invaded the territory of Egesta and engaged in disorderly plunder. However, they were surprised and defeated by the Egestæans and Carthaginians, who recaptured the entire booty.
*   **Internal Military Outrages:** In Kerasus, a captain named Klearetus persuaded his company to plunder a Kolchian village that had previously maintained peaceful relations and provided a friendly market. Klearetus intended to steal the booty using one of the vessels, but he was slain when the attack was repelled.

## Disputes and Distribution of Gain
The distribution of captured wealth often led to conflict between allies or leaders.

### The Conflict of King Margad and Guthorm
Following an expedition to Bretland that yielded immense booty, King Margad attempted to claim the entirety of the silver for himself, disregarding his friendship with Guthorm. Margad demanded that Guthorm either accept the king's determination or fight for the booty, while also demanding Guthorm's ships. 

Guthorm, refusing to suffer such loss and disgrace, prayed for aid from God and his uncle, Saint Olaf. He vowed to give one-tenth of any booty gained to the holy man's house. After winning the battle and killing King Margad and his followers, Guthorm returned home with the booty. He fulfilled his vow by giving every tenth penny to Saint Olaf's shrine; the silver was so plentiful that Guthorm had a seven-foot-high image made from it.
