# Historical Writers of Bohemia

Bohemia has been a highly fruitful region for the production of historical writers, spanning from early chroniclers to modern academic scholars. The development of this literary tradition has been influenced by the country's political shifts, religious conflicts, and the evolution of the national language.

## Early and Renaissance Historians
During the 16th century, a period of comparative tranquillity allowed native literature to develop extensively. Three historians of merit emerged during this era. Wenceslas Hajek of Libocan, a priest who died in 1553, is the most well-known. His major work, *Kronika ceska*, was dedicated to Emperor Ferdinand I. Because it was produced under the auspices of government officials, the work possesses a strong Romanist and dynastic tendency. While it was translated into several languages and frequently quoted, it is noted for being grossly partial and inaccurate.

In contrast, Bartos (also known as Bartholomew or "pisar"), who served as the secretary for the city of Prague and died in 1539, wrote the *Chronicle of Prague*. This work is considered to have great historical value, particularly in its description of the troubles in Prague and Bohemia during the reign of King Louis. A third writer, Sixt of Ottersdorf (1500-1583), authored the *Memorials of the Troubled Years 1546 and 1547*, which details the unsuccessful Bohemian rising against Ferdinand I of Austria.

## The Bohemian Brethren
The community of the Bohemian Brethren, indirectly founded by Chelcicky, exerted a significant influence on Bohemian literature. Their historical and theological works were primarily written in the national language, which helped refine and polish the tongue. 

Among the historians of the brotherhood was Wenceslas Brezan, who wrote a *History of the House of Rosenberg*; however, only the biographies of Peter and William of Rosenberg have survived. Fragments of Blahoslav's *History of the Unity* also exist. Much of the brotherhood's historical output perished in the 17th century during the destruction of anti-Roman books in Bohemia. Other notable literary contributions from the community include the *Bible of Kralice* (1593) and the works of John Amos Komensky (Comenius), specifically his allegorical tale *Labyrinth of the World*.

## Modern Historical Scholarship
In more recent eras, Bohemia has continued to produce prolific historical writers. Wenceslas Tomek (1818-1905) authored many historical works, the most important of which is *Dejepis miesta Prahy* (history of the town of Prague). 

Other significant contributors to the field include:
* **Jaroslav Goll (b. 1846):** Author of numerous works, with a particular focus on the community of the Bohemian Brethren.
* **Dr. Anton Rezek:** A writer of important historical studies who co-founded the *Cesky Casopis Historicky* (Bohemian Historical Review) in 1895 alongside Professor Goll.
* **Professor Joseph Kalousek:** A scholar of the early history of Bohemia and the author of a study on the ancient constitution (*Statni pravo*) of Bohemia.
* **Dr. Vaclav Flajshans:** A publisher of studies regarding the writings and life of John Huss.
* **Professors Pic and Niederle:** Authors of learned archaeological studies focusing on the earliest periods of Bohemian history.

Additionally, modern histories of Bohemian literature have been written in the national language by Mr. Jaroslav Vlaek, Dr. Vaclav Flajshans, and Dr. Karel Tieftrunk.
