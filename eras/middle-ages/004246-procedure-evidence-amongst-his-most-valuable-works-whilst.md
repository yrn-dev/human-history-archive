# Bartolus de Saxoferrato

## Life and Academic Career
Bartolus (1314–1357) was a prominent Italian jurist and a professor of civil law at the university of Perugia. Born in 1314 at Sassoferrato, located in the duchy of Urbino, he is frequently referred to as Bartolus de Saxoferrato. He was the son of Franciscus Severi and a mother from the Alfani family.

His legal education began under Cinus at Perugia, and he later continued his studies at Bologna under Jacobus de Belvisio and Oldradus. In 1334, he was promoted to the degree of doctor of civil law. Bartolus's professional reputation grew significantly following his appointment to a chair of civil law at the university of Perugia in 1343. Through his many years of lecturing, he elevated the law school of Perugia to a status equal to that of Bologna.

## Legal Methodology and Contributions
Bartolus is recognized as the most famous master of the dialectical school of jurists. While some writers suggest he introduced the dialectical method of teaching law, the archive notes that this method had been used in the previous century by Odofredus, a pupil of Accursius. However, the successors of Odofredus had abused the method, creating writings that were often unprofitable due to an excess of dialectical forms. Bartolus's merit lay in his ability to employ the dialectical method advantageously as a teacher while discouraging its abuse.

Beyond the dialectical method, Bartolus is credited with reviving the exegetical system of teaching law, which had been neglected since Accursius rose to ascendancy. He provided this system with new life and integrated a practical interest into his teaching. This practicality was derived from his own judicial experience, gained while serving as an assessor to the courts at Pisa and Todi prior to his appointment as a professor.

## Major Works and Death
Among his most valuable written contributions are the treatises *On Procedure* and *On Evidence*. Additionally, he authored a *Commentary on the Code of Justinian*, which has been regarded as significant in some countries.

Bartolus died in 1357 at Perugia. He was interred in the church of San Francisco, where a magnificent monument marks his burial with the simple inscription "Ossa Bartoli."
