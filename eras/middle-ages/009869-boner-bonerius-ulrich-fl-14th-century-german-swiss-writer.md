# BONER (or BONERIUS), ULRICH

Ulrich Boner, also referred to as Bonerius, was a German-Swiss writer of fables who flourished during the 14th century. Born in Bern, he was a member of an old Bernese family.

## Life and Clerical Status
Records mention Boner between the years 1324 and 1349, though there is no mention of him before or after this period. Regarding his professional and religious life, it is believed that he took clerical orders and became a monk. However, because evidence indicates that he later married, it is certain that he only received the "tonsure." This status entitled him to the benefit of the *clerici uxoriati*, a designation that allowed individuals to return to secular life after divesting themselves of their clerical garb.

## Literary Work: Der Edelstein
Boner wrote a collection of fables in Middle High German titled *Der Edelstein*, completed around 1349. The collection consists of one hundred fables. In composing this work, Boner relied principally on the *Anonymus* (edited by I. Nevelet in 1610) and the works of Avianus, who wrote in the 4th century. 

Boner is noted for treating his source materials with originality and considerable freedom. His writing is characterized by a clear and simple style. While the collection maintains a necessarily didactic tone, Boner relieved this through the use of touches of humour.

## Dedication and Publication
Boner dedicated *Der Edelstein* to Johann von Rinkenberg, a poet and Bernese patrician who served as the *advocatus* (Vogt) of Brienz and died circa 1350. The work was printed in Bamberg in 1461. It has been claimed that this publication was the first book printed in the German language.

## Scholarship and Editions
*Der Edelstein* has been the subject of several scholarly editions and translations:
* **Editions:** G.F. Benecke edited the work in Berlin (1816), and Franz Pfeiffer edited it in Leipzig (1844).
* **Translations:** K. Pannier provided a translation into modern German, published in Reclam's *Universal-Bibliothek* in Leipzig (1895).
* **Further Studies:** The work has been discussed by G.E. Lessing in *Zur Geschichte und Literatur* (*Werke*, ix.), and C. Waas authored *Die Quellen der Beispiele Boners*, published in Giessen in 1897.
