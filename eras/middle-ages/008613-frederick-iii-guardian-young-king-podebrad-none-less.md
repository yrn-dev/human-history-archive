# Frederick III

## Accession and Guardianship
Frederick III succeeded Albert II as German king and was subsequently crowned emperor. His rise to power occurred during a period of significant instability in Central Europe. Following the death of King Albert in 1439, a son named Ladislaus Posthumus was born to Albert and his wife Elizabeth. Ladislaus succeeded to the kingdoms of Bohemia and Hungary, as well as the duchy of Austria. Due to the infancy of Ladislaus, Frederick III became the regent of Austria and acted as the guardian for the young king.

In Bohemia, Ladislas remained in Austria under the guardianship of his uncle, Frederick (who was then the duke of Styria and later became Emperor Frederick III). During this time, Bohemia lacked a regular government and was characterized by constant conflicts between rival parties of the nobility.

## Administration and Challenges
The rule of Frederick III was marked by disorder and struggle. This instability is attributed in part to the feebleness of Frederick's own character and, in part, to the ambitions of his brother, Albert, who desired to share in Frederick's dignities.

Frederick's guardianship extended beyond Ladislaus; he also acted as the guardian for Sigismund of Tirol, who was a minor. However, his governance was not well-received by his subjects. The people of Tirol eventually grew weary of his administration, leading to Sigismund being declared of age in 1446. Similarly, the estates of Austria expressed discontent with his rule, which eventually culminated in an open revolt.

## Political Context in Bohemia
While Frederick III served as guardian to the young king, the Bohemian estates were navigating a complex constitutional landscape. Following the recognition of the Compacts at Iglau in Moravia, the nobility had been split into two factions: a national party led by George of Podebrad and a Roman or Austrian division led by Ulrich of Rosenberg.

These parties reached an agreement known as the "Letter of Peace" (_list mirny_). Under this agreement, signatories pledged to recognize the Compacts and support John of Rokycan as the archbishop of Prague, despite the refusal of the Church of Rome to recognize him. In exchange, the national party withdrew the candidature of Prince Casimir of Poland for the throne, which facilitated the eventual succession of Albert's heir, Ladislas. In 1446, a general meeting of the estates from Bohemia, Moravia, Silesia, and Lusatia took place, which was of exceptional importance for Bohemian constitutional history, decreeing that members of the estates be divided into three bodies called _curiae_.
