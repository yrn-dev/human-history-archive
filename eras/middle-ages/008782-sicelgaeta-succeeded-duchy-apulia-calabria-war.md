# Sicelgaeta and the Succession of Apulia and Calabria

## Overview of Sicelgaeta
Sicelgaeta was a Lombard princess who entered into a marriage with Robert Guiscard, the *dux Apuliae et Calabriae*. Through this union, she became the mother of Roger, who is described as having been born "in the purple."

## The Succession of 1085
Following the death of Robert Guiscard in 1085, a succession dispute arose regarding the duchy of Apulia and Calabria. Although Robert Guiscard had destined his eldest son, Bohemund I (also known as Marc), for the throne of Constantinople, the duchy of Apulia and Calabria was succeeded by the younger son, Roger.

## The War between Bohemund and Roger
The transition of power led to a war between the two brothers: Bohemund and Duke Roger. This conflict was eventually resolved through the mediation of Urban II. The composition of the war resulted in Bohemund being awarded Otranto along with other possessions.

## Subsequent Regional Conflicts
In 1096, Bohemund was involved in an attack on Amalfi, which had revolted against Duke Roger. Bohemund conducted this operation alongside his uncle, the count of Sicily. During this period, bands of crusaders began passing through Italy toward Constantinople. This event coincided with Bohemund's own ambitions to carve out an eastern principality, potentially realizing his father's policy of the *Drang nach Osten*.

## Regional Context of Apulia and Calabria
The territories involved in the succession—Apulia and Calabria—were regions of significant historical and geographic importance. Calabria, a peninsula between the Adriatic Sea and the Gulf of Tarentum, was known in antiquity for its fertility, pastures, olives, vines, and fruit trees. Its most north-westerly cities included Brundisium and Tarentum, the latter of which was famous for its dye-works and wool.

Historically, the region of Calabria showed evidence of prehistoric populations from the stone and early bronze ages, characterized by round tower-like *specchie* or *truddhi* and menhirs (*pietre fitte*). The name "Calabria" became the general name for the peninsula after the Roman foundation of a colony at Brundisium in 246-245 B.C. and the final subjection of Tarentum in 209 B.C.
