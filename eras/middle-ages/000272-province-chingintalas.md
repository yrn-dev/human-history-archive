# PROVINCE OF CHINGINTALAS

The province of Chingintalas is a region located at the verge of the Desert, situated geographically between the north and the north-west. It is described as having an extent of sixteen days' journey and is characterized by the presence of numerous villages and towns. The province belongs to the Great Kaan.

## Demographics and Population
The population of Chingintalas is composed of three distinct races of people:
*   Saracens
*   Idolaters
*   Nestorian Christians

## Natural Resources and Mineral Wealth
At the northern extremity of the province, there is a mountain known for containing excellent veins of ondanique and steel. This mountain is also the source of a specific substance from which the Salamander is made. Contrary to beliefs in other parts of the world that the Salamander is a beast, the text asserts that it is actually a substance found within the earth.

## Geographical Identification and Debates
The exact identification of Chingintalas has been a subject of difficulty due to vague geographical definitions and a name that is not traced in other authors. It is noted as a digression in the travels of Marco Polo, who departs from and later returns to Shachau. While Kamul lies toward the north-west, Chingintalas is positioned between the north-west and the north.

Various scholars and sources have proposed different identifications for the province:
*   **Kienkien:** Some evidence, including the presence of "steel and ondanique" and the existence of many towns and villages, suggests the region is Kienkien (also spelled Hinkin or Ghinghin).
*   **Chi-kin:** Palladius suggests it may be Chi-kin-talas ("Chi-kin plain" or valley), named after a defile and lake on the route from Kia-yü kwan to Ansi chow. However, a discrepancy exists regarding distance; Chinese accounts place Chi-kin two hundred and fifty or sixty *li* from Su-chow, whereas the text claims a ten-day journey is required.
*   **Dzungaria:** Other theories suggest the name may be a representation of *Dzegun Tala* (meaning "Left Side" or north-east side) or *Chingin Tala* (meaning "The Vast Plain").

## Regional Context
The province is situated in a region where the Great Kaan's boundary is a point of contention. While the neutral city of Karakhoja lies between the states of the Kaan and Kaidu, the country to the north and north-east of Kamul is considered to be within the Kaan's territory, which is where Chingintalas is sought.
