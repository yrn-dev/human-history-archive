# Danzig

## Historical Origins and Early Rule
The origins of Danzig are unknown, though the city is recorded as an important town as early as 997. Throughout its early history, it was held by various powers, including Denmark, Poland, Brandenburg, and Pomerania. In 1308, the city fell under the control of the Teutonic knights, a period during which Danzig experienced long-term prosperity and became one of the four primary towns of the Hanseatic League.

## Transition to Polish Sovereignty
By 1455, the Teutonic Order had become thoroughly corrupt, leading Danzig to shake off its rule. The city submitted to the king of Poland and was formally ceded to him, along with the entirety of West Prussia, through the peace of Thorn. While Danzig remained nominally subject to Poland—including representation at the election of Polish kings and in the Polish diets—it functioned as a free city. In this capacity, it governed a significant territory that included more than thirty villages.

## Conflict and Prussian Possession
Danzig suffered severe damage during various wars throughout the 17th and 18th centuries. In 1734, after declaring in favor of Stanislus Leszczynski, the city was besieged and captured by the Saxons and Russians. The first partition of Poland in 1772 resulted in Danzig being separated from the Polish kingdom, and by 1793, the city came into the possession of Prussia.

## Napoleonic Era and Restoration
During the war between Prussia and France in 1807, Marshal Lefebvre captured and bombarded the city; Lefebvre was subsequently granted the title of duke of Danzig. Following the peace of Tilsit, Napoleon declared Danzig a free town under the protection of Saxony, Prussia, and France, and restored its ancient territory. However, the presence of a French governor and the enforcement of the continental system nearly ruined the city's trade. Danzig was returned to Prussia in 1814.

## The Free City of Danzig and the League of Nations
Following later conflicts, Danzig and its immediate district were to be constituted as the "free city of Danzig" under the guarantee of the League of Nations. A President of Danzig and a high commissioner appointed by the League were tasked with drawing up a constitution in agreement with city representatives. This high commissioner was also responsible for handling initial differences between Poland and the city.

The boundaries of the city were to be delimited by a commission consisting of one representative from Poland, one from Germany, and three from the associated and allied powers.

## Relations with Poland and Germany
A convention, fixed by the principal associated and allied powers, was established between Poland and Danzig. This agreement included the following provisions:
* **Customs and Trade:** Danzig was included within Polish customs frontiers, although a free area was maintained in the port.
* **Infrastructure:** Poland was ensured the free use of all docks, waterways, and other port facilities. Poland also maintained control and administration of the through railway system within the city and the Vistula.
* **Communications and Rights:** Postal, telephonic, and telegraphic communications between Danzig and Poland were secured, and the convention provided against discrimination against Poles within the city.
* **Diplomacy:** Poland was placed in charge of the diplomatic protection of Danzig's citizens abroad and its foreign relations.

Additionally, a convention between Germany, Poland, and Danzig was to ensure suitable railroad communication across German territory on the right bank of the Vistula between Poland and Danzig, while Poland granted free passage from East Prussia to Germany. The principal allied and associated powers were also responsible for drawing up regulations to ensure East Prussia had equitable and full use of and access to the Vistula.
