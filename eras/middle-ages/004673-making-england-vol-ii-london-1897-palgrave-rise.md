# Historical Authorities on Early England and the Anglo-Saxons

The study of early English history and the Anglo-Saxon period relies upon a combination of primary ancient texts and the analysis provided by modern historical authorities. These sources collectively document the transition of Britain from its early states through the era of the Anglo-Saxon kings and up to the Norman Conquest.

## Primary Historical Authorities

The foundation of knowledge regarding early Britain and the Anglo-Saxons is built upon several key primary texts. Among the chief authorities are Gildas, who wrote *De Excidio Britanniae*, and Nennius, the author of *Historia Britonum*. Other essential early records include the *Hist. Eccl.* by Bede and the *Saxon Chronicle*.

Legal and administrative frameworks of the period are preserved in the *Anglo-Saxon Laws* and various charters, such as those found in the *Cartularium Saxonicum*. Additionally, the *Leges Wallicae* serve as a recorded legal source from the era.

## Modern Historical Scholarship

A wide array of modern scholars has synthesized these primary records to provide comprehensive histories of the English nation. Notable among these is Sir F. Palgrave, who authored *The Rise and Progress of the English Commonwealth* and a *Sketch of the “Early History of England.”* Other significant contributions include J.R. Green's *The Making of England* and J.M. Kemble's *The Saxons in England*.

The political and constitutional development of the region has been examined by W. Stubbs in *Constitutional History* and E.A. Freeman in *History of the Norman Conquest*. Further historical perspectives are provided by Sh. Turner in *History of the Anglo-Saxons* and T. Hodgkin, who wrote the *History of England from the Earliest Times to the Norman Conquest*.

## Specialized Studies in Law and Society

Beyond general histories, specialized research has focused on the social and legal structures of Anglo-Saxon life. F. Seebohm has contributed studies on both *The English Village Community* and *Tribal Custom in Anglo-Saxon Law*. The evolution of land and legal systems is further detailed in P. Vinogradoff's *The Growth of the Manor* and the work of Sir F. Pollock and F.W. Maitland in *History of English Law*.

Maitland also provided analysis of the *Domesday Book and Beyond*, while H.M. Chadwick authored *Studies on Anglo-Saxon Institutions* and *The Origin of the English Nation*.

## Material Culture and Archaeology

The physical history of early England is documented through archaeological and artistic studies. G. Baldwin Brown's *The Arts in Early England* examines the period's aesthetic contributions. Numismatic evidence is provided by C.F. Keary's *Catalogue of Anglo-Saxon Coins in the British Museum*.

Research into burial customs and ancient sites includes R.C. Neville's *Saxon Obsequies* and C. Roach Smith's *Collectanea Antiqua*. Additionally, the study of early dwellings is represented by the work of M. Heyne, including *Über die Lage und Construction der Halle Heorot* and *Deutsche Hausaltertümer*, as well as R. Henning's *Das deutsche Haus*.
