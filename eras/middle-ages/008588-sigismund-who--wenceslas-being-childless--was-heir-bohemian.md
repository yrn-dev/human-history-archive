# Sigismund and the Bohemian Succession

Sigismund was a member of the Luxemburg dynasty, which ruled over Bohemia until the dynasty became extinct upon his death in 1437. He was the brother of King Wenceslas IV, a sovereign characterized by weakness and irresolution.

## Role in the Hussite Conflict
During the reign of Wenceslas, Bohemia experienced incipient religious conflict. The influence of the Church of Rome had been weakened by attacks on clerical immorality by priests such as Waldhauser and Milic, and further alienated by a church schism involving rival pontiffs. Consequently, much of the Bohemian nation supported the cause of Huss. While Wenceslas remained irresolute, Queen Sophia openly favored Huss, who served as her confessor.

Sigismund, as king of the Romans, provided Huss with a letter of safe conduct to attend the council of Constance. However, Huss was declared a heretic and burnt on July 6, 1415. This execution triggered a civil war in Bohemia, where Huss was revered by the majority of the population. On September 2, 1415, the nobles of Moravia and Bohemia met in Prague and issued the *Protestatio Bohemorum*. In this document, they defended Huss as a "good, just and catholic man" and declared that those claiming heresy existed in Bohemia were "sons of the devil" and "liars." This protest served as a declaration of war against the Roman church and marked the start of the Hussite wars.

## Military Campaigns and the Crusade
Following the death of his brother, Sigismund claimed the Bohemian crown as his successor. His arrival at Kutna Hora was preceded by renewed rioting in Prague and the expulsion of many Germans who adhered to the Church of Rome. On March 1, 1420, Pope Martin V proclaimed a crusade against Bohemia. 

On June 30, Sigismund led a large international army, including Bohemians, to occupy the castle of Prague. The crusaders viewed the city as a heretical community because its inhabitants accepted other evangelical truths and used the sacred chalice. The attempt to conquer the city failed after the Taborites, led by Zizka, repulsed an attack on the Vitkov hill. Sigismund subsequently abandoned the siege. He suffered another defeat on November 1, 1420, during an attempt to relieve the garrison at the Vysehrad fortress.

## Reconciliation and the Compacts
After the failure of military efforts, it became obvious that Bohemia could not be conquered. While a council met at Basel to examine Hussite demands, Sigismund influenced the Germans to make one final attempt to subdue the country by force. This effort ended on August 1, 1431, when the Bohemians defeated the Germans at Domazlice.

Negotiations began at Basel in 1431, with Prokop the Great leading the Hussite embassy. By 1433, the Bohemians returned home, where political dissensions emerged between the "army of the towns" (Taborites) and the "army of the nobles" (aristocratic Hussites). These two forces clashed at Lipan near Kolin on May 30, 1434. The defeat of the Taborites and the death of their leaders, including the two Prokops, allowed the moderate party to seek reconciliation with the Church of Rome and Sigismund.

The Bohemians recognized Sigismund as their sovereign in exchange for religious concessions formulated in the Compacts. These agreements granted the Bohemians the right to preach the gospel freely and the right of communion in both kinds. Additionally, the Compacts placed certain limits on the ability of the clergy to acquire worldly goods.
