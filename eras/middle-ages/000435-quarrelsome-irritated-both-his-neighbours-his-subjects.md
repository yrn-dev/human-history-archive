# Duke Frederick II, the Quarrelsome

## Overview of the Babenberg Dynasty
Under the House of Babenberg, Austrian policy was primarily characterized by a general attitude of loyalty toward the emperors and ongoing warfare with eastern neighbors, specifically the Hungarians. The conflicts with Hungary are described as a monotonous sequence of forays, occasionally supported by the forces of the Empire, which eventually resulted in the gradual eastward advance of Austria. This traditional loyalty to the imperial house was further reinforced through several marriages between the Babenbergs and the imperial family.

## Departures from Imperial Loyalty
Despite the general trend of loyalty, certain members of the Babenberg house deviated from this policy. Margrave Leopold II deserted Emperor Henry IV during the investiture struggle. As a result, Henry IV deprived Leopold of Austria and granted it to Vratislav II, the duke of the Bohemians. However, Vratislav was unable to maintain his position and was driven out, allowing Leopold to regain possession of the mark in 1083 and subsequently reconcile with Henry.

## The Reign of Duke Frederick II
A similar conflict occurred between Emperor Frederick II and Duke Frederick II, the latter of whom was known as "the Quarrelsome." The emperor claimed certain rights in Austria, ignoring the privilege of 1156, and summoned the duke to attend his Italian diets. 

Duke Frederick II had irritated both his subjects and his neighbors. The emperor received complaints regarding the duke's confiscations and exactions. After the duke refused to appear before the princes on three separate occasions, Emperor Frederick II placed him under the ban. The emperor declared the duchies of Styria and Austria to be vacant.

## Imperial Invasion and Aftermath
In 1236, aided by the duke of Bavaria, the king of Bohemia, and other princes, the emperor invaded the country. Meeting very little opposition, the emperor imposed changes upon the constitution of Austria, declared the duchies to be immediately dependent upon the Empire, and established Vienna as an imperial city.

Following the emperor's departure, Duke Frederick II returned and was back in possession of his former power by 1239, while the imperial changes were ignored. The duke continued a career of oppression and violence until June 1246, when he was killed in battle by the Hungarians. With his death, the family of Babenberg became extinct.
