# The Great Kaan's Inquiries into Foreign Manners

The Great Kaan, a prince of immense puissance, demonstrated a keen interest in the governance, customs, and religious practices of distant lands. This curiosity was most evident during his interactions with the Two Brothers, who traveled for a full year northward and north-eastward to reach his court. Upon their arrival, the Great Kaan received them with hospitality and honor, initiating a series of detailed inquiries regarding the structures of power and the social manners of the West.

## Inquiries into Governance and Statecraft

The Great Kaan's initial questions focused on the administration of power and the maintenance of authority. He sought specific information regarding emperors, inquiring about the methods they used to maintain their dignity and the manner in which they administered justice within their respective dominions. Furthermore, he questioned the Two Brothers on the protocols of warfare, asking how these emperors went forth to battle.

Beyond the imperial level, the Kaan extended these inquiries to other forms of leadership. He asked similar questions regarding the conduct and administration of kings, princes, and other potentates.

## Inquiries into Christian Manners and the Papacy

Following his interest in secular governance, the Great Kaan turned his attention to the religious and cultural customs of the West. He specifically inquired about the manners of the Christians, with a particular focus on the Pope of Rome. His questions encompassed:

*   The nature and role of the Pope.
*   The functions and organization of the Church.
*   The various activities and events that occurred at Rome.
*   The general customs of the Latins.

The Two Brothers, who were proficient in the Tartar language, were able to provide the Kaan with a truthful, orderly, and sensible account of these particulars.

## Religious Interactions and the Koran

The archive's sources indicate that the Great Kaan's interest in religion was sometimes influenced by the reports of those at his court. In one instance, certain Christians quoted a text from the Koran to the Kaan, claiming it enjoined the killing of polytheists. The Kaan summoned the Mullahs to ask why they did not act upon this divine injunction. When the Mullahs responded that the time for such action had not yet come, the Kaan ordered their execution. This order was only overturned through the intercession of Ahmad and the introduction of a divine who was able to smooth over the application of the text.

Additionally, the Kaan expressed resentment toward Saracens when some Mussulman merchants refused to eat dishes sent to them by the Emperor. This offense led to the revival of an order from Chinghiz that prohibited the slaughter of animals by cutting their throats under pain of death, a rule that remained in place for seven years.

## Diplomatic Missions and the Holy Sepulchre

The Kaan's interest in the Pope led him to send the Two Brothers as his envoys to Rome. To facilitate this mission, he provided them with a tablet of gold bearing his orders. 

During a subsequent journey back to the Great Kaan, the Two Brothers and Mark traveled to Jerusalem. This visit was conducted at the specific enjoinment of the Great Kaan, who desired that they obtain oil from the lamp on the Sepulchre to bring back to him.
