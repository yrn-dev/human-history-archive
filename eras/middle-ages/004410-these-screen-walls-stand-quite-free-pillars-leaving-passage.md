# Screen-Walls and Pillars of S. Clemente

The basilica of S. Clemente is noted for its high level of interest due to the preservation of its original church-fittings and ritual arrangements, which are more perfectly retained than those of other basilicas. The current structure was built upon the foundations of an earlier church that had been abandoned and filled with earth. It is probable that the earlier building was overwhelmed during the ruin of the city in 1084, when Robert Guiscard burnt public buildings from the Capitol to the Lateran, making it more convenient to construct a new edifice at a higher level than to repair the old one.

## Architectural Layout and the Atrium
The existing building is preceded by an atrium, which serves as the only remaining perfect example of such a feature in Rome. In the center of this atrium is a *cantharus*, a fountain used for ablutions. Entry to the atrium is provided via a portico constructed from earlier fragments that were put together carelessly.

## The Chorus Cantorum and Screen-Walls
A significant portion of the nave, approximately one-third, is occupied by the *chorus cantorum*. This area is enclosed by a low marble screen standing about 3 feet high. While the screen is a work of the 9th century preserved from the old church, it has been newly arranged. The slabs of white marble feature patterns in low relief and are decorated with ribbons of glass mosaic dating to the 13th century. These screen-walls are positioned quite free of the pillars, which leaves a passage between them.

## Ritual Furnishings and the Sanctuary
The church contains several specific ritual elements located around the choir:
* **The Gospel-Ambo:** Located on the ritual north, this structure is octagonal in form and features a double flight of steps extending eastwards and westwards.
* **The Paschal Candlestick:** Situated to the west of the gospel-ambo, this great candlestick possesses a spiral shaft decorated with mosaic.
* **The Epistle-Ambo:** Located to the south, opposite the gospel-ambo, this structure is square in plan. It contains two marble reading-desks facing east and west, used for the reading of the gradual and the epistle, respectively.

The sanctuary is positioned two steps above the choir. It is separated from the choir by another section of the same marble screen used in the *chorus cantorum*. Within the sanctuary, the altar is situated beneath a lofty *ciborium* supported by marble columns and a canopy.
