# Administrative Districts and Hides in Early Kingdoms

In the administrative organization of various early kingdoms, territories were divided into districts to facilitate governance and the collection of dues. These districts were placed under the authority of royal reeves, referred to in various terms such as *praefectus*, *praepositus*, or *cyninges gerefa*.

## District Centers and Administration
The royal reeves typically operated from specific centers, which could be fortresses (*urbs regis* or *cyninges burg*) or royal villages (*villa regalis* or *cyninges tun*). These locations served as meeting-places and markets for the inhabitants of the district. It was to these centers that the local population was required to render their services and payments.

## District Size and Classification
In early periods, the standard size for these administrative districts was generally 300, 600, or 1200 hides. However, the archive records the existence of significantly larger divisions. These larger categories comprised districts containing 2000, 3000, 5000, or 7000 hides.

## The Shires of Wessex
The shires of Wessex—including Berkshire, Wiltshire, and Hampshire—fall into the category of these larger divisions. From the 8th century onwards, each of these shires was governed by its own earl, known as a *dux*, *princeps*, or *aldormon*. These officials are sometimes described as *subreguli*. Many, and potentially all, of these earls were members of the royal family, suggesting that they may have originally held a kingly title.
