# Artium ad Theologiam, Soliloquium, and De septem itineribus

The works *De Reductione Artium ad Theologiam*, *Soliloquium*, and *De septem itineribus aeternitatis* are treatises authored by Bonaventura. These texts are categorized among his smaller works, which are regarded as his most important writings.

## Authorship and Significance
Bonaventura was recognized by his contemporaries with the eulogistic title "Doctor Seraphicus." His standing in ecclesiastical history is further evidenced by his placement in Dante's *Paradiso*. In 1482, he was formally canonized by Sixtus IV, and later, in 1587, Sixtus V ranked him as the sixth among the great doctors of the church.

The treatises *De Reductione Artium ad Theologiam*, *Soliloquium*, and *De septem itineribus aeternitatis* are noted for containing the majority of what is individual in Bonaventura's teaching.

## Context within Bonaventura's Works
The complete works of Bonaventura, as organized in the seven-volume folio Lyons edition, are divided into several categories:
* **Expositions and Sermons:** These occupy the first three volumes.
* **Commentary on the Sentences of Lombardus:** Spanning two volumes, this work is celebrated by medieval theologians as the incomparably best exposition of the third part of the *Sentences*.
* **Minor Treatises:** The final two volumes contain various smaller works, including a life of St Francis.

Among these minor treatises, the most famous and significant include the *Itinerarium Mentis ad Deum*, the *Breviloquium*, and the three aforementioned works: *De Reductione Artium ad Theologiam*, *Soliloquium*, and *De septem itineribus aeternitatis*.
