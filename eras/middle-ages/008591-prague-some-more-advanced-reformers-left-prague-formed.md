# Prague

Prague is the capital of Bohemia and serves as one of the primary administrative and educational centers of the region. It is designated as one of two autonomous municipalities in Bohemia, with a recorded population of 204,478. The city is a center of higher learning, hosting two universities—one Czech and one German.

## Religious Conflict and the Hussite Wars
In the later reign of King Wenceslas, Prague became a focal point for incipient religious conflict. The influence of the Church of Rome in Bohemia had been weakened by priests such as Waldhauser and Milic, who attacked clerical immorality. This tension escalated following the trial and execution of Huss, who was declared a heretic and burnt on July 6, 1415.

In response, the nobles of Moravia and Bohemia convened at Prague on September 2, 1415. They issued the *Protestatio Bohemorum*, a document protesting Huss's execution and describing him as a "good, just and catholic man." The nobles declared their intent to defend the law of Jesus Christ and its preachers regardless of human decrees. This protest acted as a declaration of war against the Roman church and initiated the Hussite wars.

## The Thirty Years' War and Imperial Conflict
Prague is noted as the location where the Thirty Years' War began. Tensions rose in 1617 when Ferdinand, duke of Styria—a known persecutor of Protestants—was crowned king at Prague on June 17. Conflict was precipitated in December 1617 when the abbot of Brevnov and the archbishop of Prague ordered the suppression of Protestant services in churches on their domains, violating the Letter of Majesty. This led Protestant members of the diet to assemble in Prague on May 21, 1618, eventually leading to a confrontation at the Hradcany palace on May 23.

Later, in 1620, imperial forces under the command of Bouquoi, Tilly, and the duke of Bavaria reached the outskirts of Prague on November 7. Following the Bohemian defeat at "Bila Hora" (White Hill) the next morning, the imperialists occupied the port of Prague on the left bank of the Vltava (Moldau). King Frederick subsequently fled the city. The city saw further turmoil during the war; in 1648, the Swedish general Konigsmark took part of Prague, marking the final event of the Thirty Years' War.

## Habsburg Patronage and Political Shifts
During the reign of Rudolph, Prague served as the sovereign's favorite residence and the center of the vast Habsburg dominions. A patron of the arts, Rudolph contributed significantly to the embellishment of the city. However, his mental health declined, leading his brother Matthias to travel to Prague to discuss the appointment of a coadjutor and succession arrangements.

In the 18th century, Prague remained central to Bohemian political life. On October 16, 1720, the estates met at Prague to sanction the female succession to the throne and recognize the Pragmatic Sanction. During the subsequent conflict over the throne, Charles, elector of Bavaria, occupied Prague, and many Bohemian knights and nobles pledged allegiance to him on December 19, 1741.

## Military Sieges and 19th Century Events
Prague was a frequent objective in mid-18th century warfare. In September 1740, Prussian forces reached the city, and the Austrian garrison surrendered six days later. Later, in 1742, the French army under Broglie was surrounded at Prague by the forces of Prince Charles. The siege of Prague was eventually raised on September 14, 1742.

In the 19th century, Prague hosted a Pan-Slavic conference that foreshadowed territorial readjustments occurring in 1919. This gathering dispersed after Austrian troops suppressed an insurrection within the city.

## Administration and Education
Administratively, Prague is one of two autonomous municipalities in Bohemia. The city's rectors of the two universities hold *ex officio* positions in the local diet, which consists of 242 members. The city remains a cornerstone of Bohemian education, contributing to the fact that Bohemia has the lowest proportion of illiterates among the Austrian provinces.
