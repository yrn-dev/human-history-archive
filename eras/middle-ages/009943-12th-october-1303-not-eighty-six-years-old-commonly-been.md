# 1303: Papal Transitions and Artistic Records

The year 1303 marked a significant period of transition within the papacy, characterized by the end of the reign of Boniface VIII and the subsequent election of Benedict XI. This era was defined by intense political conflict between the Holy See and various European powers, as well as the continued activity of influential Roman artistic families.

## The Papacy of Boniface VIII
Boniface VIII, born Benedetto Gaetano to a noble family in Anagni, served as pope from 1294 until 1303. Educated in civil and canon law in Italy and possibly Paris, he rose through the ecclesiastical ranks as a notary at the papal court and a cardinal. He was elected pope at Naples on December 24, 1294, following the abdication of Celestine V. Unlike his predecessor, who practiced monastic poverty, Boniface VIII asserted the lordship of the papacy over all the world's kingdoms and was crowned in Rome in January 1295 with great pomp.

His nine-year reign was marked by controversy rather than the peace he initially planned. Boniface VIII sought to recover the Holy Land from the infidel but instead became embroiled in disputes with European powers. His tenure was characterized by arrogance and avarice, leading to numerous enemies. Key conflicts included:
* **France:** His most noted conflict was with Philip IV of France.
* **England:** He attempted to try Edward I on charges of interfering with a papal fief, though the English parliament declared in 1301 that Scotland had never been a fief of Rome.
* **Germany and Hungary:** He refused to recognize Albert of Austria as the rightful German king until 1303 and declared that the crown of Hungary should fall to the house of Anjou.
* **Other Interests:** He humbled Eric VI of Denmark and failed in his policy of supporting the house of Anjou in Sicily. Additionally, his efforts to build estates for his family alienated the Colonna family.

## The Election and Reign of Benedict XI
Following the conclusion of Boniface VIII's papacy in 1303, Niccolo Boccasini was elected pope on October 22, 1303, taking the name Benedict XI. Born in Treviso in 1240, Benedict XI had been a member of the Dominican order since 1254, eventually serving as the order's general in 1296. He had previously served as a papal legate in Hungary in 1302.

Benedict XI's papacy, which lasted from 1303 to 1304, focused on conciliation. He worked to reconcile the enemies created by Boniface VIII, specifically the Colonnas, France, and King Frederick II of Sicily. Despite this conciliatory approach, he excommunicated William of Nogaret and the Italians responsible for the capture of Boniface VIII in Anagni on June 7, 1304. Benedict XI died in Perugia on July 7, 1304; reports suggested he may have been poisoned, with suspicion falling on Nogaret.

## Artistic Activity in Rome
During this period, the Cosmati family remained prominent in Rome. This family of architects, sculptors, and mosaic workers spanned four generations. Records from existing inscriptions indicate that Giovanni Cosmati was active in 1294, and Adeodato Cosmati produced work in 1296 and 1303.
