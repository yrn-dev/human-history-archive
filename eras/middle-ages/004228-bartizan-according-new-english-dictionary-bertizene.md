# BARTIZAN

## Definition and Function
A bartizan is a small turret featuring battlements. It is corbelled out at the angle of a tower or wall for the purpose of protecting a warder and providing him with the ability to see around the structure. These turrets are generally equipped with arrow-slits or oylets.

## Etymology and Linguistic Origins
According to the *New English Dictionary*, the term "bartizan" is derived from *bertizene*. This is considered a Scottish corruption of "brattishing" or "bratticing," which in turn originates from the Old French word *bretesche*. In its original sense, *bretesche* referred to a battlemented parapet. It is noted that Sir Walter Scott was apparently the first to use the term in this manner.

Further linguistic analysis indicates that *bretesche* (and the related *bertisca*) was a term used to describe any structure of attack or defence made of boards. Specifically, it referred to timber roofs and parapets that were frequently placed on top of flanking-towers within medieval fortifications. 

## Evolution of the Structure
The architectural application of the term evolved over time. While it initially described boarded timber structures, the derivative term "bartizan" later came to be applied to watch-towers or *guérites* constructed of masonry that projected from the main wall. 

In contrast to the military use of the term, the related English word "brattice" has evolved in modern usage to describe a fence placed around dangerous machinery or a pit.
