# THE MONASTERY OF ST. BARSAMO

The Monastery of Saint Barsamo is a religious institution located on the borders of Tauris. It is dedicated to Saint Barsamo, described as a most devout Saint.

## Monastic Life and Practices
The monastery is led by an Abbot and is home to many monks. These monks wear a habit similar to that of the Carmelites. To avoid idleness, the monks engage in the continual knitting of woollen girdles. These items are placed upon the altar of St. Barsamo during religious services.

The monks also travel throughout the province to beg, in a manner similar to the Brethren of the Holy Spirit. During these journeys, they present the knitted woollen girdles to gentlefolks and friends. These girdles are highly sought after and are considered excellent for the removal of bodily pain.

## History and Significance of Saint Barsamo
Saint Barsamo, also known as Barsauma (meaning "The Son of Fasting"), was a native of Samosata and served as an Archimandrite of the Asiatic Church. His ecclesiastical history was marked by conflict; he opposed the Nestorians, yet he became viewed as obnoxious by the orthodox for spreading the Monophysite Heresy. Consequently, he was condemned by the Council of Chalcedon in 451 and died in 458.

Barsamo is recognized as a Saint of fame within the Armenian and Jacobite Churches. While several monasteries were dedicated to him, the most celebrated was located near Malatia. This specific monastery was noted for possessing the relics of St. Peter and Barsauma. It also served as a meeting-place for Synods and was occasionally the residence of the Jacobite Patriarch. The fame of this institution extended to the Mahomedans, as evidenced by its inclusion in Bákúi’s Geog. Dictionary under the name *Dír-Barsúma*.

## Geographic Context: Tauris
The monastery is situated on the borders of Tauris, a noble city located in the province of YRAC. Tauris is a major center for trade and handicrafts, particularly known for weaving valuable and beautiful stuffs of gold and silk. Due to its strategic position, merchandise from regions such as India, Baudas, and Cremesor is brought to the city, making it a significant market for precious stones and a destination for Latin merchants, especially those from Genoa.

The population of Tauris is a diverse medley of different classes, including Persians, Georgians, Jacobites, Nestorians, Armenians, and the natives of the city, who are worshippers of Mahommet and known as Taurizi. The city is surrounded by charming gardens containing various large and excellent fruits.
