# Shropshire

Shropshire is a region in England containing various market towns, ecclesiastical rectories, and geological sites. It is home to the river Tern and the Shropshire Union canal.

## Market Towns and Localities
Market Drayton is a market town located in the Newport division of Shropshire. It is situated 178 miles northwest of London. The town is known as a center for agricultural trade and possesses large iron foundries. It is located within the parish of Drayton-in-Hales (a name sometimes applied to the town itself), and it is also referred to as Drayton Magna. The manor of the town was held successively by the abbots of Combermere in Cheshire and St Ebrulph in Normandy. The church of St Mary in the town contains Norman remains, though it has been modernized through restoration.

## Notable Residents and Figures
Several individuals of historical note have associations with Shropshire:

*   **Francis Henry Egerton, 8th Earl of Bridgewater (1756-1829):** Educated at Christ Church, Oxford and Eton, Egerton was a fellow of All Souls (1780) and an F.R.S. (1781). He was a zealous antiquarian, a fair scholar, and a naturalist. He held the rectories of Whitchurch and Middle in Shropshire, although a proxy performed the duties of these positions. He succeeded his brother in the earldom in 1823 and lived the latter part of his life in Paris. Upon his death in February 1829, the earldom became extinct. He left £12,000 and the Egerton MSS (concerning the literature of Italy and France) to the British Museum. Additionally, he left £8,000 to the president of the Royal Society to fund a treatise "On the Power, Wisdom and Goodness of God, as manifested in the Creation."
*   **Randolph Caldecott (1846-1886):** An English sculptor, painter, and prolific illustrator, Caldecott worked as a bank clerk in Whitchurch, Shropshire, from 1861 until 1872 before moving to London to study at the Slade School.

## Geology and Natural History
The region contains various fossil records. Specifically, *Prestwichia rotundata* has been identified in Coal M., Shropshire, and *Hemiaspis limuloides* has been found in Leintwardine, Shropshire, dating to the L. Ludlow period.
