# The Byzantine Empire: The Eternal Continuation of Rome

## Overview
The Byzantine Empire is often framed as a separate entity from the Roman Empire, yet to its own citizens, it was simply *Romania*—the Roman Empire. While the Western Roman Empire collapsed in 476 CE, the eastern half, centered in Constantinople, survived for another millennium. This state represented a complex synthesis: it maintained Roman law and administrative structures, embraced Greek culture and language, and centered its identity on Orthodox Christianity.

Historians frequently debate the term "Byzantine," a label coined by 16th-century scholars long after the empire's fall. Some argue that the shift from Latin to Greek under Heraclius (r. 610–641) marked a fundamental break from Romanity. Others contend that because the empire maintained an unbroken line of legal succession and imperial authority from Augustus, it remained the legitimate Roman state until the fall of Constantinople in 1453.

## Key Facts
*   **The Corpus Juris Civilis:** Under Emperor Justinian I, the empire compiled the *Corpus Juris Civilis*. This codification of Roman law became the foundation for most modern legal systems in Continental Europe.
*   **The Great Schism (1054):** Tensions between the Patriarch of Constantinople and the Pope in Rome culminated in a formal split between the Eastern Orthodox and Roman Catholic churches, reflecting deep political and theological rifts.
*   **The Theodosian Walls:** For over a thousand years, the triple-layered walls of Constantinople were the most formidable fortifications in the world, protecting the empire from Huns, Persians, and Arabs until the advent of gunpowder.
*   **Greek Fire:** A closely guarded state secret, this incendiary weapon could burn on water, allowing the Byzantine navy to repel several massive naval sieges of the capital.

## Did You Know?
1. **They never called themselves "Byzantines":** The inhabitants of the empire referred to themselves as *Rhomaioi* (Romans).
2. **Preservation of Antiquity:** Much of the classical Greek literature and philosophy known today survived because Byzantine scholars meticulously copied and preserved ancient manuscripts.
3. **The Caesaropapism Model:** The Emperor was viewed as God's vicegerent on earth, wielding supreme authority over both the state and the church.
