# Benedictine Priory of St James

The Benedictine monastery dedicated to St Mary and St James was established around 1150 in Birkenhead, a seaport of Cheshire, England, located on the river Mersey. The foundation was created by Hamon de Mascy, who served as the third baron of Dunham Massey.

## Administration and Privileges
The priory's financial stability was primarily maintained through revenues derived from tolls collected at the Mersey ferry. The prior of the monastery held a significant political position, sitting in the parliament of the earls of Chester. In this capacity, the prior enjoyed the full privileges and dignities associated with a Palatinate baron.

## Physical Remains
The priory was eventually purchased via private subscription and transferred to the municipality in 1896. Visitors to the site can still view several architectural remains, including a fine crypt, the refectory, the chapel, and the lodgings of the prior.

## Related Religious Orders
While the priory at Birkenhead was Benedictine, other religious orders such as the Dominicans and Franciscans were active in England and abroad during the medieval and early modern periods.

### The Dominican Order
The Dominicans established their first house in England at Canterbury in 1221. The order was sanctioned by Pope Honorius III on November 22, 1216, and St Dominic was appointed papal major-domo in 1218. Historically, the Dominican name became closely linked with the Inquisition, as the office of Inquisitor in various countries, including Spain, was typically held by members of this order. 

The order's commitment to mendicancy changed over time; in 1425, Martin V relaxed the law of corporate poverty for certain houses, and Sixtus IV extended this mitigation to the entire order fifty years later, meaning the Dominicans ceased to be mendicant. In Japan, the order faced persecution; for example, the vice-provincial Navarette was decapitated after declaring he recognized only the Emperor of Heaven rather than the Emperor of Japan.

### The Franciscan Order
The Franciscans established a house at Canterbury in 1225. The order was officially approved by a papal bull issued by Honorius III on November 29, 1223. Like the Dominicans, Franciscans were involved in missionary work and faced hardships in Japan. One Franciscan monk, Juan de Santa Martha, was executed in August 1618 after refusing deportation to New Spain, stating that he would remain in Japan to preach.

## Influence on Other Communities
During the 13th century, both the Dominicans and Franciscans exerted significant influence over Beguine communities in upper Italy, Germany, and France. This influence was so pervasive in Latin-speaking countries that the tertiaries of these two orders were commonly referred to as *beguini* and *beguinae*. Some Beguine associations were eventually converted entirely into houses of Franciscan, Dominican, or Augustinian tertiaries.
