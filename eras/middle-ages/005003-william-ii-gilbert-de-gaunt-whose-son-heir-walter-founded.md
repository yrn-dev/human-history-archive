# Bridlington and the Gaunt Family

## Early History and the Norman Conquest
Bridlington (historically referred to as Brellington or Burlington) was established as a borough prior to the Norman Conquest, as evidenced by the Domesday survey's mention of four burgesses. During the reign of Edward the Confessor, the town was part of the possessions of Earl Morcar and held a value of £32. However, following the Conquest, Bridlington suffered from Norman ravages, and its value decreased to 8s. by the time of the Domesday survey. Due to the forfeiture of Earl Morcar, the town came into the possession of the king.

## The Grant to Gilbert de Gaunt
The town was granted by William II to Gilbert de Gaunt. Gilbert's son and heir, Walter, further developed the area by founding a priory. To support this religious institution, Walter endowed it with other lands as well as the manor of Bridlington. Following this endowment, the importance of the town began to increase steadily.

## Royal Confirmations and Privileges
The gift made by Walter de Gaunt was confirmed by Henry I and several subsequent kings. King Stephen provided additional support by granting the right to have a port. In 1200, King John granted the prior of Bridlington an annual fair on the morrow, feast, and vigil of the Assumption of the Virgin Mary, as well as a weekly market held on Saturdays.

Further privileges were granted in 1446 by Henry VI, who gave the prior three new yearly fairs. These were held on the day, vigil, and morrow of the Nativity of the Virgin Mary, as well as on the Translation and Deposition of St John, a former prior of Bridlington. In 1546, Henry IV granted the convent and prior exemption from subsidies, tenths, and fifteenths; in exchange, prayers were to be sung for the king and his queen at every mass at the high altar.

## Ownership of the Manor
Following the Dissolution, the manor of Bridlington remained with the crown until 1624. At that time, Charles I granted the manor to Sir John Ramsey. Upon the death of Sir John, his brother and heir, Sir George Ramsey, sold the manor in 1633 to thirteen inhabitants of the town, acting on behalf of all the manor's tenants.

These thirteen lords were supported by twelve other inhabitants selected by the freeholders. When the number of lords eventually dropped to six, seven new lords were chosen from the assistants. Under this system, a chief lord is elected annually, a practice that continues to hold good.

## Municipal Incorporation
The fact that thirteen inhabitants were permitted to hold the manor suggests that the town possessed a form of incorporation during the 17th century. However, a formal incorporation charter was not granted until 1899, at which point Bridlington was created a municipal borough.
