# Impact of the Thirty Years' War on Various German Towns

The Thirty Years' War was a period of significant instability and destruction for numerous settlements across Germany. Archive records indicate that several towns suffered severe damage, pillaging, and strategic occupations as they were held by various belligerents throughout the conflict.

## Aschaffenburg and Lüneburg
Aschaffenburg, a town originally established as a Roman settlement where the 10th and 23rd Roman legions were stationed, experienced great suffering during the Thirty Years' War. The town was held in turn by the various belligerents involved in the conflict.

Similarly, the town of Lüneburg, which had been a principal member of the Hanseatic league and the capital of the duchy of Brunswick-Lüneburg from 1235 to 1369, suffered heavily during the course of the war.

## Baden and Wolfenbüttel
The town of Baden suffered severely from the various combatants of the Thirty Years' War. The French were particularly destructive, pillaging the town in 1643 and laying it in ashes in 1689.

In 1627, the town of Wolfenbüttel was forced to surrender due to the tactics of the Austrian besieging commander, General Count Pappenheim. The surrender was compelled by Pappenheim's contrivance of damming up the river Ocker, which flowed through the town.

## Brieg and Dusseldorf
Brieg, located in the Prussian province of Silesia on the left bank of the Oder, suffered greatly during the Thirty Years' War. This town, which had previously been burned by the Hussites in 1428 and fortified in 1595 by Duke Joachim Frederick, later faced further conflict during the war of the Austrian succession, where it was heavily bombarded by Prussian forces.

Dusseldorf, a village on the Dussel that was raised to the rank of a town in 1288 by Count Adolf of Berg, also suffered greatly during the Thirty Years' War. It experienced further hardship during the War of the Spanish Succession before eventually recovering its prosperity under the patronage of the electoral prince John William of the Palatinate.
