# Badenoch

## Geography and Landscape
Badenoch is a district located in south-east Inverness-shire, Scotland. It is bounded to the north by the Monadhliath mountains and to the east by Braemar and the Cairngorms. Its southern border is defined by the Grampians and Atholl, while Lochaber bounds the district to the west. Although its area is somewhat undefined, it is estimated to measure 15 miles from north to south and 36 miles from northeast to southwest.

The region is primarily a wild mountainous tract, with many hills exceeding 3000 feet in height, though this excludes the great glens and the valley of the Spey. The district is abundantly watered by the Spey and its various tributaries, and its principal lakes are Loch Ericht and Loch Laggan. The forests of Gaick, Feshie, Drumochter, and Alder provide some of the best deer country in the Highlands.

## Infrastructure and Population
The Highland railway traverses the district, running from Boat of Garten to Dalnaspidal. There are very few industries in the area. Population groups are concentrated at Kingussie and other locations situated on or near the Spey.

## History of Ownership and Lordship
The lordship of Badenoch was owned by the Comyns from 1229 until 1313. In 1371, Robert II granted the lordship to his son, Alexander Stewart (1343–1405), who was the 1st earl of Buchan and became known as the "Wolf of Badenoch."

Following a period of reversion to the crown, the lordship was bestowed in 1452 upon the 1st earl of Huntly. The title of lord of Badenoch continues to be given to the marquess of Huntly.
