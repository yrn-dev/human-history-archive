# Histoire généalogique de la maison d'Auvergne

The *Histoire généalogique de la maison d'Auvergne* is a historical work authored by the scholar Baluze. Published in 1709, the text serves as a genealogical history of the house of Auvergne and includes "Proofs" to support its claims.

## Context of Production and Forgeries
The publication of Baluze's work was the culmination of a period marked by the fabrication of spurious documents intended to support the pretensions of the house of Bouillon. This trend of producing false genealogies had previously appeared in the *Histoire de la maison d'Auvergne* published by Christophe Justel in 1645. Additionally, in 1672, the historian of Dauphiny, Chorier, included a forged deed in the second volume of his history to link the La Tours of Auvergne with the La Tours of Dauphiny.

A systematic "manufactory" of forged documents was later organized by Jean de Bar, a close companion of the cardinal de Bouillon. These forgeries were sophisticated enough to deceive prominent scholars. On July 23, 1695, Baluze, along with Dom Thierry Ruinart and the founder of Diplomatics, Dom Jean Mabillon, acted as experts and issued a unanimously favorable report on these documents.

## Controversy and Publication
The authenticity of the documents became a subject of a "war of pamphlets" fueled by the enemies of cardinal de Bouillon. In March 1698, Baluze wrote a letter in response to these challenges, though it proved nothing. By 1700, Jean de Bar and his accomplices were arrested, and a lengthy inquiry led to their declaration of guilt in 1704.

Despite these legal findings, Baluze remained obstinate, convinced that the incriminated documents were genuine. With the financial encouragement and support of cardinal de Bouillon, Baluze first produced a *Table genealogique* in 1705. This was followed in 1709 by the *Histoire généalogique de la maison d'Auvergne*. This work is noted for unfortunately including the very deeds that had been officially pronounced spurious.

## Aftermath and the Author's Fate
Shortly after the publication of the *Histoire généalogique*, Baluze suffered the consequences of his association with his patron. When the cardinal de Bouillon fell into disgrace, Baluze was exiled from Paris and stripped of his pensions, appointments, and benefices. 

Baluze continued his scholarly pursuits during his exile, publishing *Historiae Tutelensis libri tres*, a history of his native town, in 1717. He eventually returned to Paris, where he died on July 28, 1718, remaining unconvinced of his errors regarding the Auvergne documents. Historical analysis of his correspondence with the cardinal suggests that Baluze may have been a victim of clever cheats rather than an accomplice to the forgeries.

## Related Bibliography and Scholarship
The *Histoire généalogique de la maison d'Auvergne* (1708/1709) is listed alongside other regional histories, such as Andre Imberdis's *Histoire generale de l'Auvergne* (1867), J. B. M. Bielawski's *Histoire de la comte d'Auvergne et de sa capitale Vic-le-Comte* (1868), and B. Gonot's *Catalogue des ouvrages imprimes et manuscrits concernant l'Auvergne* (1849).
