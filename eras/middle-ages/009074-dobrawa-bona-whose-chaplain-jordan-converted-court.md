# Dobrawa

## Role in the Christianization of Poland
Dobrawa, also referred to as Bona, was a Bohemian princess and the wife of Mieszko, who is recognized as the first Christian prince of Poland. Dobrawa played a pivotal role in the religious transformation of the Polish court. Through her chaplain, Jordan, the court was converted from paganism to Catholicism.

## Family and Legacy
Dobrawa and Mieszko were the parents of Boleslaus I, known as "The Great," who succeeded his father as the ruler of Poland in 992. Boleslaus I continued the religious trajectory established by his parents, recognizing the essential superiority of Christianity over other religions. He deliberately associated himself with the new faith, a decision that contributed to his designation as "Great."

Boleslaus I furthered the Christian infrastructure of the region by purchasing the relics of Adalbert, the bishop of Prague, after the saint was martyred by pagan Slavs on April 23, 997. Boleslaus placed these relics in the church of Gnesen, a foundation of his father, Mieszko. This location subsequently became the metropolitan see of Poland.

## Political Context of the Era
During the reign of Dobrawa's son, Boleslaus I, the Polish principality on the Vistula was elevated to the status of a great power. Boleslaus expanded the territory by seizing Pomerania in 996 to gain a seaboard and occupying the city of Cracow, which had previously been a Czech city, during a period of trouble in Bohemia.

In the year 1000, Boleslaus hosted Emperor Otto III at Gnesen. Impressed by Boleslaus, the emperor conferred upon him the royal crown, stating that he was too worthy to be merely a *princeps*. Although Boleslaus had been crowned by Otto III, he crowned himself king a second time in the final year of his life, which was 1025.
