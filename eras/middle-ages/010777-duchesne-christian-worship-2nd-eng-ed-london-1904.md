# Christian Worship

*Christian Worship: its Origin and Evolution* (English translation by M.L. McClure, London, 1902; 2nd edition, 1904) is a scholarly work authored by Louis Marie Olivier Duchesne. The text serves as a study of the Latin liturgy prior to the era of Charlemagne and provides significant insight into the origins of Christian worship, particularly within France.

## Author Biography
Louis Marie Olivier Duchesne was a French ecclesiastic and scholar born on September 13, 1843, in Saint Servan, Brittany. His early academic interests were shaped by two scientific missions: one to Asia Minor in 1876 and another to Mount Athos in 1874. While these experiences initially led him toward the study of the ancient history of the Christian churches in the East, his primary focus eventually shifted to the Western church.

Duchesne earned the degree of *docteur es lettres* in 1877. This achievement was based on two theses: a dissertation titled *De Macario magnete* and an *Etude sur le Liber pontificalis*. In the latter, Duchesne utilized critical acumen to determine the interrelation of different editions and the origin of the chronicle, while establishing the precise value of its evidence. 

Following his doctoral work, Duchesne served as a professor at the Catholic Institute in Paris for eight years. During this tenure, he was noted for teaching church history according to the rules of scientific criticism, a method that was rare in France at the time and viewed as bold or rash by traditionalist exegetists, eventually leading to the suspension of his course. In November 1885, he was appointed as a lecturer at the Ecole Pratique des Hautes Etudes.

Duchesne's further contributions to scholarship include a two-volume learned edition of the *Liber pontificalis* (published in 1886 and 1892). He was elected to the Academie des Inscriptions et Belles-Lettres in 1888 and later served as the director of the French school of archaeology in Rome. His other notable works include *Memoire sur l'origine des dioceses episcopaux dans l'ancienne Gaule* (1890), *Fastes episcopaux dans l'ancienne Gaule* (published in two volumes in 1894 and 1899), and *Catalogues episcopaux de la province de Tours* (1898).

## Scope and Content of Christian Worship
The work *Christian Worship* (originally *Origines du culte chretien, etude sur la liturgie latine avant Charlemagne*) examines the evolution of liturgical practices. The archive's sources indicate that the text contains specific analyses regarding:

*   **The Mass and Liturgical Books:** The work includes a section on "The Mass in the East" and a detailed examination of "The Books of the Latin Rite."
*   **Church Architecture:** The text addresses the "Dedication of Churches."
*   **Festivals and Observances:** Duchesne's work is cited as a reference for the history of various religious observances, including the Feast of the Ascension and the ceremonial practices associated with Ash Wednesday.

## Scholarly Application
Due to its critical approach to liturgical history, *Christian Worship* is utilized as a reference in various historical and ecclesiastical studies. It is cited in discussions regarding the retention of the Feast of the Ascension in Lutheran churches and the Anglican Church, as well as the history of the festival celebrating the resurrection and bodily assumption of the Virgin. In the latter case, the festival is noted to have been borrowed by Rome from the East and later adopted by the Gallican Church alongside the Roman liturgy.
