## Administrative and Military Preventive Measures

The archive documents several instances where authorities took specific measures to prevent undesirable outcomes. In feudal Japan, Yoritomo expressed apprehension that Yoshitsune and Yukiiye might raise a revolt in Kyushu, Shikoku, and the Sanyo-do after they had gone westward from Kyoto. To address this, Oye no Hiromoto proposed a preventive strategy. With the Emperor's sanction, Yoritomo appointed a land-steward (*jito*) and a high constable (*shugo*) in each province. Yoritomo himself served as the lord high constable (*so-tsuihoshi*) and chief land-steward (*so-jito*) for sixty-six provinces. The purpose of these appointments was to ensure the Bakufu maintained control over local affairs to check any preparations or hostile movements.

## Measures Regarding Horse Breeding and Industry

In England, the introduction of gunpowder and lighter armor for cavalry led to a demand for better-bred horses. King James I, a sportsman, sought to improve English horses by importing Arab blood, beginning with the "Markham Arabian" from Constantinople. Despite initial prejudice and the failure of this first venture, James I continued his efforts by purchasing Place's White Turk. His successor, Charles I, continued this patronage. This shift in breeding prompted a memorial to be presented to Charles I, requesting that measures be taken to prevent the disappearance of the "old stamp of horse" that was considered fit for the defense of the country.
