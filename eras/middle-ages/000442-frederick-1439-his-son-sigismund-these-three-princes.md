# Succession and Regency in the Austrian Lands (1439)

The year 1439 marked a significant transition of power within the various territories of the Austrian lands, involving the succession of several princes and the establishment of regencies due to the infancy of heirs.

## The Death of King Albert II
In 1439, King Albert II died. He had previously served as the German king starting in 1438 and had assumed the crowns of Hungary and Bohemia following the death of his father-in-law, Sigismund, king of Hungary and Bohemia. Albert had been married to Elizabeth, the daughter of Sigismund.

Immediately following Albert II's death, a son was born to him and Elizabeth named Ladislaus Posthumus. Ladislaus succeeded to the kingdoms of Hungary and Bohemia, as well as the duchy of Austria. Because of his infancy, Ladislaus remained in Austria under the guardianship of his uncle, Frederick.

## The Division and Succession of the Southern Lands
The territories of Styria, Carinthia, and Tirol had been ruled in common by the sons of Duke Leopold III: William, Leopold, Ernest, and Frederick. William and Leopold died early in the 15th century. In 1406, the remaining brothers, Ernest and Frederick, divided their lands; Ernest became the duke of Styria and Carinthia, while Frederick became the count of Tirol.

By 1439, further successions occurred within these lines:
*   Ernest had been succeeded in 1424 by his sons, Frederick and Albert.
*   Frederick, the count of Tirol, was succeeded in 1439 by his son, Sigismund.

At the time of King Albert II's death in 1439, three princes were reigning: the two sons of Ernest (Frederick and Albert) and the son of Frederick (Sigismund of Tirol).

## The Rule of Emperor Frederick III
Frederick, who succeeded Albert II as the German king and was subsequently crowned emperor as Frederick III, assumed several roles of authority. In addition to his own kingship, he acted as the guardian for Sigismund of Tirol, who was a minor at the time. He also served as the regent of Austria due to the infancy of Ladislaus Posthumus.

The reign of Frederick III was characterized by disorder and struggle. This instability is attributed in part to the feebleness of Frederick's own character and in part to the ambitions of his brother, Albert, who desired to share in his dignities. This period of governance led to discontent among the estates of Austria, who eventually headed an open revolt. In Tirol, the population grew weary of his government, and Sigismund of Tirol was eventually declared of age in 1446.

## Later Consolidation under Maximilian I
The fortunes of the family were later arrested by Frederick's son, Maximilian (Emperor Maximilian I). Maximilian worked to recover hereditary lands and expand the house of Habsburg's power in eastern Europe. In 1490, he made an arrangement with his kinsman, Sigismund of Tirol, which brought the county of Tirol under his rule. Upon the death of Emperor Frederick in 1493, Maximilian united all of the Austrian lands under his own sway.
