# Thurn, Wenceslas of Ruppa, and Ulrich of Kinsky

The figures of Count Thurn, Wenceslas of Ruppa, and Ulrich of Kinsky were prominent members of the "advanced party" of Bohemian Protestants during the early 17th century. Their actions were central to the escalating religious and political tensions in Bohemia during the reign of King Matthias.

## Political Context and the Succession of Ferdinand
The conflict involving these figures arose from the question of succession to the Bohemian throne, as King Matthias was childless. Matthias sought to secure the throne for his cousin, Ferdinand, the duke of Styria. Ferdinand was regarded as a cruel persecutor of Protestants in Styria and a fanatical adherent of the Church of Rome. Despite this, state officials in Bohemia used "not very scrupulous means" to persuade the estates to accept Ferdinand as heir. He was subsequently crowned at Prague on June 17, 1617.

Following this coronation, the policy of King Matthias became more pronouncedly ultramontane, influenced by Ferdinand. The king's councillors, all of whom adhered to the Church of Rome, openly expressed their desire for the Catholic Church to recover its ancient hold over Bohemia.

## The Role of Count Thurn
Count Thurn emerged as a leader of the Bohemian Protestants. He was among the few nobles who had refused to vote for the recognition of Ferdinand as the heir to the throne. Thurn and his followers believed that a conflict with the crown was inevitable. They concluded that it was more advantageous to face the weak King Matthias than to wait for his younger and more fanatical successor, Ferdinand.

## The Crisis of December 1617
The situation was precipitated in December 1617 by an incident involving the abbot of Brevnov (Braunau) and the archbishop of Prague. These officials ordered the suppression of Protestant religious services in churches located on their domains. This action was viewed as a direct infringement of the agreement between the Utraquist and Romanist estates that had been established when King Rudolph signed the Letter of Majesty.

In response, the defenders of the Protestant faith invited all Protestant members of the diet to convene in Prague. They assembled on May 21, 1618, and decided to march in full armour to the Hradcany palace to present their complaints to the councillors of King Matthias.

## The Secret Meeting and Action
On May 22, 1618, a secret meeting was held. This gathering included Count Thurn, Wenceslas of Ruppa, Ulrich of Kinsky, and other members of the more advanced party. During this meeting, the group decided to put to death the most influential councillors of King Matthias. On the following day, May 23, the representatives of the Bohemian Protestants proceeded to the Hradcany.
