# Khorasan

Khorasan is a region of historical and strategic significance, noted in the archive's records for its natural wonders, its role in the production of weaponry, and its geopolitical importance.

## Natural History and Sacred Trees
Eastern accounts describe the existence of two vast cypress-trees in Khorasan that were held sacred by the Magians. These trees, one located at Farmad near Tuz and the other at Kashmar near Turshiz, were believed to have grown from shoots brought from Paradise by Zoroaster. The tree at Kashmar was approximately 1,450 years old and measured 33¾ cubits in girth. In the 9th century, this tree was cut down by order of the Khalif Motawakkil. The branches required 1,300 camels for transport, while the trunk was moved to Baghdad on rollers. Upon the convoy's arrival within one stage of the palace, the Khalif was killed by his own guards.

## Metallurgy and Weaponry
Khorasan is associated with the production of scimitars. While some historical sources, such as Dupré, asserted that no steel mines existed within the province and that the steel used for Persian blades arrived as disks from Lahore, other records indicate that Indian steel remained the primary material for the scimitars of Khorasan and Damascus.

## Geopolitical Significance
In the late 19th century, Khorasan was viewed as a critical strategic asset. According to the publication *Novoye Vremya* (December 1, 1888), there was a perceived necessity for Russia to bring Khorasan under its exclusive influence. This was due to the fact that a convenient road runs through Khorasan toward Herat, which would allow the region to serve as a base of supply for military operations directed against India.

## Avicenna's Travels in Khorasan
The scholar Avicenna spent a period of his life wandering through the districts of Merv and Nishapur to the borders of Khorasan while seeking an opening for his talents. This period of instability followed the end of the Samanid dynasty in December 1004 and Avicenna's decision to decline offers from Mahmud the Ghaznevid. 

During this time, Avicenna had hoped to find asylum with Shams al-Ma'ali Qabus, the ruler of Dailam. Qabus was a scholar and poet known for his generosity; however, around 1012, he was starved to death by his own revolted soldiery. Shortly after this event, Avicenna was stricken by a severe illness. He eventually found stability at Jorjan, near the Caspian, where a friend provided him with a dwelling. In this location, Avicenna lectured on astronomy and logic, wrote several treatises for his patron, and began the commencement of his *Canon of Medicine* during his stay in Hyrcania.
