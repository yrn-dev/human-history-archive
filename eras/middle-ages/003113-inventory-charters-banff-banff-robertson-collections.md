# Inventory of Charters of Banff and Robertson's Collections

The *Inventory of Charters of Banff* and Robertson's *Collections for a History of the Shires of Aberdeen and Banff* (published by the Spalding Club) are historical records pertaining to the region of Banffshire, Scotland. These works exist alongside other regional records, such as the *Inventory of Charters of Cullen* (1887), W. Cramond's *Annals of Banff* (1891), and W. Watt's *Aberdeenshire and Banff* (1900).

## The Royal Burgh of Banff
Banff serves as the capital of Banffshire and is designated as a royal, municipal, and police burgh. It is a seaport situated on high ground on the left bank of the mouth of the Deveron, located 50 miles northwest of Aberdeen. The town is noted for its antiquity, with its first charter granted by Malcolm IV in 1163. Subsequent privileges were granted to the burgh by Robert Bruce in 1324 and Robert II in 1372.

The town's architecture includes the modern Banff Castle, which stands on the site of an old castle where Archbishop Sharp was born. Other significant public buildings include the town hall, which features a 100-foot high spire, the county buildings, a masonic hall, and the academy. The academy is a Grecian-style structure with an extensive museum that once belonged to the Banff Institution. This museum contains examples of the work of the astronomer James Ferguson and was curated for a few years by Thomas Edward, known as the "working naturalist."

## Economy and Infrastructure of Banff
The economy of Banff is diversified across several sectors:
*   **Manufacturing:** The town produces leather, woollens, rope, and sails. It also houses iron foundries, brick-yards, timber-yards, breweries, distilleries, and ship-building operations.
*   **Trade:** The fishing trade is important to the region. Primary exports include fish, cattle, potatoes, dairy produce, and grain, while coal and timber are the main imports.
*   **Transport:** The town is served by two railway stations: one at the harbour (connecting to Keith and Portsoy) and one at Bridge of Banff (connecting to Aberdeen via Inveramsay).

## Regional Context and Governance
Banffshire is a county that, as of 1901, had a population of 61,488. The chief towns of the shire include Banff (population 7,161), Buckie (6,549), and Keith (4,753), followed by Cullen, Portsoy, and Dufftown. 

In terms of governance, Banff and Cullen are royal burghs. Together with Elgin, Inverurie, Kintore, Peterhead, and Macduff, they form the Elgin group of parliamentary burghs, which returns one member to parliament. Legally, Banffshire forms a sheriffdom alongside Aberdeen and Kincardine shires. A resident sheriff-substitute is based in Banff, though they also sit in Dufftown, Buckie, and Keith.

## Neighboring Settlements and Industry
The region is characterized by significant maritime and agricultural activity. Macduff, a police burgh and seaport located one mile east of Banff on the right bank of the Deveron, was originally the fishing village of Doune. It was purchased by the 1st earl of Fife around 1732 and renamed Macduff by the 2nd earl, who obtained a royal charter for the burgh in 1783. Macduff's harbour, constructed by the duke of Fife, is described as more accessible and safer than that of Banff.

Buckie is another major fishing town in the shire, located 17 miles west of Banff. It is a chief seat for line fishing in Scotland and attracts one of the largest fleets during the herring season, which runs from June to September.

Agricultural enterprise in the shire was heavily encouraged by the earls of Fife and the earls of Findlater (later earls of Seafield). Between 1811 and 1845, a Seafield planted over 31 million forest trees—including larches, hardwoods, and Scots firs—across 8,223 acres of enclosed ground in Nairn, Moray, and Banff counties.
