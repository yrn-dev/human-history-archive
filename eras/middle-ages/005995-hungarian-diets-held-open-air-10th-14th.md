# Hungarian Diets in the Open Air

## The Rákos Field
The Rákos field is a historic site located to the north of Pest. This location is distinguished in Hungarian history as the place where the Hungarian diets were conducted in the open air. These outdoor assemblies took place from the 10th century through to the 14th century.

## Political Context of the Hungarian Diet
The Hungarian diet has served as a central body in the political history of the region. In the 19th century, the diet was a focal point for demands regarding the restoration of the independence of the kingdom of Hungary. Following the war of 1866 and the treaty of Prague, the Hungarian diet was able to establish its own terms. These terms revoked attempts made over the previous hundred years to absorb Hungary into a greater Austria and recognized no union between Hungary and other parts of the monarchy except that which was based on the Pragmatic Sanction.

Under these arrangements, the emperor was required to be crowned at Pest and take the ancient oath to the Golden Bull. The kingdom of Hungary, which included Croatia and Transylvania, was to maintain its own parliament.

## Notable Figures and Parliamentary Activity
Members of the Hungarian diet have included prominent statesmen such as Ferencz Lajos Akos Kossuth (1841– ). The son of Lajos Kossuth, Ferencz Lajos Akos Kossuth was educated at London University and the Paris Polytechnic. Although he was elected as a member of the Hungarian diet twice as early as 1867, he refused to accept the mandate on both occasions. He later took an active role in Hungarian politics after being returned for Cegléd in 1896 and Tapolca on April 10, 1895. 

By the autumn of 1898, Kossuth became the leader of the "Independence Party," also known as the obstructionists. In this capacity, he exercised significant influence over the public and parliament during the administrations of Stephen Tisza, Szápáry, Khuen-Haderváry, and Szell between 1898 and 1904. Following the elections of 1905, which provided his party with a large majority, he assisted in the construction of the Wekerle ministry.

## Geographic Context of the Region
The historic Rákos field is situated within a region containing several other notable landmarks. To the north of the field lies the royal castle of Gödöllö and its park, located 23 miles away. In the city of Budapest, the Városliget park—spanning 286 acres—contains the zoological garden and museums for ethnography and agriculture. Other nearby sites include Margaret island in the Danube, where St Margaret, daughter of King Bela IV in the 13th century, established a convent, and the remains of the Roman colony of Aquincum, which features an aqueduct, a temple, a castrum, baths, and an amphitheatre.
