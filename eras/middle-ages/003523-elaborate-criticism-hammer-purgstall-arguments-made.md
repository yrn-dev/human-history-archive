# Joseph von Hammer-Purgstall

Joseph von Hammer-Purgstall was a scholar and author from Vienna whose works spanned a wide array of historical, geographical, and linguistic subjects, with a particular focus on the Orient and the Ottoman Empire. His contributions include detailed histories, critical reviews, and interpretations of medieval symbols and figures.

## Works on the Ottoman Empire and the Orient
Hammer-Purgstall authored *The History of the Ottoman Empire* (translated into French by J. J. Hellert between 1835 and 1843), which includes accounts of the Barbarossas, though these accounts are noted to require correction by other authorities. He also wrote *Geschichte des Osman Reiches* (and *Gesch. des Osmanischen Reichs*, published in Pest in 1840), a work cited as a means for Orientals to be enlightened regarding the history and geography of their own country.

In his analysis of historical disputes, Hammer-Purgstall provided a "simple and satisfactory" explanation regarding the transport of Bajazet. He argued that reports of Bajazet traveling in a cage were based on a misunderstanding of the Turkish word *kafe*, which refers to a covered litter or palanquin drawn by two horses and typically used for the harem of an Eastern monarch. According to Hammer-Purgstall, this litter featured iron lattice-work, and Bajazet's use of it was attributed to his indignant state of mind and his desire to avoid seeing his Tartar conquerors.

## Analysis of Baphomet and the Knights Templars
In his works *Mysterium Baphometis relevatum, &c.* and *Die Schuld der Templer*, Hammer-Purgstall revived old accusations against the Knights Templars regarding the worship of Baphomet. He interpreted the term Baphomet as signifying the baptism of fire or the baptism of *Metis*, linking the practice to the impurities of the Gnostic Ophites. To support this interpretation, he pointed to the architectural decorations found within the churches of the Templars.

These arguments were subject to an elaborate criticism published in the *Journal des Savans* in March and April 1819. The critique was authored by M. Raynouard, who was known as a defender of the Knights Templars.

## Contributions to Other Fields of Study
Hammer-Purgstall's scholarly reach extended into linguistics, biography, and general history:

*   **Linguistics and Music:** He published a German review in *Jahrbuecher der Literatur* (1826, Bd. 35 and 36) of *The Seven Seas*, a dictionary and grammar of the Persian language by Ghazi ud-din Haidar. This review included copious quotations and specific information regarding the names of musical instruments.
*   **Biography:** He authored *Khlesls Leben*, published in Vienna between 1847 and 1851.
*   **General History:** His writings have been cited as sources that throw light on the contents and literary history of the travels of Marco Polo. Additionally, he provided corrections to the work of Gibbon; specifically, he noted that Gibbon was mistaken in associating ninety-two army squadrons with the names of God, clarifying that there are ninety-nine names of God, with Allah being the hundredth.
