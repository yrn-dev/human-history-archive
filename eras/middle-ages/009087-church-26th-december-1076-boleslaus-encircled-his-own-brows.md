# Boleslaus II and the Polish Monarchy

## Accession and Territorial Ambitions
Boleslaus II, known as "The Bold," served as the king of Poland from 1039 to 1081. He was the eldest son of Casimir I, succeeding his father in 1058. While Casimir I had worked painstakingly to restore tranquillity and domestic order within the kingdom, Poland had suffered territorial shrinkage since the era of Boleslaus I. Consequently, Boleslaus II aimed to restore the importance and dignity of the Polish state.

## Military Conflicts and Foreign Relations
Boleslaus II's efforts to expand and secure his borders involved several neighboring powers:

*   **Bohemia:** Bohemia was Poland's nearest enemy. Poland had recently been forced to pay tribute to Bohemia for Silesia, its oldest possession. Boleslaus II's first war with Bohemia was unsuccessful and concluded with the marriage of his sister, Swatawa, to the Czech king Wratyslaus II.
*   **Hungary:** Boleslaus II provided assistance to the fugitive Magyar prince Bela, who succeeded in winning the crown of Hungary.
*   **Russia:** Boleslaus II found more success in the East. In 1069, he placed Izaslaus on the throne of Kiev. This action confirmed Poland's overlordship over Russia and allowed Boleslaus II to use Russian auxiliaries to chastise other enemies, including Bohemia.

Later, Wratyslaus of Bohemia sought help from the emperor. A full-scale war between the Empire and Poland was avoided only because Henry IV experienced a sudden rupture with the Holy See, leading to the emperor's surrender at Canossa. While Boleslaus II did not appear to take part in that specific struggle, he maintained a positive relationship with Gregory VII. During this period, there were discussions regarding the dispatch of papal legates to restore order within the Polish Church.

## Coronation and Royal Authority
On the 26th of December 1076, Boleslaus II encircled his own brows with the royal diadem. This act of self-coronation is viewed as striking evidence that Polish kings at the time did not consider their titles to be entirely secure.

## Decline and Exile
The final recorded exploit of Boleslaus II was a second successful expedition to Kiev to reinstate his protégé, Izaslaus. Shortly thereafter, in 1079, Boleslaus II became an exile in Hungary, where he died around 1081. 

His sudden fall from power was caused by the cruel vengeance he took upon the *milites*, or the noble order. These nobles were attempting to curb royal power, following the example of their counterparts in Bohemia. Additionally, the churchmen were headed by Stanislaus Szczepanowski.
