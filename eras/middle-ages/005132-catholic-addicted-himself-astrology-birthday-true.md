# Astrology and the Earl of Bristol

## The Life and Contradictions of the Earl of Bristol
The Earl of Bristol was a prominent figure of his era, recognized for his brilliant abilities, eloquence, and success in various spheres of activity. However, his natural talents were undermined by instability of character and a restless ambition. He was described by Clarendon as a man of incomparable parts who failed to gain wisdom from misfortune or experience. Horace Walpole characterized his life as a series of contradictions.

Among these contradictions, Walpole noted that Bristol wrote against popery but eventually embraced it, and though he was a Roman Catholic, he spoke in favor of the Test Act. His political life was similarly erratic; he was a zealous opposer of the court but became a sacrifice for it, and while he was conscientiously converted during his prosecution of Lord Strafford, he later became an unconscientious persecutor of Lord Clarendon. In his military career, despite possessing romantic bravery, he remained an unsuccessful commander.

Bristol was a writer and orator whose speeches were included in the *Biog. Brit.* and the *Thomason Tracts*. His works include an *Apologie* (1643), which justified his support for the king's cause, and a comedy titled *Elvira* (1667). He is also credited with co-authoring *The Adventures of Five Hours* (1663) with Sir Samuel Tuke and wrote an adaptation from the Spanish titled *Worse and Worse*. He married Lady Anne Russell, the daughter of Francis, 4th earl of Bedford, and had two sons, Francis and John, as well as two daughters. John succeeded him as the 3rd earl of Bristol.

## Astrology and the "Birthday of True Philosophy"
In addition to his religious and political shifts, Horace Walpole recorded that the Earl of Bristol "addicted himself to astrology on the birthday of true philosophy." 

Astrology is defined as the ancient science or art of divining the future and fate of human beings based on the positions of the sun, moon, and planets. Historically, it was often identified with astronomy. A distinction exists between "natural astrology," which predicts eclipses and the motions of heavenly bodies, and "judicial astrology," which focuses on the influence of stars on human destiny.

## History and Practice of Judicial Astrology
The study of astrology began in a developed form among the ancient Babylonians and spread to other nations. It reached Greece around the middle of the 4th century B.C. and Rome before the Christian era. The Greeks contributed the significant practice of tracing an individual's horoscope based on the position of the stars and planets at the time of birth or conception. This system was later adopted by Arab astronomers and incorporated into the Kabbalistic lore of Christians and the Bani Israel.

In the Middle Ages and the Renaissance, astrologers often held dominant influence at court. Some practitioners focused on "genethliology" (drawing individual horoscopes), while others relied on physiognomy and chiromancy (palmistry), reading a person's face or hair to guess their nativity rather than calculating the stars. For example, the practitioner Cardan (1501-1576) reportedly changed the birthday of Martin Luther in his records to provide him with an unfavorable horoscope.

## Influence and Predictions
Judicial astrology has maintained a dominion over the human mind across many ages. The Roman emperor Severus was passionately addicted to divination and judicial astrology; he sought a second wife with a "royal nativity," leading him to marry Julia Domna of Emesa in Syria.

The history of astrology is marked by predictions of varying accuracy. Some astrologers predicted the immediate end of the world, such as Stoffler, who predicted a universal deluge in 1524 due to three planets meeting in the sign of Pisces. This led President Aurial at Toulouse to build a Noah's ark, though the year was actually characterized by drought. In another instance, an astrologer predicted that a king would be killed in a duel; although the queen laughed at the prediction, the king was eventually slain during a course at tilt.

In the accounts of Marco Polo, Christian astrologers are credited with correctly predicting the outcome of a battle between Chinghis Kaan and Prester John. By using a split cane and reciting a Psalm and other incantations, the Christian astrologers demonstrated that the piece representing Chinghis Kaan would move on top of the other, signaling victory.

## Transition to Modern Science
The separation of astrology from astronomy began in the 16th century. The system of Copernicus established that the earth is one of the heavenly bodies, helping astronomy rid itself of astrological influence. Figures such as Tycho Brahe and Gassendi began their studies in astrology but eventually moved toward astronomy after finding the former wanting. Kepler described astronomy as the "wise mother" and astrology as the "foolish daughter," though he believed the daughter's existence was necessary for the mother's life.
