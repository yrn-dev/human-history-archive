# Souvent doit tout son lustre au caprice des rimes

The phrase "Souvent doit tout son lustre au caprice des rimes" is a critical observation regarding the ballade, a form of poetry. This specific line was written by Boileau, who used it to attack the ballade, describing it as being "asservie a ses vieilles maximes" (enslaved to its old maxims) and suggesting that its brilliance often depends merely on the whim of its rhymes.

## The Development of the Ballade
The ballade is a type of poetry in a highly-finished and elaborate form. While it did not reach this level of sophistication until the 14th century, its origins can be traced back to the Italian *canzone de ballo*. The form first took a modern shape within Provencal literature.

The ballade began to flourish in France during the reign of Charles V. Following this period, the form became instantly popular, leading to an incalculable output of poems within a few years.

## Notable Poets of the Form
Numerous poets cultivated the ballade throughout different eras:
*   **Early Popularity:** Among those who wrote ballades abundantly were Christine de Pisan, Eustache Deschamps, Froissart, and Machault.
*   **Middle Ages:** The form was later utilized by Henri Baude and Alain Chartier. Francois Villon is noted for using the ballade to create some of the most extraordinary and admirable poems handed down from the middle ages.
*   **Later Refinements:** Clement Marot composed ballades characterized by great precision of form.
*   **17th Century:** The fashion for the ballade culminated in the 17th century with the works of Voiture, Sarrazin, Madame Deshoulieres, and La Fontaine.

## Critical Reception
Despite its popularity and the precision achieved by various poets, the ballade faced criticism from prominent figures. In addition to the critiques leveled by Boileau, the form was also attacked by Moliere.
