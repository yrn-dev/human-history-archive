# Vienna, 1860

The city of Vienna served as a significant center for the publication of historical and academic works during the mid-19th century. In 1860, the city saw the publication of the *Chronica Nestoris*.

## Historical Publications and Authorship
Vienna was a hub for scholarly research into European history, including the works of various historians and officials. In the years surrounding 1860, the city produced several key texts. For example, in 1868, Heinrich R. von Zeissberg published *Die Kriege Kaiser Heinrichs II. mit Herzog Boleslaw I.* in Vienna.

The city also hosted the publication of extensive official histories. The Austrian official work *Kriege der Kaiserin Maria Theresia; Gesch. des oesterr. Erbfolgekrieges* began publication in Vienna from 1895. Additionally, the *Mitteilungen des k.k. Kriegsarchivs* has been published in Vienna since 1887.

## Academic and Biographical Works
Vienna was the site of publication for numerous detailed biographical and historical series. A. von Arneth published the ten-volume *Geschichte Maria Theresias* in Vienna between 1863 and 1879. The city also produced the 17-volume series *Oesterreichische Gesch. fuer das Volk*, authored by various writers, starting in 1864.

Other notable publications associated with Vienna include:
* *Irmenstrasse und Irmensaule* (1815)
* *Silva de romances viejos* (1815)
* *Offizieller Bericht über die Kriegsereignisse zwischen Hannover und Preussen im Juni 1866*, published in 1867.

## Military and Political History
The intellectual output of Vienna often focused on the military conflicts and political structures of the Austrian Empire and the broader Holy Roman Empire. This is evidenced by the publication of C. von B.-K.'s *Geist und Stoff im Kriege* in 1895. The city's archives and publishing houses provided the basis for research into the House of Austria, though some general histories in English, such as those by Archdeacon William Coxe, were later superseded by the research emerging from these sources.
