# Events of 1109–1110

The years 1109 and 1110 were marked by significant military conflicts in the Levant and Eastern Europe, as well as notable architectural developments in Italy.

## The Kingdom of Jerusalem and Northern Syria
During this period, Baldwin I worked to expand the territories of Jerusalem into a compact kingdom. To achieve this, he formed alliances with Italian trading towns, most notably Genoa. In exchange for granting these towns quarters in conquered territories, Genoa provided the skill and instruments necessary for siege warfare. This strategy led to the successive reduction of Palestinian coast towns; following the capture of Arsuf and Caesarea in 1101 and Acre in 1104, the towns of Beirut and Sidon were captured in 1110. The capture of Sidon was achieved with the assistance of the Norwegians and Venetians.

In the north, Baldwin I dealt with dissensions among the Christian princes of Edessa, Antioch, and Tripoli between 1109 and 1110. He provided support to these princes to help them maintain their positions against the Mahommedan princes of North East Syria, specifically Aksunk-ur and Maudud, who were amirs of Mosul. Through these actions, Baldwin I became the practical, though largely nominal, suzerain of the three northern Christian principalities.

Simultaneously, Baldwin II, the count of Edessa, was managing his own principality. After a struggle with Tancred that was composed in 1109, Baldwin II ruled Edessa for the following decade. Because Edessa was the farthest Christian outpost in northern Syria, it faced frequent attacks from Mosul and Mardin. In 1110, Baldwin II was besieged in Edessa but was subsequently relieved by Baldwin I.

## Conflicts in Poland and Pomerania
In 1109, the first of the German wars began when Emperor Henry V, receiving material assistance from the Bohemians, invaded Silesia. This conflict consisted primarily of a war of sieges as Henry attempted and failed to take Breslau, Glogau, and Lubusz. The Polish forces avoided open field encounters, instead harrying the Germans around Breslau. The resulting retreat of the Germans was so disastrous that the area became known as "the field of dogs." This event resulted in Poland achieving complete independence for the next twenty-five years.

During this same year, Boleslaus III of Poland began a struggle to subdue Pomerania to gain access to the sea. The Pomeranians, a savage Slavonic race akin to the Wends who adhered to paganism, utilized their seaboard to maintain fleets and build towns such as Kolberg and Stettin. In 1109, Boleslaus III inflicted a terrible defeat upon the Pomeranians at Nackel, which forced them into a temporary submission.

## European Developments
In Italy, the city of Bologna saw the construction of its two famous leaning towers. The Torre Asinelli dates from 1109, and the Torre Garisenda dates from 1110. Both are square brick structures; the Torre Asinelli stands 320 feet high (4 feet out of the perpendicular), while the unfinished Torre Garisenda stands 163 feet high (10 feet out of the perpendicular).

In France, Fulk, the son of Bertrada and Fulk IV (count of Anjou), became the count of Anjou in 1109. He subsequently increased the prestige of his house and acted as a persistent opponent to Henry I of England by supporting Louis VI of France.
