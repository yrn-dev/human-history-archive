# Orden und Kongregationen and Les Moines de Constantinople

## Overview of Monastic Scholarship
The study of religious orders and monasticism is documented through various historical and specialized texts. Among the primary authorities cited for the history and spirit of monastic institutions is Max Heimbucher, author of *Orden und Kongregationen*. This work is referenced across multiple volumes of historical archives to provide specific data on a variety of religious orders, including the Benedictines, the Carthusians, and the Dominicans.

## Basilian Monachism and Constantinople
Research into Basilian Monachism and the Rules of St. Basil is supported by several key texts. Heimbucher's *Orden und Kongregationen* (1907), specifically volume i, page 11, is cited as an authority on the history and spirit of this tradition. Other significant works on this subject include:
*   *Les Moines de Constantinople* (1897) by Abbe Marin, which includes a fourth book titled *Les Moines et les empereurs iconoclastes*.
*   *Enthusiasmus und Bussgewalt beim griechischen Moenchtum* (1898) by Karl Holl.
*   *Askese und Moenchtum* (1897) by Otto Zoeckler.
*   *Hist. des Ordres Religieux* (1714) by Helyot.

Further general information on the Basilians is found in the *Kirchenlexicon* (2nd ed.) by Wetzer und Welte, and the *Realencyklopaedie* (3rd ed.) by Herzog-Hauck, specifically within the articles "Moenchtum," "Orientalische Kirche," and "Athosberg." Additionally, Alice Gardner's *Theodore of Studium* provides a general picture of monastic life and specimen passages.

## The Carthusian and Dominican Orders
The Carthusian order is extensively documented in B. Tromby's ten-volume *Storia del patriarca S. Brunone e del suo ordine* (1773). Max Heimbucher's *Orden u. Kongregationen* (1896), volume i, page 36, also serves as a reference for this order. Other studies include Hermann Lobbel's 1899 work on the founder of the order, "Der Stifter des Karthauser-Ordens," and an account of the actual life of the order by Algar Thorold, who spent several months in the noviciate at the Grande Chartreuse. For the English branch of the Carthusians, sources include E. Margaret Thompson's *Somerset Carthusians* (1895) and Dom L. Hendriks' *London Charterhouse* (1889).

Regarding the Dominican order, authorities include the works cited in the article on Saint Dominic, as well as Helyot's *Hist. des ordres religieux* (1714) and Max Heimbucher's *Orden u. Kongregationen* (1896), specifically pages 86-91. C. F. Palmer's *Life of Cardinal Howard* (1867) provides a specialized account of the Dominican province in England.

## Benedictine History
The historical record of the Benedictines is divided by era. For history up to the middle of the 12th century, Mabillon's six-volume *Annales* is the chief general authority. Early history is also covered fully in Montalembert's *Monks of the West*. For the period leading to the beginning of the 18th century, Helyot's *Hist. des ordres religieux* (1792), volumes v and vi, is utilized.

Max Heimbucher's *Orden und Kongregationen* (1896), volume i, pages 17-28, provides a useful sketch of the Benedictines. Other references include the *Kirchenlexicon* (2nd ed.) and the *Realencyklopadie* (3rd ed.). Specific studies on the Benedictines include:
*   *English Black Monks* (1897) by Ethelred Taunton.
*   *Woman in Monasticism* (1896) by Lina Eckenstein, focusing on Benedictine nuns.
*   *Geschichte der christlichen Kunst* (1896-1897) by F.H. Kraus, regarding the relationship between Benedictines and the arts.
*   *Sketch of Monastic Constitutional History* and *English Monastic Life* (1904) by F.A. Gasquet.
