# Boniface

The name Boniface (Bonifacius) has been borne by nine different popes. In the historical record, the name is associated with both a prominent missionary figure in Germany and the papacy of Boniface VIII.

## St. Boniface: Missionary and Reformer

St. Boniface was a man of action whose career was made possible by the protection of Charles. Utilizing this support, he entered heathen Germany to conduct a systematic crusade. His activities included the overturning of idols, the baptism of inhabitants, and the establishment of monasteries and churches. To assist in these efforts, he called for a group of missionary helpers from England, including monks and nuns. Notable figures among these helpers were St. Lull (his successor at the see at Mainz), St. Burchard (bishop of Wurzburg), St. Gregory (abbot at Utrecht), St. Lioba, St. Walburge, St. Thecla, and his biographer, Willibald.

Boniface's ecclesiastical influence grew steadily over several decades:
* **732:** He was created archbishop.
* **738:** He visited Rome for a third time. Upon his return, he organized the church in Bavaria into four bishoprics: Passau, Salzburg, Freising, and Regensburg.
* **741:** Pope Zacharias appointed him legate, charging him with the reformation of the entire Frankish church. With the support of Pippin and Carloman (the mayors of the palace who succeeded Charles Martel), he organized the east Frankish church into the bishoprics of Eichstadt, Buraburg, Wurzburg, and Erfurt, placing his own monks in charge.
* **742:** He presided over what is generally considered the first German council.

During this period, Boniface founded the abbey of Fulda to serve as a center for German monastic culture. He placed the abbey under the Bavarian Sturm and implemented a rule that was stricter than the Benedictine rule.

## Controversies and Conflicts

St. Boniface engaged in several theological and disciplinary disputes. He faced a controversy with Virgil, the Irish bishop of Salzburg, who held the heresy that worlds other than our own existed. Despite these leanings toward science, Virgil held his own against Boniface and was later canonized.

In France, Boniface encountered a Frankish bishop of Neustria named Adalbert (or Aldebert). Adalbert had caused significant disturbance by claiming to receive relics directly from angels rather than from Rome. By planting crosses in open fields, Adalbert encouraged people to abandon churches and gained a substantial following throughout Neustria.

## Boniface VIII

Benedetto Gaetano, known as Pope Boniface VIII, served as pope from 1294 to 1303. Born into a noble family in Anagni, he studied civil and canon law in Italy and possibly Paris. His early career included appointments to canonicates in France and Todi (June 1260), and roles as a notary and advocate at the papal court. In April 1267, while aiding King Henry III of England with Cardinal Ottoboni, he was besieged in the Tower of London by the earl of Gloucester before being rescued by the future Edward I.

After serving as cardinal deacon (1281) and cardinal priest (1291), he became influential in the Sacred College and helped Celestine V abdicate. Boniface VIII was elected pope at Naples on December 24, 1294, though the election was not unanimous due to hostility from certain French cardinals.

Unlike the monastic poverty of his predecessor, Boniface VIII asserted the lordship of the papacy over all the world's kingdoms and was crowned in January 1295 with great pomp. His reign was marked by:
* **Diplomatic Conflicts:** He involved the papacy in controversies with major European powers, including a noted conflict with Philip IV of France.
* **Territorial and Political Disputes:** He refused to recognize Albert of Austria as the rightful German king until 1303 and declared the crown of Hungary should fall to the house of Anjou. He also humbled Eric VI of Denmark.
* **Legal Battles:** He attempted to try Edward I on the charge of interfering with a papal fief, but the parliament declared in 1301 that Scotland had never been a fief of Rome.
* **Internal Failures:** His support for the house of Anjou in Sicily failed, and his attempts to build estates for his family alienated the Colonna.

## Literary Remains and Scholarship

Despite being a man of action, St. Boniface left several literary works. These include:
* **Letters (_Epistolae_):** Considered extremely important for literature, dogma, and history.
* **Grammar:** Titled _De octo partibus orationibus_.
* **Other Works:** A penitential, poems (_Aenigmata_), and _Dicta Bonifacii_, though the authenticity of the latter and some of his sermons is contested.

Scholarship on Boniface is extensive, with many monographs covering different phases of his life. Recent studies include those by G. Kurth (_Saint Boniface_, 1902) and B. Kuhlmann (_Der heilige Bonifatius, Apostel der Deutschen_, 1895). Additionally, W. Levison edited the _Vitae sancti Bonifatii_ in 1905.
