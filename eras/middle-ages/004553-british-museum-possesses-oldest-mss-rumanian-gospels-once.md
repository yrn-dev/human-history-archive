# The Rumanian Gospels of the British Museum

## Overview and Provenance
The British Museum is the repository for the oldest manuscripts of the Rumanian Gospels. These specific manuscripts were once the property of Petru Cercel, a member of the Bassarab dynasty. Petru Cercel was noted for his profound learning and his ability to speak twelve languages, which allowed him to maintain friendly correspondence with the poets and scholars of Italy. His tenure ended in 1590 when he was drowned in Constantinople by the Turks, an event resulting from the intrigues of Mihnea, who subsequently succeeded him on the throne of Walachia.

The manuscripts are distinguished by the fact that they contain the autograph signature of Petru Cercel. Following their acquisition or preservation, the text of these Gospels was published by Dr. M. Caster, with the expenses for the publication provided by the Rumanian government.

## The Bassarab Dynasty
The Bassarab dynasty, to which Petru Cercel belonged, included several prominent figures. Mircea (1386-1418) is noted for having accepted Turkish suzerainty. Other significant members included Neagoe, who founded the famous cathedral at Curtea de Argesh, and Michael, who was surnamed "the Brave" and reigned from 1592 to 1601. 

The dynasty's influence extended into the realms of law and literacy. Mateiu Bassarab, who reigned from 1633 to 1654, established the first printing-press in Rumania. Under his influence, the first code of laws was compiled and published in Bucharest in 1654. The Bassarab dynasty eventually became extinct in 1658 with Constantine Sherban.

## Other Gospel Manuscripts in the British Museum
While the Rumanian Gospels are of significant historical value, the British Museum holds other Gospel-related materials and illuminated manuscripts from various eras and traditions. These include:

*   **Byzantine Works:** The museum possesses two fragmentary leaves (Add. MS. 5111) of tables of the Eusebian canons. These leaves, likely executed in the Eastern capital during the 6th century, would have originally stood at the beginning of a copy of the Gospels. They are characterized by a gilt vellum surface and bright colors in a classical style.
*   **General Collections:** The museum's broader holdings of illuminated manuscripts are documented in official publications, such as G. F. Warner's *Illuminated MSS. in the British Museum* (1903) and the work of Birch and Jenner (1879).

## Related Manuscript Collections
The archive notes several other significant Gospel manuscripts located in different institutions for comparative context. These include the Gospels of Rossano in southern Italy and the *Gospels of Theodolinda* at Monza (dating from the 7th century). Additionally, the Victoria and Albert Museum at South Kensington holds the *Gospels of Charlemagne*.
