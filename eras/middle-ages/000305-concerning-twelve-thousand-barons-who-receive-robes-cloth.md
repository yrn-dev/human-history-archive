# The Twelve Thousand Barons and the Imperial Robes of Gold

In the court of the Great Kaan, a specific group of 12,000 men, distinguished by the name of *Keshican*, hold a privileged position. These 12,000 Barons and Knights are the recipients of extraordinary displays of imperial generosity, specifically regarding their raiment, which serves as a visible marker of their status and their relationship to the Sovereign.

## The Imperial Gifts of Raiment
The Great Kaan provides these 12,000 Barons with robes made of silk and gold. These garments are designed to be precisely like those worn by the Great Kaan himself, although the Emperor's own robes are grander, richer, and costlier. The purpose of this similarity in dress is to present the Barons as the comrades of the Emperor.

The robes are garnished in a rich and costly manner with pearls, gems, and other precious stones. The value of these garments is immense; a single suit may be worth as much as 10,000 golden bezants. In addition to the robes, the Sovereign bestows upon each man a fine golden girdle of great richness and value. Furthermore, the Barons receive a pair of boots made of *Borgal* (also termed *Camut*), which are curiously wrought with silver thread. When fully dressed in these appointments, it is noted that every man among the 12,000 looks like a king.

## The System of Thirteen Changes
The distribution of these costumes is not a one-time event but a recurring imperial custom. Thirteen times throughout the year, the Great Kaan presents these suits of raiment to his Barons and Knights. 

There is an established order governing which dress is to be worn at each of the thirteen feasts. A different colour is assigned to each festival, and the Barons must wear the same colour as the Emperor on those occasions. The system is organized such that for each of the thirteen changes, the 12,000 men are all dressed in one uniform colour; for the next change, they are all dressed in another colour, and so on, until thirteen different colours have been utilized.

## Scale and Significance
The scale of this operation is described as a "huge business," with costs that are scarcely possible to calculate. It is asserted that no other prince in the world could maintain such customs. This tradition is highlighted as a central feature of the Great Kaan's festivals, including the great feast held annually on his birthday and the festival held on New Year's Day.

## Distinction from Other Administrative Barons
It should be noted that these 12,000 *Keshican* are distinct from the twelve great Barons to whom the Great Kaan has committed the affairs of thirty-four great provinces. Those twelve administrative Barons reside together in a handsome palace within the city of Cambaluc and possess significant power, including the authority to choose governors for the provinces. While the twelve administrative Barons manage the judges and clerks of the provinces and report important affairs to the Emperor, the 12,000 Barons are specifically identified by their role in the imperial costume ceremonies and their designation as *Keshican*.
