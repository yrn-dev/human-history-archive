# BACONTHORPE, JOHN

John Baconthorpe (also referred to as Bacon, Baco, or Bacconius) was a learned Carmelite monk who died in 1346. Known by the epithet "the Resolute Doctor," he was born in Baconthorpe, Norfolk. Evidence suggests he was the grandnephew of Roger Bacon.

## Education and Academic Career
Baconthorpe was raised in the Carmelite monastery of Blakeney, located near Walsingham. He pursued higher education at the universities of Oxford and Paris. During his time in Paris, he gained a reputation as the "Princeps" of the Averroists, although the scholar Renan suggests that his efforts were primarily focused on justifying Averroism against accusations of heterodoxy.

## Ecclesiastical and Political Life
In 1329, Baconthorpe was elected as the twelfth provincial of the English Carmelites. He is noted for advocating the subordination of the clergy to the king, a position that appears to have anticipated the views of Wycliffe.

In 1333, he was summoned to Rome. Records indicate that he initially maintained the authority of the pope regarding cases of divorce, though he later retracted this opinion. He eventually died in London in 1346.

## Written Works and Legacy
Baconthorpe's primary work is titled *Doctoris resoluti Joannis Bacconis Anglici Carmelitae radiantissimi opus super quattuor sententiarum libris*, which was published in 1510 and has since been released in several editions. 

His intellectual influence persisted long after his death; nearly three centuries later, his work was still being studied at Padua, which served as the final home of Averroism. Additionally, Lucilio Vanini has spoken of Baconthorpe with great veneration.
