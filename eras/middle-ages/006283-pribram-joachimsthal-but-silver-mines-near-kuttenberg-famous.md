# Silver Mining in Bohemia: Kuttenberg, Pribram, and Joachimsthal

The region of Bohemia possesses a diverse array of mineral wealth, with silver extraction historically serving as a primary industry. While various metals and minerals are found throughout the territory, silver has been notably extracted at Pribram, Joachimsthal, and Kuttenberg.

## Kuttenberg
The town of Kuttenberg originated from its silver mines, the existence of which dates back to the first part of the 13th century. The city experienced rapid development and, by the early 14th century at the start of the Hussite troubles, had become the most important city in Bohemia after Prague. Due to its significance, it served as a favorite residence for several Bohemian kings.

A notable political event occurred in Kuttenberg on January 18, 1410, when Wenceslaus IV signed the decree of Kuttenberg. This decree granted the Bohemian nation three votes in the elections to the faculty of Prague University, compared to one vote for the other three "nations."

The town's history was marked by severe conflict. The mining population was predominantly German and fanatically Catholic, contrasting with the Czech and utraquist population of Prague. In the autumn of 1410, as reprisals for Hussite outrages in Prague, the miners of Kuttenberg captured, beheaded, burned, or threw Hussites alive into disused mine shafts. It is said that 1,600 people perished in these acts, including the clergy and magistrates of the town of Kaurim.

In 1420, Emperor Sigismund used the city as a base for an unsuccessful attack on the Taborites. The city was subsequently taken by Zizka. In 1422, imperial troops burned Kuttenberg to prevent it from falling back into the hands of the Taborites, though Zizka eventually took the place again, leading to a new period of prosperity under Bohemian auspices.

The decline of Kuttenberg began in 1541 when its richest mine was hopelessly flooded. The city further suffered during the Bohemian insurrection against Ferdinand I, during which it lost all its privileges. Its ruin was completed by the horrors of the Thirty Years' War and repeated visitations of the plague. Following the peace, attempts to repair the mines failed, and the town became impoverished, suffering further devastation by fire in 1770. The mines were abandoned at the end of the 18th century. Although the government reopened one mine in 1874, work was discontinued in 1903.

## Joachimsthal
Joachimsthal is a mining district in Bohemia where a rich silver mine was discovered in 1516. This mine was appropriated by the count of Schlitz, who commissioned the striking of a large number of silver coins, the first of which were dated 1518. These coins featured an effigy of St Joachim and were named *Joachimsthaler* (or sometimes *Schlickenthaler*).

The *Joachimsthaler* was designed to be the silver equivalent of the gold gulden used in Germany since the 14th century. The name *Joachimsthaler* was eventually shortened to *thaler* (with variants such as *daler*, *dalar*, and *tallero*), which in turn became the root for the word "dollar." The first English use of the word "dollar" referred to this specific silver coin. In addition to silver, uranium and radium are extracted near Joachimsthal.

## Pribram
Pribram is another site of mineral extraction in Bohemia. In addition to silver, lead is extracted at this location.
