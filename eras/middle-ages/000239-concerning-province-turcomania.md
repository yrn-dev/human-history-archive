# CONCERNING THE PROVINCE OF TURCOMANIA

The province of Turcomania is a region characterized by a diverse population and a landscape of mountains and downs. The inhabitants are divided into three distinct classes: the Turcomans, the Armenians, and the Greeks.

## Population and Culture
The Turcomans are described as a rude people who possess an uncouth language of their own. They are worshippers of Mahommet. Due to the geography of the region, which consists of downs and mountains providing good pasture, the primary occupation of the Turcomans is cattle-keeping.

The other two classes of people, the Greeks and the Armenians, live mixed with the Turcomans within the villages and towns. Unlike the pastoral Turcomans, the Greeks and Armenians occupy themselves with handicrafts and trade.

## Industry and Trade
The province is renowned for the production of high-quality textiles. The inhabitants weave what are described as the handsomest and finest carpets in the world. Additionally, they produce a great quantity of rich and fine silks in various colors, including cramoisy, as well as other stuffs.

The region is also noted for its livestock. Valuable mules are reared in the country, as well as excellent horses known as *Turquans*.

## Geography and Administration
The chief cities of Turcomania include Conia, Casaria, and Savast; the latter is noted as the place where Messer Saint Blaise suffered martyrdom. The province contains numerous other towns and bishops' sees.

Politically, the people of Turcomania are subject to the Tartar of the Levant, who serves as their Suzerain.
