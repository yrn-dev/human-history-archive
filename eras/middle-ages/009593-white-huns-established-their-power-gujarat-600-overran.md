# White Huns

The White Huns, also known as the Ephthalites, were a significant and enigmatic tribe that played a major role in the history of Persia and India during the 5th and 6th centuries. They are referred to by various names across different historical records: Byzantine writers called them *Ephthalitoi*, *Euthagitoi*, *Nephthalitoi*, or *Abdeloi* (the latter being an attempt to render a name likely sounding like Aptal or Haptal). In Arabic and Persian sources, they are known as Haital, and in Armenian as Haithal, Idal, or Hepthal. In India, they were called Hunas. The term "White Huns" (*Leukoi Ounnoi*) refers to their fair skin.

## Origins and Migration
The Ephthalites emerged from a nomadic section of the Yueh-Chi who remained in Central Asia on the steppes of Turkestan. They established their dominion in the extensive plains of Sogdiana on the eastern side of the Caspian, where they were known as the Euthalites or Nepthalites. In this region, they abandoned the pastoral life of Scythia; their king resided in Gorgo (later known as Carizme), exercising legal authority over a population whose luxury was supported by the labor of the Sogdians.

## Invasions of India
Beginning around the year 470—approximately twenty-five years after the death of Attila—the White Huns began raiding India. Unlike other groups, they did not migrate into India permanently but instead moved to and fro, looting the region and returning with their spoils to their own country. 

These irruptions are noted primarily for the atrocities committed by the invaders, who resembled the Huns of Attila in their barbarism. Their most capable leader, Mihiragula, has been described as the "Attila of India." He is recorded as having a fondness for the expensive amusement of rolling elephants down precipitous places to watch them suffer. These abominations eventually provoked his Indian tributary princes to revolt, leading to his overthrow in 528.

## Political Influence and the Gurjaras
The period of Hun tyranny lasted until the White Huns were overthrown on the Oxus by the Turks around 565. Despite this, native dynasties in India either survived or established themselves during this era. For example, in Kathiawar, a chief named Bhatarka founded a dynasty at Valabhi around 500 on the ruins of Gupta power.

A new power, the Gurjaras (believed to be ancestors of the Gujar caste), appeared from the north, having likely entered India alongside the White Huns. The Gurjaras established power over Gujarat and, by approximately 600, overran north-eastern Kathiawar. They made the raja of Valabhi their tributary and established a branch at Broach between 585 and 740.

## Decline and Legacy
The final end of the Ephthalite raids into India was caused not by Indians, but by the destruction of their central establishment on the Oxus in 565 by the Turks, who acted in alliance with the Persians. Following this break-up, the Ephthalites dissolved rapidly into the surrounding populations. It is said that some of the chief Rajput clans of North India in Rajputana are descended from these White Huns.
