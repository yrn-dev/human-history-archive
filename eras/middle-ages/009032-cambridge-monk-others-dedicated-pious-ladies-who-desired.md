# Osbern Bokenam

Osbern Bokenam was an English author who, according to his own accounts, was born on October 6, 1393. There is some scholarly discussion regarding his origins; Dr. Horstmann suggests he may have been a native of Bokeham (now Bookham) in Surrey, deriving his name from that location. However, a concluding note found in his *Lives of the Saints* describes him as "frere Austyn of Stoke Clare," identifying him as a "Suffolke man."

## Literary Works and Style
Bokenam is known for writing a series of thirteen legends focused on holy women and maidens. These poems were primarily composed using stanzas of seven and eight lines, and nine of these legends include prologues. In terms of literary influence, Bokenam was a follower of Lydgate and Chaucer, and it is believed he was specifically influenced by Chaucer's *Legend of Good Women*. 

Bokenam wrote in the Suffolk speech and adopted a plain style. He justified this lack of ornamentation by stating that the "finest flowers" of literary decoration had already been plucked by Gower, Lydgate, and Chaucer. While he utilized various sources, his primary source was the *Legenda Aurea* written by Jacobus de Voragine, the archbishop of Genoa, whom Bokenam refers to as "Januence."

## Dedications and Manuscripts
The first of Bokenam's legends, titled *Vita Scae Margaretae, virginis et martiris*, was written for Thomas Burgh, who was a monk at Cambridge. Other legends in the series were dedicated to pious ladies who wished to read the history of the saints who shared their names.

A unique copy of Bokenam's work is preserved as Arundel MS. 327 in the British Museum. According to the concluding note in this manuscript, the work was completed in 1447. The scribe, Thomas Burgh, presented the manuscript to an unnamed convent so that the nuns might remember him and his sister, Dame Betrice Burgh.

## Other Writings and Editions
In addition to the legends, Bokenam's work includes a dialogue written in both English and Latin. This dialogue, which features a "Frere answerynge" and a "Secular asking" at the grave of Dame Johan of Acres, details the lineal descent of the lords of the honour of Clare from 1248 to 1356. This piece is also found in Dugdale's *Monasticon Anglicanum*.

Bokenam's poems have been edited in two notable editions:
* *Lyvys of Seyntys*, edited in 1835 for the Roxburghe Club.
* *Osbern Bokenams Legenden*, edited by Dr. Carl Horstmann in 1883 and published in E. Kolbing's *Altengl. Bibliothek*, vol. i.

## Life and Travels
Beyond his writing, Bokenam was a traveler. He visited Italy on at least two separate occasions and made a pilgrimage to Santiago de Compostela in 1445.
