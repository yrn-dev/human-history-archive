# Sympathius and the Revision of the Basilica

## Overview of the Basilica
The Basilica was a code of law written in the Greek language. It was developed to resolve significant legal uncertainty that existed throughout the East Roman empire during the 9th century regarding authorized legal sources. This uncertainty had been caused by conflicting interpretations of the legislation of the emperor Justinian by 6th-century jurists. These conflicting opinions created a system of teaching that stripped Justinian's legislation of its authority, leaving imperial judges unable to determine the rules of law required to regulate their decisions.

## Early Attempts at Reform
Prior to the creation of the Basilica, the emperor Leo the Isaurian attempted to remedy these legal issues. However, his efforts at reform were viewed as being calculated to increase the uncertainty of the law rather than resolve it.

## The Role of Basil the Macedonian
The task of purifying the administration of justice and reducing the law into an intelligible code fell to Basil the Macedonian. While there has been controversy regarding the specific part Basil played in framing the new code, he formally abrogated ancient laws that had fallen into desuetude. It is considered probable that he ordered a revision of the ancient laws that were to remain in force, organizing them into forty books.

## Expansion and Further Revisions
Following Basil, his son, Leo the Philosopher, enlarged and redistributed the code into sixty books. There are claims, based on the authority of the 12th-century canonist Theodorus Balsamon, that a further revision was conducted by Constantine Porphyrogenitus, the son and successor of Leo. Balsamon cited passages from the Basilica in his preface to the *Nomocanon* of Patriarch Photius that differed from the text revised by Leo. However, the weight of authority suggests that no further revision occurred after the formal process undertaken during the reign of Leo.

## The Commission under Sympathius
During the reign of the emperor Leo, a formal revision of the code was conducted. To achieve this, Leo appointed a commission of jurists. This commission was placed under the presidency of Sympathius, who held the position of captain of the body-guard.
