# Dorotheenor Minoritenkirche

The Dorotheenor Minoritenkirche is a Roman Catholic church located in the city of Breslau (known in Polish as Wraclaw). The city serves as an episcopal see and the capital of the Prussian province of Silesia, situated on the navigable Oder river.

## History and Architecture
The Dorotheenor Minoritenkirche was founded in 1351 by the emperor Charles IV. Architecturally, the church is noted for its high-pitched roof. It is recognized as one of the most notable Roman Catholic churches within the city.

## Context of the Inner Town
The church is situated in the inner town of Breslau. This area is characterized by narrow streets that retain much of their ancient character and contain several secular and religious medieval buildings of significant beauty and interest. The inner town is located on the left bank of the Oder and was previously surrounded by ramparts, which were dismantled after 1813 and replaced by beautiful promenades.

## Other Notable Religious Structures in Breslau
The Dorotheenor Minoritenkirche is part of a collection of significant Roman Catholic sites in the city, which also include:
* **The Cathedral:** Dedicated to St John the Baptist, construction began in 1148 and was completed at the end of the 15th century. It underwent enlargements in the 17th and 18th centuries and restoration between 1873 and 1875. The cathedral contains sculptures, paintings, and a high altar made of beaten silver.
* **The Kreuzkirche:** A brick building from the 13th and 14th centuries known for its historical monuments, including the tomb of Henry IV, duke of Silesia, and its stained glass.
* **The Sandkirche:** Dating from the 14th century, this church was dedicated to Our Lady on the Sand and served as the church of the Augustinian canons until 1810.
