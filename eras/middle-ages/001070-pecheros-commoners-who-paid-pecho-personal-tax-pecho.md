# Pecheros

The *pecheros* were a social class of commoners in Spain and Spanish America defined by their obligation to pay the *pecho*, a form of personal tax. The term *pecho* is derived from the Latin *pectus*, meaning "breast," which was extended in Spanish to refer to the person.

## Role in Local Administration

The *pecheros* played a role in the administration of the *ayuntamiento*, the Spanish term for a town council or the district over which such a council held administrative authority. The word *ayuntamiento* originates from the Latin *adjungere* and originally signified a "meeting." In certain regions of Spain and within Spanish America, these councils were also referred to as the *cabildo* or chapter (from the Latin *capitulum*).

The composition of the *ayuntamiento* included official members and *regidores*, or regulators. These *regidores* were selected in varying proportions from two distinct social groups: the *hidalgos* (nobles, or *hijos de algo*, meaning "sons of somebody") and the *pecheros*. The activities of the *regidores* within the lay *cabildos* were overseen by a *corregidor*, a royal judge who served as the permanent president or chairman.

## Social Distinction and Legal Status

The distinction between the *hidalgo* and the *pechero* was a primary social division, based on the requirement to pay the personal tax. While the *pecheros* were subject to the *pecho*, the *hidalgos* were not. In modern Spain, this specific distinction between *hidalgo* and *pechero* has been abolished, although the constitution and powers of the *ayuntamientos* have undergone various modifications over time.
