# Bardas Phocas and the Rebellion of 987

## Military Career and Recall
Bardas Phocas was a general of the Roman Empire in the East. He had previously revolted during a prior reign, an act that resulted in his internment in a monastery. However, he was later recalled to service to oppose the rebellion of Bardas Sclerus. Sclerus, an ambitious general from the powerful landed aristocracy of Asia Minor, had been deposed from his post as general in the East by the eunuch Basileios, the president of the senate. Sclerus had seized control of the Asiatic provinces and posed a direct threat to Constantinople. Bardas Phocas engaged Sclerus in three battles; although defeated in the first two, Phocas was victorious in the third, successfully suppressing the revolt in 979.

## The Rebellion of 987
Following the defeat of Sclerus, Phocas continued to serve as the general in the East. He remained in this position until 987, at which point he rebelled against the reigning emperors. During this uprising, Phocas was proclaimed emperor by his own troops. Evidence suggests that the minister Basileios may have been privy to this rebellion. The primary cause of the revolt was dissatisfaction regarding the energy being displayed by the emperor.

## Political Context of the Era
The rebellion of Bardas Phocas occurred during the joint reign of Basil II and his brother Constantine. Basil II, the son of Romanus II and Theophano, was crowned on April 22, 960. Following the death of their father in 963, the brothers served as nominal emperors while their stepfather, Nicephorus Phocas, and subsequently John Tzimisces, held actual power. 

Upon the death of John Tzimisces on January 10, 976, Basil and Constantine assumed sovereignty. However, Constantine exercised no power and focused on pleasure, adhering to the Byzantine principle that only one individual governed when multiple *basileis* co-regned. While Basil II was a brave soldier and an able general, he initially did not show the full extent of his energy. Instead, he spent time learning administrative business and military science while the administration was managed by the eunuch Basileios, who hoped the young emperors would serve as his puppets.
