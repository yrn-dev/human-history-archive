# The Black Death: How Plague Reshaped Europe

## Overview
The Black Death, which ravaged Europe between 1347 and 1351, was one of the most devastating pandemics in human history. Caused by the bacterium *Yersinia pestis* and transmitted primarily via fleas on black rats, the plague entered Europe through trade routes from Central Asia, landing first in the ports of Sicily and Crimea. 

While the immediate effect was a catastrophic loss of life—estimated at 30% to 60% of the European population—the long-term consequences were transformative. The plague acted as a violent catalyst that accelerated the collapse of the medieval feudal system. With a sudden, massive shortage of labor, surviving peasants found themselves with unprecedented bargaining power. They demanded higher wages and lower rents, effectively breaking the shackles of serfdom and paving the way for a more fluid, market-based economy. Furthermore, the inability of the Church to stop the pestilence led to a gradual decline in absolute papal authority, planting the early seeds of skepticism and humanism that would eventually bloom into the Renaissance.

## Key Facts
*   **The Three Strains:** The plague manifested in three forms: **Bubonic** (swelling of lymph nodes or "buboes"), **Septicemic** (infecting the blood), and **Pneumonic** (infecting the lungs and spreading through coughing).
*   **Social Upheaval:** The labor shortage led to the *Statute of Labourers (1351)* in England, an attempt by the elite to freeze wages. This tension contributed directly to the Peasants' Revolt of 1381.
*   **Medical Ignorance:** Medieval doctors believed in "miasma"—the idea that disease was spread by "bad air." This led to the use of aromatic herbs and the iconic (though later period) plague doctor masks.
*   **Economic Shift:** The death toll led to an abundance of land and livestock for the survivors, increasing the standard of living for the lower classes for the first time in centuries.

## Did You Know?
1. **The Origin of "Quarantine":** The word comes from the Italian *quaranta giorni* (forty days), referring to the period ships were required to wait in isolation off the coast of Venice to ensure they weren't carrying the plague.
2. **The Flagellant Movement:** Some people believed the plague was a divine punishment from God and formed groups called Flagellants, who traveled between towns whipping themselves publicly to seek forgiveness.
3. **Artistic Shift:** The plague introduced the *Danse Macabre* (Dance of Death) motif in art, depicting skeletons leading people of all social ranks to the grave, emphasizing that death is the great equalizer.
