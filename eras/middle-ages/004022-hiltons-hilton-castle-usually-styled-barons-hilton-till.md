# Hiltons of Hilton Castle

The Hiltons of Hilton Castle were a prominent family within the palatinate of Durham. During the 12th century, they were among the ten palatinate barons, and were considered one of the most important of these lords.

## Status and Title
Within the palatinate of Durham, the bishop maintained his own barons. The Hiltons of Hilton Castle were usually styled as "Barons of Hilton" until the family became extinct in 1746. This title was accorded to them independently of peerage dignity.

## Role in the Palatinate of Durham
The palatinate of Durham featured a specific administrative and judicial structure. The bishop's council was responsible for regulating judicial affairs and was composed of the sheriff, the clergy, and the barons. This council later produced the courts of Marshalsea and Admiralty, as well as the Chancery. 

The palatinate's administrative landscape also included other officers such as the chancellor, the chamberlain, the coroners, and the sheriff. Until the 15th century, the steward served as the most important administrative officer. Additionally, a palatine exchequer was organized during the 12th century, and a palatine assembly existed to represent the whole county, focusing primarily on fiscal matters.

## Historical Context of the Title "Baron"
The term "baron" was introduced to England following the Conquest to describe "the man" of a great lord or the king—specifically those who had performed homage. Those who held land directly from the king were known as *barones regis* and were theoretically members of his council and bound to perform stipulated services.

While the term was often associated with those who held large amounts of land, the status of barons evolved. Originally connected to land tenure, the position shifted when presence in the council of the realm (which became the House of Lords) was determined by the king's will via a writ of summons rather than land tenure. This change is attributed by some, such as Stubbs, to Edward I.

The specific style of "baron" as a title was introduced by Richard II in 1387. Prior to this, the prefix *Dominus* (lord) was used, which was also common among simple knights. Early patents of creation often used the formula "Lord A. (and) Baron of B.," though grantees frequently styled themselves as "Lord" only.

## Other Palatine Barons
The Hiltons were part of a broader system of palatine lordships. In the 12th century, other important palatinate barons in Durham included:
* The Lumleys of Lumley Castle
* The Hansards of Evenwood
* The Conyers of Sockburne
* The Bulmers of Brancepeth

Similar palatine characters existed in other regions. In the earldom of Chester, the Venables family served as "barons of Kinderton" until 1679. In Ireland, the title was also not uncommon due to the palatine nature of great lordships, with examples including the barons of Slane, the barons of the Naas, and the barons of Galtrim.
