# CONCERNING THE PERSON OF THE GREAT KAAN

The Great Kaan, specifically identified as Kúblái Kaan, was a ruler of immense puissance who presided over a vast empire. His administration and personal life were characterized by elaborate court rituals, a complex system of governance, and a penchant for grand festivals and hunting expeditions.

## Court and Administration
The Great Kaan maintained a sophisticated administrative structure to manage his territories. He appointed twelve Barons who were charged with overseeing all of his affairs, including the authority to dispose of offices and lands at their discretion. These Barons also managed the requests of supplicants who arrived at court seeking employment.

Among his officials was a Saracen named Achmath, a "shrewd and able man" who held significant influence over the Kaan. It was later revealed that Achmath used sorcery to gain the Kaan's absolute faith and reliance, allowing him to direct the Kaan's actions.

To maintain security and communication, the Kaan employed a guard of twelve thousand horse known as the Keshican. He also established an extensive system of posts and runners, referred to as the Yam, which utilized government hostelries to ensure rapid communication across many provinces. In terms of economic administration, the Kaan introduced a form of currency made from the bark of trees, processed to resemble paper, which was used throughout his country.

## Personal Life and Residence
The Kaan resided in the city of Cambaluc, where he occupied a great palace. This palace featured arsenals, towers, and gates, and was associated with a "Divine" Park and the Green Mount. The Kaan also maintained a Bamboo Palace in Shangtu, to which he migrated annually.

The Kaan's personal life included several wives and a connection to the Kungurat tribe, which was noted for competitive examinations in beauty. In his private pursuits, the Kaan was an avid hunter. He kept a variety of animals for the chase, including lions, leopards, wolves, lynxes, and the Búrgút eagle. His hunting expeditions were highly organized, involving hawking establishments and the use of great tents adorned with sable and ermine. During these tours, the Kaan traveled in a litter.

## Festivals and Rituals
The Great Kaan observed two primary annual festivals. The first was his birthday, celebrated on the 28th day of the September moon. This was the greatest feast of the year, during which Tartars and all governments owing allegiance to the Kaan offered presents. On this day, Christians, Saracens, and Idolaters performed solemn devotions, lighting lamps and burning incense to pray for the Emperor's health, happiness, and long life.

The second major event was the New Year's festival, known as the White Feast. During the great festivals, the Emperor bestowed robes of cloth of gold upon twelve thousand Barons, with each receiving thirteen changes of clothing. These celebrations often included the presence of jugglers and other artistes who performed in the dining halls after dinner.

## Military and Justice
The Kaan's military history included significant conflicts, most notably the revolt of his uncle, Nayan. Kúblái Kaan marched against Nayan and fought a battle involving the use of Nakkára and bretesches. Following the conflict, the Kaan ordered Nayan's death, though he took care to avoid the shedding of royal blood.

In his governance, the Kaan was noted for his justice and his willingness to provide help to his people during times of murrain or dearth through the granting of remissions. He also improved the infrastructure of his realm by ordering the planting of trees along the highways to create avenues.
