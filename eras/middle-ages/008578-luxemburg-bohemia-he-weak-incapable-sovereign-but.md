# The House of Luxemburg in Bohemia

The rule of the Luxemburg dynasty over Bohemia began in the 14th century and continued until the dynasty's extinction upon the death of Sigismund in 1437.

## Accession of John of Luxemburg
The transition to Luxemburg rule followed a period of instability. After the end of the Premyslide dynasty in 1306, Bohemia saw the reign of Henry, duke of Carinthia, who had married a daughter of King Wenceslas II. Henry eventually became unpopular due to accusations that he unduly favored German settlers, leading the Bohemians to depose him. Seeking to maintain their right to elect their own sovereign, the Bohemians chose John of Luxemburg, the son of Henry, king of the Romans.

John of Luxemburg strengthened his connection to the country through his marriage to Princess Elizabeth, a daughter of Wenceslas II. However, he showed little personal interest in Bohemia, spending most of his life in other lands. His military campaigns were extensive, ranging from Lithuania in the north to Italy in the south. During his reign, it became a proverb that nothing in the world could be accomplished without the help of God and the king of Bohemia.

## Foreign Policy and Conflict
John of Luxemburg's primary political sympathy lay with France, with whom he maintained a close alliance. He eventually died fighting as an ally of France at the battle of Crecy in 1346. He is buried in the church of St Michael, with parts of which date to 1320.

During his tenure, John was a "constant thorn in the side" of Casimir III, the king of Poland. The two monarchs clashed over claims to the Polish crown and the territory of Silesia, which was then divided into seventeen principalities. Conflict escalated when Casimir invaded Silesia, captured Prince Charles of Bohemia, and took Wschowa, leading to a siege of Cracow by the Czechs. Peace was eventually restored in 1346 through the mediation of the Holy See. Prior to this, in early 1335, a conference at Trencsen had seen Casimir induce the king of Bohemia to relinquish claims to the Polish throne in exchange for a free hand in Silesia.

## The Reign of Charles IV
Following the death of John at Crecy, his son Charles was elected emperor. In contrast to his father's adventurous nature, Charles IV was a cautious sovereign. He shared Casimir III's desire for the maintenance of peace, and as a result, relations between Bohemia and Poland were not seriously disturbed during his rule.

## Decline under Wenceslas IV
The rule of the house of Luxemburg over Bohemia began to decline during the reign of Charles's son, Wenceslas IV, who ascended the throne at seventeen years of age. Wenceslas is described as a weak and incapable sovereign. His reign was characterized by a continual struggle with powerful Bohemian nobles, who imprisoned the king on two separate occasions.

Wenceslas also engaged in a dispute with the archbishop, an event that resulted in the death of John of Nepomuk. While older historians have leveled exaggerated accusations against Wenceslas, these are attributed largely to the fact that the king and his queen, Sophia, supported the cause of church reform for a period, which drew the displeasure of Romanist writers.
