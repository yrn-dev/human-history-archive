# BONIFACE, SAINT (680-754)

Saint Boniface, known by his birth name Wynfrith, was a Saxon monk and missionary recognized as the apostle of Germany. Born into a good Saxon family at either Kirton or Crediton in Devonshire, he pursued early studies in theology and grammar at Exeter and subsequently at Nutcell, near Winchester, where he studied under Abbot Winberht. After distinguishing himself as a preacher and scholar, he left his monastery in 716 to serve as a missionary in Frisia.

## Early Missions and Papal Commission
Boniface's initial efforts in Frisia were interrupted, likely due to the hostility of Radbod, the king of the Frisians, who was engaged in war with Charles Martel. Following this, Boniface traveled to Rome at the end of 717. In 719, Pope Gregory II commissioned him to evangelize Germany and counteract the influence of Irish monks in the region. 

After crossing the Alps and visiting Thuringia and Bavaria, Boniface returned to Frisia upon learning of King Radbod's death. Working under the direction of Willibrord, the first bishop of Utrecht, he preached successfully for three years. Around 722, he visited Thuringia and Hesse, where he baptized many heathens and won over several chieftains. Following these successes, he was summoned to Rome and consecrated as a bishop on November 30, 722, after swearing an oath of obedience to the pope.

## Organization of the German Church
With letters of recommendation to Charles Martel, Boniface's mission was expanded to include both the conversion of heathens and the suppression of heresy. Under the protection of Charles, Boniface conducted a systematic crusade that involved overturning idols, founding monasteries and churches, and baptizing populations. He recruited a group of missionary helpers from England, including monks and nuns such as St. Lull, St. Burchard, St. Gregory, Willibald, St. Lioba, St. Walburge, and St. Thecla.

Boniface's career was marked by significant organizational achievements:
* **Bavaria:** After a third visit to Rome in 738, he organized the Bavarian church into four bishoprics: Passau, Salzburg, Freising, and Regensburg.
* **East Frankish Church:** In 741, Pope Zacharias appointed him legate, charging him with the reformation of the entire Frankish church. With the support of Pippin and Carloman (successors to Charles Martel as mayors of the palace), he established four bishoprics in the east Frankish church: Eichstadt, Buraburg, Wurzburg, and Erfurt, placing his own monks in charge.
* **Mainz:** Between 746 and 748, Boniface became the bishop of Mainz. He established the see of Mainz as a pre-eminent center, becoming metropolitan over Utrecht, the Rhine bishoprics, and those he had founded in Germany.
* **Fulda:** He founded the abbey of Fulda to serve as a center for German monastic culture, placing it under the Bavarian Sturm and implementing a rule stricter than the Benedictine rule.

In 742, Boniface presided over what is generally considered the first German council. In 747, a synod of Frankish bishops sent a formal statement of submission to papal authority to Rome, a move that countered the powerful tendencies toward national churches seen under the Merovingians.

## Conflicts and Literary Works
Boniface encountered several theological and disciplinary disputes. He engaged in a controversy with Virgil, the Irish bishop of Salzburg, who held the heresy that worlds other than our own existed. In France, Boniface opposed a Frankish bishop of Neustria named Adalbert (or Aldebert), who claimed to receive relics directly from angels rather than from Rome and encouraged people to desert churches in favor of crosses planted in open fields.

Despite being primarily a man of action, Boniface produced several literary works. These include his *Epistolae* (Letters), a grammar titled *De octo partibus orationibus*, poems known as *Aenigmata*, a penitential, and the *Dicta Bonifacii*. Some sermons are also attributed to him, though their authenticity is contested.

## Death and Legacy
In 752, Boniface did not appear to participate in the anointing of Pippin as king of the Franks. In 754, he resigned his archbishopric in favor of Lull to return to his original goal of a mission to Frisia. On June 5, 754, Boniface and his companions were massacred by heathens near Dockum. His remains were later transferred to Fulda.

Boniface is often described as the "proconsul of the papacy." His legacy lies in his organizing genius and his success in bringing the missionary movement—previously dominated by independent or schismatic Irish monks—under the direct control of Rome. He is credited with welding scattered centers of faith together through a zeal for unity.
