# The Brigantes

The Brigantes were a considerable tribe of barbarians inhabiting northern Britain. Their name is Celtic for "free, privileged" or "mountaineers." They were among the most significant of the thirty tribes that divided the country before the loss of British freedom.

## Territory and Settlement
The territory of the Brigantes extended from the mouth of the Abus (Humber) in the east to the Belisama (the Ribble, or according to other sources, the Mersey) in the west. Their lands reached as far north as the Wall of Antoninus. This region encompassed the entirety of Lancashire, Durham, Westmorland, and Cumberland, as well as most of Yorkshire and a portion of Northumberland. Their chief town was Eburacum (York). Additionally, the town of Olicana, identified as the urban district of Ilkley in the West Riding of Yorkshire, served as one of the towns for the tribe. A branch of the Brigantes also established a settlement in the south-east corner of Ireland, situated near the river Birgus (Barrow).

## Roman Contact and Conquest
The Brigantes first encountered the Romans during the reign of Claudius, at which time they were defeated by Publius Ostorius Scapula. Later, during the reign of Vespasian, they submitted to Petillius Cerealis. Despite these events, they were not finally subdued until the time of Antoninus Pius.

To maintain control over the region, the Romans utilized strategic infrastructure. The Roman road known as Watling Street crossed the Cheviots at Brownhartlaw. Near this location was the camp of *Ad Fines*, which was used as a means to hold the warlike Brigantes to the south—as well as the Otadeni and Gadeni to the north—in check.

## Religion and Culture
The Brigantes worshipped a pantheon that included an eponymous goddess, Brigantia, whose name appears on various inscriptions. Records also indicate the existence of a god named Bergans (or Brigans).

## Related Terminology
The French word "brigandine" derives from the terms *brigandi* or *brigantes*, referring to light-armed foot soldiers. In the middle ages, brigandine referred to a specific type of foot soldier's armour, which consisted of a padded tunic made of leather or canvas and lined with rings of iron or closely sewn scales.
