# The Invasion of Timur (1395)

## Overview of the Invasion
In 1395, the regions of the Volga were ruined by an invasion led by Timur. While Timur's forces caused extensive destruction within the Volgan regions, they did not penetrate as far as Moscow.

## Impact on the Golden Horde and Muscovy
The raid conducted by Timur proved to be of service to the Russian prince, Basil I. Dmitrevich, because the invasion nearly wiped out the Golden Horde. Following this event, the Golden Horde entered a state of anarchy that lasted for the next twelve years.

During this twelve-year period of instability, no tribute was paid to the khan. Instead, the Moscow treasury collected vast sums of money intended for military purposes.

## The Reign of Basil I. Dmitrevich
Basil I. Dmitrevich (1371-1425) succeeded his father, Dmitri (Demetrius) Donskoi, in 1389. He married Sophia, the daughter of Vitovt, the grand-duke of Lithuania. Under his leadership, the grand-duchy of Muscovy asserted its supremacy over surrounding principalities and became practically hereditary.

Despite the temporary reprieve from tribute following Timur's invasion, Basil had previously been compelled to pay tribute to the grand khan, Tokhtamuish, and received his investiture, or *yarluik*, from the Golden Horde. Later, in 1412, Basil found it necessary to make a visit of submission to the Horde to address the long-deferred obligations.

## Territorial Expansion and Internal Affairs
Basil I. Dmitrevich expanded the reach of Muscovy through various means:
* **Annexations:** He annexed the principality of Suzdal, as well as Murom, Kozelsk, Peremyshl, and other locations.
* **Vassalage:** He reduced the grand-duchy of Rostov to a state of vassalage.
* **Treaties:** He acquired territory from the republic of Great Novgorod via treaty.

The reign of Basil I. Dmitrevich was also marked by significant hardship and political shifts. The population was decimated by a terrible visitation of the "Black Death." Additionally, in 1408, Muscovite territory was ravaged by the Mirza Edigei, although Moscow itself was not taken.

## Ecclesiastical Events
A major ecclesiastical event occurred in 1425 when Vitovt, the grand-duke of Lithuania, elevated Gregory Tsamblak, a Bulgarian, to the metropolitan see of Kiev. This action had the immediate political consequence of weakening Muscovy's hold on the south-western Russian states.
