# Black Stones of Cathay

In the country of Cathay, there exists a specific type of black stone found in beds within the mountains. These stones are extracted through digging and are utilized by the population as a primary source of fuel, serving as a substitute for firewood.

## Properties and Use as Fuel
The black stones of Cathay are noted for their efficiency as fuel. When these stones are kindled and the fire is supplied at night, they remain alight until the morning. Because they burn better and are less expensive than wood, they have become the dominant fuel source throughout the country, to the extent that no other fuel is used.

While Cathay possesses an abundance of wood, it is not burned for fuel. This preference for the black stones is driven by both cost and the immense demand for heat. The population is vast, and there is a widespread culture of bathing; every person typically maintains such a bath at least three times a week, and daily during the winter. Furthermore, men of wealth and noblemen maintain private baths for their own use. Given these requirements, the available wood would be insufficient to meet the needs of the people.

## Context of Infrastructure and Governance
The use of these stones exists within a broader system of imperial administration under the Great Kaan. The Emperor has implemented various public works to assist his subjects and travelers. For instance, he has ordered that all highways, including those through uninhabited tracts, be planted with rows of great trees spaced a few paces apart to ensure travelers do not lose their way. In areas where the ground is too sandy or desert-like for trees to grow, the Emperor causes pillars or stones to be set up as landmarks.

Additionally, the Great Kaan manages the welfare of his people through the creation of public granaries. When corn is abundant and cheap, the Emperor purchases large quantities and stores them in provinces, where the corn is maintained so that it remains viable for three or four years to provide relief during times of dearth.
