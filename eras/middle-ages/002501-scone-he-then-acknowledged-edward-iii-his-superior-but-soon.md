# Scone

Scone is a location of significant historical importance in Scotland, primarily serving as the site for the coronation of Scottish monarchs.

## Royal Coronations
The site has been used for the crowning of various claimants to the Scottish throne. On November 30, 1292, John de Baliol was crowned king of Scotland at Scone. This event followed a dispute over the vacancy of the throne caused by the death of Margaret, the "maid of Norway," a granddaughter of King Alexander III. John de Baliol had been selected for the crown by King Edward I of England after a court consisting of twenty-four Englishmen and eighty Scotsmen decided in Baliol's favor based on the principles of feudal law for an indivisible inheritance.

Later, in 1306, Robert Bruce was crowned at Scone. He was joined prior to this coronation by Sir James of Douglas, also known as the "Good" or the "Black Douglas," who had cast his lot with Bruce after his offer of allegiance to Edward I was refused.

## The Baliol Family and the Scottish Throne
The ascent of John de Baliol to the throne at Scone was the culmination of his family's rise in status. The Baliol family was founded in England by Guy (or Guido) de Baliol, a Norman baron who arrived with William the Conqueror. His descendant, Bernard de Baliol, built Castle Barnard in Durham. By the time of John de Baliol's father (also named John), the family had increased its wealth through marriage to Dervorguila, daughter of Alan, earl of Galloway.

The younger John de Baliol (1249–1315) claimed the Scottish crown through his maternal grandmother, Margaret, who was the eldest daughter of David, earl of Huntingdon. His primary rival for the throne was Robert Bruce, earl of Annandale. Before his coronation at Scone, Baliol had acknowledged Edward I as the overlord of Scotland and sworn fealty to him. Following his coronation, Baliol performed homage to Edward at Newcastle.

## Conflict and Succession
The stability following the coronation at Scone was short-lived. Relations between Baliol and Edward I were disturbed when a Scottish vassal brought a case to Edward, who acted as Baliol's overlord. By 1296, the conflict had escalated to the point where Antony Bek, the bishop of Durham, received the surrender of John de Baliol at Brechin.

The subsequent period of Scottish history was marked by intermittent warfare. Sir James of Douglas, a supporter of Robert Bruce, conducted various raids on the English border and fought at the battle of Bannockburn, where he commanded the left wing alongside Walter the Steward.
