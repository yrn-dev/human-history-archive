# John of Wartenberg

## Appointment as Burgrave
John of Wartenberg was appointed to the position of burgrave in Bohemia by King Louis. This appointment occurred in 1522 upon the arrival of King Louis in Bohemia from Hungary, where Louis had also been elected king. 

The appointment of John of Wartenberg was part of a broader restructuring of the Bohemian government. Upon his arrival at Prague, King Louis dismissed all existing Bohemian state officials. Among those removed was the powerful Leo of Rozmital, who had previously served as the supreme burgrave and had ruled the country almost without control during the nominal reign of the young king. In addition to appointing John of Wartenberg as burgrave, King Louis appointed Charles of Munsterberg—a grandson of King George and a cousin of Prince Bartholomew—to serve as the regent of Bohemia during the king's absences.

## The Office of Burgrave
The title of burgrave is the English form of the German *Burggraf* and Flemish *burg* or *burch-graeve* (derived from the medieval Latin *burcgravius* or *burgicomes*). The term literally translates to the count of a fortified town or a castle. This office is considered equivalent to that of a *châtelain* or a castellan (*castellanus*).

In the context of the German Empire, the office of burgrave evolved into a sinecure by the end of the 13th century. However, the title maintained a quasi-royal significance when held by feudal nobles who possessed the status of princes of the Empire. Due to this significance, the title continues to be included among the subsidiary titles of several sovereign princes. For example, the king of Prussia is still styled the burgrave of Nuremberg, as his ancestors held that specific burgraviate for more than 200 years.
