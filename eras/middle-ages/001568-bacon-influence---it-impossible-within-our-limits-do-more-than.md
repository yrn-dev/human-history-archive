# Bacon's Influence

The influence of Bacon's views on subsequent thinkers is observed across the fields of philosophy, natural science, and ethics. While some perspectives suggest his impact was immediate and lasting, other historical evidence indicates a more complex relationship with his contemporaries.

## Influence on Philosophy and Psychology
Bacon is regarded by many as the father of the most characteristic elements of English psychological speculation. This is evidenced by the connection between his work and the *Essay* by Locke. Specifically, the fundamental principle of Locke's *Essay*—that all ideas are the product of reflection and sensation—is briefly stated in the first aphorism of the *Novum Organum*. Furthermore, the general atmosphere of Locke's treatise is considered characteristic of the *Novum Organum*. Bacon described his own role in this intellectual awakening by stating that he "rang the bell which called the wits together."

## Influence on Ethics and Utilitarianism
In the sphere of ethics, Bacon is viewed as a forerunner of the empirical method. The inductive method discussed in the *Novum Organum*, along with the spirit of the *De Augmentis* (book vii), serves as the foundation for theories that construct moral codes through the inductive examination of human consciousness and the results of actions. Utilitarianism is identified as a natural result of applying the Baconian experimental method to the phenomenon of conduct.

## Contrasting Perspectives on Immediacy
Despite the lasting influence on later thinkers, there is evidence that Bacon's impact was not immediate. A notable example is Hobbes, who served as Bacon's secretary. In his works, Hobbes makes no critical reference to Bacon, nor does he mention Baconian induction.

## Roger Bacon
Distinct from the later philosopher, Roger Bacon was a 13th-century figure of high rank, comparable to Thomas Aquinas, Bonaventura, and Albertus Magnus. While these contemporaries enjoyed wider renown during their own time, modern criticism has shifted the balance in favor of Roger Bacon. Some modern views suggest he was a modern thinker in advance of his age, possessing conceptions of science that were clearer and more just than those of his namesake. 

However, this view is considered by some to be exaggerated. It is argued that Bacon was not a schoolman but a systematic and keen thinker working within a well-beaten track, though his contemporaries were being drawn away from that track by metaphysics and theology. He displayed no consciousness of his own originality. In a prophetic mood, Roger Bacon's approach is cited as an example for those performing creative analysis on human institutions.

## Political Conduct and Misunderstandings
In a separate matter of state necessity, Bacon's conduct regarding subsidies for the queen led to significant misunderstanding and offense. During a crisis, the House of Lords requested a conference regarding a triple subsidy to be collected in a shorter timeframe than usual. Bacon spoke against this proposed conference, arguing that while a communication from the Lords could be received, the actual deliberation must be handled by the Commons alone. His motion to reject the conference was carried.

Later, while Bacon approved of the increased subsidy, he opposed the proposal to raise it in four years instead of the customary six. He suggested that such heavy demands would be impossible for the people to meet, potentially leading to trouble and discontent, and proposed raising the money via imposition or levy. His motion failed, and the four-year subsidy passed unanimously. Although his actions were prompted by a desire to serve the queen, he was accused of seeking popularity, and his mistake regarding the country's ability to meet the taxation caused deep offense.
