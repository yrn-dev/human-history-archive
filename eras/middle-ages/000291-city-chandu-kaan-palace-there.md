# THE CITY OF CHANDU AND THE KAAN’S PALACE

## Overview and Location
Chandu is a city built by the reigning Kaan. It is situated in a direction between north and north-east, located three days' ride from the previously mentioned city in the archive's records. In some archival notes, the city is referred to as Shangtu.

## The Kaan’s Palace
The city is notable for a very fine palace constructed of marble. The interior of the palace is characterized by rooms that are entirely gilt and decorated with paintings. These artworks depict a variety of subjects, including birds, beasts, men, flowers, and trees. The execution of these paintings is described as being of such exquisite art that they inspire delight and astonishment in those who view them.

## Seasonal Residency and Governance
The Great Kaan distributes his year across different locations to manage his time and avoid the heat. He spends six months at his chief palace in the royal city of Cambaluc from September through February. Following this, from March to May, he engages in a great hunting expedition toward the sea. After returning to Cambaluc for three days, he travels to the city of Chandu, where he resides at the Kaan's Palace during the months of June, July, and August.

This seasonal migration also impacts the administration of other cities. For example, during the three months the Kaan spends at Chandu, the city of Cambaluc is left in the charge of ministers, such as Achmath, who must send for the Kaan's orders from Chandu whenever an emergency arises.
