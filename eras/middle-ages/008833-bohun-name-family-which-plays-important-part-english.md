# BOHUN

The Bohuns were a family that played a significant role in English history during the 13th and 14th centuries. The family name was derived from a village located in the Cotentin, situated between Coutances and the estuary of the Vire. While the Bohuns arrived in England at or shortly after the Norman Conquest, the details of their early history in the country remain obscure.

## Rise to Prominence
The foundation of the family's greatness is attributed to Humphrey III. During the later years of Henry I, Humphrey III served as a *dapifer*, or steward, within the royal household. He married the daughter of Milo of Gloucester. During the reign of Stephen, Humphrey III occupied an ambiguous position, initially siding with the king before later supporting the empress. He remained loyal to Henry II throughout various changes and fought against the rebels of East Anglia at Farnham in 1173. Humphrey III lived until 1187.

## The Earls of Hereford and Essex
Humphrey III was succeeded in the family estates by his grandson, Henry, as Humphrey had outlived his eldest son, Humphrey IV. Henry was the son of Margaret, a sister of William the Lion, which connected him to the royal house of Scotland. This alliance likely aided Henry in obtaining the earldom of Hereford from John in 1199. From this period, the Bohuns became foremost among the Marcher barons, as their lands were primarily located on the Welsh Marches.

Henry de Bohun was one of the twenty-five barons elected by his peers to enforce the terms of the Great Charter, appearing alongside the earls of Gloucester and Clare. During the subsequent civil war, Henry fought for Louis and was captured at the battle of Lincoln in 1217. He died on a pilgrimage on June 1, 1220, having taken the cross earlier that year.

His son and heir, Humphrey V, returned to a path of loyalty to the crown. Before 1239, he was permitted to inherit the earldom of Essex from his maternal uncle, William de Mandeville. However, by 1258, Humphrey V shifted his allegiance from the royal cause to the baronial cause. During the Oxford parliament of 1258, he served as a nominee of the opposition on a committee of twenty-four tasked with reforming the administration. Humphrey V eventually returned to his allegiance following the alliance between Llewelyn of North Wales and Montfort.

## Political and Social Standing
The Bohuns were ranked among the nobility of the realm and formed alliances with other prominent families, including the Plantagenets, the Talbots, the St. Johns, the Despensers, and the Veres. They also contracted alliances with the Courtenays of Devonshire.

In terms of state offices, the Bohuns held the position of Lord High Constable. This office, which originally combined the roles of master of the horse and commander of the royal armies, had been granted as a grand serjeanty along with the earldom of Hereford by the empress Maud to Milo of Gloucester. The office was carried to the Bohuns, the earls of Essex and Hereford, via Milo's heiress.

## Succession of the Constableship
The office of Lord High Constable eventually descended from the Bohuns to the Staffords, the dukes of Buckingham, through a coheiress of the Bohun family. The office later merged with the crown following the attainder of Edward Stafford, the third duke of Buckingham, during the reign of Henry VIII.
