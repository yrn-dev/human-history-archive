# Historia Saracenica

The *Historia Saracenica* is a historical work attributed to Georgius Elmacinus (also referred to as Al Mekin). It serves as a source for the history of the East and the Saracens, and has been utilized by various historians to reconstruct narratives of the region.

## Editions and Translations
The work was edited and provided with a Latin translation by Thomae Erpenius (Erpenius), published in Leiden (Lugd. Batavorum) in 1625. Regarding the quality of this version, Edward Gibbon noted that Erpenius is said to have translated a corrupt manuscript in a hasty manner, resulting in a version that is frequently deficient in both sense and style.

## Authorship and Context
The author, Georgius Elmacinus, is identified as a Christian who lived during the 10th century. He is categorized as one of the early historians of the East, specifically a native of Egypt. In the broader context of Arabian and Eastern historiography, the *Historia Saracenica* is distinguished from other types of historical writing, such as the "dry annalist" or the "tumid and flowery orator."

## Historical Utility
The *Historia Saracenica* has been cited as an authority in the study of the Eastern Empire. Specifically, it is listed as a source for information regarding the reign of Basil, a period that marked the highest point of power for the Eastern empire since the time of Justinian I. It is grouped alongside other contemporary and subsequent chronicles, such as the works of Leo Diaconus, Psellus, George Cedrenus, and the Asiatic chronicles of Yahyā of Antioch.
