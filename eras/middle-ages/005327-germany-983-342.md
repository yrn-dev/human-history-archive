# Germany

## Early Monarchy and the Saxon Emperors
The emergence of a new line of Saxon emperors began with Henry the Fowler, who was elected King of Germany in 919 by an assembly of prelates, princes, and German nobles. He was succeeded in 936 by his son, Otto I, known as Otto the Great. Otto I was elected as successor at Aix-la-Chapelle and was later crowned emperor in 962 after descending upon Rome at the invitation of John XII. 

Otto I's reign was marked by a struggle for ascendancy between the Emperor and the Pope; Otto eventually degraded John XII due to the Pope's treachery. This period is characterized as a restoration of the "ancient tussle" between these two powers to a state of dignity and decency. Otto I was followed by Otto II, who reigned from 973 to 983, and subsequently by Otto III, who reigned from 983 to 1002.
