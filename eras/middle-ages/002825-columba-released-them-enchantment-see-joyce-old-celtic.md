# St. Columba and the Children of Lir

## The Romance of the Children of Lir
According to tradition, the four children of Lir dwelt upon the waters of the Margy, a brook located by the side of the remains of Bonamargy Abbey. The abbey is situated to the east of Ballycastle, a seaport and watering-place on the north coast of County Antrim, Ireland. The children of Lir had been changed into swans by their step-mother. They remained in this state until St. Columba released them from their enchantment. This well-known romance is linked to a widespread belief throughout Ireland that killing a swan brings ill-fortune.

## Monastic Foundations and Influence
St. Columba is associated with the establishment of monastic settlements in Scotland, most notably his foundation on the island of Iona. These settlements became centers for the Celtic style of book ornamentation, which subsequently spread to other houses across Britain. 

A significant extension of this influence occurred in the year 635 when Aidan, a monk from Iona, was sent to the kingdom of Northumbria at the request of King Oswald. Aidan founded the see and abbey of Lindisfarne on the Holy Isle off the Northumbrian coast. The brethren who accompanied Aidan established the school of Lindisfarne, which produced a series of finely ornamented and written manuscripts in the Celtic style. One of the most perfect surviving examples is the *Lindisfarne Gospels* (also known as the *Durham Book* or *St. Cuthbert's Gospels*), completed in the early 8th century.

## Burial and Legacy
St. Columba's remains are laid at Downpatrick. He is buried there alongside the bodies of St. Patrick and St. Brigid. St. Brigid, a patron saint of Ireland born at Faughart in County Louth, was known for establishing the first cell in Ireland under a large oak tree at Kil-dara.
