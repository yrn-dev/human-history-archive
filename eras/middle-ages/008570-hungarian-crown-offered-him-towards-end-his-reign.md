# Wenceslas II and the Premyslide Dynasty

## Early Life and Accession
Wenceslas II became the nominal ruler of Bohemia at the age of twelve. He was the son of Ottakar, who had died when Wenceslas was only seven years old. Following Ottakar's death, Bohemia was governed for a time by Otto of Brandenburg, a nephew of Ottakar, acting as the guardian for the young sovereign. Otto's period of rule was marked by unpopularity, leading to an insurrection and a period of complete anarchy within the country. Stability was eventually restored through the intervention of Rudolph of Habsburg.

## Rise to Power
Despite becoming the nominal ruler at twelve, actual power was initially held by Zavis of Falkenstein. Zavis was a prominent Bohemian noble who had married Kunegunda, the mother of the king. Eventually, the influence of Zavis became invidious to Wenceslas, who ordered Zavis to be beheaded in 1290. At nineteen years of age, Wenceslas began to govern Bohemia himself. His period of rule is described as a time of great happiness for the country.

## Territorial Expansion and the Hungarian Crown
Under the rule of Wenceslas II, the Bohemian sphere of influence expanded. Poland accepted his rule, and the Hungarian crown was offered to him.

## Conflict and Death
Towards the end of his reign, Wenceslas became involved in a war with Albert, the archduke of Austria and future king of the Romans. Wenceslas died suddenly in 1305 while he was in the process of preparing an invasion of Austria.

## Succession and the End of the Dynasty
Wenceslas was succeeded by his son, Wenceslas III, who was sixteen years old at the time of his father's death. Wenceslas III ruled over Bohemia for only one year. During his brief reign, he planned a warlike expedition against Poland, a country over which the Bohemian sovereigns continued to maintain their claim. In 1306, Wenceslas III was murdered by unknown assassins. His death marked the end of the Premyslide dynasty's rule over Bohemia.
