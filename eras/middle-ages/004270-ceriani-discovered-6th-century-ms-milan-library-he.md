# The Apocalypse of Baruch and the Discoveries of Ceriani

## Discovery of the Apocalypse of Baruch
The Apocalypse of Baruch is a long-lost apocalypse that was recovered through the efforts of Ceriani. The text has survived exclusively in a Syriac version. Ceriani discovered a manuscript (MS.) of this version dating to the 6th century within the library at Milan.

## Publications and Translations
Following the discovery of the 6th-century manuscript, Ceriani produced several publications of the work. In 1866, he published a Latin translation in *Monumenta Sacra* (I. ii. 73-98). He later published the text itself in 1871 in *Mon. Sacra.* (V. ii. 113-180), and subsequently released a photo-lithographic facsimile of the work in 1883.

The Latin translation provided by Ceriani was reproduced in 1871 by Fritzsche in *Libri Apocryphi V. T.* (pp. 654-699). Other notable editions of the book include those by Kneucker, *Das Buch Baruch* (1879), and Gifford in the *Speaker's Apoc.* ii.

## Known Fragments and Later Translations
Certain portions of the Apocalypse of Baruch were known prior to Ceriani's discovery of the full manuscript. Specifically, chapters lxxviii through lxxxvi, which consist of Baruch's epistle to the nine and a half tribes in captivity, had been previously published. These sections appeared in Syriac and Latin in the Paris and London Polyglots. Additionally, they were published in Syriac from a single MS. in Lagarde's *Libri V. T. Apocryphi Syr.* (1861), and by Charles in 1896 from ten MSS.

The complete book was eventually translated into English by Charles and into German by Ryssel (appearing in Kautzsch's *Apok. und Pseud.*, 1900, ii. pp. 413-446).

## Other Contributions of Ceriani
Beyond the Apocalypse of Baruch, Ceriani is associated with the publication of other significant manuscripts. He published the poetical and prophetical books of the *Codex Ambrosianus* at Milan—a Syriac translation of the Septuagint column of the *Hexapla* made by Paul, bishop of Tella, in A.D. 616-617—using photolithography in *Mon. Sacr. et Prof.* Additionally, Ceriani issued a facsimile of the *Ambrosian Homer*, one of the most ancient illustrated manuscripts.
