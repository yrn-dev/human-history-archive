# The Flagellants and Events of 1349

## The Flagellants of Europe
In the years 1259 and 1349, groups known as Flagellants swarmed through the streets of European cities. These individuals appeared naked and engaged in the act of thrashing themselves until they bled, utilizing iron whips and leather thongs. 

The Flagellants were penitents who were likely influenced by an ancient belief that the remission of sins could not occur without the shedding of blood. While the specific mindset of the Flagellants is not clearly documented, their actions align with broader historical patterns of blood-letting rites.

## Comparative Rituals of Blood-Shedding
The practice of lacerating the body is found among various other groups. Worshippers among the Tatars and Semites have been known to lacerate themselves before their god. For example, in I Kings xviii. 28, the priests of Baal performed a rain-making ceremony in which they used lances and knives to gash themselves until blood gushed out. 

In the Syriac language, the word *ethkashshaph* literally means "to cut oneself" and serves as the regular equivalent for "to make supplication." Similarly, Arabs and Greeks practiced cutting themselves with knives and scratching their faces during mourning. Such practices were forbidden by Hebrew law and were also prohibited in many canons of the Eastern churches. 

According to Robertson Smith, these rites were not merely intended to evoke heavenly pity, but were meant to renew or tie a blood-bond between the faithful and God by shedding blood in a holy place or on a holy stone.
