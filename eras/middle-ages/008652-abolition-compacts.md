# Abolition of the "Compacts"

The abolition of the "Compacts" refers to a specific political and religious demand made by the estates of Bohemia during the reign of King Maximilian. This movement was rooted in the complex ecclesiastical disputes and the shifting loyalties of the Bohemian people toward various forms of church reform.

## Context of the Compacts in Bohemia
The Compacts were legal enactments that had previously granted freedom of worship to the most moderate Utraquists. These individuals held beliefs that differed very little from the Roman church, with the primary exception being their claim to the right to receive communion in both kinds. 

Despite providing freedom for moderate reformers, the Compacts were utilized by Ferdinand I as an instrument of oppression. Specifically, Ferdinand I used these enactments to justify the oppression of the advanced Utraquists and the Bohemian Brethren.

## The Reign of Maximilian
Maximilian succeeded his father, Ferdinand I, as the king of Bohemia without opposition. In his youth, Maximilian had been educated primarily by Protestant tutors and had openly expressed strong sympathy for the party of church reform. Because of this, he initially secured the support of the Bohemian church reformers, while the Romanists and retrograde Utraquists remained traditionally aligned with the house of Habsburg.

However, Maximilian's reign did not satisfy the expectations of those seeking church reform. While he generally refused to sanction measures against Protestants—despite the advice of the Jesuits, who were gaining significant influence in Bohemia—he also published new decrees against the Bohemian Brethren. Over time, Maximilian began to attend the functions of the Roman church again after a long absence. His tenure was characterized by an indifference toward ecclesiastical disputes, which may have been rooted in religious scepticism.

## The Demand for Abolition
In 1567, Maximilian visited the Bohemians for the first time since his accession to the throne. Like other princes of the Habsburg dynasty during this period, he faced the constant difficulty of raising funds to finance warfare against the Turks. 

When Maximilian requested that the Bohemians grant him supplies for this military purpose, the estates responded by presenting their own demands regarding religious matters. Their primary request was that the celebrated Compacts should cease to form part of the laws of the country. This demand for abolition was driven by the fact that the Compacts had been used as a tool for oppression against the Bohemian Brethren and advanced Utraquists.
