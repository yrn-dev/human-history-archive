# Thomas of Woodstock

## Titles and Appointments
Thomas of Woodstock, the duke of Gloucester, was created earl of Buckingham on July 15, 1377, during the coronation of Richard II. Following his death, the title of Gloucester was granted to Thomas le Despenser, while the title of earl of Buckingham was borne by his son, Humphrey.

## Political Conflicts
During the reign of Richard II, Thomas of Woodstock and other lords appellant were opposed by Thomas le Despenser. Thomas le Despenser, who was the husband of Constance and the son of Edward le Despenser, supported Richard II against Woodstock and the other lords appellant in 1397. In the course of these events, Thomas le Despenser was created earl of Gloucester.

## Death and Aftermath
Thomas of Woodstock died after the events of 1397. His death was later linked to the downfall of Thomas le Despenser; after Despenser deserted Richard II in 1399, he was degraded from his earldom for his participation in the death of Gloucester.

## Succession of the Buckingham Title
The earldom of Buckingham passed from Thomas of Woodstock to his son, Humphrey. Upon Humphrey's death, the title was held in her own right by his sister, Anne. Anne married Edmund Stafford, earl of Stafford, and the title eventually passed to her son, Humphrey Stafford, earl of Stafford, who was created duke of Buckingham in 1444. This line continued until 1521, when Edward, the 3rd duke, was executed and attained.
