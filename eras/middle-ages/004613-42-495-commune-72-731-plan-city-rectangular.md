# Rectangular Urban and Architectural Planning

The use of rectangular planning is a recurring feature in the development of cities, fortifications, and architectural structures across various historical periods and regions, ranging from Roman urbanism in Italy to colonial layouts in South America.

## Roman and Medieval Urbanism in Italy
In the city of Brescia, located in Lombardy, Italy, the urban plan is characterized by a rectangular layout where streets intersect at right angles. This specific arrangement is a peculiarity inherited from Roman times. While the area enclosed by the city's medieval walls is larger than the original Roman town, the Roman settlement occupied the eastern portion of the current city. 

Archaeological evidence in Brescia includes the Piazza del Museo, which marks the site of the former forum. On the north side of this square stands a Corinthian temple featuring three *cellae*. This structure, excavated in 1823, is attributed by some to Hercules, though it is more likely the city's Capitolium, erected by Vespasian in A.D. 73. Other remains from the forum area include a building on the south side—identified as either a *curia* or a basilica—and a theatre located to the east of the temple.

## Colonial Planning in South America
The city of Buenos Aires, the capital of Argentina, also exhibits rectangular planning in its older sections. The streets of the old city are narrow and designed to enclose rectangular blocks of uniform size, running nearly parallel to the cardinal points of the compass. This uniformity, combined with the level ground and the initial style of buildings, originally gave the city a monotonous and uninteresting appearance. However, as the city grew in population and wealth, the architecture became more diverse. This strict rectangular plan was not closely followed in the suburbs and newer additions to the city.

## Rectangular Elements in Fortifications and Architecture
Rectangular forms were frequently employed in military and civic architecture:

*   **Fortifications:** The ancient Etrurian city of Cosa, founded as a Roman colony in 273 B.C., featured strongly fortified walls approximately one mile in circuit. These walls included seventeen projecting rectangular towers. The masonry of these walls consists of polygonal blocks on the lower part and rectangular blocks on the upper part. Additionally, Norman architecture utilized the rectangular form for keeps, such as the one found in the Tower of London.
*   **Civic Structures:** The basilica was designed as a covered forum to accommodate business, legal tribunals, and merchants. In its normal form, a basilica consisted of an arrangement of colonnades in two stories surrounding a rectangular space covered by a roof. Within these structures, magistrates presiding over tribunals sometimes used platforms that were rectangular or curved in plan.

## Cartographic Applications of Rectangular Coordinates
In the field of cartography, rectangular coordinates are utilized for specific types of mapping. The projection by rectangular spheroidal co-ordinates is used for the 6-in. and 1:2500 Ordnance sheets of the United Kingdom, as well as various Ordnance maps of England. This projection is particularly suitable for cadastral plans because it allows for the convenient plotting of rectangular co-ordinates for the numerous traverse or trigonometrical points required for such constructions.
