# Sanctorum

In the context of ecclesiastical records and liturgical structures, the term *Sanctorum* appears primarily in relation to the Roman Breviary and the extensive biographical collections known as the *Acta Sanctorum*.

## Liturgical Use in the Breviary
Within the Roman Breviary, the *Proprium Sanctorum* refers to the special offices dedicated to saints. The Breviary is organized into four seasonal parts—autumn, summer, spring, and winter—and each part contains several components. These include the Psalter, the *Proprium de Tempore* (the special office of the season), the *Proprium Sanctorum*, the *Commune Sanctorum* (general offices for saints), and Extra Services.

The *Proprium Sanctorum* and the *Proprium de Tempore* present a difficulty in harmonization. This challenge is only partially addressed by the thirty-seven chapters of general rubrics. To provide further assistance, a "Catholic Churchman's Almanack" called the *Ordo Recitandi Divini Officii* is published by various dioceses and countries. This publication provides minute directions for proper reading for every day.

## The Acta Sanctorum
The *Acta Sanctorum* is a major collection of legends and biographies of saints, which are arranged according to the calendar by day. The project was originally conceived by Heribert Rosweyde, a Jesuit father, who issued a prospectus in 1607 titled *Fasti sanctorum quorum vitae in Belgicis Bibliothecis manuscriptae*. Rosweyde intended to produce eighteen volumes of saints' lives compiled from manuscripts, accompanied by sober notes. Although he collected significant material, he died in 1629 before the work began.

The project was subsequently carried on by John Bolland, a Jesuit father sent to Antwerp, who expanded the scope of the work. The publication began in 1643 with two volumes for January, followed by three volumes for February in 1658, three for March in 1668, and three for April in 1675. Other collaborators included Godfried Henschen (Henschenius), who worked with Bolland from 1635 to 1681, and Daniel van Papenbroeck (Papebroch), who collaborated from 1659 to 1714. The period of Papebroch's involvement is considered the most brilliant in the history of the *Acta Sanctorum*, though his critical approach earned him many enemies.

The work continued until the Society of Jesus was suppressed in 1773. The final volume published was volume iii of October, which appeared in 1770.

## Editions and Related Works
There are three primary editions of the *Acta Sanctorum*:
* **Original Edition:** Published in Antwerp, Tongerloo, and Brussels, consisting of 63 volumes (1643–1902).
* **Venice Edition:** This edition stopped at volume v of September (1734–1770).
* **Paris Edition:** This edition stopped at volume xiii of October, consisting of 61 volumes (1863–1883).

Additionally, the abbe Rigollot edited a volume of tables. Other related scholarly works include the *Memoire historique sur les Bollandistes* (1835) by L.P. Gachard, Cardinal J.B. Pitra's *Etudes sur la collection des Actes des Saints* (1880), and van Hecke's "De ratione operis Bollandiani."

## The Bollandists
The *Acta Sanctorum* is published by the Bollandists, who are Belgian Jesuits. Their work serves as a primary source for various hagiographical accounts; for example, the chief sources for the life of St Dominic include the account by Jordan of Saxony and evidence from the Process of Canonization, both of which are found in the Bollandists' *Acta sanctorum* under August 4.
