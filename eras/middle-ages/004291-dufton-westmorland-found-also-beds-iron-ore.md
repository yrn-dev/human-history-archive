## Mineralogy and Mining
The area around Dufton is noted for its metalliferous veins, particularly those that yield silver and lead ores. These geological formations have produced some of the largest and most perfect crystals of colourless barytes. 

## Regional Context
Dufton is located within Westmorland, a region with a diverse historical and natural profile. Historically, Westmorland was part of the territory inhabited by the Brigantes, a people of northern Britain. The name "Brigantes" is Celtic for "free, privileged" or "mountaineers." Their territory was extensive, stretching from the Belisama (the Ribble or Mersey) in the west to the mouth of the Abus (Humber) in the east, and extending north to the Wall of Antoninus. This territory encompassed Westmorland, as well as Cumberland, Durham, most of Yorkshire, the whole of Lancashire, and portions of Northumberland.

In terms of natural history, the moist mountainous districts of Westmorland are known to be a wild habitat for *Impatiens Noli-me-tangere*, a succulent annual herb also known as the touch-me-not.

The broader region of Westmorland has been home to significant legal and ecclesiastical figures, such as Richard Burn (1700-1785). Born at Winton, Westmorland, in 1709, Burn served as the vicar of Orton in Westmorland starting in 1736. He held the position of justice of the peace for both Cumberland and Westmorland and was appointed chancellor of the diocese of Carlisle in 1765. Burn is remembered as a legal writer whose works, including *Justice of the Peace and Parish Officer* (1755) and *Ecclesiastical Law* (1760), became standard authorities in their respective fields.
