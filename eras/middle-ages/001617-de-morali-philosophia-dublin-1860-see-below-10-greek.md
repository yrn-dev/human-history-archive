# De Morali Philosophia and the Greek Grammar of Roger Bacon

## De Morali Philosophia
*De Morali Philosophia* is a work attributed to Roger Bacon. It is identified as the seventh part of the *Opus Majus*. This specific text was not included in the edition produced by S. Jebb, but it is discussed at considerable length within the *Opus Tertium* (specifically in chapter xiv). Extracts from *De Morali Philosophia* have been provided by Charles on pages 339-348. A printed version of the work was published in Dublin in 1860.

## The Greek and Hebrew Grammars
In addition to his philosophical and scientific treatises, Roger Bacon authored linguistic studies. *The Greek Grammar of R. Bacon and a Fragment of his Hebrew Grammar* was published in 1902. This edition included notes and an introduction provided by S. A. Hirsch and E. S. Nolan.

## Context within Bacon's Collected Works
The aforementioned texts are part of a broader body of works by Roger Bacon. His printed output includes several significant titles:
*   **Scientific and Alchemical Works:** *Speculum Alchimiae* (1541), which saw translations in English (1597) and French (1890); *De Mirabili Potestate Artis et Naturae* (1542), translated into English in 1659; and *Libellus de Retardandis Senectutis Accidentibus* (1590), known in English as the "Cure of Old Age" (1683).
*   **The Opus Majus and Related Parts:** The *Opus Majus ad Clementem IV* was edited by J. H. Bridges (1897) and S. Jebb (1733). Other parts of this larger work include *Specula Mathematica* (the fourth part) and *Perspectiva* (the fifth part, published in 1614).
*   **Other Philosophical and Medical Tracts:** Bacon's works include *Metaphysica Fratris Rogeri* (1905) and the *Compendium Studii Philosophiae*. A collection of small tracts published in Frankfort in 1603 contains *Secretum Secretorum*, *Tractatus Trium Verborum*, *Speculum Secretorum*, *Breve Breviarium*, *Verbum Abbreviatum*, and *Excerpta de Libra Avicennae de Anima*.
*   **Additional Publications:** J. S. Brewer published *Opera hactenus Inedita* in 1859, which contained the *Opus Minus*, *Opus Tertium*, and *De Secretis Operibus Naturae*. Robert Steele also published a volume titled *Opera hactenus inedita* in 1905.
