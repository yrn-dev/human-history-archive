# Aschaffenburg

Aschaffenburg is a town in Germany located in the kingdom of Bavaria. It is situated on the right bank of the Main river at the point where it meets the Aschaff, near the foot of the Spessart and 26 miles southeast of Frankfort-on-Main. As of 1900, the population was 18,091, increasing to 25,275 by 1905.

## Early History and Development
Originally a Roman settlement, the town served as a station for the 23rd and 10th Roman legions. Following the Roman period, the Frankish mayors of the palace constructed a castle upon the ruins of the Roman *castrum*. Early religious foundations include a Benedictine monastery and a chapel to St Martin, both established by Bonifacius.

The town's infrastructure and status grew through the efforts of ecclesiastical leaders. In 989, Archbishop Willigis constructed a stone bridge over the Main. Around 1122, Adalbert further increased the importance of the town through various means.

## Architecture and Landmarks
The town features several significant buildings:
* **Johannisburg:** Built between 1605 and 1614 by Archbishop Schweikard of Cronberg, this structure houses a collection of paintings, engravings, and a library containing *incunabula*.
* **Stiftskirche (Cathedral):** Founded in 980 by Otto of Bavaria, the cathedral primarily dates from the 13th and early 12th centuries. It contains a sarcophagus holding the relics of St Margaret (1540) and various monuments created by the Vischers.
* **Other Structures:** The town includes a Capuchin hospital, several mansions belonging to the German nobility, and a theatre that previously served as the house of the Teutonic order. The old abbey buildings now house an archaeological museum.

## Political and Educational Significance
Aschaffenburg has been noted for its educational institutions since the 10th century, maintaining a lyceum, gymnasium, seminarium, and other schools. 

The town has also served as a site for significant political and religious gatherings. A synod was held there in 1292. In 1474, the town hosted an imperial diet preceding the one in Vienna; this meeting resulted in a concordat sometimes referred to as the Aschaffenburg Concordat.

## Modern History and Economy
The town experienced great suffering during the Thirty Years' War, during which it was held by various belligerents. In the mid-19th century, between 1842 and 1849, King Louis constructed a country house to the west of the town called the *Pompeianum*, designed as an imitation of the house of Castor and Pollux at Pompeii. Later, in 1866, the Prussians achieved a severe victory over the Austrians in the vicinity of the town.

The local economy is supported by the manufacture of liqueurs, lime, tobacco, cellulose, ready-made clothing, and coloured and white paper. Additionally, the town engages in a considerable export trade via the Main, shipping wine, cattle, and wood.

The town churchyard contains the graves of Christian Brentano (who died in 1851) and his brother Klemens, while Wilhelm Heinse is also buried within the town.
