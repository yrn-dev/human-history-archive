# PROVINCE OF SUKCHUR

## Geography and Access
The province of Sukchur is located within the larger general province of Tangut. Accessing Sukchur requires a journey of ten days traveling between the north-east and east from a preceding province. This intervening region is characterized by a near-total absence of human dwellings, leaving little for historical record. Upon completing this ten-day journey, one arrives in Sukchur, a region containing numerous villages and towns.

## Administration and Demographics
The chief city of the province is Sukchu. The population of the province is divided religiously, consisting partly of Christians and partly of Idolaters. Specifically, the archive notes the presence of Nestorian Christians within the province. All inhabitants of Sukchur are subject to the authority of the Great Kaan.

## Economy and Natural Products
Sukchur is recognized as a producer of rhubarb, which is cited as one of the most renowned and valuable products of the region. Additionally, the province is associated with the production of musk. In the broader context of the province of Kansu, where Sukchur is situated, the industry for both musk and rhubarb is managed by the Tanguts. 

Regarding the musk trade, traders from Ssŭ-ch’uan and Canton travel to the region to purchase the commodity, sometimes paying four times its weight in silver for high-quality musk. The purity of the musk is determined by examining its color. It is noted that Tibetans may adulterate the product by mixing it with blood and tsamba. The optimal period for purchasing musk is from the seventh to the ninth moon, which corresponds to the period from the latter part of August to the middle of November. 

Terminology for musk varies by language; Mongols refer to it as *owo* and Tibetans call it *latsé*. The highest quality is known as "white musk," termed *tsahan owo* in Mongol and *latsé karpo* in Tibetan, though it is unclear if "white" refers to the musk itself or the hair on the skin of the musk pouch.
