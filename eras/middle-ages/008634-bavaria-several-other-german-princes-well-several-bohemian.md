# Political and Military Interactions Between Bavaria, German Princes, and Bohemia

The history of Bohemia is marked by frequent political and military intersections with the German states, specifically the Duchy of Bavaria and various German princes. These interactions ranged from dynastic competitions for the Bohemian throne to large-scale military crusades and imperial interventions.

## Dynastic Competition and the Bohemian Throne
Following the death of Louis, Bohemia entered a period of disorder and near-anarchy. Because the previous two kings had resided primarily in Hungary, the country lacked a strong indigenous government. This vacuum led to several candidates vying for the throne, including the dukes Louis and William of Bavaria, as well as several other German princes. Archduke Ferdinand of Austria also laid claim to the throne as the husband of Anna, daughter of King Vladislav. To resolve the succession, the diet entrusted the election to twenty-four members chosen from the three estates. On October 23, 1526, Ferdinand of Habsburg was elected king, establishing the permanent accession of the Habsburg dynasty to the Bohemian throne.

## Crusades and Military Conflicts
In the 15th century, Bohemia faced several invasions by German forces. In 1422, Sigismund invaded Bohemia but was defeated by Zizka at Nemecky Brod. Later, in 1426, the Bohemians secured a decisive victory over the Germans at Aussig. In 1427, several German princes launched a new crusade against the Hussites. This force included 1000 English archers serving as the bodyguard to Henry Beaufort, the bishop of Winchester and papal legate. However, the crusaders were seized by panic at Tachau and Mies, fleeing across the mountains into Bavaria. Following these successes, the Bohemians transitioned to the offensive, repeatedly invading the German states and Hungary.

## Imperial Interventions and the House of Babenberg
Earlier historical records indicate that the emperor Frederick II, in 1236, invaded Austria after placing Duke Frederick "the Quarrelsome" under the ban. This invasion was supported by the duke of Bavaria, the king of Bohemia, and other princes. The emperor declared the duchies of Austria and Styria vacant and made Vienna an imperial city, though these changes were largely ignored after the duke returned to power in 1239.

## The Battle of White Hill and the Bavarian Alliance
In the early 17th century, the Bohemian crown became vacant upon the death of Matthias. The Bohemian estates deposed Ferdinand and elected Frederick, elector palatine, as king. In response, Ferdinand—now the emperor—sought to reconquer Bohemia. He was joined by the powerful duke Maximilian of Bavaria, as well as aid from Poland, Spain, several Italian states, and the Lutheran elector of Saxony. 

A large imperialist army, commanded by the duke of Bavaria, Bouquoi, and Tilly, entered Bohemia in September 1620. On November 8, the imperial forces attacked the Bohemian army at a position known as "Bila Hora" (White Hill). The Bohemians were defeated within a few hours, and the imperialists occupied the port of Prague. Consequently, King Frederick fled the city the following morning.

## Other Regional Conflicts
In 1743, during a campaign involving French and Bavarian armies, the forces of the Empire converged on southern Bavaria. Prince Lobkowitz marched from Bohemia toward the Naab, while Prince Charles moved along the Danube and Khevenhueller advanced from Salzburg. The Bavarians suffered a severe reverse near Braunau on May 9, 1743, and their strongholds surrendered one by one to Prince Charles.

## Cultural and Linguistic Tensions
Beyond military conflict, the relationship between Bohemia and the German states was characterized by linguistic and racial tension. Early attempts to introduce Christianity to Bohemia came from Germany but were met with little success due to an innate distrust of the Germans. This antipathy is also evident in the rhymed chronicle known as the chronicle of Dalimil, written during the reign of King John (1310-1346).

In the 19th century, the Habsburg empire attempted to exterminate the national Bohemian language, exclusively using German in government offices and schools. Later, in 1897, Count Badeni attempted to remedy this by decreeing that government officials in Bohemia must prove knowledge of both Bohemian and German. This decree caused violent opposition among German inhabitants of Austria and led to the fall of Badeni's cabinet.
