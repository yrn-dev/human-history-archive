# Buhtur

## Life and Early Career
Buhtur, born as al-Walid ibn 'Ubaid Allah (820-897), was an Arabian poet. He was born in Syria at Manbij (Hierapolis), located between the Euphrates and Aleppo. He belonged to the tribe of Tai, the same tribe as the poet Abu Tammām. During his youth, Buhtur visited Abu Tammām at Horns; following a commendation from Abu Tammām to the authorities at Ma'arrat un-Nu'man, Buhtur was granted a yearly pension of 4000 dirhems, which was approximately £90.

## Poetry and Themes
Buhtur later moved to Bagdad, where he composed verses praising the members of the court and the caliph Motawakkil. Despite spending a long period as a resident of Bagdad, he dedicated a significant portion of his poetry to the praise of Aleppo. His love-poetry is largely dedicated to a maiden of Aleppo named Alwa.

In addition to his own original works, Buhtur compiled a collection of early poems known as the Hamasa, a practice he shared with Abu Tammām.

## Legacy and Publication
Buhtur died in 897 at Manbij Hierapolis. In the 10th century, his poetry was collected and edited in two different versions: one edition was arranged by subjects, while the other was organized alphabetically based on the last consonant in each line. His work was published in Constantinople in A.D. 1883.

Biographical information regarding Buhtur can be found in the *Book of Songs* (vol. xviii. pp. 167-175) and in McG. de Slane's translation of Ibn Khallikān's *Biographical Dictionary* (vol. iii. pp. 657 ff.).
