# Fontes Rerum Bohemicarum

## Overview
The *Fontes Rerum Bohemicarum* is a valuable collection of historical documents pertaining to the history of Bohemia. Published in Prague during the latter part of the 19th century, this collection serves as a primary resource for the study of the region's past.

## Academic Standing and Influence
Within the field of Bohemian historical research, the *Fontes Rerum Bohemicarum* is regarded as a superior resource that has superseded earlier collections of documents, specifically the *Rerum Bohemicarum Antiqui Scriptores* authored by Marquard Freher (also referred to as Freherus). 

The collection is utilized by historians and researchers for specific textual evidence. For example, the text found in volume iii, pages 492-604, published in 1882, is cited as the best text for certain historical inquiries.

## Context within Bohemian Historiography
The publication of the *Fontes Rerum Bohemicarum* occurred alongside a broader evolution of Bohemian historical scholarship in the 19th century. During this period, several earlier historical works by authors such as De Florgy, Pelzl, and Pubitschka were superseded by Frantisek Palacky's *Geschichte von Bohmen*, published in Prague between 1844 and 1867. While Palacky's work concludes with the year 1526, subsequent history posterior to that date has been addressed by other scholars, including Gindely and Rezek. 

Additionally, Professor Adolf Bachmann published a *Geschichte Bohmens* in two volumes (1899 and 1905) covering the period up to 1526, though this work is noted for having a strongly marked German tendency. Other continuous accounts of Bohemian history from the start of the 15th century were provided by Professor Ernest Denis in works such as *Jean Hus, et la guerre des Hussites* (1878), *Fin de l'independance boheme* (1890), and *La Boheme depuis la Montagne Blanche* (1903).
