# The Corn Stores of the Great Kaan

The Great Kaan implemented a comprehensive system of agricultural reserves and food distribution across his empire to protect his subjects from the effects of dearth and scarcity. This system relied on the strategic accumulation of grain during periods of abundance to ensure stability during times of crisis.

## Accumulation and Storage of Grain
The Emperor monitors the availability and cost of corn throughout his lands. When he observes that corn is abundant and prices are cheap, he purchases large quantities of the crop. These supplies are stored in great granaries located across all his provinces. 

The storage process is managed with such care that the corn is preserved and remains viable for three or four years. This system of providence is applied across the entire empire, with supplies stored in various locations based on a calculation of the necessities and wants of the local populations.

## Types of Corn and Distribution
The reserves are not limited to a single crop but include all varieties of corn, specifically wheat, barley, millet, rice, and panic. In the event of a scarcity affecting a particular type of grain, the Emperor causes that specific kind to be issued from the stores.

To ensure general cheapness and accessibility, the Emperor regulates the price of the issued grain. For example, if the market price of corn is one bezant per measure, the Emperor may provide it at a rate of one bezant for four measures, or at any other price necessary to maintain affordability for all people. Through these measures, the people of the empire are prevented from suffering from dearth.

## Relief During Calamities
The Great Kaan employs a network of messengers who travel throughout his kingdoms, lands, and provinces. These messengers are tasked with reporting to the Emperor whether the people are afflicted by dearth resulting from storms, unfavourable seasons, locusts, or similar calamities.

When such suffering is confirmed, the Emperor provides the following relief:
* **Tax Remission:** No taxes are exacted for that year from those who have suffered.
* **Direct Aid:** The Emperor supplies the afflicted people with his own corn to be used for both food and seed.

## Charity in Cambaluc
In the city of Cambaluc, the Emperor maintains a specific system of charity for the indigent. He identifies a large number of impoverished families, which vary in size—some consisting of six, eight, or ten members. Each of these selected families is supplied annually with wheat and other corn sufficient to last the entire year.

Additionally, the Emperor provides a daily dole at the Court. Every person who chooses to attend receives a hot, freshly baked loaf of bread. This daily distribution is open to all and is attended by approximately 30,000 people every day of the year.
