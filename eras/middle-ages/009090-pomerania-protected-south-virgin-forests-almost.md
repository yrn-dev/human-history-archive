# Pomerania

Pomerania is a maritime province characterized by its strategic access to the sea. In the early 12th century, the region was protected to the south by almost impenetrable morasses and virgin forests.

## Early Inhabitants and Conflict with Poland
During the reign of Boleslaus III, King of Poland (1086–1139), Pomerania was inhabited by a savage and valiant Slavonic race related to the Wends. This population maintained a stubborn adherence to paganism. Due to their possession of a seaboard, the Pomeranians were able to build relatively large towns, such as Kolberg and Stettin, and maintain fleets. These resources allowed them to ravage the territories of their southern neighbors, the Poles, at will.

In an effort of self-defence, Boleslaus III sought to subdue the province to gain sea access. This struggle began in 1109, when Boleslaus secured a temporary submission from the Pomeranians following a terrible defeat at Nackel. Resistance continued, notably during 1120–1124, when the vassal Prince Warceslaus of Stettin rebelled. The conflict was characterized by stout resistance; Boleslaus eventually secured the submission of Warceslaus, but only after 8,000 of his followers had been expatriated and 18,000 had fallen. The intensity of this resistance led Boleslaus to conclude that the region required christianization.

## The Duchy of Pomerania and Brandenburg
By the 16th century, Pomerania was organized as a duchy. Barnim XI (1501–1573), the son of Bogislaus X, became duke in 1523. He initially ruled jointly with his elder brother, George, and after George's death in 1531, he shared the duchy with his nephew, Philip I, while Barnim himself retained the duchy of Pomerania-Stettin.

The political stability of the region was threatened by the margrave of Brandenburg, who sought to annex Pomerania. This quarrel was resolved in 1529 through a treaty that freed Pomerania from Brandenburg's supremacy. However, this independence was conditional: should the ducal family become extinct, the duchy was to revert to Brandenburg.

During his rule, Barnim XI adopted the doctrines of Martin Luther and joined the league of Schmalkalden. Because this position left him without supporters, he was forced to submit to Emperor Charles V, pay a heavy fine, and accept the *Interim* issued from Augsburg in May 1548. Barnim XI handed his duchy to his grand-nephew, John Frederick, in 1569 and died in Stettin on June 2, 1573.

## The City of Greifswald
The town of Greifswald was founded around 1240 by traders from the Netherlands. In 1250, Duke Wratislaw of Pomerania granted the town a constitution and Lubeck rights. By 1270, Greifswald had joined other Hanse towns—including Lubeck, Wismar, Rostock, and Stralsund—and participated in their wars against the kings of Norway and Denmark.

The city's control shifted frequently during later conflicts:
* **Thirty Years' War:** Imperialists turned the city into a fortress but vacated it to the Swedes in 1631. It remained Swedish after the peace of Westphalia.
* **17th and 18th Centuries:** The elector of Brandenburg captured the city in 1678, though it was restored to Sweden the following year. In 1713, the Russians desolated the city. Denmark took possession in 1715.
* **Final Transition:** Sweden regained protection of the city in 1721. This arrangement lasted until 1815, when Greifswald and the entirety of Swedish Pomerania were acquired by Prussia.
