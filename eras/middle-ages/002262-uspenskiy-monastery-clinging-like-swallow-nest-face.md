# Uspenskiy Monastery

## Location and Setting
The Uspenskiy monastery is situated in the Crimea, located between the town of Bakhchi-sarai and Chufut-Kaleh. The monastery is noted for its precarious positioning, as it clings to the face of the cliffs in a manner described as being like a swallow's nest.

## Regional Context
The monastery is positioned near Bakhchi-sarai, a town located in a narrow gorge in the government of Taurida, approximately 20 miles by rail south-southwest of Simferopol. Bakhchi-sarai, whose name in Turkish means "garden-palace," served as the residence of the Tatar khans of the Crimea from the close of the 15th century until 1783. The town is characterized by an oriental appearance and contains no fewer than thirty-six mosques. Its primary structure is the palace, or *Khan-sarai*, which was originally built in 1519 by Abdul-Sahal-Ghirai. Although destroyed in 1736, the palace was later restored under the command of Potemkin to receive Catherine II. A mausoleum attached to the palace houses the tombs of many khans.

To the east of Bakhchi-sarai, by a distance of two and a half miles, lies Chufut-Kaleh (also known as "Jews' city"). This site is situated on cliffs that are lofty and almost inaccessible. Formerly the primary seat of the Karaite Jews of the Crimea, Chufut-Kaleh is currently deserted, with the exception of the rabbi.

## Religious Significance
The Uspenskiy monastery serves as the site of a great pilgrimage, which occurs annually on the 15th (29th) of August.
