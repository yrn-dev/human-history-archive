# Baldwin II and the Recovery of the Latin Empire

## Accession and Regency
Baldwin II (1217-1273) served as the emperor of Romania. He was the younger son of Yolande, who was the sister of Baldwin I. His father, Peter of Courtenay, had been the third emperor of Romania, followed by his son Robert. Upon Robert's death in 1228, the imperial succession passed to Baldwin, who was eleven years old at the time.

Because of his youth, the barons appointed John of Brienne, the titular king of Jerusalem, to serve as emperor-regent for life. Under the terms of the arrangement, Baldwin was designated to rule the empire's Asiatic possessions once he reached twenty years of age. The agreement further stipulated that Baldwin would marry Mary, the daughter of John of Brienne, and would assume full imperial sovereignty upon John's death. This marriage contract was fulfilled in 1234.

## Decline of the Latin Empire
The Latin empire faced a period of decline following the death of Emperor Henry in 1216, while Greek power simultaneously advanced. The expectations that John of Brienne could restore the empire proved to be disappointed. By the time Baldwin governed, the realm had shrunk significantly, consisting of little more than Constantinople.

Baldwin's tenure was characterized by a desperate financial situation. Much of his life was spent in a state of mendicancy, begging at various European courts for support.

## Diplomatic Missions to France and Flanders
In 1236, Baldwin traveled to the West to seek the resources necessary to recover the lost territory of his realm. During this journey, he visited Rome, Flanders, and France, attempting to raise both money and men. These efforts were successful, and by 1240, he returned to Constantinople via Hungary and Germany, leading a considerable army. However, circumstances prevented him from achieving any significant results with this force.

Baldwin returned to the West again in 1245, visiting Italy first and then spending two years in France. During this absence, the empire was governed by Philip of Toucy and the empress Maria. While in France, Baldwin obtained money from King Louis IX in exchange for relics. In 1249, Baldwin accompanied King Louis at Damietta.

## Financial Distress and Final Years
The extremity of Baldwin's financial straits eventually led to a desperate act: he handed over his only son, Philip, to merchants as a pledge to secure loans of money. Philip was later redeemed from this hostage situation by Louis IX. Baldwin spent the remainder of his "inglorious reign" conducting mendicant tours across western Europe.

The rule of Baldwin came to an end in 1261 when Michael Palaeologus captured Constantinople. Baldwin escaped the city in a Venetian galley, traveling first to Negropont, then to Athens, then to Apulia, and finally arriving in France. Even after the loss of the city, he continued his role as titular emperor, which remained the same as it had been during his reign: begging for help from the western powers.
