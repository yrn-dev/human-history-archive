# The Deposition of Sigismund and the Bohemian Crisis (1421)

## The Diet of Caslav and the Deposition
On June 1, 1421, a meeting of the diet was held at Caslav. During this assembly, it was decided that Sigismund was to be deposed, resulting in the cessation of royal authority in Bohemia. The diet determined that a Polish prince should be selected as the new sovereign. To manage the state in the interim, a provisional government was established, consisting of twenty men drawn from various parties.

## Conflict and the Regency of Sigismund Korybutovic
Following the deposition, Sigismund invaded Bohemia again in 1422. However, he suffered a decisive defeat at Nemecky Brod (Deutschbrod) at the hands of Zizka. Subsequently, a Polish prince named Sigismund Korybutovic arrived in Bohemia. He was recognized as regent by a large majority of the population, though his tenure was brief; after only a few months, he was recalled by the rulers of Poland due to the influence of the papal see.

## Internal Strife and the Hussite Parties
The departure of Prince Korybutovic led to the first outbreak of civil war between two factions of Hussites: the advanced Taborite party and the moderate Hussites, who were also known as Utraquists or Calixtines. While isolated disturbances had occurred between these groups previously, this period marked the start of open conflict.

## Reunification and the German Crusades
The Bohemians were reunited by the threat of a German invasion and the return of Prince Korybutovic. In 1426, they achieved a decisive victory over the Germans at Aussig. Prince Korybutovic participated in this victory, but he eventually incurred the dislike of the extreme Hussites and was forced to leave Bohemia. This event ended all hopes of establishing an independent Slav dynasty in the region.

In 1427, several German princes launched a new crusade against the Hussites. This force included 1000 English archers who served as the bodyguard to Henry Beaufort, the bishop of Winchester and papal legate. The crusaders experienced sudden panic at Tachau and Mies (Stribro) upon approaching the Hussites, leading to a hurried flight across the mountains into Bavaria. Following this success, the Bohemians shifted to the offensive, repeatedly invading the German states and Hungary.

## Background of the Conflict
The instability in Bohemia followed a period of dynastic transitions. The Luxemburg dynasty had ruled Bohemia until the death of Sigismund in 1437. Prior to this, the Bohemians had asserted their right to elect their own sovereign, choosing John of Luxemburg after the unpopular reign of Henry, duke of Carinthia. 

The specific conflict involving Sigismund's claim to the crown began after the death of his brother. Sigismund arrived at Kutna Hora (Kuttenberg) to claim the Bohemian crown, while Pope Martin V proclaimed a crusade against Bohemia on March 1, 1420. Sigismund's forces, comprising men from various countries and Bohemians, occupied the castle of Prague on June 30, viewing the city as a heretical community because its inhabitants accepted other evangelical truths and used the sacred chalice. Sigismund's attempts to conquer the city failed after the Taborites, led by Zizka, repulsed an attack on the Vitkov (Zizkov) hill. He was further defeated on November 1, 1420, at the battle of the Vysehrad while attempting to relieve a besieged garrison.

## Related Events in Bohemia
During the broader period of unrest, other Bohemian towns experienced similar turmoil. In 1421, the town of Kolin was captured by the men of Prague. German inhabitants of the town who refused to accept "the four articles" were expelled. Later, in 1427, Kolin declared against Prague and was besieged by Prokop the Great, eventually surrendering to him at the end of that year.
