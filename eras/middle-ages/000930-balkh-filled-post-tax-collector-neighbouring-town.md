# Balkh

Balkh is a city located in Afghanistan, situated approximately 46 miles south of the Oxus and about 100 miles east of Andkhui. Positioned on the right bank of the Balkh river at an elevation of 1,200 feet above the sea, the city is identical to the ancient sites of Zainaspa or Bactra.

## Historical Significance and Geography
Historically known as the "mother of cities," Balkh served as a great trade center of Asia. In antiquity, the plains of Balkh were green with cultivation. However, researchers have noted the gradual formation of a longitudinal ridge or anticlinal through the great Balkh plain of Afghan Turkestan. This geological upheaval effectively shuts off the northern affluents of the Oxus basin from joining the river, which may explain the climatic and physical changes the region has undergone since its peak.

## Mongol Conquest and Decline
The city suffered mercilessly under Chinghiz. Despite yielding without resistance, the population was marched into the plain under the pretext of being counted and was subsequently massacred. The Mongols leveled all buildings capable of defense and fired the city and its gardens. The province continued to be harried by Chaghataian inroads for a long period. 

Sixty years after the visit of Marco Polo, the traveler Ibn Batuta described the city as uninhabited and in ruins, noting that the painted walls traced with azure and the remains of colleges and mosques were still visible. Another account by Clavijo describes the city as very large and surrounded by a broad earthen wall thirty paces across, though breached in many parts, with cotton sown in large portions of the interior area.

## Architectural Remains
The modern site of Balkh is largely a mass of ruins and debris. The outer walls, which are in utter disrepair, have a perimeter of approximately 6-1/2 to 7 miles; the south-eastern borders feature a rampart or mound indicating Mongol origin. To the north-east, a moated and walled fort and citadel sit upon a barren mound, though only a few pillars remain.

Notable structures include:
* **Masjid Sabz:** A mosque with a green-tiled dome, said to be the tomb of Abul Narsi Parsar, a Khwaja.
* **The Madrasa:** Only the arched entrance of this traditionally recent building remains.
* **Top-i-Rustam:** A circular structure 50 feet high, 50 yards in diameter at the base and 30 yards at the top. It contains four circular vaults in the interior and four external passages leading to them, constructed from sun-dried bricks.
* **Takht-i-Rustam:** A wedge-shaped structure built of pise mud (mud mixed with straw and puddled).

The archive notes that earlier Buddhist constructions have proven more durable than the Mahommedan buildings. The ruins may include the Nan Vihara mentioned by the Chinese traveler Hsuan Tsang, and several other stupas (topes) are found in the neighborhood.

## Modern Status and Administration
Currently, the city consists of a small bazaar, a colony of Jews, and about 500 houses of Afghan settlers. The west (Akcha) gate leads under three arches that are likely remnants of a former Jama Masjid. The town is garrisoned by a few hundred kasidars. 

Since 1858, the bulk of the population has moved to Takhtapul, located 8 miles east of Balkh, where the Afghan Government is situated. The regular troops of Afghan Turkestan are also cantoned at Takhtapul, near Mazar-i-Sharif.

## Notable Figures
Balkh is the ancestral home of the Persian father of the Arabian philosopher Avicenna (980-1037). Avicenna's father served as a tax-collector in the neighboring town of Harmaitin under Nuh II ibn Mansur, the Samanid amir of Bokhara.
