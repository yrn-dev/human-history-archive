# Bonda and the Evolution of "Husband"

The term *bonda* is an Old English word that serves as the linguistic root for the modern English word "husband." Its etymological history reveals a transition from a term describing a householder to one associated with servitude, primarily due to phonetic and conceptual confusion with other similar terms.

## Etymology and Original Meaning
The word *bonda* is derived from the root of the verb *bua*, which means to dwell or to have a house. Because of this origin, it is cognate with the English word "boor" and the German word *Bauer*. In its earliest usage, *bonda* referred to a householder.

## Legal Status and Social Transition
In the laws of Canute, the term was used as an equivalent to the Old English *ceorl* (churl). However, the social standing of the *bonda* shifted following the Norman Conquest. As the position of the churl became less free, the meaning of "bond" began to approximate that of a "villein," and eventually, it came to be associated with a "serf."

## Confusion with "Bond" and "Bondage"
The shift in meaning from a householder to a servant or slave was the result of an early linguistic confusion between *bonda* and the word "bond," meaning "that which binds." The latter is a phonetic variant of "band" and is derived from the Teutonic root *bindan* (to bind).

This "wrong connexion" also influenced the development of the word "bondage." Originally, "bondage" properly referred to "tenure in villeinage," but due to the confusion with the concept of binding, it became synonymous with slavery. A remnant of the original meaning of the term survives in the word "bondager."

## The Bondager System
A "bondager" is generally defined as a servant. Specifically, in Northumberland and the south of Scotland, the term was used for a female outworker. Under this system, a married farm-labourer who lived in a cottage attached to a farm was required, as a condition of his tenancy, to supply this female worker for field-labour. In some instances, the labourer was also responsible for providing her with board and lodging. This specific social arrangement originated from a dearth of field-labour.
