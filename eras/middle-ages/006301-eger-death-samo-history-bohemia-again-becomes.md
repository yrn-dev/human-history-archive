# Eger

Eger is a town located in Bohemia. In the context of the region's administrative and demographic records, it is listed as one of the important towns of the area, with a population of 23,665.

## Historical Significance and Military Events
The site near the present town of Eger is associated with a major military engagement in the 7th century. In 630, the ruler Samo led his forces to a great victory against the Franks in a battle that took place at Wogatisburg, which is believed to be located near Eger.

In later centuries, Eger served as a site for diplomatic agreements. In 1459, the treaty of Eger was used to formally cede the fortress of Königstein from Bohemia to Saxony.

## Geography and Regional Context
Geographically, Eger is situated within the watershed of the Elbe. The Eger river, along with its affluent the Tepl, is one of the rivers that flows into the Elbe. In the 18th century, the Eger valley was a point of strategic movement for military forces; specifically, Marshal Maillebois manoeuvred toward the valley in an attempt to establish contact with Broglie during the French operations in Bohemia.

## Political and Social Activity
Eger has also been a center for political agitation. During the period of the language ordinances of 1897—which sought to introduce the Czech language into government offices throughout the kingdom—the town became a site for great meetings. These gatherings were attended by Germans from across the frontier and resulted in serious disturbances.
