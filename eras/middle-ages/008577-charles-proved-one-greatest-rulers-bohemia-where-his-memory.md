# Charles IV of Bohemia

Charles IV was a prominent ruler of Bohemia and the son of King John. He was elected as the sovereign of the German electors at Rense in 1346, prior to succeeding his father as the king of Bohemia. He is regarded as one of the greatest rulers of the country, and his memory remains revered among his countrymen, alongside that of George of Podebrad.

## Domestic Achievements and Urban Development
Charles's reign was marked by significant efforts to stabilize and expand the Bohemian state. He succeeded in re-establishing order in a country that had been in a highly disturbed state due to incessant feuds during the reign of his father, John, who had been frequently absent from Bohemia. 

Prague served as Charles's favorite residence. He greatly enlarged the city through the foundation of the *nove mesto* (new town), an initiative that trebled the city's population and increased its physical extent three times over. Furthermore, he enhanced the city's importance by founding the famous university of Prague.

## Legal and Religious Affairs
Charles attempted to codify the laws of Bohemia, which were contradictory and obscure at the time. However, this effort failed because of resistance from the country's powerful nobility.

During his reign, the first symptoms of a church reform movement appeared in Bohemia, which would later gain world-wide importance. Although Charles has faced accusations of being unduly subservient to the Church of Rome, he provided protection to several priests who supported the cause of church reform.

## Foreign Policy and Conflict
Charles's approach to foreign policy differed from that of his father. While relations with France grew colder, Charles favored an alliance with England toward the end of his reign.

His early career involved conflict with Emperor Louis IV. Louis had seized Tirol after declaring the marriage of Margaret Maultasch and John Henry (son of King John of Bohemia) void. Because John of Bohemia and the Luxemburg family were prominent enemies of the emperor, John entered into an alliance with Pope Clement VI. In July 1346, the emperor stopped short of accepting the election of Charles, then the margrave of Moravia, as German king. Consequently, Charles attacked Tirol.

## Death and Legacy
Charles died in 1378 at the age of sixty-two. His death is attributed to being prematurely exhausted by arduous work. He is remembered as one of the rulers whose memory has most endeared itself to the people of Bohemia.
