# Kilchurn Castle

## Location and Setting
Kilchurn Castle is located at the extreme north-eastern end of Loch Awe, which is the longest freshwater lake in Scotland. Situated in mid-Argyllshire, the castle stands on an islet that becomes part of the mainland during periods when the water is low. 

The castle is positioned within a landscape of contrasting characters. The upper extremity of Loch Awe, where the castle is located, is described as majestic. In contrast, the lower half of the lake is characterized as tame and pastoral. Loch Awe itself sits 116 feet above the sea, covers an area of nearly 16 square miles, and extends 23 miles from Ford to Kilchurn Castle. The lake follows a south-west to north-east direction, with its breadth ranging from one-third of a mile to 3 miles at its upper end. At this upper end, the loch forms a crescent shape, with one arm extending toward Glen Orchy and the other toward the point where the river Awe exits the lake.

## Characteristics and Influence
The ruins of Kilchurn Castle are described as imposing. Due to the romantic nature of its surroundings, the castle has become a favorite subject for landscape painters.

## Regional Geography
The area surrounding the castle includes several notable landmarks. The river Awe, which issues from the north-western horn of the loch, is known for providing excellent salmon and trout fishing. Approximately 2 miles from the loch is the village of Dalmally, which is noted as one of the pleasantest villages in the Highlands and experiences great vogue during the midsummer season. Additionally, the loch contains numerous islands, the most well-known being Inishail, which holds the ruins of a convent and church that was suppressed during the Reformation.
