# Alexander of Hales and John of Rochelle

## Academic Succession at Paris
In the mid-13th century, the academic landscape of Paris included the theologian Alexander of Hales. He was a figure of significance in the educational sphere, serving as a teacher to students such as the Franciscan theologian Saint Bonaventura (also known as John of Fidanza). 

Following the tenure of Alexander of Hales, the position was taken over by his successor, John of Rochelle. Saint Bonaventura studied under John of Rochelle, and eventually, in 1253, Bonaventura succeeded John of Rochelle in his chair.

## Saint Bonaventura
Saint Bonaventura was born in 1221 at Bagnarea in Tuscany. His mother destined him for the church, and it is recorded that he received the name Bonaventura from St Francis of Assisi, who provided him with a miraculous cure. Bonaventura entered the Franciscan order in 1243.

His academic and professional ascent was rapid:
* **1250:** He was granted permission to read upon the *Sentences*.
* **1253:** He succeeded John of Rochelle in his chair.
* **1255:** He received the degree of doctor.
* **1256:** Due to his high reputation, he was elected general of his order.

As general, Bonaventura exercised authority over the order's members, including Roger Bacon, whom he interdicted from lecturing at Oxford and required to remain under the order's surveillance in Paris.

## Later Life and Ecclesiastical Career
Saint Bonaventura played a role in the election of Gregory X. In recognition of this, Gregory X granted him the titles of bishop of Albano and cardinal. He was also insisted upon to attend the great council of Lyons in 1274, the location where he died.
