# Turks

## Early History and Expansion
The presence of Turks in the region of Macedonia began in the 9th century through Byzantine efforts to confirm their positions via Asiatic colonization. These early Turkish immigrants became known as Vardariotes and were settled near Salonica. In Western Turkestan, Turks were also responsible for the breaking up of the Ephthalites around 565.

Later, the Ottoman Turks emerged as a dominant force among the various small principalities that had formed following the collapse of the Seljuk empire, also known as the empire of "Roum." For several centuries, the Ottomans maintained a tolerantly hostile relationship with the dwindling empire of Constantinople. Rather than attacking the Bosphorus, they established a foothold in Europe at the Dardanelles. Using this route, they steadily expanded their territory into Bulgaria, Yugo-Slavia, Illyria, Epirus, and Macedonia.

## Governance and Society
The Ottoman Turks possessed a strong sense of unity and spoke a single language. They were characterized as superior soldiers who practiced Moslem habits of frugality and temperance. In the Balkan regions, the Turks encountered Serbs (Yugo-Slavs) and Bulgarians, whom they found to be culturally similar and likely similar in racial admixture, though the Balkan peoples possessed a slightly higher Nordic element and less of the Mongolian and dark Mediterranean strains found in the Turks.

Upon conquering these territories, the Turks converted some of the local populations to Islam. The Christians who remained were disarmed and given the monopoly of tax-paying. Through these methods, the Ottoman princes consolidated an empire extending from Roumania and Hungary in the west to the Taurus mountains in the east, establishing Adrianople as their chief city and surrounding the empire of Constantinople.

## Dominion over Epirus and Albania
The Turks eventually gained control of Epirus. In the beginning of the 15th century, the title of Despot of Epirus was held by Charles II Tocco, lord of Zante and Cephalonia, under the recognition of emperor Manuel Comnenus. However, in 1431, Murad (Amurath) II deprived the Tocco family of their possessions. Although Scanderbeg, the king of Albania, seized a considerable part of Epirus in 1443, the region fell to the Venetians after his death. Subsequently, the territory passed from the Venetians to the Turks, under whose dominion it remained.

In the region of Upper Albania, the territory is so narrow and impracticable that most resident tribes were considered free and paid no tribute to the Turkish government. The Pachas found it too expensive and difficult to extort taxes from these tribes, as the cost of collection exceeded the potential gain. This situation was also true for Lower Albania (Epirus) prior to the time of Ali Pacha, though no such exemptions were allowed in Middle Albania.
