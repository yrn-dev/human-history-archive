# Bohemund V and the Connection Between Antioch and Cyprus

## Succession and Governance
Bohemund V succeeded his father, Bohemund IV, in 1233. Like his father, he held the titles of prince of Antioch and count of Tripoli. During his reign, Bohemund V resided primarily in Tripoli. Consequently, the governance of Antioch was left to its commune and its bailiff.

## Political Alliances and Conflicts
The reign of Bohemund V was characterized by specific external relations. He maintained an alliance with the Templars, mirroring the political leanings of his father. Conversely, he faced hostility from Armenia. This conflict persisted until 1251, the year of Bohemund V's death. Peace was eventually achieved through the mediation of St Louis and the marriage of the future Bohemund VI to the sister of the Armenian king.

## The Connection to Cyprus
Bohemund V established a political and familial link between the history of Antioch and that of Cyprus through his first marriage in 1225. He married Alice, who was the widow of Hugh I. of Cyprus. This union connected the two regions for a period of time.

This Cypriot connection was later maintained under the reign of Bohemund V's son, Bohemund VI. In 1250, Plaisance, the sister of Bohemund VI, married Henry I. of Cyprus, who was the son of Hugh I. This marriage ensured the continuation of the bond originally formed by the marriage of Bohemund V and Alice.

## Lineage and Succession
Bohemund V was the son of Bohemund IV and his wife Plaisance, the daughter of Hugh of Gibelet. In addition to his first marriage to Alice, Bohemund V married Luciana, the daughter of the count of Segni and nephew of Innocent III. 

Bohemund V was succeeded in 1251 by his son, Bohemund VI, who was born in 1237 to Luciana. Bohemund VI continued the family's involvement in regional politics, establishing himself in Antioch in 1252 and later procuring the recognition of his nephew, Hugh II (the son of Henry I. and Plaisance), as king of Jerusalem in 1257.
