# How the Two Brothers Took the Envoys’ Counsel and Went to the Court of the Great Kaan

The journey of the two brothers, Nicolo and Maffeo Polo, Venetian merchants who resided in Constantinople, led them through the Crimea and Kazan to the city of Bocara. This city, described as the best in all Persia, was the territory of a king named Barac (also called Borrak Khan). According to archival notes, Borrak Khan was the great-grandson of Chagatai and the sovereign of the Ulús of Chagatai from 1264 to 1270.

## Arrival at Bocara and the Envoys' Counsel
Upon arriving at Bocara after crossing a desert, the two brothers found themselves in a position where they could neither proceed further forward nor turn back. Consequently, they remained in the city for three years. During this period, they encountered a party of envoys traveling to the Court of the Great Kaan, the Lord of all the Tartars in the world. These envoys had come from Alau, the Lord of the Levant (identified in the archive as Hulagu).

The envoys were amazed to see the brothers, as they had never before encountered Latins in that region of the world. The envoys offered their counsel to the brothers, suggesting that following their advice would bring "great honour and profit." They explained that the Great Kaan had a great desire to see Latins, as he had never seen any before. The envoys promised that if the brothers accompanied them to the Court, the Kaan would treat them with liberality and great honour. Furthermore, they assured the brothers that traveling in their company would provide perfect security and protection from being molested.

## Journey to the Court of the Great Kaan
After making the necessary arrangements, the two brothers set out with the envoys. Their journey lasted a full year, during which they traveled in a northward and north-eastward direction. While the brothers witnessed many marvels of various kinds during this trek, the specific details of these sights were left to be recounted by Messer Mark, the son of Messer Nicolo.

## Reception by the Great Kaan
Upon their arrival at the Court, the Great Kaan received the two brothers with hospitality and great honour. He expressed significant pleasure at their visit and engaged them in a series of detailed inquiries. The Kaan first questioned them regarding the emperors, specifically asking how they administered justice in their dominions, how they maintained their dignity, and the manner in which they went forth to battle. He sought similar information regarding other potentates, princes, and kings.

Following these inquiries, the Great Kaan asked about the customs of the Latins, the Church, the Pope of Rome, and the activities occurring in Rome. Because the two brothers were proficient in the Tartar language, they were able to provide truthful, orderly, and sensible answers to all of the Kaan's questions.

## Aftermath and Later Missions
The brothers made a very favorable impression on the Great Kaan, who became greatly interested in the civilization of Christendom. This interest led the Kaan to request one hundred learned and intelligent men acquainted with the Seven Arts. These men were intended to be capable of entering into controversy and proving to idolaters and other folk that the Law of Christ was the best.

The Great Kaan eventually sent the two brothers back as his envoys to the Pope, providing them with a tablet of gold bearing his orders on their behalf. After returning to Christendom and visiting the city of Acre and Venice, the brothers eventually departed Venice once more to return to the Great Kaan. On this subsequent journey, they were accompanied by Mark, the son of Messer Nicolo.
