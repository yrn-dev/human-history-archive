# BALADHURI

## Biography
Abu-l-'Abbas Ahmad ibn Yahya ibn Jabir al-Baladhuri was an Arabian historian who was Persian by birth. Despite his origins, he demonstrated strong sympathies toward the Arabs; Mas'udi notes that Baladhuri authored a work specifically intended to refute the Shu'bites. 

Baladhuri's professional life was closely tied to the caliphate, where he served at the courts of caliphs al-Musta'in and al-Mutawakkil. Additionally, he served as a tutor to the son of al-Mu'tazz. He died in 892, and his death was the result of the use of a drug known as *baladhur*, from which he derived his name.

## Historical Works
Baladhuri is most recognized for his work *Futuh ul-Buldan* (Conquests of Lands). This text serves as a digest of a larger, now lost, work and provides an account of the early caliphs and the early conquests of Mahomet. In producing this work, Baladhuri is said to have been diligent in the collection of traditions, traveling to Mesopotamia and various parts of north Syria for research purposes. The *Futuh ul-Buldan* was edited by M. J. de Goeje as *Liber expugnationis regionum*, with editions published in Cairo (1901) and Leiden (1870).

Another significant contribution to history was his work *Ansab ul-Ashraf* (Genealogies of the Nobles). It is reported that Baladhuri had written forty parts of this work by the time of his death. The eleventh book of this series was published by W. Ahlwardt in Greifswald in 1883, and another part of the work is known via manuscript.

## Other Literary Activities
Beyond his primary historical treatises, Baladhuri was engaged in linguistic work, producing several translations from the Persian language into Arabic.
