# AYMER, OR AETHELMAR, OF VALENCE

Aymer of Valence, also known as Aethelmar, was a bishop of Winchester who died in 1260. He was the half-brother of King Henry III.

## Family Background and Arrival in England
Aymer was the son of Hugo of Lusignan, the count of La Marche, and Isabelle of Angouleme. Isabelle was the second wife of King John and married Hugo of Lusignan in 1220. Aymer and his siblings traveled to England in 1247 with the objective of securing court preferment.

## Appointment to the See of Winchester
In 1250, King Henry III succeeded in securing the see of Winchester for Aymer by applying strong pressure to the electors. This appointment was considered unsuitable in every respect. Aymer was described as being ignorant of the English language, illiterate, and maintaining a mode of life that was entirely secular. Due to these factors, he became the focus of popular indignation directed toward foreign favourites.

## Political Conflict and Exile
Aymer's tenure was marked by political strife. During the parliament of Oxford in 1258, Aymer and his brothers repudiated a new constitution that had been prepared by the barons. Following this, he was pursued to Winchester and besieged within Wolvesey castle. He was eventually forced to surrender and leave the kingdom.

## Final Years and Death
Because Aymer had never been consecrated, the chapter of Winchester held a new election in 1259. Despite this, Aymer managed to obtain the support of the pope. While he was traveling back to England, he was struck by a fatal illness in Paris, where he died in 1260.
