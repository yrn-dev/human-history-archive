## The Council of Constance
Between 1414 and 1418, a Council of the whole church was convened at Constance. The primary objective of this gathering was to settle the Great Schism and discuss the scandalous disorder within the church, with the ultimate aspiration of completely reuniting Christendom. This effort resulted in the election of Pope Martin V as the head of a reunited Christendom.

The Council is also noted for its proceedings regarding John Huss, a learned Czech who had delivered lectures at the university of Prague based on the teachings of the Englishman Wycliffe. Huss was invited to the Council under a promise of safe conduct from the emperor. However, upon his arrival, he was seized and put on trial for heresy. During the trial, Huss was ordered to recant certain opinions; he refused to do so until he was convinced of his error, rejecting the view that he must recant simply because his superiors required it. Consequently, in 1415, Huss was burnt alive. The Council also condemned the bones of Wycliffe to be burnt.

The execution of Huss led to an insurrection of the Hussites in Bohemia, marking the beginning of a series of religious wars that contributed to the break-up of Latin Christendom. In response to this insurrection, Pope Martin V preached a Crusade.
