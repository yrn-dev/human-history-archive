# HOW THE KAAN REWARDED THE VALOUR OF HIS CAPTAINS

## The Imperial Lineage of Kúblái Kaan
Kúblái Kaan is a descendant of Chinghis Kaan, who was the first sovereign of all the Tartars and a man of great ability, eloquence, and valour. Chinghis Kaan originally established his sovereignty in 1187, eventually conquering eight provinces and gathering an astonishing multitude of followers. Kúblái is the sixth Lord in this imperial succession. He ascended to the throne in the year of Christ 1256, a transition brought about by his own great worth, ability, and valour. Although his kinsmen and brothers disputed his claim, Kúblái maintained his position through his personal valour and his legal right as a direct descendant of the imperial line.

By the year 1298, Kúblái had reigned for forty-two years. Having come to the throne at approximately forty-three years of age, he was about eighty-five years old by 1298. Before his ascension, he had frequently participated in wars, where he established a reputation as an excellent captain and a gallant soldier. Once he became the Great Kaan, he only personally led wars on one occasion, which occurred in the year of Christ 1286.

## Recognition of Military Valour
The Great Kaan's administration placed a high value on military achievement and leadership. Among his most celebrated captains was Bayan, who is described as the greatest of Kúblái's captains. 

The Kaan's own family was characterized by military prowess. Kúblái had twenty-two male children by his four lawful wives and an additional twenty-five sons by his concubines. These sons are described as great chiefs and valiant, good soldiers. Furthermore, seven of the children born to his four lawful wives govern vast provinces or realms as kings, noted for being gallant and able men. This tradition of valour is continued by the Kaan's grandson, Temur (the son of the eldest son, Chinkin), who is described as a brave and able man.

## Methods of Reward and Honor
The Great Kaan utilized specific honorary objects and privileges to reward and distinguish his captains and officials. These rewards included:

*   **Honorary Tablets:** The Kaan employed "Paizah," which are Golden Honorary Tablets. Additionally, there were "Gerfalcon Tablets."
*   **Ceremonial Items:** The use of umbrellas served as a form of reward or distinction.
*   **Robes of Gold:** During great festivals, the Emperor provided robes of cloth of gold to twelve thousand barons. These recipients received thirteen changes of these robes.

## The Keshican Guard
To maintain his security and honor his military establishment, the Great Kaan maintained a specialized guard known as the Keshican. This guard consisted of twelve thousand horse.
