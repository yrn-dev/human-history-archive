# Roger Bacon

Roger Bacon was a scholar of the Middle Ages whose writings are characterized as a sustained attack on the ignorance of his era. His work emphasized the necessity of collecting knowledge and the critical importance of experiment, a focus described as the "burthen" of his philosophy.

## Philosophical Approach and Methodology
Bacon advocated for a shift away from the reliance on dogmas and authorities, urging mankind to instead "look at the world." He identified four primary sources of ignorance that hindered human progress:
*   Respect for authority
*   Custom
*   The sense of the ignorant crowd
*   The vain, proud unteachableness of human dispositions

Bacon believed that by overcoming these obstacles, a "world of power" would become accessible to humanity. His insistence on empirical evidence and experimentation is noted as a revival of the spirit of Aristotle.

## Relationship with Aristotle and Orthodoxy
Despite his alignment with the spirit of Aristotle, Bacon was highly critical of how Aristotle's works were treated in his time. He argued that scholars, rather than facing facts, spent their time studying "abominable" and "bad Latin translations" of the philosopher. In an intemperate expression of this frustration, Bacon wrote that if he had his way, he would burn all the books of Aristotle, asserting that studying them only increased ignorance, produced error, and resulted in a loss of time.

Because he lived in an age where people were passionately convinced of the finality and completeness of their beliefs—and were disposed to resent reflections upon them—Bacon often had to disguise his views. He attempted to make his writings appear to square with orthodoxy to avoid the threat of prison or worse.

## Academic Bibliography and Scholarship
The study of Roger Bacon's life and doctrines has been addressed by various scholars. E. Charles's 1861 work, *Roger Bacon, sa vie, ses ouvrages, ses doctrines d'apres des textes inedits*, is considered perhaps the best work on the subject, though it has been countered by the more critical interpretations of Schneider (1873) and K. Prantl, who provided a severe criticism of Bacon's logical doctrines.

Other specialized studies include:
*   **Psychology and Cosmology:** Works by K. Werner (1879).
*   **Hebraism:** S. A. Hirsch's *Early English Hebraists* (1899) and a 1905 *Book of Essays* treat Bacon as a Hebraist.
*   **Philosophy and Theology:** Karl Pohl's *Das Verhaeltniss d. Philos. zur Theol. bei Roger Bacon* (Neustrelitz, 1893) and articles appearing in the *Westminster Review* (lxxxi. 1 and 512).
*   **Physics and History:** S. Vogl's *Die Physik Roger Bacos* (1906) and E. Fluegel's study of Bacon's position in the history of philosophy (1902).

## Popular Legend
Beyond his academic and philosophical contributions, Bacon is the subject of popular legends. These include the *Famous Historie of Fryer Bacon* (1615), R. Greene's *Friar Bacon and Friar Bungay* (1587 or 1588), and *A Piece of Friar Bacon's Brazen Heade's Prophesie* (1604).
