# Book-keeping and the Ledger

## The Role of the Ledger
The ledger serves as the center of all book-keeping systems. Other books used in the process are often referred to as "subsidiary books," a term used because they are maintained primarily for practical convenience. These are also known as "books of entry" or "books of first entry" because transactions are recorded in them first before being classified into the ledger. Additionally, there are "auxiliary books" or "statistical books," which are used for statistical purposes and do not form the basis for subsequent entries into the ledger.

## Evolution of Accounting Records
In the early stages of book-keeping, the ledger was not a complete record of all transactions, containing only those accounts deemed necessary to keep accessible. Many early records focused exclusively on transactions with other business houses, which were known as personal accounts. 

Initially, transactions that reduced indebtedness were recorded by date underneath the transactions that created the debt, with the reduction subtracted from the total sum. Because this method proved inconvenient, book-keepers began using two separate accounts: one for the creation of indebtedness and a "contra account" for transactions that extinguished or reduced it. These two accounts were placed on opposite sides of the ledger, leading to the development of the Dr. and Cr. account system currently in general use.

## Personal and Real Accounts
A collection consisting solely of personal accounts is considered an incomplete record of a business's transactions and is insufficient for preparing a statement of financial position. To address this, "real accounts" were added to the ledger. Real accounts record the disposal of and acquisition of different classes of property.

Real accounts follow the same principles as personal accounts:
*   **Dr. side:** All expenditure by the business is recorded here.
*   **Cr. side:** All receipts are recorded here.

The value of assets that remain the property of the business is determined by the excess of debit entries over credit entries.

## Determining Financial Position
When personal and real accounts are properly updated, a business can prepare a statement of assets and liabilities at any time to show its financial position. This statement can also be used to ascertain the profit made by the business, provided that the movements of capital into and out of the business during the period, as well as the financial position at the start of the current financial period, can be ascertained.
