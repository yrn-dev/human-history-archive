# University of Bologna

The University of Bologna is a renowned institution of higher learning founded in the 11th century. While legendary accounts attribute its foundation to Theodosius the Great in A.D. 425, its historical rise to prominence began later. It gained a European reputation as a school of jurisprudence, beginning with Pepo, the first known teacher of Roman law at Bologna around 1076, followed by his successor Irnerius and their followers, known as the glossators.

## Legal Studies and the Glossators
The university became a center for the study of civil and canon law. Among the most celebrated figures was Bulgarus, a 12th-century jurist born in Bologna and regarded as the "Golden Mouth" (*os aureum*) and the most famous of the "Four Doctors" of the law school. These Four Doctors included Bulgarus, Hugo de Porta Ravennate, Jacobus de Boragine, and Martinus Gosia. While tradition suggests they were all pupils of Irnerius, some scholars consider this inadmissible for all but Bulgarus. 

Bulgarus and Martinus Gosia led opposing schools; Bulgarus adhered strictly to the letter of the law, while Martinus Gosia's school accommodated law to what opponents called the equity of "the purse" (*aequitas bursalis*). The school of Bulgarus eventually prevailed, influencing subsequent scholars such as Accursius, Azo, and Joannes Bassianus. Bulgarus also served as a trusted adviser to Emperor Frederick I and played a leading role at the diet of Roncaglia in 1158. His commentary *De Regulis Juris* is noted as the earliest extant work of its kind from the school of the Gloss-writers.

Other significant legal figures associated with the university include Azo (c. 1150-1230), a professor of civil law and pupil of Joannes Bassianus whose *Readings on the Code* were highly influential in tribunals. In the realm of canon law, the university counted professors such as Azo Lambertaccius (13th century), author of *Questiones in jus canonicum*, and Azo de Ramenghis (14th century), author of *Repetitiones super libro Decretorum*. Additionally, Bartolus de Saxoferrato studied at Bologna under Jacobus de Belvisio and Oldradus, receiving his doctor of civil law degree in 1334.

## Academic Evolution and Faculty
Beyond jurisprudence, the university expanded its curriculum over the centuries. Anatomy was taught there in the 14th century, and by the 17th century, the university featured great anatomists such as Marcello Malpighi. In 1650, Giovanni Domenico Cassini was appointed professor of astronomy; he later conducted observations on the comet of 1652 before moving to the Paris observatory. The university also became a site for Dominican activity, as the order established houses in great university towns, including Bologna, to occupy academic chairs.

## Student Population and Campus History
The student body fluctuated significantly over the centuries. Between the 12th and 15th centuries, numbers generally ranged between three and five thousand, though it is said that by 1262, the population reached nearly ten thousand, including the poets Dante and Petrarch. Other records indicate that at the end of the 15th century, the university contained 10,000 students, primarily studying civil law.

Despite its fame, the university functioned as an autonomous corporation without a fixed residence for much of its early history. Professors lectured in their own homes or in rooms provided by civic authorities. This changed in 1520 when law professors were granted apartments in a building belonging to the church of S. Petronio. In 1562, under the order of Pius IV and the direction of Cardinal legate Carlo Borromeo, a university building was constructed. This move was likely partly disciplinary, as Bologna had come under the dominion of the papacy in 1506. This building served as the communal library starting in 1838, and its courtyard displays the arms of students elected as representatives of their faculties or nations. Since 1803, the university has been established in the 16th-century Palazzo Poggi.

## Later History and Administration
The university experienced a period of decline between 1815 and 1848, during which student numbers dropped to about one hundred in some years due to government political persecutions. By 1859, enrollment rose to 355. In more recent records, the university possesses four faculties and is attended by approximately 1,700 students. Notably, women have been numbered among its professors more than once.

The university's presence also fostered a trade in bookselling during the 12th century. Dealers known as *stationarii* sold books on commission, as well as parchment and writing materials, and were involved in binding and decorating. They were sometimes distinguished from *librarii*, a term that originally referred to copyists.
