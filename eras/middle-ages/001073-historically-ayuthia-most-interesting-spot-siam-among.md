# Ayuthia

Ayuthia is a city in Siam, referred to by the Siamese as *Krung Kao*, which translates to "the Old Capital." It is located at 14deg 21' N. and 100deg 32' E. Historically, it is considered the most interesting spot in Siam.

## Geography and Infrastructure
The city is situated on a large island surrounded by the river Me Nam, which is broken up into a network of creeks. The local population, numbering approximately 10,000, primarily resides in floating houses that are characteristic of lower Siam; these houses are moored to the edges of the creeks, which serve as thoroughfares. 

Ayuthia is located 42 miles from Bangkok and is positioned on the northern main line of the state railways.

## History and Architecture
Founded in the 14th century, Ayuthia served as the capital of Siam for more than four centuries. The city is noted for its ruins, which include fortifications, churches, pagodas, and palaces. These remains are of significant archaeological interest and are deeply marked by Hindu influence, while also possessing strong individual and generic peculiarities. The departed glories of the city were recorded by early European travellers, who first brought Siam to the knowledge of the West and established the foundations for trade and foreign intercourse.

The city suffered two major destructions by the Burmese, the first occurring in 1555 and the second in 1767. Following the second destruction, Ayuthia ceased to be the capital of the country. Today, a large collection of relics is housed in the local museum.

## Administration and Economy
Ayuthia serves as the headquarters of a high commissioner and is the chief town of one of the richest agricultural provincial divisions of Siam. The government is organized as an ordinary provincial division, known as a *Monton*. The government offices are located in spacious buildings that previously served as a royal summer retreat.

The economy of the region is primarily agricultural. Great quantities of paddi are sent annually to Bangkok via rail and river. In exchange, the agriculturist peasantry imports cloth and other goods to meet their needs; there is no other trade in the area. Additionally, an ancient masonry enclosure used for the capture of elephants is located outside the town and remains in periodic use.

## Regional Relations
The influence of Ayuthia extended to other regions of Siam. For example, the city of Korat, the capital of the *Monton* of Nakawn Racha Sema, has been part of Siam or tributary to it since the founding of Ayuthia in the 14th century, despite occasional periods of independence or temporary subjection to Cambodia.
