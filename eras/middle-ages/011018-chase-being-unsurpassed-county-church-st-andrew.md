# Regional Landscapes and Ecclesiastical Architecture

## Landscapes of Devonshire and Dorsetshire
In Devonshire, England, the market-town of Ashburton is situated in a valley surrounded by hills. It is located a short distance from the river Dart, and the scenery in the neighbourhood of Holne Chase and Buckland is described as being unsurpassed in the county.

In Dorsetshire, the most prominent geological formation is the Chalk, which occupies the central and most elevated part of the county. This formation sweeps in a south-westerly direction as a belt of high ground approximately 12 miles in width, extending from Cranborne Chase through Frampton, Blandford, and Milton Abbas to Dorchester. From Dorchester, the Chalk outcrop narrows and turns south-eastward via Bincombe and Portisham to West Lulworth, eventually forming the ridge of the Purbeck Hills and ending at the sea as the headland between Swanage and Studland Bays.

## The Church of St. Andrew
Several churches dedicated to St. Andrew are noted across different regions:

*   **Ashburton, Devonshire:** The church of St. Andrew is a cruciform building featuring a lofty tower. Constructed early in the 15th century, it contains a fine old oak roof over the north aisle. The church also houses a tablet dedicated to the 1st Baron Ashburton and solicitor-general John Dunning (1731-1783), with an inscription written by Dr. Johnson.
*   **Derby, Derbyshire:** St. Andrew's in Derby was designed by Sir G. G. Scott and is built in the Decorated style.
*   **Dumfries, Scotland:** St. Andrew's is a Roman Catholic church built between 1811 and 1813 in the Romanesque style. It serves as the pro-cathedral for the diocese of Galloway.

## Other Notable Ecclesiastical Sites
The archive records various other significant religious structures:

**England**
In one county, the number of churches of the highest interest is described as unsurpassed, even excluding the cathedral churches of Rochester and Canterbury. Notable examples include the church of Lyminge, founded by Æthelburga (wife of King Edwin of Northumberland) and rebuilt in 965 by St. Dunstan using Roman material. Other significant sites include the Norman church at Barfreston, known for its ornament around the east window and south doorway, and the Early English Hythe church. Additionally, the church of Cobham is noted for possessing one of the richest collections of ancient brasses in England.

In Ashburton, the grammar school was founded in 1314 as a chantry. Nearby, Buckfast Abbey, a Benedictine house before the Conquest and refounded for Cistercians in 1137, was restored in 1882 by a French Benedictine community; its fine Perpendicular abbot's tower remains. In Derby, All Saints' church features a tower built between 1509 and 1527 that stands 210 feet tall, while the Roman Catholic church of St. Mary is cited as one of the best examples of A. W. Pugin's work.

**Scotland**
In Dumfries, St. Michael's (1746) is the church attended by Robert Burns, who is buried there in a magnificent mausoleum in the south-east corner alongside his wife, Jean Armour. The Gothic church of Greyfriars (1866-1867) stands on the site of the town's old castle and a Franciscan monastery. In Dornoch, the cathedral was built by Bishop Gilbert de Moravia (d. 1245). After being damaged by fire in 1570 and subsequently neglected, it was restored in 1837 by the 2nd duke of Sutherland and now serves as the parish church.
