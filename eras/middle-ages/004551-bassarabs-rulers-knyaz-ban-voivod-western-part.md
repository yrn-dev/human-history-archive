# The Bassarab Dynasty

The Bassarabs (or Bassaraba) were a ruling dynasty in Rumania that governed Walachia from the beginning of its history until 1658. The dynasty became extinct with the reign of Constantine Sherban in 1658.

## Origins and Early Rule
The exact origin of the family and the name "Bassarab" remains unexplained, though several conjectures exist. Some Servian chroniclers suggest a connection to their own nationality, identifying "Sarab" with "Serbia" or "Sorb." Other theories suggest an oriental origin, a view reflected in the heraldic signs of the crescent and the star. Additionally, the Bassarab shield features three heads of negroes; this is believed to be of late western origin based on a popular etymology that linked the latter part of the name to "Arabs," who were interpreted as Moors (blacks).

The first appearance of the Bassarabs as rulers—holding the titles of *voivod*, *ban*, or *knyaz*—occurred in the southern parts of Transylvania and the western part of Rumania, an area originally known as Little Walachia. Their early influence extended to the old dukedoms of Almash and Fogarash, located on the right bank of the Aluta (Olt), and reached south to Craiova and Severin. Tradition holds that the foundation of the Walachian principality was established by a member of the family who migrated from Transylvania, first settling in Tirgovishtea and Campulung.

## Dynastic Conflict and Lineage
Starting in the 13th Century, the Bassarabs split into two rival factions. These were the descendants of two brothers, Dragul and Dan. The line of Dragul became known in history as "Drakul," a term meaning "devil." This was a nickname bestowed by the rival faction and became associated with the family due to the extreme cruelties of Vlad Tsepesh (1431–1476) and Vlad Drakul (1433–1446), both of whom are depicted in popular legend as being fiendishly cruel.

Throughout the dynasty's history, almost every prince and voivod utilized a Slavonic surname. This is attributed to the influence of the Slavonic Church, to which the Rumanians belonged.

## Prominent Rulers and Achievements
Several members of the Bassarab dynasty are noted for their specific contributions or historical significance:

*   **Mircea (1386–1418):** A prominent ruler who accepted Turkish suzerainty.
*   **Neagoe:** The founder of the cathedral at Curtea de Argesh.
*   **Petru Cercel:** A scholar of profound learning who spoke twelve languages and maintained correspondence with poets and scholars in Italy. He owned the oldest manuscripts of the Rumanian Gospels, which bear his autograph signature. He was drowned in Constantinople in 1590 following intrigues by his successor, Mihnea.
*   **Michael the Brave (1592–1601):** One of the most prominent members of the family.
*   **Mateiu Bassarab (1633–1654):** He established the first printing-press in Rumania. Under his influence, the first code of laws was compiled and published in Bucharest in 1654.
