# AVERROES

## Early Life and Education
Averroes, born as Abūl-Walīd Muḥammad ibn-Aḥmad Ibn-Muḥammad ibn-Rushd (1126–1198), was an Arabian philosopher born in Cordova. During his early years, he focused on mastering a diverse curriculum that included philosophy, medicine, mathematics, jurisprudence, and theology, studying under the approved teachers of his era.

## Career and Political Appointments
The prime of Averroes' life coincided with the final period of Mahommedan rule in Spain under the Almohades. He was introduced to the prince Yusef by Ibn-Tufail (also known as Abubacer), who served as Yusef's philosophic vizier. Averroes was well-versed in the Malekite system of law and was appointed as the cadi of Seville in 1169. He continued to hold similar appointments for the following twenty-five years, spending time in Morocco, Cordova, and Seville. 

During this period, he likely served as a physician to Yusef al-Mansur. The prince enjoyed engaging Averroes in discussions regarding the theories of philosophy and how they related to the faith of Islam. Averroes also maintained a friendship with Avenzoar (Ibn-Zuhr), who is described as the greatest of Moslem physicians.

## Intellectual Climate and Conflict
In the Islamic society of the time, the pursuit of free thought and science was heavily dependent on the favor of the monarch and the tastes of the wealthy. Speculative studies were viewed with deep distrust and dislike by the ignorant fanaticism of the multitude. Those who did not limit themselves to the natural science of the Koran were deemed Zendiks, or infidels.

These tensions culminated around the year 1195, when Averroes was accused of pursuing heretical studies and holding heretical opinions. As a result, he was stripped of his honors and banished to a location near Cordova, where his actions remained under close watch. This period saw a broader effort in Andalusia to eliminate liberal culture, sparing only the arithmetic, astronomy, and medicine necessary for practical life.

## Final Years and Legacy
The period of persecution eventually passed, and Averroes was recalled to Morocco once the passion of the people had been satisfied. He was restored to his honors, though he survived this restoration for only a brief period. He died in the year preceding the death of his patron, al-Mansur. In 1199, the political power of the Moslems ended, marking the conclusion of the culture of liberal science associated with Averroes.

Averroes left behind several sons, some of whom followed in the footsteps of their grandfather by becoming jurists. He was also influenced by the work of Avempace (Ibn Bājja), one of the earliest and most distinguished Arab philosophers of Spain.
