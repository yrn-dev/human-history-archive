# Saint Boniface

Saint Boniface (680–754), known by his real name Wynfrith, was an English missionary recognized as the apostle of Germany. Born into a good Saxon family at either Kirton or Crediton in Devonshire, he entered the monastic life while still young. His early education in theology and grammar took place first at Exeter and subsequently at Nutcell, located near Winchester, where he studied under the abbot Winberht.

## Early Missionary Efforts
Wynfrith distinguished himself as a preacher and scholar. Despite having every inducement to remain in his monastery, he departed in 716 to serve as a missionary to Frisia, following the example of other Saxon monks. This initial mission was short-lived; he was forced to return, likely due to the hostility of Radbod, the king of the Frisians, who was engaged in war with Charles Martel.

## Papal Commission and German Missions
At the end of 717, Wynfrith traveled to Rome. In 719, Pope Gregory II commissioned him to evangelize Germany and to counteract the influence of Irish monks in the region. After crossing the Alps, he visited Thuringia and Bavaria. Upon learning that King Radbod had died, he returned to Frisia. There, he preached successfully for three years under the guidance of Willibrord, his countryman and the first bishop of Utrecht.

Around 722, Boniface visited Thuringia and Hesse, where he converted and baptized many heathens and won over several chieftains. After reporting his success to Pope Gregory, he was summoned back to Rome. On November 30, 722, he took an oath of obedience to the pope and was consecrated as a bishop.

## Expansion of Mission and Later Life
Following his consecration, Boniface's mission was expanded. He returned to the continent with letters of recommendation for Charles Martel, tasked with both the conversion of heathens and the suppression of heresy. He is credited with the conversion of the Hessians, Thuringians, and Frisians. His career ended in martyrdom in Holland.
