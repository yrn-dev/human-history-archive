# Thomas Percy, Bishop of Dromore

Thomas Percy was an English cleric and editor, best known for his work in recovering and publishing early English and Scottish folk-songs. He was born in 1729 in a house dated 1580, located in the market town of Bridgnorth, Shropshire.

## The Reliques of Ancient English Poetry
In 1765, Percy published the *Reliques of Ancient English Poetry*. This work represented a serious effort to recover English and Scottish folk-songs from the recitations of old people who had preserved them by heart. 

The publication of the *Reliques* occurred during a period of growing interest in popular poetry. While some collections of Scottish ballads existed from the beginning of the 18th century and Danish ballads had been printed in the middle of the 16th century, Percy's work, along with Allan Ramsay's *Evergreen and Tea Table Miscellany*, marked a significant movement toward the recovery of these oral traditions.

## Literary Context and Influence
The *Reliques* focused on popular songs, which are understood as *Volkslieder*—songs with words composed by the people, for the people, and handed down through oral tradition. These ballads are characterized by a straightforward diction and artless melody. 

The influence of this style of poetry can be seen in the lyrics of several major poets, including Wordsworth, Coleridge, and Goethe. Percy's editorial work coincided with similar efforts in other countries; for instance, Madame de Chenier composed an essay on the ballads of Greece while Percy was editing the *Reliques*. Later, the work of Scott regarding the Forest and Liddesdale, as well as the work of Grimm, Herder, and Goethe in Germany, furthered the study of national songs.

## Dromore
Thomas Percy held the title of Bishop of Dromore. Dromore is a town in County Down with a population of 2,307. The area is noted for having mineral springs that are both sulphurous and chalybeate. In terms of ecclesiastical organization, Dromore is the site of a Roman Catholic diocese, while the county of Down is also part of the Protestant diocese of Down.
