# Ashby-de-la-Zouch

Ashby-de-la-Zouch is a market-town located in England within the Bosworth parliamentary division of Leicestershire. It is situated 118 miles north-west by north from London and is accessible via the Leicester-Burton branch of the Midland railway. As of 1901, the population of the urban district was 4,726.

## History and Name
At the time of the Domesday survey, the area was part of the estates belonging to Hugh de Grentmaisnel. It was subsequently held by Robert Beaumeis. Through female descent, the estate passed to the family of la Zouch, from whom the town derived the adjunct to its name; prior to this, the location had been known as Essebi or Ashby.

The town's development as a commercial center is evidenced by a 1219 record in which Roger la Zouch was granted a two days' fair at the feast of St Helen and a weekly market, in exchange for a fine of one palfrey. By the 18th century, Ashby was recognized as one of the best markets for horses in England. During this period, the town also hosted prosperous factories producing hats as well as cotton and woollen stockings.

## Ashby Castle and Notable Residents
In the 15th century, the manor was held by James Butler, the earl of Ormond. Following his attainder in 1461, the manor was granted to Lord Hastings. In 1474, Lord Hastings received a royal licence to fortify and build a castle and to empark 3,000 acres.

The castle served as a place of detention for Mary, queen of Scots, in 1569, while she was under the custody of the earls of Shrewsbury and Huntingdon. During the Civil War, the castle was held and fortified for the king by Colonel Henry Hastings, and King Charles visited the site in 1645. Following the conclusion of the war in 1648, the castle was dismantled by order of parliament. Extensive remains of the castle are still located to the south of the town. The site also features in Sir Walter Scott's novel *Ivanhoe*.

## Landmarks and Geography
The town's church, St Helen, is a Perpendicular building that was enlarged and restored in 1880. The church contains monuments of the Huntingdon family and an old finger-pillory used for punishing misbehaviour within the church. Other local landmarks include a Queen Eleanor cross, created by Sir Gilbert Scott to commemorate the countess of Loudoun.

The area is known for the Ivanhoe baths, established in 1826. These baths utilize saline waters containing bromine, which are used for rheumatic and scrofulous complaints. The springs for these baths are located at Moira, three miles to the west.

## Industry and Infrastructure
The neighboring district contains extensive coal-mines, including those at Moira. From Moira, the Ashby-de-la-Zouch canal runs south to connect with the Coventry canal.
