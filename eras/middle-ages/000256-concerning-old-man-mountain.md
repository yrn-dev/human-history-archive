# CONCERNING THE OLD MAN OF THE MOUNTAIN

The Old Man of the Mountain was the title of the leader of a sect known as the Assassins, Hashíshîn, or Muláhidah. According to the accounts of Messer Marco Polo, who gathered information from several natives of the region, the Old Man dwelt in a country called Mulehet, a name meaning "Place of the Aram." This territory is associated with the kingdom of the Ismailites and is referred to in various historical records as Mulahi or Mulihet.

## Titles and Territory
The title "Old Man of the Mountain" is a translation of the popular Arabic title *Shaikh-ul-Jibal*. While Crusaders applied this name to the chief of the sect settled in the mountains north of Lebanon, it is noted that the title properly belonged to the Prince of Alamút. This leader avoided the titles of Sultan, Malik, or Amir, and his territory was known as the *Balad-ul-Jibal*. The sect maintained strongholds in the castles of Alamut and Girdkuh.

## Training of the Assassins
The Old Man, referred to in their language as Aloadin, employed a specific method to train his followers. He enclosed a valley between two mountains and transformed it into a garden designed to be the largest and most beautiful ever seen. This garden was filled with a wide variety of fruit and featured elegant palaces and pavilions adorned with exquisite painting and gilding.

To convince his people that this place was actually Paradise, the Old Man fashioned the garden after the description of Paradise given by Mahommet. This included runnels that flowed freely with water, honey, milk, and wine. The garden was also populated by numerous beautiful damsels who sang sweetly, played various instruments, and danced. The Saracens of that region believed that this garden was indeed Paradise.

## Decline and Legacy
The sect eventually faced destruction through the expeditions of Hulákú. Despite this apparent destruction, records indicate the sect survived into later periods. 

In more recent history, a successor to the *Viex de la Montaingne* established headquarters at Bombay. This representative used the large income received from the faithful to maintain a racing stable and served as a promoter of the Bombay Turf. In 1866, a legal action was brought in an English High Court by a portion of the Bombay community—specifically Khojas who wished to sever themselves from the peculiarities of the sect to become respectable Sunnis. The court action sought to exclude the Agha Khan from rights over the Khojas and transfer community property to Orthodox Mahomedans; however, the judgment was rendered in favor of the Old Man of the Mountain.
