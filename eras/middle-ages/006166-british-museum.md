# British Museum

The British Museum is a major repository of historical documents, manuscripts, and artifacts. Its collections span a vast array of disciplines, including theology, music, physics, and art, and are organized into various specialized catalogs and manuscript collections.

## Biblical and Theological Collections
The museum holds an extensive array of biblical texts and religious manuscripts from different eras and languages. These include early codices such as the *Codex Alexandrinus* and a probable Northumbrian copy of the *Codex Amiatinus*. The collection features a 9th-century Hebrew *Pentateuch*, a Vulgate written in A.D. 1148 for the monastery of Ste Marie de Parco in Louvain, and a 13th-century Latin Bible that belonged to Robert de Bello, the abbot of St Augustine's, Canterbury.

The museum also preserves significant versions of the English Bible, including an early Wycliffite version from the late 14th century (belonging to Thomas of Woodstock, duke of Gloucester), the 42-Line Bible printed at Mainz between 1452 and 1456, and Tyndale's Quarto Edition of the New Testament printed in Cologne in 1525. Further additions include the first printed English Bible from 1535 and the first edition of the Authorized Version from 1611. Other religious texts in the archive include a late 12th-century Psalter of Westminster Abbey (Royal MS. 2A. xxii) and a late 13th-century Lectionary of the use of Paris (Add. M.S. 17,341).

## Academic and Scientific Manuscripts
The British Museum houses several important manuscripts concerning physics, mathematics, and theology. These include the *Communia Naturalium*, a work on the fundamental notions of physics, and *Baconis Physica*, which is located among the additional manuscripts. Theological fragments held by the museum include the *Compendium Studii Theologiae* and a fragment titled *Quinta Pars Compendii Theologiae*.

## Historical Documents and Art
The museum's archives contain a vast mass of correspondence and documents related to historical figures, such as Burghley. These are found across several major catalogs, including the Cotton, Harleian, Royal, Sloane, Egerton, and Additional manuscripts, with the Lansdowne manuscripts being particularly significant for Burghley's correspondence.

The museum's art and artifact collections include:
*   **Illuminated Manuscripts:** A 14th-century Italian school work by Durandus titled *De Divinis Officiis* (Add. MS. 31,032) and a work by Valerius Maximus from approximately A.D. 1475, executed for Philippe de Comines (Harley M.S. 4374).
*   **Artifacts:** A 4th-century terra cotta model of a Roman bugle.
*   **Prints:** A print depicting the trial of Louis XVI.

## Music and Bookbinding
The museum maintains a *Catalogue of MS. Music*, which includes references to musical instruments described or represented in illuminated manuscripts. This is supplemented by an appendix provided by Augustus Hughes-Hughes.

The museum is also a primary source for the study of bookbinding. Various authorities have published works based on its holdings, including W.Y. Fletcher's *English Bookbindings in the British Museum* (1895) and *Foreign Bookbindings in the British Museum* (1896), as well as H.B. Wheatley's *Remarkable Bindings in the British Museum* (1889).

## Administration and Cataloguing
Samuel Ayscough (1745–1804) served as a significant figure in the museum's administrative history. After beginning his tenure in the cataloguing department, he was appointed assistant librarian around 1785. Ayscough was responsible for several major organizational tasks, including the publication of a two-volume catalogue of then-undescribed manuscripts in 1782. He also contributed a third share to the British Museum catalogue of 1787 and catalogued 16,000 ancient rolls and charters. Ayscough died at the British Museum on October 30, 1804.
