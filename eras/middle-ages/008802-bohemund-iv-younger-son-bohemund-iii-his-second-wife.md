# Bohemund IV

Bohemund IV was the younger son of Bohemund III and his second wife, the princess Orguilleuse. He held the titles of count of Tripoli and prince of Antioch during the late 12th and early 13th centuries.

## Succession and Territorial Claims
Bohemund IV first became the count of Tripoli in 1187. Following the death of his father, Bohemund III, in 1201, he succeeded to the principality of Antioch. This succession occurred to the exclusion of Raymund Rhupen, the son of Bohemund IV's elder brother, Raymund.

The legitimacy of Bohemund IV's succession was the subject of a protracted dispute that lasted for many years. Raymund Rhupen's claim was championed by Leo of Armenia, who was the great-uncle of Raymund Rhupen. This conflict occupied the attention of Pope Innocent III.

## Conflicts with Armenia
The struggle for Antioch involved various political and military shifts. Bohemund IV's position was supported by the Greek inhabitants of Antioch and the Templars, who held estates in Tripoli. In 1207, Bohemund IV granted the Greek inhabitants of Antioch their own patriarch. 

Conversely, Leo of Armenia was supported by the Hospitallers. Between 1210 and 1211, Leo appealed to both the emperor Otto IV and Pope Innocent III. In 1216, Leo successfully captured Antioch and established Raymund Rhupen as the prince. However, this arrangement lasted less than four years; Bohemund IV regained possession of the principality by the time of Leo's death in 1220. The dispute effectively ended with the death of Raymund Rhupen in 1221, after which Bohemund IV reigned in both Antioch and Tripoli.

## Religious and Political Relations
Bohemund IV's reign was marked by significant friction with the Hospitallers. He proved himself to be a determined enemy of the order, an antagonism that led to his excommunication in 1230.

On the international stage, Bohemund IV's relations with the Holy Roman Empire were inconsistent. During the crusade of 1228–29, he initially joined emperor Frederick II but subsequently deserted him. As a result of this desertion, Bohemund IV was excluded from the operation of the treaty of 1229.

## Death and Legacy
Bohemund IV died in 1233. Shortly before his death, he had concluded a peace agreement with the Hospitallers, and Pope Gregory IX had released him from the excommunication imposed in 1230. He was succeeded by his son, Bohemund V, who was born to his wife Plaisance, the daughter of Hugh of Gibelet. Like his father, Bohemund V served as both prince of Antioch and count of Tripoli and maintained an alliance with the Templars.
