# The Siege of Barletta (1503)

The siege of Barletta was a military engagement that took place in 1503 in the region of Apulia, Italy. The conflict involved the French army, which conducted the siege, and the Spanish army, which was responsible for the defense of the town.

## The Combat of the Knights
During the course of the siege, a specific combat occurred in the neighbourhood of the town, located between Corato and Andria. This engagement featured thirteen picked knights from France and Italy. The combat resulted in a victory for the Italian knights. This event was later celebrated by Massimo d' Azeglio in his work titled *Disfida di Barletta*.

## Geography of the Region
Barletta is a seaport town and episcopal see situated on the E.S.E. coast of Apulia in the province of Bari. It is located 34-1/2 miles west-north-west of Bari by rail. The town is characterized by its good harbour and several historical structures:
* **The Cathedral:** A Romanesque cathedral that was begun at the close of the 12th century and expanded in later periods.
* **S. Sepolcro:** A Gothic church also built at the close of the 12th century.
* **The Castle:** Located behind the cathedral, the castle dates from 1537.
* **Statue of Heraclius:** A bronze statue of the emperor Heraclius, standing 14 feet in height, is positioned in front of the former church.

In the vicinity of Barletta, seven miles to the north-west, are the salt-works of Barletta, which are known today as Margherita di Savoia. Additionally, the city of Corato is located in the same province of Bari, 26 miles west of Bari by steam tramway. Corato is situated in the center of an agricultural district and is described as a clean and well-kept town.

## Economic Profile
By 1901, the population of Barletta was 42,022. Records from 1904 indicate that the harbour was cleared by 461 steamers (with a total tonnage of 364,904) and 508 sailing-vessels. The town's trade involved imports valued at £92,486, consisting of timber, coal, and sundries. Exports were valued at £180,699, consisting primarily of oil, sulphur, tartar, tartaric acid, and wine.
