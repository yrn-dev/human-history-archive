# Brienne-la-Vieille

Brienne-la-Vieille is a village located in north-eastern France, situated 1½ miles south of the town of Brienne-le-Château.

## Architecture and Landmarks
The village is home to a church featuring architecture from the 12th and 16th centuries, which contains fine stained windows. The church's portal originally belonged to the ancient abbey of Bassefontaine; the ruins of this abbey are located near the village.

## Regional Context: Brienne-le-Château
Brienne-la-Vieille is situated near Brienne-le-Château, a town in the department of Aube located 1 mile from the right bank of the Aube and 26 miles north-east of Troyes. As of 1906, the town had a population of 1,761. 

The town is characterized by several notable landmarks:
* **The Château:** An imposing building from the latter half of the 18th century constructed by the cardinal de Brienne. It houses a significant collection of pictures, including historical portraits from the 17th and 18th centuries.
* **The Church:** Dating from the 16th century, the church is noted for its quality stained glass.
* **Napoleon's Statue:** A statue commemorates Napoleon's time in Brienne from 1779 to 1784, during which he attended a military school that was later suppressed in 1790.

The town has historical significance as a site of fighting between Napoleon and the Allies in 1814. Additionally, brewing is conducted within the town.

## The Counts of Brienne
Under the Carolingian dynasty, Brienne-le-Château served as the capital town of a French countship. In the 10th century, the town was captured by two adventurers, Gobert and Engelbert; the noble house of Brienne descended from Engelbert.

The house of Brienne produced several figures of international prominence:
* **John of Brienne (1148–1237):** A member of a noble family in Champagne, John became the king of Jerusalem through his marriage to Mary of Montsserrat. He led 100,000 Latins during the fifth crusade to conquer Egypt, achieving the siege of Damietta. In 1229, he was elected emperor of the East (emperor-regent for life) during the minority of Baldwin II. He fought and conquered the Greek emperor John III (Vatatzes). In his later years, he sold several relics—including the holy sponge, the holy lance, parts of the true cross, and the crown of thorns—to King Louis IX.
* **Walter V:** The count of Brienne, count of Lecce (Apulia), and duke of Athens. He fought the Greeks in Thessaly but was defeated and killed near Lake Copais in 1311.
* **Walter VI:** Son of Walter V, he attempted to reconquer Athens in 1331. He served under Philip of Valois against the English and later obtained dictatorial powers in the republic of Florence, though his tyrannical behavior led to his expulsion. He was appointed constable of France by John the Good and died at the battle of Poitiers in 1356.

## Succession and Later History
The countship of Brienne passed through several houses following the death of Walter VI. His sister and heiress, Isabelle, married Walter of Enghien, transferring the title to the house of Enghien. Subsequently, John of Luxemburg-St Pol (who died around 1397) became count of Brienne through his marriage to Margaret of Enghien.

The house of Luxemburg held the countship until Margaret Charlotte of Luxemburg sold it to a man named Marpon. Marpon then ceded it in 1640 to Henri Auguste de Loménie, whose wife, Louise de Béon, was a descendant of the house of Luxemburg-Brienne. The Limousin house of Loménie produced several statesmen, including the minister of Louis XV, the celebrated cardinal Étienne Charles de Loménie de Brienne (1727–1794). Members of the Loménie family served as the final lords of Brienne.
