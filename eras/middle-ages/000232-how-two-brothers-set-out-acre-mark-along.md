# The Departure of the Two Brothers and Mark from Acre

The journey of the two brothers and Mark from the city of Acre was part of a series of diplomatic missions between the West and the court of the Great Kaan. Their travels were characterized by significant delays caused by the Papal interregnum, a period during which no Pope was elected for two years.

## Departure from Venice and Arrival in Acre
After spending time in Venice and observing that no Pope had been appointed, the two brothers decided they could no longer postpone their return to the Great Kaan. They departed from Venice, taking Mark, the son of Messer Nicolo, with them. Upon arriving in Acre, they sought counsel with a Legate, identified in some records as Tedaldo (or Tebaldo) Visconti, the Archdeacon of Liège.

The brothers requested the Legate's permission to travel to Jerusalem to obtain oil from the lamp on the Sepulchre. This oil was to be carried to the Great Kaan as he had previously enjoined. After receiving permission, they traveled to Jerusalem, secured the oil, and returned to Acre.

## The First Attempted Return
The brothers informed the Legate that because there was still no sign of a Pope being elected, they wished to return to the Great Kaan to avoid further delay. The Legate agreed and provided them with letters for the Great Kaan. These letters served as testimony that the brothers had acted in good faith to fulfill their charge but were unable to do so due to the absence of a Pope.

The brothers set forth from Acre and reached Layas. However, shortly after their arrival there, they received news that the Legate they had known had been elected Pope, taking the name of Pope Gregory of Piacenza (also referred to as Pope Gregory X). The Pope sent a message to Layas requesting that the brothers not proceed further on their journey but instead return to him immediately. To facilitate this, the King of Hermenia provided a galley to transport the two ambassador brothers back to the Pope at Acre.

## Presentation to Pope Gregory X
Upon their return to Acre, the brothers presented themselves before the new Pope with humble reverence. Pope Gregory X received them with honor and satisfaction, granting them his blessing. To assist them in their mission to the Great Kaan, the Pope appointed two learned Churchmen from the Order of Preachers to accompany them: Friar William of Tripoli and Friar Nicolas of Vicenza.

The Pope provided the party with proper credentials and letters in reply to the messages sent by the Great Kaan. Additionally, he granted the missionaries the authority to bestow every kind of absolution and to ordain bishops and priests. As gifts for the Great Kaan, the Pope sent many fine vessels made of crystal.

## Final Departure and Journey to the Great Kaan
Once all necessary arrangements and credentials were secured, the group—consisting of the two brothers, the two Friars, and Mark—departed from Acre together. They traveled to Layas and continued their journey toward the East.

The journey to the court of the Prince lasted a full year, during which the party traveled in a northward and north-eastward direction. Along the way, they witnessed many marvels, the details of which were later recorded by Mark.

## Arrival at the Court of the Great Kaan
Upon arriving at the court, the two brothers were received by the Great Kaan with hospitality and honor. The Kaan questioned them extensively regarding the administration of justice and the maintenance of dignity by emperors, kings, and other potentates, as well as their conduct in battle.

Furthermore, the Great Kaan inquired about the customs of the Latins, the Church, and the Pope in Rome. Because the two brothers were proficient in the Tartar language, they were able to provide the Kaan with a truthful and orderly account of these matters.
