# The West Saxon Royal Family and the Origins of Wessex

The West Saxon royal family presided over the kingdom of Wessex, a territory located in the south of England between Sussex and "West Wales" (which was eventually reduced to Cornwall). In its early stages, the kingdom also held some territory to the north of the Thames.

## Claims of Ancestry and Stock
While the kingdom of Wessex is traditionally associated with the Saxons, the West Saxon royal family claimed to be of the same stock as that of Bernicia. Bernicia was one of two kingdoms—alongside Deira—located north of the Humber that eventually united to form Northumbria. Because of this claim, the past connections of the West Saxon royal family appear to have lain with the Angli.

Broadly, the Angles and Saxons, along with their allies, originated from the Teutonic stock. This group spoke a tongue belonging to the Germanic or Teutonic branch of the Indo-European (or Indo-Germanic) family. The dialects used by the invaders of Britain specifically belonged to the Low German group of the West Germanic branch.

## Foundation and Early History
According to the *Anglo-Saxon Chronicle*, the origin of Wessex is attributed to Cerdic, who is said to have arrived some eighteen years after Ella (or Ælle), the founder of Sussex who landed in 477. However, these dates are considered to have no value, and the story of Cerdic's arrival is viewed with suspicion.

Bede identifies the invaders of Britain as belonging to three different nations: the Jutes, the Saxons, and the Angli. According to Bede, the Saxons founded the kingdoms of Wessex, Sussex, and Essex. The Jutes occupied southern Hampshire and Kent, while the Angli founded the remaining kingdoms.

## Political Supremacy and Expansion
The West Saxon dynasty achieved significant influence over other regions. Before the supremacy of Æthelberht of Kent, the West Saxon king Ceawlin held a similar position of power. In later periods, under the West Saxon dynasty, the kingdoms of Surrey, Sussex, Essex, and Kent were sometimes assigned as dependent kingdoms to members of the royal family. For example, during the reign of King Ecgbert of Wessex, Kent was entrusted to his son Aethelwulf; upon Aethelwulf's accession to the throne of Wessex in 839, Kent was given to Aethelstan, who was likely Aethelwulf's son.

The West Saxons, along with the Mercians, continued their conquests of the British people even after they had become Christians.

## Conflict and Resistance
The expansion of the Saxons of the West was met with resistance from the native populations. According to one account, the hereditary prince of the Silures in South Wales, Arthur, defeated the Saxons of the West and the Angles of the North in twelve successive battles. Additionally, the Romano-Britons attempted to defend themselves against Saxon attacks in the east and south by hiring Saxon mercenaries, though these mercenaries did not remain faithful to their employers.
