# Moravia

Moravia is a region located in Central Europe, serving as a geographical link between the Danubian plain and the kingdom of Bohemia. It borders Bohemia to the south-east and is positioned such that Bohemia is easily accessible through Moravia.

## Medieval Political Struggles and the House of Premysl
During the 13th century, Moravia was ruled by Premysl Ottakar II while he was still under the sovereignty of his father, King Wenceslas I of Bohemia. During the final years of Wenceslas I's reign, the kingdom was plagued by internal discord. During this period, Premysl Ottakar II emerged as the chief leader of the malcontents. However, a reconciliation between the father and son occurred before the death of Wenceslas I.

Upon his accession to the Bohemian throne, Premysl Ottakar II became one of the most significant kings of Bohemia. His power reached its zenith following a decisive victory in 1260 at the battle of Kressenbrunn, where he defeated Bela, the king of Hungary. His territories expanded to include Carinthia, Istria, and parts of northern Italy, stretching from the Giant Mountains in Bohemia to the Adriatic. Because of his immense wealth and military strength, his contemporaries referred to him as "the man of gold" and "the man of iron," respectively.

## Conflicts in the 15th Century
In the 15th century, Moravia became a focal point of conflict between King George of Podebrad, the only Hussite king of Bohemia, and his opponents. Initially, King George maintained a prosperous reign supported by the towns and the smaller nobility. However, he eventually entered a protracted struggle with the papal see, leading to the formation of a confederacy against him in 1465 by Romanist nobles at Zelena Hora.

This conflict escalated when Pope Paul II excommunicated George of Podebrad and released Bohemians from their oath of allegiance. King Matthias of Hungary, formerly an ally of George, shifted his support to the league of Zelena Hora. This led to warfare in which King Matthias invaded Moravia and seized possession of Brunn, the capital of the country. On May 3, 1469, Matthias was proclaimed king of Bohemia during a meeting of Catholic nobles held at Olmutz in Moravia.

## Military and Political Administration in Later Eras
In the 18th century, Moravia was part of the administrative territories overseen by high-ranking military officials of the Austrian empire. In 1769, Maria Theresa and Kaunitz appointed Loudon as the commander-in-chief in both Moravia and Bohemia, a post he held for three years. Loudon, a distinguished soldier who later became a field-marshal and commander-in-chief of the armed forces of Austria, died on duty on July 14, 1789, at Neu-Titschein in Moravia.

By the late 19th century, Moravia was involved in the transition of Austria into a modern constitutional state. Following the laws of December 1867, a parliamentary ministry known as the "Buerger Ministerium" was appointed. This ministry included Herbst and Giskra, who were the leaders of the German party in Moravia and Bohemia. These leaders were part of a Liberal majority composed of representatives from commercial enterprise, trade, and industry who sought to transform Austria into a great industrial state through free trade and railway enterprise.
