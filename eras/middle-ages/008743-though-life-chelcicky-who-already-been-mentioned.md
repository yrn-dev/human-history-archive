# Peter Chelcicky and the Bohemian Brethren

## Peter Chelcicky
Peter Chelcicky was an independent religious writer who lived during the period of the Hussite wars. Born in the final years of the 14th century, he died in 1460. His intellectual and religious approach has led him to be characterized as the Tolstoy of the 15th century. 

Chelcicky's primary ideas were defined by a horror of bloodshed and a firm determination to accept all decrees of worldly authorities unresistingly, even those that were unjust. While he was a strenuous enemy of the Church of Rome, he did not join any of the Hussite parties. His most significant work is the *Sit viry* (the net of faith). He also produced polemical writings in the form of letters addressed to Bishop Nicolas of Pelhrimov and Archbishop John of Rokycan, as well as a work titled *Postilla*.

## The Bohemian Brethren
Although Chelcicky lived an isolated life, he is considered the indirect founder of the community known as the "Bohemian Brethren." This community exerted a significant influence on Bohemian literature. The Brethren wrote almost all of their theological and historical works in the national language, a practice that served to make the language more polished and refined.

One of the most renowned achievements of the brotherhood was the *Bible of Kralice*, a translation of the Scriptures. This was a joint effort by several divines of the community and was first printed in 1593 at Kralice in Moravia.

## Leadership and Literary Activity
Brother Gregory, who was surnamed the patriarch of the brotherhood, authored a large number of writings focused primarily on theological matters. His most important works include the book *On good and evil priests* and *Letters to Archbishop Rokycan*.

Following the death of Brother Gregory in 1480, the community experienced a period of discord, which resulted in a surge of literary activity. Numerous controversial works were produced by members of the community, including Augusta (a bishop), and brothers Jaffet, Blahoslav, and Lucas. Additionally, a young priest named Jan Bilek wrote an account regarding the captivity of Bishop Augusta.

## Historical Works and Comenius
The Bohemian Brethren produced numerous historical works, though the majority of these were destroyed in the 17th century during a period when nearly all anti-Roman books in Bohemia were perished. Only fragments of the *History of the Unity* (the history of the brotherhood) by Blahoslav have been preserved. Similarly, of the *History of the House of Rosenberg* written by the brotherhood's historian Wenceslas Brezan, only the biographies of Peter and William of Rosenberg survive.

The most prominent writer associated with the brotherhood was John Amos Komensky, also known as Comenius (1592-1670). Comenius authored 142 works, many of which were written in his native tongue, including numerous educational and devotional writings. His most important work in the native language is *Labyrinth of the World*, an allegorical tale that is perhaps the most famous work written in Bohemian.
