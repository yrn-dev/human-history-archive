# Asher ben-Yehiel

## Life and Exile
Asher ben-Yehiel, also known as Rosh, was a Jewish rabbi and codifier born approximately in 1250 in the Rhine district. During the 13th century, persecutions were inflicted upon the Jews in Germany, which endangered Asher and forced him to flee to Spain. Following this enforced exile, he was appointed as the rabbi of Toledo. 

This period of exile resulted in Asher becoming impoverished. His experience coincided with a significant shift in the status of medieval rabbis; prior to the 14th century, rabbis typically supported themselves through secular professions—most notably medicine—and did not receive a salary for their rabbinic duties. This practice changed during Asher's era.

## Intellectual Profile and Beliefs
A disciple of Meir of Rothenburg, Asher's sole interest was the Talmud. He is described as a man of profound and narrow austere piety. He held a determined opposition to the study of philosophy, a stance that made him antipathetic to the spirit of Spain. Despite Asher's opposition, the Jews of Spain remained devotees of rabbinical lore as well as secular sciences.

## Literary Contributions and Systematization
Asher was the first among German rabbis to demonstrate a strong talent for systematization. His primary work was a compendium of the Talmud, compiled between 1307 and 1314. This *Compendium* was characterized by a greater simplicity than previous works of its kind and showed deference to German authorities. To a large extent, his work superseded the work of 'Al-phasi and is included in most editions of the Talmud.

## Legacy and Family
Asher's son, Jacob, who died in Toledo before 1340, continued the work of legal codification. Jacob authored the four *Turim*, a popular and profound codification of rabbinical law. The *Turim* served as the standard code until Joseph Qaro used it as the direct basis for the widely accepted Code of Jewish law known as the *Shulhan 'Arukh*. Asher ben-Yehiel died in Toledo in 1327.
