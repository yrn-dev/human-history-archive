# The Defeat of Baldwin I and the Count of Blois

## The Latin Empire of Romania
Baldwin I was the emperor of Romania, as well as the count of Hainaut and Flanders. He was a prominent leader of the fourth crusade, an expedition that resulted in the conquest of the majority of the East Roman empire, the capture of Constantinople, and the establishment of the Latin empire of Romania. Baldwin was elected emperor on May 9, 1204, after the crown was refused by Henry Dandolo, the doge of Venice. He was crowned one week later and was regarded as a pious, virtuous, and popular leader who strictly observed his crusading vows.

The empire was organized on feudal principles, with the emperor serving as the feudal superior to princes who were granted portions of the conquered lands. Baldwin's own territory included Constantinople, adjacent regions on the Asiatic and European sides, several islands—including Chios, Lesbos, Tenos, and Lemnos—and other outlying districts.

## Conflicts in Thrace
To secure the empire, it was necessary to break Greek resistance in Thrace and secure Thessalonica. This objective led to a collision between Baldwin and Boniface of Montferrat, a rival candidate for the imperial throne. Boniface, who was to receive a large territory in Macedonia as the king of Saloniki, opposed Baldwin's plan to march to Thessalonica, hoping to remain independent of the empire and avoid doing homage. This dispute was further aggravated by antagonism between the Lombards and the Flemings.

While Baldwin insisted on the march to Thessalonica, Boniface laid siege to Hadrianople, where Baldwin had appointed a governor. To avoid an inevitable civil war, an agreement was reached through the efforts of the count of Blois and Dandolo. Under this agreement, Boniface was appointed commander of the forces marching to conquer Greece and received Thessalonica as a fief from the emperor.

## The Battle of April 1205
During the winter of 1204-1205, the Franks continued their conquests in Bithynia, with Baldwin's brother, Henry, participating. However, in February, the Greeks in Thrace revolted. They relied on the assistance of John (Kaloyan), the king of Bulgaria, whose offers of alliance the emperor had unwisely rejected. Following the expulsion of the garrison of Hadrianople, Baldwin marched to besiege the city, accompanied by Marshal Villehardouin and Dandolo, the count of Blois.

The emperor's forces were small, consisting of 140 knights and a train of sergeants and archers. Baldwin had sent a messenger to recall Count Henry and his troops; had he waited for his brother and a supply of 20,000 Armenians, he might have faced the enemy with equal numbers and superior discipline. Instead, the Latins besieged the rebels of Hadrianople, spending the holy week framing engines for the destruction of fellow Christians and pillaging the countryside for food.

The Frankish forces were soon harassed by Coman light cavalry. The marshal of Romania issued a strict order that the cavalry should form on the sound of the trumpet and that no one should engage in a dangerous, desultory pursuit under pain of death. This order was first disobeyed by the count of Blois, which led the emperor into ruin. Although the Comans initially fled, they turned and encompassed the exhausted Frankish squadrons after a pursuit of two leagues.

## Fate of the Emperor and the Count
The battle, which took place on April 14, 1205, ended in a total defeat for the crusaders. The count of Blois was slain on the field, and Emperor Baldwin was captured. 

For a period, the emperor's fate remained uncertain, and his brother Henry assumed the regency. It was not until the middle of July that it was confirmed Baldwin was dead. While it appears he was initially treated well as a valuable hostage, the Bulgarian monarch eventually sacrificed him in a sudden outburst of rage, possibly due to the Franks taking control of Philippopolis. Accounts of his death vary: one contemporary writer claims his feet and hands were cut off and he was thrown into a valley where he died three days later, while King John wrote to Pope Innocent III stating that Baldwin died in prison. Henry was crowned emperor in August.

## Other Figures of Blois
The archive contains separate records regarding other individuals associated with Blois in different historical contexts. In the 14th century, Charles of Blois was involved in a struggle for the possession of the duchy of Brittany against John of Montfort. Charles was defeated and captured by the English in 1347. He was later aided by Bertrand du Guesclin, the constable of France. However, Charles of Blois was ultimately slain at the fight at Auray in September 1364, a battle in which Du Guesclin was also captured.
