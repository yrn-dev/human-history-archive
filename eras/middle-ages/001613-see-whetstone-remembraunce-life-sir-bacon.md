# The Bacon Family

The Bacon family produced several prominent figures in English history, most notably Sir Nicholas Bacon and his son Francis Bacon, both of whom held high offices of state and influenced the intellectual and legal landscapes of their respective eras.

## Sir Nicholas Bacon
Sir Nicholas Bacon (1509–1579) served as the lord keeper of the great seal of England during the reign of Queen Elizabeth. Born at Chislehurst, he was the second son of Robert Bacon of Drinkstone, Suffolk. He received his education at Corpus Christi College, Cambridge, where he graduated with a B.A. in 1527, and later spent time studying in Paris. Upon returning to England, he entered Gray's Inn and was called to the bar in 1533.

Bacon's public career began in 1537 as the solicitor of the court of augmentations. He acquired various estates, primarily in the eastern counties, following the dissolution of the monasteries. His political ascent continued with his appointment as a member of parliament for Dartmouth in 1545 and as attorney of the court of wards and liveries in 1546. By 1550, he became a bencher, and in 1552, he served as treasurer of Gray's Inn. Although he held Protestant sympathies, he maintained his office in the court of wards during the reign of Mary, though he was forbidden from leaving England.

The most significant period of his life began with the accession of Elizabeth in 1558. Through his close friendship with his brother-in-law, Sir William Cecil (later Lord Burghley), he was appointed lord keeper of the great seal in December 1558, and was subsequently knighted and named a privy councillor. In this capacity, he presided over the House of Lords during the opening of Elizabeth's first parliament and helped secure the archbishopric of Canterbury for Matthew Parker. While he opposed making war on France due to England's poverty, he supported a closer union with foreign Protestants to counter the religious policies of Scotland and France. In 1559, he was authorized to exercise the full jurisdiction of lord chancellor. However, in 1564, he was temporarily dismissed from court due to royal suspicion regarding his involvement in the publication of a pamphlet titled "A Declaration of the."

## Francis Bacon
Francis Bacon (1561–1626), also known as Baron Verulam and Viscount St Albans, was an English essayist, statesman, and philosopher. Born on January 22, 1560/1, at York House in the Strand, London, he was the youngest son of Sir Nicholas Bacon. His mother, a daughter of Sir Anthony Cooke, was a cultured woman skilled in classical studies and a warm adherent of the Puritan or Reformed Church.

Due to delicate health, Bacon likely received much of his early instruction at home before entering Trinity College, Cambridge, in April 1573. During his three years there, he studied the sciences but concluded that the existing methods and results were erroneous. While he maintained a reverence for Aristotle, he despised the prevailing Aristotelian philosophy, viewing it as fruitless and useful only for disputation. He believed philosophy required a new method and a true purpose, a conception he carried with him upon leaving the university.

Bacon's later career was marked by his role as a judge and official. In October 1608, he became treasurer of Gray's Inn. His legal career was not without controversy; he faced censure in Parliament, though he maintained he was the "justest judge that was in England these fifty years." Records indicate that no attempt to reverse his numerous decrees was successful, supporting his plea of innocence regarding accusations of wilful perversions of justice.

Contemporaries noted the uniqueness of Bacon's writing style. His friend Sir Tobie Matthews described him as a man of rare and diverse knowledge who expressed ideas in an elegant, abundant, and "ravishing" way, utilizing metaphors and allusions in a manner perhaps unseen since the beginning of the world.

## Roger Bacon
Distinct from the later statesman and his father, Roger Bacon (c. 1214–c. 1294) was an English man of science and philosopher born near Ilchester in Somerset. Though his family was initially well-off, they were despoiled of their property and several members were exiled during the reign of Henry III.

Roger Bacon studied at Oxford, though sources clarify he did not attend Brasenose or Merton, as neither existed at the time. He was recognized by contemporaries such as Robert Grosseteste, the bishop of Lincoln, and Adam de Marisco. It is suggested he took orders in 1233. He later traveled to France to study at the university of Paris, which was then the intellectual center of Europe. During his time there, the Dominicans and Franciscans were leading theological discussions, with Albertus Magnus and Thomas Aquinas representing the Dominicans, and Alexander of Hales serving as the oracle for the Franciscans.
