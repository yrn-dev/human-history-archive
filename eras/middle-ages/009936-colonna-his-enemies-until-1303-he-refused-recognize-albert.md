# The Colonna Family

The Colonna are an illustrious noble house of Rome with significant historical presence in the city's political and ecclesiastical affairs. The family has maintained great possessions in the Campagna of Rome, although they alienated their original fief of Colonna to the Rospigliosi.

## Conflict with the Papacy

The Colonna were frequently involved in domestic feuds and power struggles with the pontiffs. During the reign of Pope Boniface VIII (1294–1303), the family became enemies of the pope due to his attempts to build up great estates for his own family. Boniface VIII, who ardently asserted the lordship of the papacy over all the world's kingdoms, was described by some as an enemy of the Colonna.

This hostility culminated in September 1303 at Anagni. At the instigation of Philip the Fair, Sciarra Colonna and William of Nogaret entered the papal palace. There, they insulted and reportedly beat the aged Pope Boniface VIII under his own roof. Boniface died in the month following this event.

Following the death of Boniface VIII, his successor, Pope Benedict XI (1303–1304), worked to conciliate the enemies created by his predecessor, specifically including the Colonnas, France, and King Frederick II of Sicily.

## Family Structure and Institutions

The house of Colonna has been perpetuated through collateral branches, though the specific family line of Stephen the elder was extinguished. The family's influence extended into the church through Colonna cardinals, who founded, endowed, and protected the convent of St. Silvester. This convent was established for the daughters of the family who chose a monastic life; in the year 1318, there were twelve such women. Other family members were permitted to marry kinsmen in the fourth degree, a dispensation attributed to the close alliances and small number of noble families in Rome.

## Later History and Notable Members

The Colonna continued to be involved in Roman power struggles in later centuries. During the reign of Sixtus IV, Rome was distracted by battles between rival houses. In one such conflict, a prothonotary Colonna was tortured and beheaded after the conflagration of the pope's palace.

The family also produced figures of military and ecclesiastical distinction. Marce Antonio Colonna commanded the pope's galleys during the naval victory at Lepanto and was honored with a Roman triumph of ovation. Additionally, Otho Colonna is noted as an ancestor of Martin V.

## Origins and Legends

There have been attempts to establish the Roman origins of the Colonna family. One account suggests that a cousin of the emperor Nero escaped from Rome and founded Mentz in Germany. Furthermore, in 1417, a duke of Juliers and Guelders acknowledged his descent from the ancestors of Martin V.
