# The Rashtrakutas and Regional Power Struggles in Western India

The Rashtrakutas were a significant dynasty in the history of western India, emerging as a dominant power in the southern Deccan. Their rise and maintenance of authority were characterized by prolonged struggles for mastery against other regional powers, most notably the Chalukyas and the Gurjaras.

## Struggle with the Chalukyas
During the 7th century, the Chalukyas were the primary power in the southern Deccan. During this period, the Chalukyas overcame the Rashtrakutas and other rivals, absorbing smaller kingdoms into their empire. The southern boundary of the empire of Harsha (who died in 647 or 648) was the Nerbudda; the regions south of this boundary were under Chalukyan influence.

The balance of power shifted as the Chalukyas eventually succumbed to their ancient rivals, the Rashtrakutas. Following this victory, the Rashtrakutas succeeded to the bulk of the Chalukyan dominions. This expansion included the acquisition of Gujarat, where the Rashtrakutas established a branch line.

## Relations with the Gurjaras and Other Powers
The Gurjaras, who are believed to be the ancestors of the Gujar caste and likely entered India with the White Huns, established power over Gujarat. By approximately 600, they overran north-eastern Kathiawar and made the raja of Valabhi their tributary, establishing a branch at Broach between 585 and 740.

For roughly two centuries, from approximately 750 to 950, a balance of power existed between the Gurjaras and the Rashtrakutas. During this era, neither kingdom possessed sufficient strength to encroach significantly upon the other. However, the Rashtrakutas were prevented from pursuing larger schemes of conquest due to internal dissensions with their branch line.

## Territorial Influence and Successors
The dominions of the Rashtrakutas, along with those of the Satavahanas, Mauryas, and Chalukyas, included the outlying portion of western India consisting of the seven islands that originally formed Bombay. In the period between 450 and 750, the principal location in Bombay harbour was the city of Puri on Elephanta Island.

In other regions, such as the district of Belgaum (anciently known as Venugrama), the Rashtrakutas succeeded the Chalukyas, who had held the country from the middle of the 6th century until about 760. Following the eventual break-up of the Rashtrakuta power, a portion of their influence survived through the Rattas from 875 to 1250. The Rattas eventually made Venugrama their capital in 1210, though they later succumbed to the Yadavas of Devagiri in 1250.

## External Pressures
While the Rashtrakutas and their rivals struggled for regional control, external forces also impacted the landscape of western India. Arab invaders from Sind entered India in 710-711 and established themselves in Sind by 712. While they conducted raids, they did not attempt serious attacks on the Chalukya or Gurjara empires. However, in 770, the Arabs destroyed the city of Valabhi, ending the dynasty established there by a chief named Bhatarka.
