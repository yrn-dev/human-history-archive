# Exilles, Fenestrelles, and Chateau Dauphin

Exilles, Fenestrelles, and Chateau Dauphin (also known as Casteldelfino) are locations situated in the Alpine regions between France and Italy. These areas have been subject to territorial exchanges between France and the House of Savoy, and have served as sites of military engagement.

## Territorial Exchanges and the Treaty of Utrecht
The status of these valleys was significantly altered by the Treaty of Utrecht in 1713. Under the terms of this treaty, the valleys of Exilles, Fenestrelles, and Chateau Dauphin were ceded to Savoy. In exchange for these territories, France received the valley of Barcelonnette.

Prior to this exchange, these areas were part of the Briançonnais. This region included the upper valley of the Durance—along with the Guil and Gyronde affluents—as well as the valley of the Chisone, which contained Fenestrelles and Pragelas, and Pérouse. Additionally, the Briançonnais encompassed the valley of the Dora Riparia, which included Exilles, Bardonnèche, Oulx, and Césanne. These glens are located on the eastern slope of the Alpine chain, whereas Barcelonnette is situated on the west slope.

## Geography and Military History
The locations of Exilles and Fenestrelles are noted in historical records regarding the passages of the Alps. They are listed alongside other sites such as Coni, Demont, and La Brunette (near Suse) in accounts of the region.

Chateau Dauphin, referred to as Casteldelfino, was the site of a notable military incident during the Spanish-Piedmontese war in the Alps. During this conflict, a combat took place at Casteldelfino which was won by the king of Sardinia, who led the engagement in person.
