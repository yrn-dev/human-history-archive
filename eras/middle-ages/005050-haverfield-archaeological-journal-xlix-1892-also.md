# Francis John Haverfield

Francis John Haverfield, M.A., LL.D., is a distinguished historian and archaeologist who has served as the Camden Professor of Ancient History at Oxford University. A Fellow of Brasenose College, Oxford, and a Fellow of the British Academy, Haverfield has also been a member of the German Imperial Archaeological Institute. His academic career includes previous roles as a Student, Tutor, Librarian of Christ Church, Oxford, and Senior Censor. In 1906, he served as Ford's Lecturer.

## Contributions to Roman Britain
Haverfield is a primary authority on the Roman occupation of Britain. He authored the work *The Romanization of Roman Britain* (Oxford, 1906), which is cited as a general reference for Roman Britain and as a source for understanding the end of the Roman period in Britain. His research extends to various specific regions and structures:

*   **The Antonine Wall:** Haverfield contributed to the *Antonine Wall Report* (1899) regarding the Roman occupation of Scotland.
*   **Hadrian's Wall:** He conducted excavations, the reports of which were published in the *Cumberland Archaeological Society Transactions* between 1894 and 1904.
*   **Regional History:** He has written articles for the *Victoria County History*.

Additionally, Haverfield has authored monographs on Roman history and specific works on Mancunium and Lugudunum.

## Research on the Brigantes
Haverfield's work includes the study of the Brigantes, a people of northern Britain. The name "Brigantes" is Celtic for "free, privileged" or "mountaineers." This group inhabited a territory stretching from the Belisama (the Ribble or Mersey) in the west to the mouth of the Abus (Humber) in the east, extending north to the Wall of Antoninus. This region encompassed most of Yorkshire, Durham, Westmorland, Cumberland, part of Northumberland, and the whole of Lancashire, with Eburacum (York) serving as their chief town.

Haverfield's research in the *Archaeological Journal* (xlix., 1892) identifies inscriptions relating to the eponymous goddess of this people, Brigantia.
