# Mausoleum of Pope John XXII

## Overview and Location
The mausoleum of Pope John XXII is a significant work of 14th-century art located within the cathedral of Notre-Dame des Doms. The cathedral is situated in Avignon, a city in south-eastern France and the capital of the department of Vaucluse. The city is positioned on the left bank of the Rhone river, a few miles above where it meets the Durance.

The cathedral itself is a Romanesque building dating primarily from the 12th century. Its most prominent external feature is a gilded statue of the Virgin located on the western tower. The cathedral is situated on the Rocher des Doms, a precipitous rock and plateau at the northern end of the town, adjacent to the Palace of the Popes.

## Artistic Significance
Among the various works of art housed in the interior of the cathedral of Notre-Dame des Doms, the mausoleum of Pope John XXII is described as the most beautiful. It is regarded as a masterpiece of Gothic carving from the 14th century.

## Historical Context of Pope John XXII
Records indicate that Pope John XXII was active during the early 14th century. In 1323, he communicated with Uzbek Khan of Sarai regarding the city of Soldaia (also known as Sodaya, Soldachia, or Súdák) on the south-eastern coast of the Crimea. In this correspondence, the Pope complained that Christians had been ejected from Soldaia and that their churches had been converted into mosques.

## The Setting of Avignon
The mausoleum is located in a city characterized by its medieval fortifications. Avignon is encircled by ramparts built by the popes during the 14th century, which serve as one of the finest existing examples of medieval fortification. These strong walls feature machicolated battlements, thirty-nine massive towers, and several gateways, three of which were constructed in the 14th century. 

The cathedral and the mausoleum are located near the Palace of the Popes, a sombre complex of buildings covering more than 1 1/4 acres, which effectively dwarfs the neighboring cathedral.
