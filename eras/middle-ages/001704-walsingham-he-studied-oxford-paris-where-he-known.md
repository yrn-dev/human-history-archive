# John Baconthorpe

## Early Life and Education
John Baconthorpe, who became known as "the Resolute Doctor," was a learned Carmelite monk born in Baconthorpe, Norfolk. He is believed to have been the grandnephew of Roger Bacon. His early upbringing took place at the Carmelite monastery of Blakeney, which was located near Walsingham. Following his early education, Baconthorpe pursued further studies at the universities of Oxford and Paris.

## Academic Reputation and Philosophy
During his time at the university of Paris, Baconthorpe earned the reputation of being the "Princeps" of the Averroists. However, the scholar Renan suggests that his work in this area was not necessarily an endorsement of the philosophy, but rather an attempt to justify Averroism against accusations of heterodoxy.

## Ecclesiastical and Political Career
Baconthorpe rose within the hierarchy of his order, and in 1329, he was chosen as the twelfth provincial of the English Carmelites. In the realm of political thought, he is noted for advocating the subordination of the clergy to the king, a position that appears to have anticipated the later views of Wycliffe.

In 1333, Baconthorpe was summoned to Rome. Records indicate that he initially maintained the authority of the pope regarding cases of divorce, though he later retracted this specific opinion.

## Works and Legacy
Baconthorpe died in London in 1346. His primary scholarly contribution is the work titled *Doctoris resoluti Joannis Bacconis Anglici Carmelitae radiantissimi opus super quattuor sententiarum libris*, which was published in 1510. This text underwent several editions and remained a subject of study nearly three centuries later at Padua, which was the final home of Averroism. He is spoken of with great veneration by Lucilio Vanini.
