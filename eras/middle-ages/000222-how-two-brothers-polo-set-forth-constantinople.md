# The Departure of the Polo Brothers from Constantinople

## Origins and Initial Venture
In the year of Christ 1260, during the reign of Baldwin II at Constantinople, two brothers—Messer Nicolas Polo and Messer Maffeo Polo—were residing in the city. The brothers, described as noble, wise, and provident, had traveled from Venice to Constantinople to trade their merchants' wares. While in the city, they decided to undertake a venture of trade by crossing the Greater Sea. To facilitate this journey, they acquired a store of jewels and set forth from Constantinople, traveling across the sea to Soldaia.

## Journey Beyond Soldaia
After spending some time at Soldaia, the brothers decided to extend their travels further. They departed Soldaia and journeyed until they reached the court of a Tartar Prince named Barca Kaan. The residences of this Prince were located at Bolgara and Sara. Barca Kaan, who was regarded as one of the most liberal and courteous Princes among the Tartars, received the two brothers with great honor. In return, the brothers presented him with all the jewels they had brought with them. The Prince accepted the gift graciously and provided the brothers with a return value at least twice that of the jewels.

The brothers remained at the court of Barca Kaan for one year. During this time, a great war broke out between Barca and Aláu, the Lord of the Tartars of the Levant, leading to the mustering of great hosts on both sides.

## Passage to the Great Kaan
Following their time with Barca Kaan, the brothers encountered a party of envoys from the Great Kaan (Kúblái) in China, who were traveling to his brother Hulagu in Persia. These envoys urged the Polo brothers to visit the Great Kaan, who had not previously seen men of the "Latin" peoples. After making their arrangements, the brothers accompanied the envoys on a journey that lasted a full year, traveling in a northward and north-eastward direction. Along this route, they witnessed many marvels of various kinds.

Their travels eventually led them to the city of Bokhara. From there, they continued until they arrived at the court of the Great Kaan.

## Reception at the Court of the Great Kaan
The Great Kaan received Nicolas and Maffeo Polo with hospitality and honor. He expressed great pleasure at their visit and questioned them extensively about the administration of justice, the maintenance of dignity, and the conduct of battle by emperors, kings, and other potentates. 

Furthermore, the Great Kaan inquired about the customs of the Latins, the Church, the Pope, and the activities in Rome. Because the brothers were well-versed in the Tartar language, they were able to provide the Kaan with truthful, orderly, and sensible accounts of these matters.

## Legacy and Documentation
The experiences of these travels, and the subsequent journeys involving the son of Nicolas, Marco Polo, were later recorded. Marco Polo, who was born in 1254, spent twenty-six years acquiring knowledge of the wonders of the world. While later held as an inmate in a prison at Genoa, Marco recounted these experiences to another prisoner, Messer Rusticiano of Pisa, who reduced the accounts to writing in the year 1298.
