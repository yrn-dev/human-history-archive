# The Arrival of the Two Brothers at the City of Acre

The journey of the two brothers to the city of Acre was part of a diplomatic mission on behalf of the Great Kaan. Having served as envoys for the Great Kaan, the brothers were provided with a tablet of gold containing orders on their behalf to facilitate their travels.

## Arrival in Acre and the Papal Vacancy
The two brothers departed from Layas and arrived at the city of Acre in April of the year of Christ 1269. Upon their arrival, they discovered that the Pope had died. In the absence of a reigning Pope, the brothers sought out a man of great authority named Theobald of Piacenza, who served as the Legate for the entire kingdom of Egypt.

The brothers informed the Legate of the mission they had been sent to perform. Theobald of Piacenza expressed great surprise at their story, viewing the mission as a matter of great advantage and honor for all of Christendom. However, because the papal seat was vacant, the Legate advised the brothers that they must exercise patience until a new Pope was elected before they could execute their charge.

## Departure for Venice
Agreeing that the Legate's assessment was just, the brothers decided to use the waiting period to visit their households in Venice. They departed from Acre and traveled to Negropont, from where they continued their voyage to Venice. 

Upon arriving in Venice, Messer Nicolas discovered that his wife had passed away. She had left behind a fifteen-year-old son named Marco. The two brothers remained in Venice for two years while they waited for the election of a new Pope.

## Return to Acre and the Mission to Jerusalem
After tarrying in Venice and seeing that no Pope had been appointed, the brothers decided they could no longer postpone their return to the Great Kaan. They departed Venice, taking Mark along with them, and returned to Acre.

Once back in Acre, they met again with the Legate. The brothers requested his permission to travel to Jerusalem to obtain oil from the lamp on the Sepulchre, as the Great Kaan had enjoined them to do. After receiving the Legate's permission, they traveled to Jerusalem, secured the oil, and returned to Acre.

Upon their return, they informed the Legate that they wished to return to the Great Kaan due to the continued delay in the election of a Pope. The Legate agreed to their departure and provided them with letters for the Great Kaan. These letters testified that the brothers had arrived in good faith to fulfill their charge but were unable to do so because there was no Pope.

## Subsequent Return to Acre
In a later sequence of events, the brothers set forth from Acre to return to the Grand Kaan and reached Layas. While at Layas, they received news that the aforementioned Legate had been elected Pope, taking the name of Pope Gregory of Piacenza. The new Pope sent a message to Layas requesting that the brothers not proceed further on their journey but return to him immediately. To facilitate this, the King of Hermenia provided a galley to transport the two ambassador brothers back to the Pope at Acre.

Upon being honorably conducted back to Acre, the brothers presented themselves before the Pope with humble reverence. The Pope received them with satisfaction and honor, granting them his blessing. He appointed two learned Churchmen from the Order of Preachers—Friar William of Tripoli and Friar Nicolas of Vicenza—to accompany the brothers to the Great Kaan. The Pope provided the party with proper credentials, reply letters to the Great Kaan's messages, and several fine crystal vessels as presents. Having obtained everything necessary, the four men, accompanied by Marco, the son of Messer Nicolas, departed from Acre and traveled to Layas.
