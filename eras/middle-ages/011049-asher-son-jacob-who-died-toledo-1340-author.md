# Jacob, Son of Asher ben-Yehiel

Jacob was a medieval author and the son of the rabbi and codifier Asher ben-Yehiel. He died in Toledo before the year 1340.

## Literary Contributions
Jacob is recognized as the author of the *four Turim*. This work is described as a very popular and profound codification of rabbinical law. The *four Turim* served as the standard code of law until it became the direct basis for the *Shulhan 'Arukh*, a widely accepted Code of Jewish law authored by Joseph Qaro.

## The Father of Jacob: Asher ben-Yehiel
Jacob's father, Asher ben-Yehiel (also known as *Rosh*), was a rabbi and codifier born around 1250 in the Rhine district. Due to persecutions inflicted upon the German Jews during the 13th century, Asher fled to Spain, where he eventually became the rabbi of Toledo. This forced exile resulted in his impoverishment.

Asher's life and career marked a shift in the status of medieval rabbis. Prior to the 14th century, rabbis typically supported themselves through secular professions, such as medicine, and did not receive salaries for their rabbinic duties; however, this practice changed during Asher's era. 

A disciple of Meir of Rothenburg, Asher was characterized by austere piety and a narrow, profound focus on the Talmud. He was a determined opponent of the study of philosophy, which placed him in opposition to the general Spanish spirit of the time, as the Jews of Spain remained devotees of rabbinical lore as well as secular sciences.

Asher was the first German rabbi to demonstrate a strong talent for systematization. His primary work, a *Compendium* of the Talmud compiled between 1307 and 1314, largely superseded the work of 'Al-phasi. This *Compendium* is included in most editions of the Talmud and is noted for its simplicity and its deference to German authorities.
