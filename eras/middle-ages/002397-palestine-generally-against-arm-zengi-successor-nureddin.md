# Baldwin III and the Defense of Palestine

Baldwin III, who reigned as king of Jerusalem from 1143 to 1162, was the eldest son of Melisinda and Fulk of Jerusalem. Born in 1130, he was the first king of Jerusalem to be a native of the soil of Palestine, whereas his three predecessors had been emigrants from the West. Because of his age, his mother served as regent until 1152.

## Political and Military Challenges
Baldwin III ascended the throne during a period of instability. The position of the Franks in northern Syria was being fatally weakened by attacks from Zengi on Edessa and by the Greeks in Cilicia. Although there were few outward signs of decay initially, the power of the Latin kingdom of Jerusalem began a slow decline during his reign. A significant blow occurred the year after his accession with the loss of Edessa, which served as the most important and farthest outpost in northern Syria.

In 1147, the second crusade followed the fall of Edessa. Baldwin III, then approximately eighteen years old, participated in this crusade alongside Louis VII and Conrad III. After receiving them in Jerusalem in 1148, Baldwin planned an attack on Damascus with the two leaders, though the effort ended in signal failure.

## Strategy Against Nureddin
Following the failure of the crusade, Baldwin III focused on stabilizing the north of Palestine against Nureddin, the successor of Zengi. In 1149, Baldwin traveled to Antioch to regulate affairs following the death of Raymund, the husband of Princess Constance. To strengthen the region's defenses against Nureddin, Baldwin employed two primary strategies:
1. He renewed a politic alliance with Damascus, which had been interrupted since 1147.
2. He ceded Tellbashir, the final remnant of the county of Edessa, to Manuel of Constantinople.

## Character and Governance
Unlike his predecessors, who were described as a blend of military and clerical types, Baldwin III was of a subtler nature. He was a studious and clerkly king, well-versed in law and capable of navigating court intrigues and legal problems. He was highly skilled in the *jus consuetudinarium* of the kingdom, later known as the "assizes of Jerusalem."

According to William of Tyre, Baldwin had a deep affection for history and spent his spare time reading. He possessed a royal faculty for remembering faces and names, making him more popular with people of all social standings than any previous king. He was noted for his impromptu eloquence, wit, and excessive generosity. While he had been prone to gambling and lax morals in his youth, his adult life and marriage to Theodora were considered shining examples. He practiced abstemiousness in food and drink, believing that excess in either served as an incentive to the worst crimes. His character was such that even his enemy, Nureddin, remarked upon his death that the world had lost a prince without equal among the Franks.

## Internal Conflict and Succession
In 1152, a struggle for power emerged between Baldwin III and his mother, Melisinda, who was reluctant to relinquish the reins of power she had held during her regency. Baldwin initially sought a solemn coronation to signal his emancipation; though dissuaded from a formal ceremony, he wore his crown publicly in the church of the Sepulchre. 

The resulting conflict led to an initial agreement where Melisinda retained possession of Nablus and Jerusalem, while Baldwin kept Tyre and Acre. Baldwin later repented of this bargain, leading to further fighting through which he eventually recovered possession of his territory.
