# Arbesau

## Geographic Context and Location
Arbesau is located in the southern region of Bohemia, a kingdom and crownland of Austria. This area is characterized by its position as a borderland between the Slavonic and German worlds, bounded to the north-west by Saxony. Arbesau is situated in a region described as the "Thermopylae of Bohemia," which serves as the site for both Arbesau and the battle of Kulm.

To the east of this area, the Elbe river exits Bohemia and enters Saxony. This specific point of exit marks the lowest elevation in Bohemia, reaching an altitude of 367 ft.

## Regional Topography
The broader region surrounding Arbesau is defined by significant mountain ranges and frontiers. To the north-west lies the Erzgebirge (known in Czech as *Rudo Hori*), which forms the north-west frontier with an average altitude of 2600 ft. The highest point of the Erzgebirge is the Keilberg, reaching 4080 ft. These mountains are characterized by easy passes, good roads, cultivated areas, and numerous mining villages, often giving the landscape the appearance of a hilly undulating plain.

One notable village in the Erzgebirge is Gottesgab, which sits at an altitude of 3345 ft., making it the highest place in central Germany and Bohemia. The Erzgebirge connects to the west via the Elstergebirge with the Fichtelgebirge, and further to the Bohmerwald through the Waldsassen plateau. To the east, the Erzgebirge is separated from the Elbsandsteingebirge by the Nollendorf pass. This pass serves as an ancient military route to Saxony and was utilized by Napoleon I following the battle of Dresden in 1813.

## Surrounding Frontiers
Beyond the southern and north-western regions, Bohemia's borders are defined by other geological formations:
* **North-East:** The frontier is formed by the Sudetes, which include the Jeschkengebirge (3322 ft.), the Lausitzergebirge (2500 ft.), the Isergebirge (featuring the 3683 ft. Tafelfichte peak), and the Riesengebirge (Czech *Kroknose*). The Riesengebirge contains the Schneekoppe, which reaches 5264 ft., making it one of the highest mountains in central Europe after the Alps. Other Sudetes groups in Bohemia include the Adlergebirge (3664 ft.) and the Heuscheuergebirge (2532 ft.).
* **South-East and East:** The region is bounded by Lower Austria, Moravia, and Upper Austria.
* **South-West:** The border is shared with Bavaria.
* **Eastern Plateau:** The Bohemian-Moravian Hills form a broad series of low hills or a plateau composed of primitive rocks, reaching altitudes of 2500 ft. in certain areas.
