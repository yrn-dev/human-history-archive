# The Story of King Abenner and Prince Josaphat

## Early Life and Prophecy
In the Greek account of the story, the people of India had been converted by St Thomas, and many had adopted an eremitic life following the origin of such practices in Egypt. During this time, a powerful pagan king named Abenner rose to power. Abenner harbored a deep hatred for Christians, specifically targeting ascetics for persecution.

After a long period of being childless, Abenner had a son of matchless beauty, whom he named Josaphat. In his joy, the king summoned astrologers to predict the child's destiny. While they foretold prosperity and glory exceeding that of all previous predecessors, the most learned sage among them provided a specific caveat. He intimated that Josaphat's glory would not be found in his father's kingdom, but in another that was infinitely more exalted. Furthermore, the sage predicted that the child would adopt the very faith that his father persecuted.

## The Seclusion of Josaphat
Troubled by the prophecy and observing a devout and thoughtful nature in his son, King Abenner attempted to prevent the prediction from coming true. He selected a secluded city and commissioned the construction of a splendid palace for the prince. To ensure Josaphat remained ignorant of the Christian religion and the "sorrows of humanity," the king implemented strict controls:
* The prince was attended only by servants and tutors who were in the flower of health and youth.
* No strangers were permitted access to the palace.
* Josaphat was shielded from any knowledge of death, old age, disease, or poverty.

The king's goal was to ensure the boy only experienced pleasant things, thereby removing any inducement for him to consider a future life.

## The Influence of Barlaam
Despite the king's precautions, Barlaam, an eremite of great knowledge and sanctity living in the wilderness of Sennaritis, received a divine warning. Disguising himself as a merchant, Barlaam traveled to India and gained access to Prince Josaphat. He imparted the Christian doctrine to the prince and commended the monastic life to him.

When suspicion arose regarding Barlaam's presence, the eremite departed. However, the prince's convictions remained unshaken. As a final effort to divert his son, King Abenner summoned a magician named Theudas. Theudas removed the prince's attendants and replaced them with seductive girls, but Josaphat resisted their blandishments through prayer.

## Conversion and Later Life
Recognizing the failure of his efforts, King Abenner abandoned his attempts to change his son's mind and associated Josaphat in the government. The prince utilized his power to promote religion, leading to prosperity in all his endeavors. Eventually, Abenner himself yielded to the faith; after several years of penitence, the king died.

Following these events, Josaphat surrendered the kingdom to a friend named Barachias and departed for the wilderness. After two years of buffeting by demons and a painful search, he located Barlaam. Barlaam subsequently died, and Josaphat lived as a hermit for many years. Later, King Barachias arrived and transferred the bodies of the two saints to India, where they became the source of many miracles.
