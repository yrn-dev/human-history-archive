# Bar-Hebraeus

Bar-Hebraeus, also known as Abu'l-Faraj, was a 13th-century maphrian or catholicus of the Jacobite (Monophysite) Church. Described by Dr. Wright as one of the most learned and versatile men ever produced by Syria, he was an industrious compiler of knowledge who collected the results of research in history, science, philosophy, and theology possible in Syria during his time. While most of his works were written in Syriac, he also wrote some in Arabic, a language that had supplanted Syriac as a living speech before his era.

## Life and Ecclesiastical Career
Born in 1226 at Mala[t.]iah on the upper Euphrates, Bar-Hebraeus was the son of a physician of Jewish descent. His youth coincided with the Mongol advance into western Asia, a period of troublous times. He completed his education in Antioch, where his father eventually retired.

His ecclesiastical ascent began in 1246 when he was ordained at Tripolis as the Jacobite bishop of G[=u]b[=a]s near Mala[t.]ia. A year later, he was transferred to the diocese of La[k.]abh[=i]n, and by 1253, he became the bishop of Aleppo. His tenure in Aleppo was interrupted when an ecclesiastical superior deposed him following disputes regarding the patriarchate; however, he was restored to his see in 1258. In 1264, Patriarch Ignatius III promoted him to the rank of maphrian, the position immediately below that of patriarch. Bar-Hebraeus held this office until his death at Mar[=a]gha in 1286. He was regarded as a model of devotion to his duties and earned respect across all parties within his diocese.

## Historical Works
Bar-Hebraeus is of primary interest to modern students as an historian. His major historical contribution is the Syriac *Chronicle*, which is divided into three parts:
* **The First Part:** A history of secular events spanning from the Creation to his own time. Its later sections provide valuable information on the history of western Asia and south-east Europe. Bar-Hebraeus also produced an Arabic compendium of this secular history titled *al-Mukhta[s.]ar fi'd-Duwal* (Compendious History of the Dynasties).
* **The Second Part:** A history of the Church focusing primarily on the patriarchate of Antioch.
* **The Third Part:** A history of the eastern branch of the Syrian Church.

The *Chronicle* is noted for pursuing the double series of the Maphrians of the Jacobites and the Nestorian Catholics. However, some sources suggest that Bar-Hebraeus, along with Eutychius, represents the credulity of the 12th and 10th centuries respectively.

## Other Writings and Scholarship
Though characterized as simple and uncritical in his thought and lacking in striking originality, Bar-Hebraeus produced numerous elaborate treatises. 

For theologians, the *Au[s.]ar R[=a]z[=e]* (Storehouse of Secrets) is of special value as a doctrinal and critical commentary on the text of the Scriptures. This work has been the subject of a biography and estimate of the author published by J. Goettsberger in 1900.

Other significant works include:
* **Philosophy and Logic:** *[H.][=e]wath H[=e]khm[)e]th[=a]* (Butter of Wisdom), which is an exposition of the philosophy of Aristotle, and *K[)e]th[=a]bh[=a] dhe-Bh[=a]bh[=a]th[=a]* (Book of the Pupils of the Eyes), a treatise on dialectics or logic.
* **Science:** *Sull[=a][k.][=a] Haun[=a]n[=a]y[=a]* (Ascent of the Mind), a work on cosmography and astronomy.
* **Language and Literature:** *K[)e]th[=a]bh[=a] dh[)e]-[S.]em[h.][=e]* (Book of Ravs), a treatise on grammar, as well as various poems and ethical works.
* **Miscellaneous:** Various medical works and the *K[)e]th[=a]bh[=a] dh[)e]-Thunn[=a]y[=e] M[)e]gha[h.][h.][)e]kh[=a]n[=e]* (Book of Entertaining Stories).
