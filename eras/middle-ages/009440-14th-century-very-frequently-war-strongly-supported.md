# Bologna in the 14th Century

## Political Affiliations and Conflict
From the 12th century through the 14th century, the city of Bologna was characterized by frequent warfare. During this period, the city provided strong support to the Guelph cause. This alignment placed Bologna in direct opposition to Frederick II, as well as the neighboring cities located in Emilia and Romagna. An example of this conflict occurred in 1249, when the Bolognese captured Enzio, the son of the emperor, and held him in confinement for the remainder of his life.

## Internal Struggle and Loss of Independence
The external conflicts were eventually mirrored by internal strife, as struggles between the Ghibellines and Guelphs broke out within Bologna itself. These internal divisions weakened the commune to such an extent that it lost its independence. In 1337, Taddeo de' Pepoli established himself as the master of the town. This period of local control ended in 1350 when Taddeo's son sold the city to Giovanni Visconti of Milan.

## Transitions of Power
The city's governance continued to shift throughout the latter half of the 14th century and into the following century. Ten years after the sale to the Visconti, Bologna was given to the papacy. However, the city soon revolted and recovered its liberty. This independence was short-lived; in 1401, Giovanni Bentivoglio became the lord of Bologna, though he was killed during a rebellion in 1402. Following this, the city returned to the Visconti. After further struggles with the papacy, the Bentivoglio family secured the city again in 1438, maintaining control until 1506, when Pope Julius II drove them out and returned Bologna to the sway of the papacy.

## Early History and Governance
Bologna's path to the 14th century involved various periods of rule. It was subject to the Lombards for a short time and generally remained under the rule of the exarchate of Ravenna until Pippin gave the exarchate to the papacy in 756. The city suffered a sacking by the Hungarians in 902. While the exact date it acquired its freedom and its motto, *Libertas*, is uncertain, the first constitution of the commune of Bologna dates from approximately 1123, marking its status as a free and independent city at that time.
