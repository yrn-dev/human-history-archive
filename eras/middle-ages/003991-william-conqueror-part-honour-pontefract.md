# William the Conqueror

## Land Grants and the Honour of Pontefract
William the Conqueror exercised significant authority over the distribution of land and titles following the Conquest. In the case of Barnsley, the Domesday survey records that Ilbert de Lacy held the area as a gift from William the Conqueror. This holding was established as part of the honour of Pontefract. The overlordship of this honour remained within the de Lacy family until the reign of Stephen, at which point Henry de Lacy granted it to the monks of Pontefract.

In other regions, William the Conqueror granted the manor of Bolton Abbey and the broader district of Craven to Robert de Romili. Although the Domesday survey contains no mention of this specific grant, Robert de Romili evidently held the land in 1086.

## Administrative and Ecclesiastical Appointments
The Conqueror's influence extended to the administration of royal rights and the establishment of regional power. In the county palatine of Durham, William the Conqueror granted royal rights to Bishop Walcher. This arrangement included Bedlington and its associated hamlets, which had been purchased by Cutheard, bishop of Durham, between 900 and 915.

In Dorset, the aftermath of the Conquest saw the rise of several prominent families who arrived with William the Conqueror. These included the families of Mautravers, Peverel, St Lo, Lovell, Maundeville, and Clavel. Additionally, representatives of the Arundel, Brewer, and Mohun families held large estates in the county following the Conquest.

## Ceremonies of Knighthood
Historical records regarding the knighting of Henry I provide insight into the ceremonies performed by William the Conqueror. While Lanfranc invested Henry I with arms, William "dubbed him to rider." The term "dub" is understood to mean "to strike," a usage that dates back to the time of this ceremony. This differs from the "accolade," which etymologically refers to an embrace accompanied by a blow with the hand.
