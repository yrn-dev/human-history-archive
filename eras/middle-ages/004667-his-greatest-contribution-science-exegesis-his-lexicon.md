# Bretschneider and the Lexicon Manuale Graeco-Latinum

The scholar Bretschneider is noted in the archive for his contributions to theology and the science of exegesis. His most significant contribution to the field of exegesis was the publication of the *Lexicon Manuale Graeco-Latinum in libros Novi Testamenti*, first published in 1824, with a third edition appearing in 1840.

## The Lexicon Manuale Graeco-Latinum
The *Lexicon Manuale Graeco-Latinum* is regarded as Bretschneider's greatest work in the science of exegesis. The value of this lexicon stems from the author's extensive use of various sources to illustrate the language of the New Testament. These sources included:
* The Greek of the Septuagint.
* The Old and New Testament Apocrypha.
* The writings of Josephus.
* The works of the apostolic fathers.

## Works on the Gospel of St John
In 1820, Bretschneider published a treatise concerning the gospel of St John titled *Probabilia de Evangelii el Epistolarum Joannis Apostoli indole et origine*. This work attracted significant attention because it discussed, with marked moderation, the arguments against the authorship of John, collecting these arguments with great fullness.

The publication of the *Probabilia* led to several replies and had personal professional consequences. The minister Detlev von Einsiedel (1773-1861) denounced Bretschneider as the "slanderer of John" (*Johannisschänder*). According to Bretschneider's own autobiography, this denunciation prevented his appointment as the successor to Karl C. Tittmann in Dresden.

Despite the initial reception of the work, Bretschneider later announced in the preface to the 1822 second edition of his *Dogmatik* that he had never actually doubted the authenticity of the gospel. He claimed that the *Probabilia* had been published specifically to draw attention to the subject and to encourage a more complete defense of the gospel's genuineness.

## Theological Position and Other Writings
Bretschneider produced numerous dogmatic writings, some of which were released in several editions. In 1826, he published *Apologie der neuern Theologie des evangelischen Deutschlands*. This work served as a response to Hugh James Rose, who had published a volume of sermons in England in 1825 titled *The State of the Protestant Religion in Germany*. Rose had classified Bretschneider as a rationalist; however, Bretschneider contended that he was not a rationalist in the ordinary sense, describing himself instead as a "rational supernaturalist."

His dogmatic position is characterized as intermediate. He stood between the extreme school of naturalists—which included figures such as Julius Wegscheider, J.F. Röhr, and Heinrich Paulus—and others such as F.C. Baur and D.F. Strauss. While Bretschneider recognized a supernatural element in the Bible, he also allowed for other perspectives. Additionally, an English translation of his *Manual of the Religion and History of the Christian Church* was published in 1857.
