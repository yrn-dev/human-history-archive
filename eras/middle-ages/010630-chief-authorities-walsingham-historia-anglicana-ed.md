# Thomas Walsingham and the Historia Anglicana

## Overview of the Historia Anglicana
The *Historia Anglicana* is a significant historical work authored by T. Walsingham. In the context of historical research, it is cited as one of the chief authorities for its subject matter. The work was edited by H.T. Riley and published in London between 1863 and 1864.

## Related Historical Authorities
The *Historia Anglicana* is frequently grouped with other primary sources and scholarly works used to study medieval English history. Other key authorities include:

## Distinction from Other Figures
It is necessary to distinguish the historian T. Walsingham from other individuals of the same name or associated locations mentioned in the archive:

### Sir Francis Walsingham
Sir Francis Walsingham was a different historical figure, born at Chislehurst. He is noted for his role as a government official who utilized spies to intercept the correspondence of Anthony Babington. Babington, a Roman Catholic conspirator, had entered into a correspondence with Mary, queen of Scots, as part of a plot to murder Queen Elizabeth and her ministers. Sir Francis Walsingham, along with the Spanish ambassador Mendoza, regarded this conspiracy as the most dangerous of recent years.

### John Baconthorpe
John Baconthorpe, a learned Carmelite monk known as "the Resolute Doctor," was brought up in the Carmelite monastery of Blakeney, which was located near Walsingham. Baconthorpe studied at Paris and Oxford, where he was known as the "Princeps" of the Averroists, and later served as the twelfth provincial of the English Carmelites in 1329.
