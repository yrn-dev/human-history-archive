# Pope Paul II and the Conflict with George of Podebrad

## Political Context in Bohemia
During the early and more prosperous period of his reign, King George of Podebrad, the only Hussite king of Bohemia, based his policy on a firm alliance with Matthias Corvinus, the king of Hungary. Through the influence of Matthias, George was crowned by the Romanist bishop of Waitzen. George's primary support came from the towns and the men of the smaller nobility.

However, over time, some Romanist nobles became hostile toward the king. Through their influence, George became involved in a protracted struggle with the papal see. This conflict hindered George's far-reaching ambitions, including a period where he attempted to obtain supremacy over Germany.

## The Break with Rome
After negotiations with Rome proved unsuccessful, George assembled the estates at Prague in 1452. During this assembly, he declared his readiness to risk his crown and his life in defense of his faith, stating that he would remain true to the communion in both kinds until his death.

This declaration further embittered the Romanist party in Bohemia. In 1465, many nobles of the Roman religion met at Zelena Hora (Grunberg) and formed a confederacy against King George.

## Intervention of Pope Paul II
In the year following the formation of the confederacy, Pope Paul II provided moral support to the confederates. He did this by pronouncing a sentence of excommunication against George of Podebrad and by releasing all Bohemians from their oath of allegiance to the king.

The influence of the papacy also extended to the king of Hungary. Matthias Corvinus deserted his former ally, George, and instead provided support to the lords of the league of Zelena Hora.

## Warfare and the Rise of Matthias Corvinus
The conflict between the two parties devolved into desultory warfare. While George was initially successful, the tide turned when the king of Hungary invaded Moravia and took possession of Brunn, the capital of that country.

On May 3, 1469, Matthias was proclaimed king of Bohemia during a meeting of the Catholic nobles of Moravia and Bohemia held at Olmutz in Moravia. Although George achieved some successes over his rival in the following year, the war was brought to a temporary halt by George's death in 1471. Despite the conflicts of his reign, George of Podebrad is remembered as one of the rulers of Bohemia whose memory is most endeared to his countrymen, alongside Charles IV.
