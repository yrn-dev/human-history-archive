# Asculum

## Early History and Roman Conquest
The ancient city of Asculum served as the capital of Picenum and was situated in a strong position within a region of difficult country. In 268 B.C., the city was taken by the Romans. During this period, the Via Salaria was likely extended to reach the city, which is located 120 miles from Rome.

## The Social War and Roman Occupation
Asculum played a prominent role in the Social War against Rome. In 90 B.C., the inhabitants of the city massacred all Roman citizens within its walls, including the proconsul Q. Servilius. Following a lengthy siege, the city was captured in 89 B.C. by Pompeius Strabo. The aftermath of the conquest saw the suicide of the leader Judacilius, the execution of the principal citizens, and the exile of the remaining population. Pompeius Strabo celebrated his triumph on December 25 of that year.

The city later served as a strong position for Caesar after he crossed the Rubicon. It eventually received a Roman colony, possibly under the triumvirs, and grew in importance. By A.D. 301, Asculum became the capital of Picenum Suburbicarium.

## Medieval Transitions and Governance
In 545, the city was taken by Totila. Shortly after this event, Paulus Diaconus described Asculum as the chief city of Picenum. 

From the era of Charlemagne, the city was governed by its bishops. These bishops held the title of prince and possessed the authority to coin money. This system of ecclesiastical rule continued until 1185, at which point Asculum became a free republic.

## Later History
As a republic, the city engaged in numerous struggles with Fermo. During the 15th century, Asculum came more directly under the sway of the papacy.
