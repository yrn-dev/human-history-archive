# The Gurjaras and Rashtrakutas (750-950)

## Origins and Rise of the Gurjaras
The Gurjaras, who are believed to be the ancestors of the Gujar caste, likely entered India in connection with the Hunnish invasions. They are thought to have entered the region alongside the White Huns. The Gurjaras established their power over Gujarat and, around 600, expanded their influence by overrunning north-eastern Kathiawar. During this period of expansion, they established a branch at Broach (585-740) and compelled the raja of Valabhi to become their tributary.

At its peak, the Gurjara empire extended its reach as far east as Bengal and as far south as the Nerbudda. While the empire eventually continued in a diminished form until A.D. 1040, its earlier trajectory was interrupted by the short-lived empire of Harsha (who died in 647 or 648), under whose sway Kathiawar, Gujarat, and Malwa fell.

## Regional Power Struggles
The geopolitical landscape of the region was characterized by shifting alliances and conflicts. In the southern Deccan, the Rashtrakutas and Chalukyas struggled for mastery. By the 7th century, the Chalukyas had absorbed smaller kingdoms into their empire after overcoming other rivals, including the Rashtrakutas.

External pressures also impacted these dynasties. Arab invaders from Sind entered India in 710-711 and established themselves in Sind by 712. Although they conducted various raids, they did not initially attempt serious attacks on the Chalukya and Gurjara empires. However, in 770, the Arabs destroyed the city of Valabhi, ending the dynasty established there by a chief named Bhatarka.

## The Balance of Power (750-950)
A significant shift in power occurred when the Rashtrakutas eventually succumbed to their ancient rivals, the Chalukyas, and succeeded to the bulk of their dominions. This acquisition included Gujarat, where the Rashtrakutas established a branch line.

For a period of approximately two centuries, between c. 750 and 950, a balance of power existed between the Gurjaras and the Rashtrakutas. During this era, neither kingdom possessed sufficient strength to encroach significantly upon the other. The Rashtrakutas' ability to pursue large-scale schemes of conquest was further limited by internal dissensions with their branch line.
