# THE GREAT CITY OF BAUDAS

Baudas is described as the noblest and greatest city in its region. It served as the seat of the Calif of all the Saracens in the world, a position of authority compared to that of the Pope of all the Christians in Rome.

## Geography and Trade
The city is situated on a very great river. This waterway serves as a vital commercial artery, allowing merchants to descend from Baudas to the Sea of India. The journey to the Sea of India takes approximately eighteen days, ending at a city called Kisi. Located on the river between Baudas and Kisi is another great city, Bastra, which is surrounded by woods known for producing the best dates in the world.

Baudas is a significant hub of commerce; goods are brought to the noble city of Tauris from various regions, including Baudas. The city is also renowned for its textile industry, specifically the weaving of gold brocades and various silk stuffs. These include tissues such as *nac*, *nasich*, and *cramoisy*, many of which are richly wrought with figures of birds and beasts.

## The Fall of the City
In the year of Christ 1255, the city was taken by storm by Alaü, the Lord of the Tartars of the Levant and brother to the reigning Great Kaan. The conquest was a massive enterprise, as Baudas was defended by more than 100,000 horse in addition to foot soldiers.

Upon capturing the city, Alaü discovered the tower of the Califs, which contained the greatest accumulation of gold, silver, and other treasure ever known in one spot. When Alaü questioned the Calif on why he had amassed such wealth instead of using it to pay knights and soldiers to defend his people and kingdom, the Calif remained silent. As a punishment for his love of treasure, Alaü imprisoned the Calif in the Treasure Tower and forbade him from receiving any meat or drink, commanding him to "eat of thy treasure." The Calif remained in the tower for four days before he died. Following this event, there has never been another Calif in Baudas or elsewhere.

## Religious Conflict and Miracles
The archive records a period of great tension between the Calif of Baudas and the Christian population. One Calif bore a profound hatred for Christians, spending his days and nights contemplating how to either force them to convert to his faith or have them all slain. He sought counsel on this matter from priests and devotees of his own faith, who shared this malice.

During this time of dismay, a miracle is attributed to the intervention of God on behalf of the Christians, involving the prayer of a one-eyed cobbler which caused a mountain to move.
