# Bridport

Bridport is a market town and municipal borough located in the Western parliamentary division of Dorsetshire, England, situated 18 miles northwest of Dorchester. The town is positioned in a hilly district on the river Brit, from which it derives its name. Its main section is approximately one mile from the sea, connected by a winding street that leads to a quay surrounded by the fishing village of West Bay.

## Early History and the Conquest
Before the Norman Conquest, Bridport was a location of evident importance, consisting of 120 houses rated for all the king's services and paying geld for five hides. However, the town was utterly ruined during the merciless harrying that followed the resistance Dorset offered to the Conqueror. By 1086, the number of houses had decreased to 100, and 20 of these were in such a wretched state that they were unable to pay geld. During this early period, Bridport served as a site for a mint.

## Administrative Development
Bridport is first identified as a borough in the Pipe Roll of 1189. This record notes that the men of the town owed tallage in the amount of 53s. 10d., and that William de Bendenges owed £9 10s. for the ancient farm of the town. 

In 1252-1253, Henry III granted the town its first charter, establishing it as a free borough. This charter allowed the burgesses to hold the town at the ancient fee farm with an increase of 40s. and permitted them to choose two bailiffs to answer for the farm at the exchequer. A deed from 1381 further indicates that Henry III granted the burgesses freedom from toll.

The town was incorporated by James I in 1619, though Charles II later granted a new charter in 1667, which governed the town until 1835. In terms of governance, the municipal borough is managed by a mayor, 18 councillors, and 6 aldermen.

## Industry and Commerce
Bridport has long been recognized for its maritime industries. As early as 1213, the town was noted for the manufacture of cables and ropes. An act of parliament (21 Henry VIII) confirms that the inhabitants had produced hawsers, ropes, and cables for most other ships and the royal navy "from time out of mind." The town's principal manufactures have historically included cordage, sailcloth, linen, and fishing-nets. In the neighborhood of Bridport and Beaminster, flax and hemp are also manufactured.

Regarding trade, Bridport is one of the chief ports of the region, though its harbour is accessible only to small vessels. The town engages in import trade for coal, timber, and flax. While Edward I is recorded in the *Quo Warranto* Rolls as possessing a market there, the first existing grant of fairs and a market is dated 1593.

## Parliamentary Representation
Bridport held representation in parliament with two members from 1395 until 1867. In 1867, the number of representatives was reduced to one, and the town was eventually disfranchised in 1885.

## Geography and Infrastructure
The town covers an area of 593 acres. Geologically, the outcrop of the Inferior Oolite runs onto the coast at Bridport, and the Midford sands are well exposed in the cliff between Bridport and Burton Brandstock.

The town is served by the Great Western railway, with the line terminating at West Bay. Local landmarks include the church of St Mary, which is a cruciform Perpendicular building. As of 1901, the population of the borough was 5,710.
