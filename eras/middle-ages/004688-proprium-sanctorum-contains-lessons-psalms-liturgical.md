# Proprium Sanctorum

The *Proprium Sanctorum* is a primary section of the Roman Breviary, the book containing the offices for the canonical hours of the daily service of the Roman Catholic Church. It consists of the special offices, including lessons, psalms, and liturgical formularies, designated for the festivals of saints.

## Nature and Content
The *Proprium Sanctorum* is organized according to the days of the secular month. Its content is primarily composed of hagiological biography, which serves a purpose for the worshipper in the field of church history similar to the role of Scripture readings in biblical history. While much of this material remains largely uncritical, some portions have been revised in light of other discoveries and archaeological evidence, as was done by Leo XIII.

The constituent parts of the offices within the *Proprium Sanctorum* include:
*   **Psalms:** These include canticles from the Old Testament (such as the prayer of Hezekiah, the Song of Hannah, the Song of Moses, and the prayer of Habakkuk) and the New Testament (the Benedictus, Nunc dimittis, and Magnificat).
*   **Lessons:** These are drawn from the Fathers of the Church, the Acts of the Saints, and the Bible.
*   **Liturgical Formularies:** These include antiphons (short forms used to introduce a psalm), responsories (replies following a psalm), hymns (short poems), versicles, and collects (short prayers that sum up the congregation's supplications and the dominant idea of the festival).

## Relationship with the Breviary Structure
The Roman Breviary is divided into four seasonal parts—autumn, summer, spring, and winter. Each of these parts contains the Psalter, the *Proprium de Tempore* (the special office of the season), the *Proprium Sanctorum*, the *Commune Sanctorum* (general offices for groups of saints such as martyrs, confessors, virgins, apostles, and the Blessed Virgin Mary), and Extra Services.

A significant liturgical challenge arises from the interaction between the *Proprium Sanctorum* and the *Proprium de Tempore*. Because approximately 90% of the days in a year have been allotted to a saint over the centuries, the *Proprium Sanctorum* has encroached upon the *Proprium de Tempore*. This conflict is only partially addressed by thirty-seven chapters of general rubrics. To assist in the proper reading and navigation of these overlapping offices, a guide called the *Ordo Recitandi Divini Officii* is published in various dioceses and countries.

## Impact on Liturgical Practice
The proliferation of saints' festivals has had a practical effect on the recitation of the Psalter. Because many of these festivals utilize the same special psalms, the practice often results in the constant repetition of about one-third of the Psalter, while the remaining two-thirds are recited rarely. In contrast, the *Proprium de Tempore* would provide equal opportunities for every psalm if it were adhered to.

Furthermore, the order of lessons for special days in the *Proprium Sanctorum* interrupts the normal order of ferial offices, which dislocates the scheme for consecutive reading.
