# The Introduction of Christianity and Early Rulers of Bohemia

## The Arrival of Christianity
The introduction of Christianity into Bohemia occurred in stages, with varying levels of success depending on the origin of the missionaries. Initial attempts to establish the faith came from Germany, but these efforts were largely unsuccessful. This failure is attributed to an innate distrust the Bohemians held toward the Germans, which made them unfavorable to a creed arriving from the realm of their western neighbors.

The situation changed when Christianity approached Bohemia from Moravia. In Moravia, the doctrine had been taught by Methodius and Cyrillus, who were Greek monks from Thessalonica. This shift in origin proved more effective; around the year 873, the Bohemian prince Borivoj was baptized by Methodius. Following this event, the Bohemian people rapidly adopted the Christian faith.

## Early Rulers and Territorial Expansion
During the period following the introduction of Christianity, one of the most prominent rulers of Bohemia was Wenceslas, known as "the Holy." Wenceslas served as an ally of Germany, but in 935, he was murdered by his brother, Boleslav. Wenceslas was later canonized by the Church of Rome.

Boleslav, who succeeded his brother, was generally successful in the ensuing war with Germany triggered by the murder of Wenceslas. Under the reigns of Boleslav and his son, Boleslav II, the frontiers of Bohemia expanded in several directions. Boleslav II established his rule over Moravia and Bohemia, as well as a large portion of Silesia and the part of Poland currently known as the Austrian province of Galicia.

## Dynastic Conflict and Foreign Influence
The expansive empire created by Boleslav II did not last. His son, Boleslav III, lost all foreign possessions to Boleslav the Great, the king of Poland. The reign of Boleslav III was marked by constant civil war resulting from dissensions between him and his brothers, Ulrick and Jaromir. Although Boleslav III managed to expel his brothers, his cruelty led the Bohemians to dethrone him.

In his place, the Bohemians chose the Polish prince Vladivoj as their ruler. Vladivoj was the son of the Bohemian princess Dubravka and the brother of Boleslav the Great. To secure his hold on power, Vladivoj sought assistance from Germany and agreed to continue paying tribute.

## Evolution of the Bohemian Title
The political status of Bohemian rulers evolved through their relationship with the Holy Roman Empire. In 1088, Vratislav obtained the title of king from Emperor Henry IV after assisting him in the contest about investitures against the papal see. This title was conferred only on Vratislav personally. Later, the German king Conrad III granted the title of hereditary cupbearer of the Empire to the Bohemian prince Sobeslav (1125-1140), which provided Bohemia with influence regarding the election of emperors.

In 1156, Emperor Frederick I Barbarossa granted the title of king to the Bohemian prince Vladislav II, along with the cession of Upper Lusatia, on the condition that Vladislav II participate in Frederick's Italian campaigns. While intended to be hereditary, the title fell into abeyance after Vladislav's abdication in 1173 due to struggles among the Premyslide princes.
