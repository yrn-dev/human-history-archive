# Francis of Assisi

St. Francis of Assisi was a religious figure who served as a precursor to the internal Reformation within the church. His efforts were directed toward restoring the power of the church by first restoring its goodness.

## The Franciscan Order and Influence
Francis of Assisi founded the Franciscan Order. The influence of this order extended into various religious communities and theological developments over several centuries. For example, the Congregation of Our Lady of Lourdes, founded in 1877 with headquarters in Rochester, Minnesota, consists of sisters of the third order of St. Francis.

The order also produced significant theologians, such as Saint Bonaventura (John of Fidanza). Born in 1221 in Tuscany, Bonaventura was destined for the church by his mother. He entered the Franciscan order in 1243 and later achieved high academic and ecclesiastical standing, becoming a doctor in 1255 and the general of the order in 1256. Bonaventura is said to have received his cognomen from St. Francis of Assisi, who performed a miraculous cure on him.

## Relationship with Contemporaries
Francis of Assisi is often compared to St. Dominic. Both figures were supported by Cardinal Ugolino, who later became Pope Gregory IX. While St. Dominic's work and character may not have received the same level of general recognition as those of St. Francis, some scholars, such as Professor Grutzmacher of Heidelberg, have noted that Dominic was not inferior to Francis in the earnestness with which he strove to carry out his ideal or in the purity of his intentions.
