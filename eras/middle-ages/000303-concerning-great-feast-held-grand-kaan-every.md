# The Birthday Feast of the Grand Kaan

The Grand Kaan observes a high festival annually to celebrate his birthday. This event is regarded as the greatest feast of the year at the Kaan's Court, with the sole exception of the festival held on New Year's Day.

## Date and Observance
The Great Kaan was born on the 28th day of the September moon, and the festivities are held on that date. The practice of keeping high festivals on birthdays is a general custom among the Tartars.

## Tributes and Petitions
The birthday feast serves as a significant occasion for the delivery of tributes and the seeking of favor. All Tartars throughout the world, as well as all governments and countries that owe allegiance to the Kaan, offer great presents. The amount of these offerings is determined by fixed prescriptions or orders, according to the ability of the giver.

In addition to official tributes, many other individuals arrive with great presents for the purpose of begging for employment from the Kaan. To manage these requests, the Great Kaan has appointed twelve Barons who are charged with assigning a suitable answer to each of these supplicants.

## Religious Observances
The birthday is marked by solemn religious activities from a diverse array of people. Christians, Saracens, Idolaters, and other descriptions of people perform great devotions. These observances include the lighting of lamps, the burning of incense, and much chaunting. Each group prays to the God they worship, requesting that the Emperor be saved and granted happiness, health, and long life.

## Court Protocol and Rituals
While the specific proceedings of the birthday feast are distinct, the general protocol for the Kaan's high festivals involves a strict hierarchy and ritualized adoration. On the morning of such feasts, before tables are set, a wide array of officials and professionals present themselves in the Great Hall. This group includes:
* Kings, Dukes, Marquesses, Counts, Barons, and Knights.
* Astrologers, Philosophers, and Leeches.
* Falconers and various other officials from the surrounding areas.

The company is marshalled by rank: first the Kaan's sons, nephews, and other Princes of the Blood Imperial, followed by Kings, then Dukes, and then others according to their degree. Those unable to fit inside the hall stand outside where the Emperor can see them.

The ritual begins when a great prelate commands the company to "Bow and adore!" The attendees bow until their foreheads touch the earth, repeating this adoration four times toward the Emperor as if he were a god. Following this, they approach a highly decorated altar featuring a gold censer and a vermilion tablet inscribed with the name of the Grand Kaan, which they incense with great reverence before returning to their seats.

## Related Festivities and Traditions
The birthday feast is part of a broader culture of celebration at the Kaan's court. Other major events include the New Year's festival in February, also known as the White Feast. Additionally, the Kaan holds great festivals where twelve thousand Barons receive thirteen changes of robes made of cloth of gold.

Dining at high feasts is characterized by specific fashions of the table, including a buffet of liquors and the presence of jugglers at the banquet. It was also a common practice in that age to introduce artistes into the dining hall after dinner.
