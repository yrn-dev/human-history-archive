# Azamgarh District

## Geography and Topography
The district covers an area of 2207 sq. m. Its boundaries are defined by the river Gogra to the north, which separates the district from Gorakhpur; Ghazipur district and the river Ganges to the east; the districts of Ghazipur and Jaunpur to the south; and Fyzabad and Jaunpur to the west.

The terrain varies by location. The area situated along the banks of the Gogra consists of a low-lying tract of varying width, while the ground to the south of this area rises slightly. The land slopes from the north-west toward the south-east, although general drainage is considered very inadequate. The district is characterized by a series of parallel ridges with summits that are depressed into hollows or beds where rivers flow. Between these ridges are numerous natural reservoirs and low-lying rice lands.

## Agriculture and Industry
The soil in the district is fertile and highly cultivated. It produces magnificent crops of indigo, sugar-cane, and rice. Due to the production of indigo, there are several indigo factories located within the district.

## Population and Infrastructure
As of 1901, the population of the district was 1,529,785, which represented an 11% decrease over the preceding decade. In terms of transportation, a branch of the Bengal & North-Western railway was opened to Azamgarh town in 1898.

## History
The district was ceded to the Company in 1801 by the wazirs of Lucknow. In 1857, the area became a center of mutiny. On June 3, 1857, the 17th Regiment of Native Infantry mutinied at Azamgarh; during this event, they murdered several of their officers and transported the government treasure to Fyzabad. The district subsequently became a site of conflict between rebels and the Gurkhas. The area was not finally cleared until October 1858 by Colonel Kelly.
