# The 12th and 13th Centuries

The 12th and 13th centuries were characterized by significant developments in art, linguistics, architecture, and social structures across Europe and the Near East. This period saw the refinement of book production, the evolution of regional governance, and the emergence of specific architectural styles.

## Book Arts and Illumination
During the 12th and 13th centuries, the art of book ornamentation underwent a transition toward more conventional and minute styles. By the end of the 12th century and the start of the 13th, the application of gold shifted from a liquid state to the use of highly burnished gold leaf, which created a brilliant effect for miniatures and initials. Volumes became smaller, featuring close-packed characters and a preference for small, decorated elements over large, bold styles.

The 13th century established the "historiated" or miniature initial—an initial letter containing a miniature illustration of the text's subject—as a popular ornamental detail, particularly in the hundreds of small Bibles produced during this time. Additionally, the 13th century marked the beginning of the development of the border. Starting as a pendant from the initial with a bud or cusp terminal, these borders grew along the margins into foliage, eventually enveloping entire pages in frameworks that reflected national styles.

In England, binders during the 12th and 13th centuries excelled in "blind" stamping on leather bindings, a technique using intaglio-cut metal stamps to create cameo-form impressions without gold leaf. This art was perfected in locations such as London, Cambridge, Oxford, and Durham. A notable example from the 12th century is the binding of the Winchester Domesday Book.

## Language and Biblical Translation
In 12th-century England, the gospels were copied into the Kentish dialect. However, the 13th century is described as an "absolute blank" regarding Biblical renderings into the vernacular English. During this time, the Anglo-Norman dialect reigned supreme in the palace of the king, parliament, schools, the courts of law, and among the upper classes. English was relegated to the lower clergy, serfs, villeins, and those in hovels, farms, and outlying country districts.

Biblical translations during the 12th, 13th, and early 14th centuries were conducted in French. Some were produced in England, while others were imported and copied. One such work was the illuminated Norman Commentary on the Apocalypse, some early copies of which were written in an English hand. By the middle of the 14th century, the majority of the New Testament and the entire Old Testament had been translated into the Anglo-Norman dialect.

## Architecture and Regional Sites
Architectural developments of the 12th and 13th centuries are evident in several European regions:

*   **France:** In Auxerre, the church of St. Eusebe dates to the 12th, 13th, and 16th centuries. The church of St. Etienne, a majestic Gothic building, dates from the 13th to the 16th centuries, while the abbey church of St. Germain was built in the 13th and 14th centuries. Additionally, the ancient episcopal palace in Auxerre preserves a Romanesque gallery from the 12th century, and the choir of St. Etienne contains a 12th-century crypt with mural paintings.
*   **Corsica:** The ruins of a 12th-century cathedral stand near St. Florent in the vanished town of Nebbio. In Murato, there is a church of Pisan architecture dating to the 12th or 13th century.

## Social and Political Structures
In Scotland, the Earldom of Mar underwent a transition in the 12th century when the role of the *mormaer* (a Celtic ruler) was replaced by an earl. While a definite succession of earls does not appear until the 13th century, by the middle of that century, the earls of Mar were recognized as among "the seven earls of Scotland" and held a position of great importance.

Regarding the "age of chivalry," some writers place its golden age in the early 13th century, though others suggest it occurred two or three generations earlier. While 12th-century romances present a favorable view of chivalry, historical evidence suggests that the unselfish championship of "the ladies" and "God" was a rare exception during the middle ages.

## Other Cultural Artifacts and Scholarship
The 12th and 13th centuries also saw contributions in scholarship and textile arts:
*   **Scholarship:** In Narbonne, the Kimhi (or Qimhi) family—three biblical scholars and grammarians—worked during the 12th century and the beginning of the 13th, exerting significant influence on the study of the Hebrew language.
*   **Textiles:** A piece of stuff, 9 inches long, featuring red silk and gold thread with an ogival framing of parrots and a Persian (or Sassanian) leaf-shaped fruit device, is attributed to Rhenish-Byzantine manufacture in the 12th or 13th century.
*   **Oriental Art:** In Persian and Arabic manuscripts from the 13th to 16th centuries, there are examples of intricate, colorful, and gold-resplendent title pages and ornaments.
