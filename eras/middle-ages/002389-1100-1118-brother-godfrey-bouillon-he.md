# Baldwin I

Baldwin I (1100–1118) was the first king of Jerusalem and previously served as the prince of Edessa from 1098 to 1100. He was the brother of Godfrey of Bouillon.

## Early Life and the First Crusade
Originally a clerk in orders who held several prebends, Baldwin joined the first crusade in 1096. He accompanied his brother, Godfrey, as far as Heraclea in Asia Minor. While at Heraclea, Baldwin followed Tancred into Cilicia, driven in part by political motives and partly by jealousy. In September 1097, Baldwin wrested Tarsus from Tancred and established his own garrison there.

## Principality of Edessa
After rejoining the main crusader army at Marash, Baldwin moved east toward the Euphrates following an invitation from an Armenian named Pakrad, subsequently occupying Tell-bashir. He later received another invitation from Thoros of Edessa. Baldwin first arrived in Edessa as a protector, but became Thoros's successor in March 1098 following the latter's assassination.

For two years, Baldwin ruled Edessa, during which time he married an Armenian wife and acted as a general intermediary between the Armenians and the crusaders. He successfully maintained his position against a conspiracy of his own subjects in 1098 and against surrounding Mahommedan powers, from whom he won Seruj (Sarorgia) and Samosata. Although he visited Jerusalem with Bohemund I at the end of 1099, he returned to Edessa in January 1100.

## Kingship of Jerusalem
Following the death of his brother Godfrey, Baldwin was summoned to Jerusalem by a party seeking a successor. This summons was the result of a lay reaction against the theocratic pretensions of Dagobert, who had been relying on Norman support. Leveraging this reaction, Baldwin became the first king of Jerusalem and was crowned by the patriarch on Christmas Day, 1100.

Baldwin's early reign was marked by a struggle between church and state. In the spring of 1101, he had Dagobert suspended via a papal legate. Later that year, the two men disagreed over the amount the patriarch should contribute toward the defense of the Holy Land. This conflict concluded in 1102 with the deposition of Dagobert and the triumph of Baldwin.
