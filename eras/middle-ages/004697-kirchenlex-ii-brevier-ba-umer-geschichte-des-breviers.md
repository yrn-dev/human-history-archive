# Breviary

The Breviary is a liturgical book containing services that must be performed by specific members of the religious community. Its use is mandatory for every member of a religious order and every clerk in orders.

## Practice and Requirements
The requirements for using the Breviary involve either publicly joining in the services or reading them aloud privately. Reading aloud is defined as using the lips in conjunction with the eyes; when performed in this manner, the daily allotted services take approximately two hours to complete.

In the context of large churches, these services are typically organized into groups throughout the day:
* **Matins and Lauds:** Usually held around 7:30 A.M.
* **Prime, Terce (High Mass), Sext, and None:** Usually held around 10 A.M.
* **Vespers and Compline:** Held at 4 P.M.

Depending on the number of high masses and the amount of music involved, members may spend between four and eight hours in the choir. While the Breviary is a central requirement for clergy and religious orders, laymen do not utilize it as a manual of devotion to any significant extent.

## Translations and Editions
The Roman Breviary has been translated into several languages, including German, French, and English. The English translation was produced by the marquess of Bute in 1879, with a new edition released in 1908 that included a translation of the Martyrology. The English version is particularly noted for including skillful renderings of ancient hymns provided by J.M. Neale, J.H. Newman, and others.
