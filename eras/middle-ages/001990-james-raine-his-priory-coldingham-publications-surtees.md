# James Raine and the Priory of Coldingham

The *Priory of Coldingham* is a publication by James Raine, appearing as volume xii of the Publications of the Surtees Society. This work is notable for containing a printed fragment of Bagimond's Roll.

## Bagimond's Roll and Ecclesiastical Taxation
The material printed by James Raine in the *Priory of Coldingham* concerns the valuation of church revenues in Scotland. The origins of this data date back to 1274, when the council of Lyons imposed a tax consisting of a tenth part of all church revenues for a period of six years to provide relief for the Holy Land. Pope Gregory X entrusted the collection of this tax in Scotland to Master Boiamund de Vitia (commonly known as Bagimund), a canon of Asti.

Bagimund sought to innovate the assessment process by basing the tax on the true value of the benefices at the time of assessment, rather than relying on old conventional valuations. This proposal met with objection from the Scottish clergy. In August 1275, a council was held at Perth, and the clergy successfully persuaded Bagimund to return to Rome to convince the pope to revert to the older method of taxation. However, the pope insisted that the tax be collected according to the true value, and Bagimund returned to Scotland to oversee the process.

## The Fragment in the Priory of Coldingham
The fragment of Bagimond's Roll preserved at Durham and printed by James Raine is considered to be in a form very similar to the original. The document is organized into columns: one listing the real values of the benefices and another listing the tenth parts. Specifically, this fragment provides data for the benefices within the archdeaconry of Lothian.

While the roll originated with Bagimund's efforts, the actual taxation referenced in this specific fragment was not the tenth collected by Bagimund himself. Instead, it refers to a tenth of all ecclesiastical property in Ireland, Wales, England, and Scotland granted to Edward I of England by Pope Nicholas IV in 1288. Because of this, the fragment is regarded as supplementary to the *Taxatio Ecclesiastica Angliae et Walliae*, which was printed by the Record Commissioners in 1802.

## Historical Legacy of the Roll
Although no contemporary copy of Bagimond's Roll is known to exist, the original roll served as the basis for ecclesiastical taxation for several centuries. Its influence extended into the 16th century, as evidenced by at least three documents that provide particulars of the taxation of the Church of Scotland based upon the original roll.

## Other Works by James Raine
In addition to his contributions to the Surtees Society, James Raine authored *History and Antiquities of North Durham*, published in London in 1852.
