# Bohemund I

Bohemund I (c. A.D. 1058–1111) was the prince of Antioch and previously the prince of Otranto. His first name was Marc. He was the eldest son of Robert Guiscard, the *dux Apuliae et Calabriae*, born from an early marriage that took place before 1059.

## Early Military Career and Conflict with Alexius
Bohemund served under his father during a major attack on the East Roman empire between 1080 and 1085. During the period of 1082–1084, while Guiscard was absent, Bohemund commanded the Normans. In this capacity, he penetrated into Thessaly as far as Larissa, though he was eventually repulsed by Alexius Comnenus. This early hostility toward Alexius significantly influenced Bohemund's future career and helped shape the history of the First Crusade.

## Succession and Dispute with Roger
Following the death of Robert Guiscard in 1085, a conflict arose between Bohemund and his younger brother, Roger. Roger had been born "in the purple" to a Lombard princess named Sicelgaeta and succeeded to the duchy of Calabria and Apulia. Bohemund, whom his father had intended for the throne of Constantinople, entered into a war with Duke Roger. This conflict was eventually resolved through the mediation of Urban II, which resulted in Bohemund being awarded other possessions as well as Otranto.

## The First Crusade
In 1096, while Bohemund and his uncle, the count of Sicily, were attacking Amalfi (which had revolted against Duke Roger), bands of crusaders began passing through Italy toward Constantinople. Bohemund was seized by the zeal of the crusade; it is also suggested that he viewed the First Crusade as an opportunity to realize his father's policy of the *Drang nach Osten* and hoped to establish an eastern principality for himself.

Bohemund assembled a Norman army, described as perhaps the finest division within the crusading host. He crossed the Adriatic and followed the route to Constantinople that he had previously attempted in 1082–1084. Upon his arrival in April 1097, he maintained a "correct" attitude toward Alexius and performed homage to the emperor. While Bohemund may have negotiated with Alexius for a principality at Antioch, he received little encouragement.

From the time of his arrival in Constantinople until the reach of Antioch, Bohemund served as the real leader of the First Crusade. His leadership is credited as a primary reason the First Crusade succeeded in crossing Asia Minor.
