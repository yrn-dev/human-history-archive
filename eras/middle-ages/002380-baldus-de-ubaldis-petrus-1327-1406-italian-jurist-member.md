# BALDUS DE UBALDIS, PETRUS (1327-1406)

Petrus Baldus de Ubaldis was a prominent Italian jurist born in Perugia in 1327. A member of the noble Baldeschi (Ubaldi) family, he achieved significant academic success at a young age, being admitted to the degree of doctor of civil law when he was seventeen. His early legal education in civil law took place in Perugia under the tutelage of Bartolus, while his studies in canon law are said to have been conducted under the master Federicus Petrucius of Siena.

## Academic Career and Teaching
Following the attainment of his doctorate, Baldus moved to Bologna, where he served as a teacher of law for three years. He subsequently returned to Perugia to take up a professorship, a position he held for thirty-three years. His teaching career further extended to several other universities, including Padua, Pavia, Florence, and Pisa. During this era, the law schools at these institutions were considered rivals to the school of Bologna for preeminence. Baldus eventually died in Pavia on April 28, 1406.

## Professional Influence and Consultations
Baldus acquired a great reputation among his contemporaries, which was attributed in part to his active involvement in public affairs and in part to the fame of his legal consultations. Five volumes of these consultations were published in Frankfort in 1589. 

His professional expertise was sought by high-ranking ecclesiastical figures. In 1380, Pope Urban VI summoned Baldus to Rome to provide assistance through his consultations in a dispute against the anti-pope Clement VII.

## Pupils and Legacy
Baldus served as a master to several notable figures. Among his pupils were Cardinal de Zabarella and Paulus Castrensis. The latter, an Italian jurist of the 14th century, studied under Baldus at Perugia and was a fellow-pupil of Cardinal Zabarella. Additionally, Baldus was the master of Pierre Roger de Beaufort, who later became Pope Gregory XI.

Regarding his written legacy, Baldus's *Commentary on the Liber Feudorum* is regarded as one of his finest works. However, many of his other writings were unfortunately left in an incomplete state. Some assessments suggest that his extant works do not entirely support the immense reputation he held during his lifetime.

## Family and Legal Theory
Baldus was part of a family of distinguished legal scholars; his brothers, Petrus (1335-1400) and Angelus (1328-1407), were jurists of almost equal eminence.

In the realm of legal theory, Baldus is noted for his differing views from Bartolus. In one instance regarding a "simple and naked consciousness" without signs of concurrence, Baldus expressed a view so contrary to that of Bartolus that it was later remarked by the writer Heineccius that, for this opinion, Baldus was "now roasting in hell." Despite this, Heineccius noted that while he approved of Bartolus's theory, in practice he inclined toward the sentiments of Baldus.
