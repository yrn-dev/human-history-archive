# Douglas Cockerell

## Overview and Professional Standing
Mr. Douglas Cockerell is a practitioner of bookbinding associated with a small class of art binders located in major cities such as New York, Paris, and London. Unlike large trade binders who utilize machinery to produce high volumes of work not intended for long-term durability, art binders like Cockerell operate according to principles of first-class work that have been in continuous use since approximately the 5th century.

## Influence and Training
The modern school of design for gold-tooling on book-covers was founded by Mr. T.J. Cobden-Sanderson, whose work was characterized by a level of individuality and pre-eminence that attracted numerous imitators. Douglas Cockerell was a pupil of Cobden-Sanderson and is regarded as one of his most successful students. 

The broader revival of this art can be traced back to the initial impetus provided by William Morris. While Morris may not have fully grasped the specific possibilities or limitations of gold-tooling on leather, his genius established the spirit in which these designs were to be conceived, creating beautiful designs executed in both blind and gold.

## Artistic Style and Works
The work of Douglas Cockerell is distinguished by a scholarly appreciation of ancient methods combined with a marked originality of treatment. An example of his craftsmanship is the "Catalogue of the Pictures at Hagley Hall," which is bound in red niger morocco and features gold tooling.

## Context of the Art Binding Field
Cockerell operates within a specialized field that includes other notable artists. Mr. Alfred de Sauty developed an admirable style using delicate pointille work combined with inlaid leathers. Additionally, the field has become a lucrative and fitting area for women artists in America and England. Notable figures include Miss Sarah Prideaux, who is recognized for her original work and her contributions to literature on the subject, and Miss E.M. MacColl. Miss MacColl is credited with inventing the small curved gold line produced via a tiny wheel; her work, such as the binding for "Walton's Compleat Angler," features graceful lines and curves designed by her brother, Mr. D.S. MacColl. Another artist, Miss Joanna Birkenruth, utilizes jewels cut *en c* to recall decorative medieval bindings.
