# Ashby-de-la-Zouch

Ashby-de-la-Zouch is a location that was known previously as Essebi or Ashby. At the time of the Domesday survey, it was part of the estates belonging to Hugh de Grentmaisnel. Ownership later passed to Robert Beaumeis, and subsequently, through female descent, to the family of la Zouch, from whom the town received the adjunct to its name.

## Market Rights and Economic History
The earliest record concerning the grant of market rights for the area dates to 1219. During this time, Roger la Zouch was granted a two days' fair at the feast of St Helen, as well as a weekly market, in exchange for a fine of one palfrey. By the 18th century, Ashby had become celebrated as one of the best markets for horses in England. The town also hosted prosperous factories producing hats as well as cotton and woollen stockings.

## The Manor and Castle
In the 15th century, the manor was held by James Butler, the earl of Ormond. Following his attainder, the manor was granted to Lord Hastings in 1461. In 1474, Lord Hastings obtained a royal licence to build and fortify a castle and to empark 3000 acres.

This castle served as a place of detention in 1569 for Mary, queen of Scots, who was held there under the custody of the earls of Shrewsbury and Huntingdon. 

## Military Use and Dismantling
The castle saw further military utility during the Civil War. It was held and fortified for the king by Colonel Henry Hastings, and the site was visited by Charles in 1645. Following the conclusion of the war in 1648, the castle was dismantled by order of parliament.

## Cultural Significance
The location plays a great part in the work *Ivanhoe* by Sir Walter Scott.
