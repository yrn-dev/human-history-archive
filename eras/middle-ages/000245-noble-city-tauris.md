# THE NOBLE CITY OF TAURIS

Tauris, also known as Tabriz or Torissi, is described as a great and noble city located in a province called Yrac. It serves as a primary commercial hub and emporium on the trade routes connecting Europe to Persia and the Far East.

## Commerce and Economy
The city is characterized by its high level of prosperity, particularly for merchants who realize large profits there. The inhabitants of Tauris support themselves through handicrafts and trade, specifically weaving valuable and beautiful stuffs made of gold and silk. 

Due to its strategic position, Tauris attracts merchandise from various regions, including India, Baudas, and Cremesor (identified as Garmsir, a hot region on the northeast side of the Persian Gulf). This influx of goods makes the city a significant market for precious stones and attracts many Latin merchants, specifically those from Genoa, who visit to transact business and purchase goods.

## Population and Society
The population of Tauris is described as a medley of different classes. This diverse community includes Persians, Georgians, Armenians, Jacobites, and Nestorians. The native inhabitants of the city, who are worshippers of Mahommet, are known as Taurizi (or Tabrizís). These natives are characterized in the archive's sources as an evil generation, with further accounts noting their vanity, insolence, and faithlessness. Despite the city's commercial wealth, the people themselves are described as poor creatures.

## Geography and Surroundings
Tauris is encircled by charming gardens that produce a wide variety of large and excellent fruits, which are noted for being abundant and cheap. In terms of regional travel, the journey from Tauris to Persia takes twelve days, while Ispahan is located twenty-four days' journey from the city, with Casbin situated halfway between them. On the borders of Tauris lies the Monastery of Saint Barsamo.

## Historical Development
The importance of Tauris grew over time. While it was not as significant when Marco Polo first started for the East, it reached a high degree of prosperity during the return journey of the Polos. Evidence from a will dated December 10, 1264, suggests that Venetian presence in the city began with pioneers like Pietro Viglioni. 

The city's rise to becoming a great market for foreign and Genoese merchants occurred under Arghún Khan (1284–1291). Its prosperity further increased under Gházán, who built a new city. The destruction of Baghdad by Hulákú contributed to Tauris becoming the great political and commercial city of Asia, diverting the route of Indian products toward the Euxine. While often reckoned to belong to Armenia, it is properly the chief city of Azerbaiján.
