# The Kingdom of Jerusalem and the Reign of Baldwin I

## Territorial Expansion and Strategic Alliances
Baldwin I, the Latin king of Jerusalem from 1100 to 1118, worked to transform the poor and straggling territories he inherited into a compact kingdom. To achieve this, he secured the supremacy of lay power in Jerusalem and formed strategic alliances with Italian trading towns. Genoa, in particular, provided the necessary skill and instruments for siege warfare in exchange for the concession of a quarter in the towns that were captured.

Through these means, the coast towns of Palestine were successively reduced. Arsuf and Caesarea were captured in 1101, followed by Acre in 1104, and later Beirut and Sidon in 1110. The capture of Sidon was achieved with the assistance of Norwegians and Venetians. According to the *Heimskringla*, Sigurd, one of the sons of King Magnus of Norway, journeyed to Jerusalem and conquered many heathen cities, including Sidon, and received presents from Baldwin.

## Military Conflicts and Southern Campaigns
Baldwin I faced repeated attacks from the Egyptians in 1102, 1103, and 1105, all of which he repelled. In the final years of his reign, between 1115 and 1118, he shifted to an offensive strategy, pushing southward at the expense of Egypt. During this period, he penetrated as far as the Red Sea and established an outpost at Monreal.

Baldwin's reign ended in 1118 following an expedition to Egypt. During this campaign, he captured Farama. According to the historian Fuller, Baldwin died after eating fish caught during this expedition.

## Relations with Northern Principalities
In the north, Baldwin I acted as a practical, though largely nominal, suzerain over three Christian principalities: Tripoli, Antioch, and Edessa. Between 1109 and 1110, he was required to resolve dissensions among the Christian princes in these regions. He also provided assistance to these principalities to help them maintain their positions against the amirs of Mosul, specifically Aksunk-ur and Maudud, who were Mahommedan princes of North East Syria.

## The County of Edessa and Baldwin II
Baldwin II, originally known as Baldwin de Burg, served as the count of Edessa from 1100 to 1118 before becoming king of Jerusalem. A nephew of Baldwin I and Godfrey of Bouillon, he had participated in the first crusade at Constantinople and helped occupy Bethlehem in June 1099. After a period of service with Bohemund at Antioch, he was appointed count of Edessa by Baldwin I.

Baldwin II conducted frequent forays against Mahommedan princes. In 1104, during a large foray with Bohemund, he was captured at Balich. While he was in captivity, Tancred served as the guardian of Edessa. Baldwin II recovered his liberty at the start of 1108 and regained his principality in September 1108 after a struggle with Tancred, which was eventually composed in 1109.

Between 1109 and 1118, Baldwin II faced numerous attacks from Mosul and Mardin. He was besieged in Edessa in 1110 and was relieved by Baldwin I. In 1114, he repelled an attack by Aksunkur of Mosul, and in 1115, he helped defeat Aksunkur at Danith. Records from Matthew of Edessa also suggest that Baldwin II plundered wealthy Armenians. Upon receiving news of Baldwin I's death in 1118 while traveling to Jerusalem for Easter, Baldwin II was made king, largely due to the influence of Patriarch Arnulf.

## Contemporary Accounts and Observations
The conditions of the Holy Land during the reign of Baldwin I are documented in the writings of Daniel of Kiev, a Russian travel-writer and abbot who visited the region around 1106-1107. Daniel's accounts describe the friendly relations between the Eastern and Roman churches in Syria, though he also noted that Saracens raided almost up to the walls of Christian Jerusalem. Daniel claims to have accompanied Baldwin I on an expedition against Damascus around 1107, noting that the king treated him with marked friendliness.
