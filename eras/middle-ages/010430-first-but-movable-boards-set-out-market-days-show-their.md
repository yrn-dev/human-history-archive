# Movable Structures and Goods

## The Evolution of the Booth
The term "booth" is derived from a Teutonic root meaning "to dwell," which also gave rise to the word "bower." Originally, a booth was a temporary dwelling constructed from boughs or other slight materials. Over time, the meaning evolved to describe non-permanent erections used for commerce, such as market stalls or tents at fairs where goods were sold. In later periods, the term was applied to polling-booths, which are temporary structures used for registering votes.

In Scotland, the term *bothy* or *bothie* generally refers to a cottage or hut. Specifically, it described a barrack-like room on large farms used to lodge unmarried labourers, a practice known as the "Bothy system" that was formerly common in northern Scotland and Aberdeenshire.

The transition from temporary to permanent commercial structures is illustrated by the history of Old Fish Street in London. According to Stow, the houses there began as movable boards set out on market days to display fish for sale. After obtaining licenses to erect sheds, these structures grew into shops and eventually developed into tall houses.

## Movable Cities and Tabernacles
Historical accounts describe the tabernacle of the Israelites (Bani Israel) as a structure that functioned like a city that could be either fixed or movable. When the tabernacle was set up in the midst of their camp, three tribes pitched their tents on each side, with roads cut through the tents. This arrangement resembled a well-appointed market where all sorts of artificers worked in shops and everything was ready for sale in due order.

The placement of people around the tabernacle was hierarchical, with the priests occupying the first places, followed by the Levites. The Levites consisted of 23,880 males, reckoned from thirty days old. The community remained in one place as long as a cloud stood over the tabernacle, believing God inhabited there, and journeyed only when the cloud removed.

## Movable Mechanical Components
In various technical applications, "movable" refers to specific mechanical parts designed for function:

*   **Smiths' Bellows:** In later forms of smiths' bellows, circular boards are used. This system includes a fixed middle board and two movable boards (one uppermost and one lowest). The lowest movable board is raised by a hand lever via a connecting rod or chain and lowered by a weight.
*   **Bee-hives:** The traditional straw skep, a dome-shaped domicile, featured fixed combs. The development of the movable-frame hive appeared in 1789 with Huber's leaf-hive, which opened like the leaves of a book.
*   **Furnaces:** Destructor furnaces have employed various patent movable fire-bars, including those designed by Settle, Vicar, Riddle (rocking bars), Horsfall (self-feeding apparatus), and Healey. However, experience suggests that simple stationary fire-bars are preferable to complicated movable arrangements.

## Movable Goods and Property
The term "movable goods" appears in historical contexts regarding transport and inheritance:

*   **Transport:** In 1015 A.D., Earl Svein, while at the merchant town of Steinker in the Throndhjem fjord, ordered his people's clothes, meat, drink, and all other movable goods to be loaded onto his long-ship to escape King Olaf.
*   **Inheritance:** In certain lands, it was established that upon the death of a rich man, his movable goods were divided between his heirs and the deceased. A portion (ranging from a half to a third, or less) was buried in the earth, under a mound, or in a house built over the goods.

## Markets and Fairs
Markets have historically been granted as privileges. In the manor of Loughborough, William the Conqueror granted the land to Hugh Lupus, and later, in 1226-1227, Hugh Despenser obtained privileges including a fair on the vigil, day, and morrow of St Peter ad vincula, as well as a market every Thursday. The town eventually purchased these market rights in 1880 from the trustees of Thomas Cradock.

Other historical accounts mention markets held at a king's pier or the preparation of a market during military conflicts, such as the encounter between the Athenians and Syracusans, where the preparation of a market was used as a tactical maneuver.
