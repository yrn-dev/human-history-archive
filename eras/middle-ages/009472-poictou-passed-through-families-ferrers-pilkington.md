# Roger de Poictou and Associated Manors

## Grants of William I
Roger de Poictou was a recipient of land grants from William I. Among these grants were the manor of Bolton and another manor whose earliest orthography is recorded as Estun. 

The manor of Bolton, which appeared in early forms as Bodleton or Botheltun, was granted to Roger de Poictou by William I. Later variations of the name include Bodeltown, Boltune, Bowelton, Bolton-le-Moors, Bolton-super-Moras, and Botheltun-le-Moors. The manor of Estun, whose name is derived from the Saxon *aesc* (ash) and *tun* (enclosed place), was also granted to Roger de Poictou by William I. This location was noted in ancient deeds as *subtus lineam* due to its position below the boundary of Cheshire, which served as the frontier between the kingdoms of Mercia and Northumbria.

## Succession of the Bolton Manor
Following the initial grant to Roger de Poictou, the manor of Bolton passed through the families of Ferrers and Pilkington. It eventually came into the possession of the Harringtons of Hornby Castle. However, the Harringtons lost the manor, along with their other estates, due to their adherence to Richard III. In 1485, Henry VII granted the manor to the first earl of Derby. While the manor is currently held by various lords, the earls of Derby retain a fourth part. Additionally, the manor of Little Bolton remained distinct from Great Bolton at least from the reign of Henry III and was held by the Boltons or Botheltons until the 17th century.

## Succession of the Estun Manor
The manor of Estun transitioned from Roger de Poictou to the Greslets before the end of William I's reign, becoming part of the barony of Manchester. From 1335 to 1515, it was held by the Asshetons. In 1515, the manor passed by marriage to the Booths of Dunham Massey. The current holder is the earl of Stamford, who is the representative of the Booth family.

## The Ferrers Family and the Earldom of Derby
The Ferrers family held significant influence and land in Derbyshire. Henry de Ferrers was the greatest landholder in Derbyshire during the Domesday Survey, owning nearly the entire modern hundred of Appletree. William I also granted the manor of Belper (Beaurepaire) to Henry de Ferrers, earl of Derby. 

The earldom of Derby was likely first held by Robert de Ferrers (d. 1139), who is said to have been made an earl by King Stephen following the battle of the Standard in 1138. These early earls were known as the Earls Ferrers (from their surname), earls of Tutbury (from their residence), or earls of Nottingham (as the county was a lordship under their rule). The descendants of Robert retained the earldom until 1266, when Robert (c. 1240–c. 1279), likely the 6th earl, was deprived of his title and lands after participating in the baronial rising against Henry III. 

In 1266, Henry III granted the forfeited Ferrers estates to his son, Edmund, earl of Lancaster. Edmund's son, Thomas, earl of Lancaster, referred to himself as Earl Ferrers. The title of earl of Derby was later created in 1337 for Henry (c. 1299–1361), the grandson of Edmund and later duke of Lancaster. This title was subsequently taken by John of Gaunt, who married Henry's daughter, Blanche. John of Gaunt's son, Henry, earl of Derby, ascended the throne as Henry IV in 1399.

## Other Ferrers and Related Lineages
The Ferrers name appears in various other historical contexts:
* **Baron Ferrers of Groby:** Edward (1415–1457), a younger son of Reginald Grey, succeeded his maternal grandfather as Baron Ferrers of Groby in 1445. Edward became the ancestor of the Greys (marquesses of Dorset and dukes of Suffolk) and the earls of Stamford.
* **Military and Legal Records:** A Sir Henry Ferrers, Baronet, was once indicted for aiding and abetting the murder of a man named Stone by one Nightingale. During his arraignment, the indictment was found insufficient because it named him as a "Knight," a title he confessed he never held; he was subsequently re-indicted as a "Baronet."
* **Crusades:** An English name, De Ferrers earl of Derby, is listed among the Christians who died before St. John d’Acre.
* **Castle of the Peak:** In 1216, William Ferrers is associated with the Castle of the Peak (or Peveril Castle).
