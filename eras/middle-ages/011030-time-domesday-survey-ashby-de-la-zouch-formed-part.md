# Ashby-de-la-Zouch

Ashby-de-la-Zouch is a market town located in England within the Bosworth parliamentary division of Leicestershire. It is situated 118 miles north-west by north from London and is accessible via the Leicester-Burton branch of the Midland railway. As of 1901, the population of its urban district was 4,726.

## Early History and Ownership
At the time of the Domesday survey, Ashby-de-la-Zouch was part of the estates of Hugh de Grentmaisnel. Shortly thereafter, ownership passed to Robert Beaumeis. From Beaumeis, the estate passed through female descent to the la Zouch family. The town, which had previously been known as Essebi or Ashby, derived the adjunct to its name from this family.

In 1219, Roger la Zouch obtained a grant for a two-day fair held at the feast of St Helen, as well as a weekly market, in exchange for a fine consisting of one palfrey.

## The Manor and Ashby Castle
During the 15th century, the manor was held by James Butler, the earl of Ormond. Following Butler's attainder, the manor was granted to Lord Hastings in 1461. In 1474, Lord Hastings received a royal licence to fortify and build a castle and to empark 3,000 acres.

The castle served as a place of detention for Mary, queen of Scots, in 1569, while she was under the custody of the earls of Shrewsbury and Huntingdon. During the English Civil War, the castle was held and fortified for the king by Colonel Henry Hastings and was visited by Charles in 1645. Following the conclusion of the war in 1648, the castle was dismantled by order of parliament. The extensive remains of the castle are located to the south of the town. The site also features in Sir Walter Scott's novel *Ivanhoe*.

## Town Features and Economy
The town's church, St Helen, is a Perpendicular building that was enlarged and restored in 1880. It houses monuments of the Huntingdon family and an old finger-pillory used for punishing those who misbehaved in church. Other local landmarks include a Queen Eleanor cross, created by Sir Gilbert Scott to commemorate the countess of Loudoun.

Ashby-de-la-Zouch is known for the Ivanhoe baths, established in 1826. These baths utilize saline waters containing bromine, which are used for rheumatic and scrofulous complaints; the springs for these baths are located at Moira, three miles to the west. 

Economically, the town was celebrated in the 18th century as one of England's premier markets for horses. It also hosted prosperous factories producing hats and stockings made of cotton and wool. The neighbouring district contains extensive coal-mines, such as those at Moira, where the Ashby-de-la-Zouch canal begins its route south to the Coventry canal.

## Notable Residents
The town is the birthplace of John Bainbridge (1582–1643), an astronomer and physician. After practicing medicine and running a school, Bainbridge moved to London and became a licentiate of the college of physicians. In 1619, he was appointed to the Savilian chair of astronomy at Oxford by Sir Henry Savile. He authored *Canicularia* (1648) and *An Astronomical Description of the late Comet* (1619), and translated works by Ptolemy and Proclus.

Additionally, the English geologist Alexander Henry Green (1832–1896) was educated partly at the Ashby Grammar School, which was mastered by his father, the Rev. Thomas Sheldon Green. Green later became a professor of geology at the university of Oxford and the Yorkshire College at Leeds, and was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1886.
