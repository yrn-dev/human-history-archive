# Gregory X

## Election and Accession
Tebaldo Visconti, who became Pope Gregory X, was born in Piacenza in 1208. He was educated for the church and served as the archdeacon of Liege. His election to the papacy followed a period of significant instability; the eighteen cardinals tasked with choosing a successor to Clement IV were split into Italian and French factions. These groups wrangled for nearly three years amid great popular excitement. The deadlock was eventually broken by the eloquence of the Franciscan monk St Bonaventura, which stirred the cardinals to entrust the choice to six electors. These electors selected Visconti, who was accompanying Edward of England on a crusade at the time. 

Upon returning to Rome, Visconti was ordained as a priest on March 19, 1272, and consecrated on March 27. He served as pope from September 1, 1271, until January 10, 1276.

## The Council of Lyons and Diplomatic Efforts
Shortly after his accession, Gregory X summoned the fourteenth general council of the Catholic Church, which convened at Lyons in 1274. The council was attended by approximately 1,600 prelates and was organized to address church abuses, the condition of the Holy Land, and the eastern schism. Through the efforts of St Bonaventura, the Greeks were persuaded to agree to a temporary union with Rome. Additionally, Rudolph of Habsburg used the council to renounce all imperial rights within the States of the Church.

## Reforms and the Papal Conclave
Gregory X issued several reform decrees during his pontificate. His most celebrated contribution was the creation of a constitution that established the form of the conclave for papal elections. This procedure has largely remained the law of the church since its inception.

## Death and Legacy
Gregory X is described as a nobleman who was motivated by a sense of a great mission and a fondness for peace. He died at Arezzo on January 10, 1276, while traveling to Rome to crown Rudolph and organize a great crusade involving the kings of Sicily, Aragon, France, and England. He was later honored as a saint by the people of Piacenza and Arezzo. He was succeeded in the papacy by Innocent V.

## Relationship with Saint Bonaventura
The election of Gregory X was heavily influenced by Saint Bonaventura (John of Fidanza), a Franciscan theologian born in 1221 in Tuscany. Bonaventura, who had been a doctor and general of the Franciscan order, was instrumental in procuring Gregory X's election. In gratitude, Gregory X rewarded Bonaventura with the titles of bishop of Albano and cardinal. The Pope also insisted that Bonaventura attend the council of Lyons in 1274, the meeting at which Bonaventura eventually died.
