# Villehardouin and La Conquete de Constantinople

Geoffroi de Villehardouin was a chronicler and marshal whose work, *La Conquete de Constantinople*, serves as a primary authority for the history of the Fourth Crusade and the subsequent Latin empire. His accounts provide detailed perspectives on the sieges of Constantinople, the interactions between the crusading forces and the Byzantine Empire, and the social structures of the period.

## Historical Accounts of Constantinople
Villehardouin’s writings offer both external and internal views of Constantinople, describing the city as "souveraine" compared to all others. In his computations, he estimated the space of the city at three leagues, though this measurement has been analyzed by historians such as Gibbon, who suggested that Villehardouin may have been using the old Gallic league of 1,500 paces common in Champagne.

His work is a central source for the details of the first and second sieges and conquests of the city. Regarding the first siege, his accounts (specifically numbers 75–99) are cited alongside the original letter of the crusaders to Innocent III and the writings of Nicetas. For the second siege, his records (numbers 113–132) are used in conjunction with Baldwin’s second epistle to Innocent III and the accounts of Gunther and Dandolo.

## Observations on People and Society
Villehardouin provides insight into the military and social compositions of the era. He identified the Varangi guards as "Englois et Danois avec leurs haches" (English and Danes with their axes). He also documented the social status of the youth accompanying the knights, noting that the pages and *valets* were as noble as the knights themselves.

In his descriptions of the Byzantine leadership, Villehardouin referred to the emperor Isaac as "Sursac." This term is noted as potentially being derived from the French *Sire* or the Greek *Kur*.

## Perspectives on the Conquest and Its Aftermath
The narratives of Villehardouin are often contrasted with those of other historians, such as Nicetas. While Nicetas described the disorders of the sack of Constantinople and lamented the sacrilege and loss of wealth, Villehardouin's accounts are noted for not betraying symptoms of pity or remorse regarding these events.

Furthermore, Villehardouin and Gunther are cited as representing the sentiments of the two parties involved in the conflict. In the case of Mourzoufle, Villehardouin (numbers 141–145, 163) and Gunther provided accounts of his fate without expressing pity for him, characterizing him as a rebel or tyrant.

## Bibliographic and Scholarly Reception
*La Conquete de Constantinople* has been preserved through various editions, including those by De Wailly (Paris, 1872) and Bouchet (Paris, 1891). The work has been a foundational text for later historians and scholars:

*   **Modern Historians:** The text was utilized by Ducange in *Histoire de l'empire de Constantinople sous les empereurs francais* (1657), Edward Gibbon in *Decline and Fall of the Roman Empire*, and G. Finlay in *History of Greece*.
*   **Research and Collections:** Jean Alexandre Buchon, a 19th-century French scholar, conducted original research on the medieval French settlements in the East. This included an unfinished work titled *Histoire des conquêtes et de l'établissement des Français dans les états de l'ancienne Grèce sous es Villehardouin* (1846).
