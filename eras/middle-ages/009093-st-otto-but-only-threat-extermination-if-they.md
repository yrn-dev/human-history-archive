# Otto I (The Great)

Otto I, surnamed the Great, was a member of a new line of Saxon emperors who rose to prominence in the tenth century. He was the son of Henry the Fowler, who had been elected King of Germany in 919 by an assembly of prelates, princes, and German nobles.

## Ascent to Power and Imperial Coronation
Otto I succeeded his father as King in 936, having been elected as his successor at Aix-la-Chapelle. In 962, at the invitation of Pope John XII, Otto descended upon Rome and was crowned emperor. This event established him as the first Saxon Emperor. However, the relationship between the emperor and the papacy was strained; Otto was later forced to degrade John XII due to the Pope's treachery, and he officially deposed him in 963. Through his assumption of the imperial dignity, Otto I restored the long-standing struggle for ascendancy between the Emperor and the Pope to a state of greater dignity and decency.

## Administration of Lotharingia
During his reign, Otto I exercised a powerful personality in the governance of the empire, particularly regarding Lotharingia. Annexed to the German Empire, Lotharingia became a German province from 925. Otto believed that no layman leading the region would be sufficiently loyal to submit entirely to his politics. Consequently, he relied on the faithfulness and devotion of bishops to serve as agents of German domination and influence.

In 953, Otto appointed his brother, Bruno, as the Duke of Lotharingia, while simultaneously securing for him the archbishopric of Cologne. By combining ecclesiastical and political power, Bruno acted as an intermediary to further Germanize the Lotharingian church and the duchy. Following the deaths of Count Otto and the young Henry in 944, Otto the Great had initially given Lorraine to his daughter Liutgard's husband, Conrad the Red, Duke of Franconia. However, after Conrad and Liudulf (the German king's son) revolted against Otto in 953, Otto stripped Conrad of the duchy and transferred government to Bruno in 954.

## Conflicts with France
Otto's reign was marked by significant tension with the French monarchy over the possession of Lorraine. In 939, Otto entered a struggle with Louis IV (surnamed "d'Outremer"), the King of France, because the nobles of Lorraine had sworn fidelity to the French king. Although a period of peace seemed possible when Louis married Otto's sister, Gerberga, the war resumed with Otto supporting rebel lords within the French kingdom. This conflict did not end until the treaty of Visé-sur-Meuse in 942.

Later, Otto's influence continued to pressure the French monarchy. In 948, the council of Ingelheim excommunicated Hugh the Great, a powerful vassal who had held King Louis IV prisoner. Menaced by Otto the Great, Hugh the Great was forced to submit and restore the town of Laon to his sovereign.

## Succession and Legacy
Otto I was succeeded in 973 by his son, Otto II, who reigned until 983. Otto II continued the imperial legacy, though he faced his own challenges, including an attempt by King Lothair of France to capture him at Aix-la-Chapelle in 978. Otto II retaliated by invading France and reaching Paris, though he failed to capture the city. He was followed by a third Otto (Otto III), who reigned from 983 to 1002. The Saxon line of emperors eventually came to an end early in the eleventh century.
