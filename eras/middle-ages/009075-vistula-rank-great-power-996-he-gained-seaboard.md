# Boleslaus I "The Great"

Boleslaus I, known as "The Great," was the King of Poland who died in 1025. He was the son of the Bohemian princess Dobrawa (also called Bona) and Mieszko, who was the first Christian prince of Poland. The court had been converted from paganism to Catholicism by Jordan, the chaplain to Dobrawa.

## Rise to Power and Territorial Expansion
Boleslaus succeeded his father in 992. Described as a born warrior, he rapidly elevated the struggling Polish principality located on the Vistula to the status of a great power. In 996, he secured a seaboard by seizing Pomerania. He further expanded his territories by occupying Cracow, a city that had previously been Czech, taking advantage of internal troubles within Bohemia.

Following the death of Emperor Otto III, Boleslaus invaded Germany. During this campaign, he penetrated as far as the Elbe, occupying Meissen and Stralsund. His dominions were extended to the Saale and the Elster. He also occupied Bohemia, though he was driven out of that region by Emperor Henry IV in 1004. The conflict with Germany concluded in 1018 with the peace of Bautzen, a settlement that greatly benefited Boleslaus, allowing him to retain Lusatia. Subsequently, Boleslaus directed his military efforts against Jaroslav, the grand duke of Kiev, routing him on the banks of the Bug, which served as the boundary between Poland and Russia at the time.

## Religion and Statecraft
Boleslaus is characterized as a man of progress and insight who recognized the essential superiority of Christianity over other religions. Despite a strong love of adventure that sometimes made him appear more like a wandering chieftain than an established ruler, he deliberately associated himself with the new faith.

His sagacity was evident in his reception of Adalbert, the bishop of Prague, who was a fugitive. After Adalbert suffered martyrdom at the hands of pagan Slavs on April 23, 997, Boleslaus purchased the saint's relics. He placed them in the church of Gnesen, a foundation of his father, which subsequently became the metropolitan see of Poland.

## Royal Coronation
In the year 1000, Boleslaus hosted Otto III at Gnesen with such magnificence that the emperor declared Boleslaus too worthy to be merely a *princeps*. Consequently, Otto III conferred the royal crown upon him. Despite this earlier coronation, Boleslaus felt it necessary to crown himself king a second time in the final year of his life.
