# Letters to Archbishop Rokycan and On Good and Evil Priests

The *Letters to Archbishop Rokycan* and the book *On good and evil priests* are significant theological works attributed to Brother Gregory, a prominent figure within the community of the Bohemian Brethren. Brother Gregory, who is referred to as the patriarch of the brotherhood, produced a large volume of writings primarily focused on theological matters before his death in 1480.

## The Bohemian Brethren and Literary Influence
The community of the Bohemian Brethren was indirectly founded by Chelcicky. The group exerted a substantial influence on Bohemian literature, as nearly all of their theological and historical works were composed in the national language. This practice served to refine and polish the national tongue. 

Among the collective achievements of the brotherhood's divines was the *Bible of Kralice*, a famed translation of the Scriptures first printed in 1593 at Kralice in Moravia. Following the death of Brother Gregory, the community experienced a period of discord, which paradoxically resulted in a surge of literary activity. This era saw the production of numerous controversial works by figures such as Bishop Augusta and the brothers Jaffet, Lucas, and Blahoslav.

## Related Works and Authors
While Brother Gregory's *Letters to Archbishop Rokycan* and *On good and evil priests* are central to the brotherhood's theological output, other writers of the period addressed similar figures. Peter Chelcicky, a religious writer during the Hussite wars who died in 1460, also authored polemical writings in the form of letters addressed to Bishop Nicolas of Pelhrimov and Archbishop John of Rokycan. Chelcicky is noted for his masterpiece, *Sit viry* (the net of faith), as well as his *Postilla*. 

Archbishop John of Rokycan himself authored a Bohemian work titled *Postilla*, which is described as being similar to, though inferior to, the work of the same name written by Huss.

## Preservation of Bohemian Literature
Much of the historical and controversial literature of the Hussite period and the Bohemian Brethren was lost. A systematic destruction of Bohemian writings was carried out by the Jesuits following the battle of the White Hill in 1620, and nearly all anti-Roman books written in Bohemia were destroyed by the 17th century. 

As a result of this destruction:
* The writings of the Taborites (the extreme Hussite party) were entirely destroyed.
* Only fragments of Blahoslav's *History of the Unity* have been preserved.
* Only the biographies of Peter and William of Rosenberg remain from Wenceslas Brezan's *History of the House of Rosenberg*.
* Some works from the moderate Hussites (the Utraquists or Calixtines) survived, including *Lives of the Priests of Tabor* and *Of the Great Torment of the Holy Church*.

## Later Contributions to Bohemian Writing
The most distinguished writer associated with the brotherhood was John Amos Komensky, also known as Comenius (1592-1670). He authored 142 works, many of which were written in his native language, including numerous educational and devotional texts. His most famous work in Bohemian is the allegorical tale *Labyrinth of the World*.
