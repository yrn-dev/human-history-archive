# Basel

## Geography and Economy
Basel (referred to in French as *Bale* and in Italian as *Basilea*) is the capital of the Swiss half canton of Basel Stadt or Bale Ville. The city is divided by the Rhine river into two sections: Gross Basel to the south and Klein Basel to the north, with Gross Basel being the larger of the two. The river is crossed by several bridges, including a stone bridge built in 1905 to replace an older wooden structure.

The city is the second most populous town in the Swiss Confederation, with 109,161 inhabitants, ranking only after Zuerich. It is reputed to be the richest Swiss town, with a number of resident millionaires (in francs) exceeding those of any other town in Switzerland. This wealth and population growth are largely attributed to the 1882 opening of the St Gotthard railway. This line allows merchandise collected from central and north Europe to be stored in Basel before redistribution, resulting in a flourishing and large transit trade, despite the dingy appearance of the city's older areas. The city's infrastructure includes the Baden station in Klein Basel and the main or central railway station in Gross Basel.

## The Cathedral and Religious History
The most prominent building in Basel is the Muenster, or cathedral, which is constructed from deep red sandstone and situated on a terrace high above the Rhine. Although consecrated in 1019, the cathedral was mainly rebuilt following a disastrous earthquake in 1356 that nearly ruined the city. 

The cathedral served as a site for the great oecumenical council from 1431 to 1449; the choir was used for public meetings, while the chapter-house served as the meeting place for committees. The cathedral also features cloisters where several celebrated men are buried, including Grynaeus (d. 1541), Oecolampadius (d. 1531), and Buxtorf (d. 1732). Erasmus lived in Basel from 1521 to 1529 and was buried in the cathedral upon his death in 1536.

## The Synod of Basel
During the period of the council, the appearance of fathers from Germany, France, and Italy was slow. Cesarini, for instance, was occupied with the war against the Hussites until the disaster of Taus forced a hasty evacuation of Bohemia. Various factors contributed to the perception that the synod of Basel was doomed to failure, including the progress of heresy, reported troubles in Germany, and a war between the dukes of Burgundy and Austria. Additionally, few fathers responded to the summons of Martin V.

Eugenius IV, the successor to Martin V, shared the opinion that the synod would fail and desired to preside over the council himself. Due to impaired health—likely caused by cerebral congestion—which made traveling difficult, Eugenius IV commanded the fathers to disperse. On December 18, 1431, he appointed Bologna as the meeting place for the fathers to convene in eighteen months. This was intended to align the council session with conferences in Bologna involving representatives of the Greek church to seek union.

## Education, Art, and Civic Architecture
The university of Basel, founded in 1460 by Pius II, is the oldest university in Switzerland. In recent years, it has been expanded through the construction of detached buildings.

The city's civic architecture includes a 16th-century Rathaus (town hall) that has undergone recent restoration. Basel's museums house significant artistic and historical treasures. The museum contains a fine collection of art by Holbein, who resided in Basel between 1528 and 1531. The historical museum, located in the old Franciscan church, contains various treasures, including fragments of the *Dance of Death*, which have been wrongly attributed to Holbein.
