# Pears, The Fall of Constantinople and Related Historical Studies

The study of the fall of Constantinople and the broader history of the Greek and Byzantine worlds is documented through a variety of primary authorities and modern historical analyses. Among the modern works addressing these events is *The Fall of Constantinople*, published in London in 1885 by Pears.

## Historical Authorities and Modern Analysis
The historical record regarding the conquest of Constantinople is supported by several primary authorities. These include the works of Villehardouin, specifically *La Conquete de Constantinople*, and Robert de Clari's *La Prise de Constantinople*, which is found in Hopf's *Chroniques greco-romaines*. Other essential primary sources include the *Chronique* by Ernoul, as well as the writings of Nicetas and George Acropolites. Additionally, documents regarding the state and commercial history of the Republic of Venice, found in the works of Tafel and Thomas, provide further context.

Modern scholars have expanded upon these records. In addition to Pears, other significant works include Ducange's *Histoire de l'empire de Constantinople sous les empereurs francais* (1657), Gibbon's *Decline and Fall*, and G. Finlay's *History of Greece*. Further specialized study is provided by Gerland in *Geschichte des lateinischen Kaiserreiches von Konstantinopel* and Hopf, who contributed "Griechische Geschichte" to Ersch and Gruber's *Encyklopaedie* in 1870.

## Byzantine Scholarship and Literature
The academic study of the Byzantine era was significantly advanced by the German scholar Carl Krumbacher (1856-1909). Educated at the universities of Leipzig and Munich, Krumbacher served as a professor of modern and middle age Greek literature. His primary contribution, *Geschichte der byzantinischen Litteratur*, covers the period from the reign of Justinian until the fall of the Eastern Empire in 1453. This work was enhanced by a general sketch of Byzantine history (A.D. 395-1453) provided by H. Gelzer and a section on theology by A. Ehrhard.

Krumbacher also established the *Byzantinisches Archiv* in 1898 and the *Byzantinische Zeitschrift* in 1892. His other scholarly pursuits included a treatise on the 9th-century Byzantine poetess Casia and a study of Michael Glykas. In his work *Das Problem der neugriechischen Schriftsprache* (1902), Krumbacher expressed strong opposition to purists who sought to integrate classical styles into modern Greek literature.

## Regional History and the Turkish Conquest
The broader impact of regional conquests is evident in the history of cities such as Corinth. Following the Gothic raids of 395 and 267, the city was protected by new fortifications at the Isthmus. While the medieval town was limited to the citadel, it served as the ecclesiastical and administrative capital of Peloponnesus and maintained a silk industry and thriving trade until it was sacked by the Normans in 1147. 

Following a period of contention among Italian pretenders and its attachment to the Latin duchy of the Morea in 1210, Corinth was subject to the Turkish conquest in 1459. After this event, the city's history remained largely uneventful, with the exception of a Venetian occupation between 1687 and 1715 and a raid by the Maltese in 1611.
