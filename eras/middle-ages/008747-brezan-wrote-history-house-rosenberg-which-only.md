# Wenceslas Brezan

Wenceslas Brezan was a historian and librarian associated with the House of Rosenberg and the community of the Bohemian Brethren. He is noted for his contributions to the annals of the Rosenberg family and his affiliation with the literary traditions of the Bohemian Brethren.

## Association with the House of Rosenberg
Brezan served as the librarian for the final two members of the Rosenberg family, who were brothers: Peter Vok and William. William was created prince of Ursini-Rosenberg in 1556 and died in 1592. Peter Vok, a prominent figure in Bohemian history and a strong adherent of the Utraquist party, eventually sold Krumau shortly before his death in 1611 due to the establishment of Jesuits in the vicinity.

The Rosenberg family, also known as the *pani z ruze* (lords of the rose), had long used the ancient castle at Krumau—a town in Bohemia on the banks of the Moldau (Vitava)—as their stronghold. The family's residence at Krumau began with Henry II. of Rosenberg, who died in 1310, and the location was later raised to the rank of a city by his son, Peter I., who died in 1349.

## Literary Work and the Bohemian Brethren
Brezan is identified as one of the historians of the Bohemian Brethren, a community that exerted a significant influence on Bohemian literature. The brotherhood's historical and theological works were primarily written in the national language, which became more polished and refined through their efforts. 

Brezan authored a valuable work titled *History of the House of Rosenberg*. However, much of the historical writing produced by members of the brotherhood perished during the 17th century, a period when nearly all anti-Roman books written in Bohemia were destroyed. Consequently, only the biographies of Peter and William of Rosenberg have been preserved from Brezan's larger history. These preserved works include:
* *Zivot Vilema z Rosenberka* (Life of William of Rosenberg), 1847
* *Zivot Petra Voka z Rosenberka* (Life of Peter Vok of Rosenberg), 1880
