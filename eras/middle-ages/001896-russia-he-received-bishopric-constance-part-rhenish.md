# The Bishopric of Constance and the Territorial Evolution of Baden

## Territorial Development of Baden
In 1771, Baden was united under a single ruler, though it lacked a compact territory. At that time, the total area was approximately 1350 sq. m., consisting of isolated districts located on either bank of the upper Rhine. The process of creating territorial unity was the work of Charles Frederick, who began his reign in 1738 and came of age in 1746. Described as a wise and liberal ruler, Charles Frederick focused on the administration of justice, education, and the development of commerce and agriculture.

## Impact of the Napoleonic Wars
The Napoleonic wars provided the opportunity for Charles Frederick to expand his territories. When war broke out between Austria and France in 1792, the Badenese fought on the side of Austria. This resulted in the devastation of the country, and by 1796, the margrave was forced to cede his territories on the left bank of the Rhine to France and pay an indemnity.

Fortune shifted in 1803 when, largely due to the good offices of Alexander I., the emperor of Russia, Charles Frederick received the bishopric of Constance, other smaller districts, and part of the Rhenish Palatinate. Along with these acquisitions, he was granted the dignity of a prince elector.

## Expansion and Sovereignty
In 1805, Charles Frederick changed his allegiance and fought for Napoleon. Following the peace of Pressburg that same year, he obtained the Breisgau and additional territories at the expense of the Habsburgs. By 1806, he joined the Confederation of the Rhine, declared himself a sovereign prince, and became a grand-duke, receiving further territorial additions.

The Baden contingent continued to support France, and the peace of Vienna in 1809 rewarded the grand-duke with more territory at the expense of the kingdom of Wuerttemberg. By the time of his death in June 1811, Charles Frederick had quadrupled the area of Baden. He was succeeded by his grandson, Charles, who was married to Stephanie de Beauharnais, an adopted daughter of Napoleon. Charles continued to fight for his father-in-law until after the battle of Leipzig in 1813, at which point he joined the Allies.

## Geography of the Grand Duchy
The Grand Duchy of Baden is a sovereign German state located in the south-west corner of the empire. It is bounded by the kingdom of Bavaria and the grand-duchy of Hesse-Darmstadt to the north, Switzerland to the south, and the kingdom of Wuerttemberg and part of Bavaria to the east. The Rhine forms the western boundary, separating Baden from the imperial province of Alsace-Lorraine and the Bavarian Palatinate.

The total area of the country is 5823 sq. m., with nearly 80% consisting of mountainous terrain. The region includes the Odenwald Range to the north of the river Neckar and the Black Forest (_Schwarzwald_) extending from the river Neckar in the north to the Lake of Constance in the south. The Black Forest is divided by the valley of the Kinzig; the area south of the Kinzig has a mean height of 3100 ft. (with the Feldberg reaching 4898 ft.), while the area to the north has a mean height of 2100 ft. (with the Belchen reaching 4480 ft.). Other geographic features include the Kaiserstuhl, a volcanic group 10 m. long and 5 m. broad, and the Lake of Constance (_Boden-See_), which is shared with Switzerland and Bavaria.
