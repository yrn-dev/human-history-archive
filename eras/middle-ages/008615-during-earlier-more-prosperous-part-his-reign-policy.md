# The Reign of King George of Podebrad

## Early Policy and Alliances
During the earlier and more prosperous part of his reign, the policy of King George of Podebrad was based on a firm alliance with Matthias Corvinus, the king of Hungary. Through the influence of Matthias, George was crowned by the Romanist bishop of Waitzen. In these initial years, George's reign was very prosperous, and he found his principal support among the towns and the men of the smaller nobility. During this period of prosperity, George intended to establish a national dynasty.

## Conflict with the Papacy and Romanist Nobles
Over time, George's relationship with the Romanist nobles deteriorated, and some became hostile toward him. This hostility contributed to a protracted struggle between the king and the papal see. As a result of this conflict, several of George's far-reaching ambitions failed, including a period during which he attempted to obtain supremacy over Germany.

Following unsuccessful negotiations with Rome, George assembled the estates at Prague in 1452. There, he declared his readiness to risk his crown and his life in defense of his faith, stating that he would remain true to the communion in both kinds until his death. This declaration further embittered the Romanist party in Bohemia. In 1465, many nobles of the Roman religion formed a confederacy against the king at a meeting held at Zelena Hora (Grunberg).

## Excommunication and the Rise of Matthias Corvinus
In 1466, Pope Paul II provided moral support to the confederates by releasing all Bohemians from their oath of allegiance to George and pronouncing a sentence of excommunication against him. Under the influence of the Pope, King Matthias of Hungary deserted his former alliance with George and instead supported the lords of the league of Zelena Hora.

While George was initially successful in the resulting desultory warfare, the tide turned when Matthias invaded Moravia and captured its capital, Brunn. On May 3, 1469, at a meeting of Catholic nobles in Olmutz, Moravia, Matthias was proclaimed king of Bohemia. Although George achieved some successes over his rival in the following year, the war was temporarily halted by George's death in 1471. George of Podebrad is remembered as the only Hussite king of Bohemia and, along with Charles IV, is regarded as a ruler whose memory is most endeared to his countrymen.

## Succession and the Election of Vladislav
In his later years, the struggle against King Matthias led George to seek aid from Poland. This induced him to offer the Bohemian throne's succession to Vladislav (also known as Wladislaus or Ladislaus), the son of King Casimir of Poland, though no formal agreement was finalized.

Upon George's death, many Bohemian nobles supported the claim of Matthias of Hungary. However, after protracted negotiations, Prince Vladislav of Poland was elected at Kutna Hora on May 27, 1471. This election represented a victory for the national party and reflected the strong anti-clerical sentiment in Bohemia; while Matthias was an unconditional adherent of Rome, the Polish envoys promised that Vladislav would maintain the Compacts.

## The Reign of Vladislav
The beginning of Vladislav's reign was marked by continued struggle and desultory warfare with Matthias of Hungary, who still claimed the Bohemian throne. This conflict lasted until 1478, when a treaty concluded at Olmutz secured Bohemia for Vladislav. Under the terms of the treaty, Matthias was to retain the "lands of the Bohemian crown"—specifically Lusatia, Silesia, and Moravia—during his lifetime, after which they were to be restored to Bohemia.

While Vladislav remained faithful to his promise to maintain the Compacts and did not stop Bohemians from receiving the communion in both kinds, his general policy regarding religious controversies and state matters was reactionary. He appointed men from the section of the Utraquist party closest to Rome as government officials in Prague. This period saw the severe persecution of the Bohemian Brethren, the extreme Hussites. This reaction led to the formation of a league by nobles of the same faith and resulted in serious riots in Prague, where advanced Hussites stormed the city's three town halls. Peace was temporarily restored following a meeting of all the estates at Kutna Hora in 1485.
