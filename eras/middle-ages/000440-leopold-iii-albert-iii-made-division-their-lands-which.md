# Division of Lands between Leopold III and Albert III

## The Division of 1379
Following the childless death of Rudolph IV in 1365, his two surviving brothers, Leopold III and Albert III, entered into an agreement in 1379 to divide their lands. Under the terms of this division, Albert III retained control of Carniola and Austria proper. Leopold III was granted the territories of Tirol, Carinthia, and Styria.

## Successions of the Senior Line
The senior line of the family, which ruled in Austria, continued after the death of Duke Albert III in 1395. He was succeeded by his son, Duke Albert IV, and subsequently by his grandson, Duke Albert V. In 1438, Albert V became the German king under the name Albert II. 

Albert II married Elizabeth, the daughter of Sigismund, who was the king of Bohemia and Hungary. Upon the death of his father-in-law, Albert II assumed the crowns of both Hungary and Bohemia. He died in 1439. Shortly after his death, a son named Ladislaus Posthumus was born; Ladislaus eventually succeeded to the kingdoms of Hungary and Bohemia as well as the duchy of Austria.

## Successions of the Leopoldine Line
Leopold III was killed in 1386 during the battle of Sempach. Following his death, Albert III served as the guardian for Leopold's four nephews. These four brothers—William, Leopold, Ernest, and Frederick—initially ruled their lands (Styria, Carinthia, and Tirol) in common.

William and Leopold died early in the 15th century. In 1406, the remaining brothers, Ernest and Frederick, divided the lands between them. Ernest became the duke of Carinthia and Styria, while Frederick became the count of Tirol. 

In 1424, Ernest was succeeded by his sons, Albert and Frederick. Frederick of Tirol was succeeded by his son, Sigismund, in 1439.

## The Regency of Frederick III
Upon the death of King Albert II in 1439, Frederick (son of Ernest) succeeded him as the German king and was soon crowned Emperor Frederick III. During his reign, Frederick III acted as the guardian for the minor Sigismund of Tirol. Additionally, because Ladislaus was an infant, Frederick III became the regent of Austria.

Frederick III's rule was characterized by disorder and struggle. This was attributed in part to his own feeble character and in part to the desire of his brother, Albert, to share in his dignities. By 1446, the Tirolese had grown weary of his government, and Sigismund was declared of age. Simultaneously, the estates of Austria became discontented and launched an open revolt.
