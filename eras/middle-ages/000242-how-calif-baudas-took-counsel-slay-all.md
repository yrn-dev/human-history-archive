# The Counsel of the Calif of Baudas

## The City of Baudas
Baudas (also known as Baghdad) was a great city and the seat of the Calif of all the Saracens in the world, serving a role similar to that of Rome for the Pope of all Christians. The city was described as the noblest and greatest in its region, featuring a great river that allowed travel to the Sea of India. Merchants would descend the river for eighteen days to reach a city called Kisi to enter the sea. Along this route lay Bastra, a city surrounded by woods known for producing the best dates in the world. Baudas was also a center for the weaving of gold brocades and various silk stuffs, such as *nac*, *nasich*, and *cramoisy*, which were often richly wrought with figures of birds and beasts.

## The Calif's Intentions
In the year of Christ 1255, a Calif ruled at Baudas who harbored a great hatred for Christians. He was preoccupied day and night with thoughts of how to either force the Christians within his kingdom to convert to his own faith or to have them all slain. To achieve this, the Calif took daily counsel with the priests and devotees of his faith, who shared his malice toward the Christians. The archive notes that the Saracens throughout the world were malignantly disposed toward the body of Christians, driven by the law given by Mahommet, which commanded them to do mischief to other people—especially Christians—and to strip them of their goods.

## Reaction of the Christians
Upon learning of the Calif's designs, the Christians were filled with great dismay. In response, the wisest among them, including various priests and bishops, gathered to take counsel together. Finding no earthly resource to escape the cruel hands of the Calif, they lifted their hopes to God, their Creator, beseeching Him for protection.

## The Miracle of the Mountain
The tension culminated in a great miracle wrought by God on behalf of the Christians. A one-eyed cobbler was desired to pray for the Christians. After the cobbler finished his prayer to the Sovereign Father, a mountain rose from its place and moved to a spot that the Calif had pointed out. This event occurred while the Calif, the Saracens, and other people looked on.

The miracle left the Calif and the Saracens amazed. Consequently, a great number of Saracens converted to Christianity. The Calif himself was secretly baptized in the name of the Holy Ghost, the Father, and the Son. Because of this secret conversion, when the Calif died, a small cross was found hanging around his neck. As a result, the Saracens refused to bury him with the other Califs, placing him instead in a separate location.

## The Fall of Baudas
The reign of the Califs at Baudas ended when Alaü, the Lord of the Tartars of the Levant and brother to the Great Kaan, led a mighty host against the city. Despite the city's defenses, which included more than 100,000 horse and various foot soldiers, Alaü took Baudas by storm.

During the conquest, Alaü discovered the Tower of the Califs, which contained the greatest accumulation of gold, silver, and treasure ever known in one spot. Alaü questioned the Calif on why he had gathered such wealth instead of using it to pay knights and soldiers to defend his people and kingdom. When the Calif remained silent, Alaü mocked his love for the treasure, shutting him inside the Treasure Tower without meat or drink. The Calif remained in the tower for four days before he died. Following this event, there was never another Calif in Baudas or anywhere else.
