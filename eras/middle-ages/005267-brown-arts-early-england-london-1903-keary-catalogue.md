# Brown, The Arts in Early England and C.F. Keary's Catalogue

The study of early English history and its material culture is supported by a variety of primary and modern authorities. Among the modern scholarly works used to analyze the arts and economic artifacts of this period are G. Baldwin Brown's *The Arts in Early England* and C.F. Keary's *Catalogue of Anglo-Saxon Coins in the British Museum*.

## The Arts in Early England
G. Baldwin Brown published *The Arts in Early England* in London in 1903. This work serves as a modern authority for those researching the artistic developments of the early English period. It is situated among other specialized studies of the era's physical environment and domestic life, such as M. Heyne's works on the construction of the Halle Heorot (1864) and *Deutsche Hausaltertümer* (1900-1903), as well as R. Henning's *Das deutsche Haus* (1882).

## Numismatic Records
C.F. Keary contributed to the archival record of early England through the *Catalogue of Anglo-Saxon Coins in the British Museum*, volume i., published in London in 1887. This catalogue provides a systematic record of coinage, which serves as a primary resource for understanding the economic and political history of the Anglo-Saxon period.

## Related Historical Authorities
The works of Brown and Keary are part of a broader framework of authorities used to reconstruct the history of the Anglo-Saxons. Primary sources for this period include:
*   **Chronicles and Histories:** The *Saxon Chronicle* (edited by C. Plummer, 1892-1899), Bede's *Hist. Eccl.* (edited by C. Plummer, 1896), Nennius' *Historia Britonum* (edited by San-Marte, 1844), and Gildas' *De Excidio Britanniae*.
*   **Legal and Administrative Records:** The *Anglo-Saxon Laws* (edited by F. Liebermann, 1903) and Charters (W. de G. Birch, *Cartularium Saxonicum*, 1885-1893).

Modern historical analysis of the period has been further developed by authors such as J.R. Green in *The Making of England* (1881), T. Hodgkin in *History of England from the Earliest Times to the Norman Conquest* (1906), and H.M. Chadwick, who wrote *Studies on Anglo-Saxon Institutions* (1905) and *The Origin of the English Nation* (1907). Legal and social structures are explored in the works of Sir F. Pollock and F.W. Maitland (*History of English Law*, 1895/1898), F. Seebohm (*The English Village Community*, 1883 and *Tribal Custom in Anglo-Saxon Law*, 1903), and P. Vinogradoff (*The Growth of the Manor*, 1905).
