# Franciscus Severi

## Biography and Family
Franciscus Severi was an Italian man whose primary historical record in the archive is as the father of the noted jurist Bartolus. His wife, the mother of Bartolus, belonged to the family of the Alfani.

## Connection to Bartolus de Saxoferrato
Through his son, Bartolus (1314–1357), Franciscus Severi is linked to the development of civil law in Italy. Bartolus, born in Sassoferrato within the duchy of Urbino, became a professor of civil law at the university of Perugia and is recognized as the most famous master of the dialectical school of jurists. 

Bartolus's academic trajectory included studies under Cinus at Perugia, and subsequently under Jacobus de Belvisio and Oldradus at Bologna, where he earned his doctor of civil law degree in 1334. In 1343, Bartolus was appointed to a chair of civil law at the university of Perugia. His tenure there lasted many years and elevated the law school of Perugia to a status equal to that of Bologna.

## Legal Contributions of the Son
While Franciscus Severi is mentioned as the paternal figure, the archive focuses on the professional merits of his son. Bartolus is credited with reviving the exegetical system of teaching law, which had been neglected since the time of Accursius, and providing it with new life. He also integrated practical interest into his teaching, drawing from his own judicial experience as an assessor to the courts at Pisa and Todi.

Although some writers attributed Bartolus's reputation to the introduction of the dialectical method, the archive notes that this method had been used in the previous century by Odofredus, a pupil of Accursius. Bartolus's specific merit lay in employing the dialectical method advantageously while discouraging its abuse. Among his most valuable written contributions are his treatises *On Evidence* and *On Procedure*, as well as his *Commentary on the Code of Justinian*.

## Death and Legacy
Bartolus died in 1357 at Perugia. He was interred in the church of San Francisco, where a magnificent monument was placed to record his remains, bearing the simple inscription "Ossa Bartoli."
