# Boniface

## Literary Contributions
Although primarily recognized as a man of action, Boniface produced several literary works. His *Epistolae* (Letters) are considered extremely important for the study of literature, dogma, and history, though they are difficult to date. An edition of these letters was produced by Dummler in the *Monumenta Germaniae historica* in 1892.

Other writings attributed to Boniface include:
* A grammar titled *De octo partibus orationibus* (edited by Mai in *Classici Auctores*, t. vii.).
* A collection of poems known as *Aenigmata* (edited by Dummler in *Poetae latini aevi Carolini*, i. 1881).
* A penitential.
* A series of *Dicta Bonifacii* (edited by Nurnberger in *Theologische Quartalschrift*, Tubingen, vol. 70, 1888), although the authenticity of these specific texts is difficult to either prove or refute.
* Various sermons, the authenticity of which is contested.

Migne's *Patrologia Latina* (vol. 89) contains a reproduction of the works of Boniface as edited by Giles in 1844.

## Historical Studies and Biographies
Numerous monographs have been written regarding the life and various phases of Boniface, though no single work is regarded as completely satisfactory. Researchers seeking indications of these monographs may consult Ulysse Chevalier's *Bibliographie* (2nd ed.) or Potthast's *Bibliotheca medii aevi*.

Recent scholarly contributions include:
* B. Kuhlmann's *Der heilige Bonifatius, Apostel der Deutschen* (Paderborn, 1895).
* G. Kurth's *Saint Boniface* (2nd ed., 1902).
* W. Levison's edition of the *Vitae sancti Bonifatii* (Hanover, 1905).

## Other Figures Named Boniface
The name Boniface (*Bonifacius*) has been held by nine different popes. Among them, Boniface VIII is the subject of significant historical authority and literature. His registers were published by Digard, Faucon, and Thomas in Paris starting in 1884. 

Studies on Boniface VIII include H. Finke's *Aus den Tagen Bonifaz VIII.* (Munster, 1902), which is described as epoch-making despite being dreary, and R. Scholz's *Die Publizistik zur Zeit Philipps des Schonen und Bonifaz VIII.* (Stuttgart, 1903). Additionally, K. Wenck explored the question of whether Boniface VIII was a heretic in an article for von Sybel's *Historische Zeitschrift* (Munich, 1905).

Specific literature also exists regarding the works of Boniface VIII, such as *Unam Sanctum*—discussed by C. Mirbt in *Quellen zur Geschichte des Papsttums* (2nd ed., Tubingen, 1901) and in the *Kirchenlexikon* (xii, 1901)—and *Clericis laicos*, which is addressed in Gee and Hardy's *Documents Illustrative of English Church History* (London, 1896).
