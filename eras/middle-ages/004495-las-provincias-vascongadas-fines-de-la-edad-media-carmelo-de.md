# Las Provincias Vascongadas a Fines de la Edad Media

## Overview of the Basque Provinces
The Basque Provinces, known as *Provincias Vascongadas*, are located in north-eastern Spain. This division comprises three provinces: Alava, Guipuzcoa, and Biscay (also referred to as Vizcaya). Geographically, the territory forms a triangle. It is bounded to the north by France and the Bay of Biscay, to the east by Navarre, and to the south and west by the provinces of Logrono, Burgos, and Santander.

As of 1900, the region had a population of 603,596 and an area of 2,739 square miles, making it the third most densely populated area in Spain. Among the three provinces, Guipuzcoa is the only one that is entirely Basque, while Alava is the least so. The capital of Alava, Vitoria, is said to have been founded by the Gothic king Leovigild in 581.

## Historical and Tribal Foundations
The current civil provincial divisions are of uncertain date. Archive sources suggest that the ancient limits of the dioceses of Calahorra, Bayonne, and Pamplona likely corresponded more closely to the boundaries of ancient tribes than the modern provinces do. These tribes included the Vascones, the Varduli, the Caristi, and the Autrigones, who possessed differing dialects.

## Historiography and Archival Research
The history of the Basque Provinces has been documented through various specialized works and rigorous archival study. D. Carmelo de Echegaray, serving as the Cronista de las Provincias Vascongadas, worked alongside other historians—including F. Soraluce and D. Serapio Mugica—to examine, index, and catalogue the municipal archives of all towns. This effort led to the discovery of several important manuscripts and missing documents.

Key literary contributions to the study of the region include:
*   **Legislative and Rights Records:** *El Guipuzcoano instruido* (1780) provides a dictionary-style account of the duties, rights, and obligations of Basque citizens. The *fueros* (local laws) have been analyzed in works by Cayetano Manrique and A. Marichalar, Marques de Montesa. Additionally, D. Pablo de Gorosabel’s *Noticia de las cosas memorables de Guipuzcoa* includes legislative acts up to May 1900, with the final volume contributed by C. de Echegaray.
*   **Regional and Natural History:** D. Guillermo Bowles wrote on the Basque Provinces in his 1775 *Introduccion a la Historia Natural, y a la Geografia Fisica de Espana*. R. P. M. de Larramendi, S.J., authored the *Corografia de Guipuzcoa* (c. 1750), which is considered indispensable for understanding the customs and manners of the people.
*   **Specific Provincial Histories:** Dr. E. de Labayru wrote a *Historia de Bizcaya*, which was later summarized in a *Compendio* by Fermin Herran in 1903.
*   **General Surveys:** The *Diccionario Geografico-Historico de Espana* (1802) contains local information on Guipuzcoa, Alava, and the Lordship of Vizcaya, though it is noted for a strong bias toward the central government.

## Economic and Social Development
The region's industrial history is highlighted by the development of its mining sector. Specifically, the progress of the iron mines in the province of Vizcaya from 1870 to 1899 is fully described in *Las Minas de hierro de la provincia de Vizcaya*.
