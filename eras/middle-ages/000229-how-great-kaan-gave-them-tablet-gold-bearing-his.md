# The Tablets of Authority of the Great Kaan

In the administration of the Mongol Empire, the Great Kaan utilized specific physical tokens known as Tablets of Authority (also referred to as *Paizah* or *Páïzah*) to denote rank, grant privileges, and issue imperial commands. These tablets served as official credentials that ensured the bearer's status and requirements were recognized throughout the Kaan's dominions.

## The Golden Tablet and Diplomatic Envoys
The Great Kaan employed golden tablets as high-level diplomatic instruments. When the Kaan charged two brothers with a commission to act as his envoys to the Pope, he provided them with a Tablet of Gold. This specific tablet bore inscriptions ordering that the three ambassadors be supplied with everything necessary in every country they traversed, including escorts and horses, as well as any other requirements they might have.

Similarly, upon the eventual departure of Messer Marco and the two brothers to return to their own country, the Prince presented them with two golden Tablets of Authority. These tablets were intended to secure their liberty of passage across all his dominions and guaranteed that all necessaries would be provided for them and their entire company, according to their choices.

## Hierarchy and Classification of Tablets
The Tablets of Authority were strictly ordered according to the rank of the officer holding them. The material and design of the tablet indicated the bearer's level of command:

*   **Captain of 100:** Held a tablet made of silver.
*   **Captain of 1,000:** Held a tablet made of gold or silver-gilt.
*   **Commander of 10,000:** Held a tablet of gold featuring an engraved lion's head.

The weight of these tablets also varied by rank. Tablets for captains of 100 and 1,000 each weighed 120 *saggi*. The tablet for the commander of 10,000, distinguished by the lion's head, weighed 220 *saggi*.

## Inscriptions and Accompanying Warrants
Each tablet featured a specific device inscribed upon it, which stated: “By the strength of the great God, and of the great grace which He hath accorded to our Emperor, may the name of the Kaan be blessed; and let all such as will not obey him be slain and be destroyed.”

In addition to the physical tablet, holders received written warrants (referred to in some sources as *Yarlígh* or *Iarlekh*). These written patents declared the specific powers and privileges granted to the bearer. In some instances, these warrants were accompanied by red seals or stamps.

## Use as Rewards and Ritual Objects
The Great Kaan used the granting of higher-degree Tablets of Authority as a means of rewarding valor. Following the defeat of Nayan, the Kaan advanced barons according to their deserts; those who had behaved well in battle were promoted in rank (such as a captain of 1,000 being made a captain of 10,000) and were given Tablets of Authority of a higher degree than they previously held.

Beyond administrative and military use, tablets also appeared in ritual contexts. During the Great Kaan's festivals, after the company performed four repetitions of adoration—bowing until their foreheads touched the earth—they approached a highly decorated altar. This altar held a censer of gold and a vermilion tablet inscribed with the name of the Grand Kaan, which the attendees would incense with great reverence.
