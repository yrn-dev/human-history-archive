# Aleppo (1131)

The events surrounding Aleppo in 1131 are primarily characterized by the military conflict between the forces of the city and the leadership of Antioch.

## The Defeat of Bohemund II
In 1131, Bohemund II was defeated and slain on his northern frontier. The attack was carried out by a Mahommedan army originating from Aleppo. Bohemund II, the son of Bohemund and Constance of France, had been born in 1108. He had arrived in Antioch from Apulia in 1126, a city that had been under the regency of Baldwin II since 1119 following the fall of Roger, the successor of Tancred. 

Prior to his death in 1131, Bohemund II had married Alice, the younger daughter of Baldwin, in 1127. His brief career included joining Baldwin II in an attack on Damascus in 1127 and experiencing difficulties with Joscelin of Edessa. Historical assessments of Bohemund II note that he demonstrated the courage of his father, though his death in 1131 prevented him from potentially demonstrating other qualities possessed by the first Bohemund.

## Regional Political Context
The conflict in 1131 occurred within a broader landscape of instability in northern Syria. The region was marked by frequent fighting and the influence of various princes and commanders. Kasim Aksonkor served as the Prince of Mosul and Aleppo, appearing in records as early as 1126. 

The broader struggle for power in the area involved the Atabeks, Turkish commanders who acted as "Father of the Prince." One such figure, Zenghi, eventually established military fame through thirty campaigns in the service of the sultan and caliph. Zenghi was invested with the command of Mosul and Aleppo, positioning himself as a champion to avenge the cause of the prophet. He successfully stormed the city of Edessa after a twenty-five-day siege and recovered conquests beyond the Euphrates from the Franks.

## Related Figures and Governance
The military environment of the early 12th century was also shaped by Baldwin II, who served as the count of Edessa (1100–1118) and later as the king of Jerusalem (1118–1131). Baldwin II's reign was characterized by nearly constant warfare in northern Syria. From his base in Edessa, he conducted numerous forays against Mahommedan princes, facing attacks from Mosul and Mardin. In 1114, he repelled an attack by Aksunkur of Mosul, and in 1115, he assisted in defeating Aksunkur at Danith. Baldwin II is credited with extending the kingdom of Jerusalem to its widest limits during his thirteen-year reign.

## Geography of the Aleppo Vilayet
Aleppo served as a central hub for the surrounding region. To the north lies the town of Killis, located in the Aleppo vilayet approximately 60 miles from the city. Killis is situated in a fertile plain known for producing some of the finest olive oil in Syria and sits on the carriage-road connecting Aleppo to Birejik and Aintab. 

Birejik, also located in the Aleppo vilayet, is a town in North-West Mesopotamia built on a limestone cliff on the left bank of the Euphrates. It serves as a critical crossing point on the road from Aleppo to Mosul, Diarbekr, and Urfa. Historically known as Apamea-Zeugma, Birejik commanded the crossing with a strong castle known as Kala Beda.
