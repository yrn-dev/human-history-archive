# Mediate Theological Positions

In the history of theology, several figures and schools of thought have occupied "mediate" or intermediate positions, seeking to bridge the gap between opposing extreme views or rival schools of doctrine. These positions often emerge as attempts to reconcile conflicting interpretations of faith, law, or scripture.

## Ash'ari and Islamic Theology
Abu-l Hasan 'Ali ibn Isma'il ul-Ash'ari (873-935) was an Arabian theologian born in Basra who spent most of his life in Bagdad. Born into an orthodox family, he initially became a pupil of the Mu'tazalite teacher al-Jubba'i and remained a Mu'tazalite until he reached the age of forty. In 912, Ash'ari returned to the faith of his fathers. 

He became a distinguished champion of this faith by utilizing the philosophical methods he had acquired during his time in the "school of heresy." His resulting theology occupied a mediate position between extreme views on most points. This theological approach eventually became the dominant position among the Shafi'ites. While it is said that Ash'ari wrote over a hundred works, only four or five are known to be extant.

## Mediation in Early Christianity
In the early development of Christian thought, Barnabas is noted for having played an essentially mediating role. His position was intermediate between Pauline Christianity and the Christianity of the Israelites (referred to in sources as the "Jewish" context). This intermediate stance was a characteristic common among Christian Hellenists. Due to this position, Barnabas is spoken of with respect in the Clementines, whereas Paul is discountenanced for being a radical in relation to the Law.

## German "Mediation" Theology
In the 19th century, a specific movement known as "mediation" theology (*Vermittelungstheologie*) emerged in Germany. Gottfried Christian Friedrich Lücke (1791-1855), a German theologian who taught at the universities of Bonn and Göttingen, was a central figure in this movement. In 1827, Lücke helped found the *Theologische Studien und Kritiken*, which served as the primary organ for mediation theology.

Other German theologians of the era also navigated intermediate paths between opposing schools:
* **Bretschneider:** His dogmatic position was intermediate between the extreme school of naturalists (including Julius Wegscheider, J.F. Röhr, and Heinrich Paulus) and the positions held by F.C. Baur and D.F. Strauss. Bretschneider identified himself as a "rational supernaturalist," recognizing a supernatural element in the Bible while still allowing for rational inquiry.
* **Isaac August Dorner (1809-1884):** A German Lutheran divine and professor at several universities, including Tübingen, Kiel, Konigsberg, Bonn, Gottingen, and Berlin. Dorner wrote the *Entwicklungsgeschichte der Lehre von der Person Christi* (published in parts between 1835 and 1839), which served as an indirect reply to the work of his colleague, Strauss.

## Political and Ecclesiastical Mediation
Mediation has also occurred at the intersection of state governance and theology. Marcian, the emperor of the East from 450 to 457, attempted to mediate between rival schools of theology during the Council of Chalcedon in 451.

In later German state churches, the coexistence of liberal (rationalist) and conservative (orthodox) theologies created a landscape where rigid interpretations of theological formulas were avoided. This was facilitated by ecclesiastical unions that combined different confessions under a common government, allowing for a divergence in doctrinal positions to exist side by side.
