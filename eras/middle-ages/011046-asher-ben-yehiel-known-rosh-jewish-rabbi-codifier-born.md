# ASHER BEN-YEHIEL

Asher ben-Yehiel, commonly known as Rosh, was a Jewish codifier and rabbi who lived from approximately 1250 until 1327. Born in the Rhine district, he later relocated to Spain after being endangered by persecutions directed at German Jews during the 13th century. Upon his arrival in Spain, he was appointed as the rabbi of Toledo.

## Life and Philosophy
The transition to Spain was marked by financial hardship, as his enforced exile left him impoverished. This period of his life coincided with a significant shift in the professional status of medieval rabbis. Prior to the 14th century, rabbis typically supported themselves through secular professions—most notably medicine—and did not receive a salary for their rabbinic duties.

A disciple of Meir of Rothenburg, Asher ben-Yehiel was characterized by a narrow and profound austere piety. His intellectual interests were focused solely on the Talmud. Because of this focus, he was a determined opponent of the study of philosophy. This stance placed him in opposition to the prevailing Spanish spirit of the time, as the Jews of Spain remained devoted to rabbinical lore as well as the secular sciences.

## Literary Contributions and Systematization
Asher ben-Yehiel was the first among German rabbis to demonstrate a strong talent for systematization. His primary work, a *Compendium* of the Talmud compiled between 1307 and 1314, functioned as a compendium. This work largely superseded the previous work of 'Al-phasi. 

The *Compendium* is distinguished from earlier similar works by its greater simplicity and the deference it showed toward German authorities. Due to its utility, the *Compendium* is included in most editions of the Talmud.

## Legacy and Family
The influence of Asher ben-Yehiel extended through his son, Jacob, who died in Toledo before 1340. Jacob authored the four *Turim*, a popular and profound codification of rabbinical law. The *Turim* served as the standard code of law until it became the direct basis for the widely accepted Code of Jewish law known as the *Shulhan 'Arukh*, authored by Joseph Qaro.
