# George of Podebrad

George of Podebrad was the only Hussite king of Bohemia. He is remembered by his countrymen as one of the rulers of Bohemia whose memory has most endeared itself to them, alongside Charles IV.

## Rise to Power and Early Reign
Before ascending to the throne, George of Podebrad served as the leader of the national party of nobles. This party emerged during a period of succession disputes following the deaths of King Sigismund (1437) and his son-in-law, Albert of Austria. The Bohemian nobility was divided between the national party and the Roman or Austrian division, led by Ulrich of Rosenberg. These factions eventually reached an agreement called the "Letter of Peace" (_list mirny_), in which they pledged to support John of Rokycan as archbishop of Prague and recognize the Compacts. As part of this agreement, the national party abandoned the candidacy of Prince Casimir of Poland for the throne.

During the more prosperous early part of his reign, King George's policy was based on a firm alliance with Matthias Corvinus, the king of Hungary. Through the influence of Matthias, George was crowned by the Romanist bishop of Waitzen. His primary support base consisted of the towns and the men of the smaller nobility. During this period, George intended to establish a national dynasty.

## Conflict with the Papacy and Internal Strife
Over time, George became involved in a protracted struggle with the papal see, fueled in part by the influence of hostile Romanist nobles. This conflict hindered George's broader ambitions, including an attempt to obtain supremacy over Germany. After negotiations with Rome failed, George assembled the estates at Prague in 1452. There, he declared his readiness to risk his crown and his life to defend his faith, vowing to remain true to the communion in both kinds until his death.

Hostility from the Romanist party intensified, leading many nobles of the Roman religion to form a confederacy against the king at a meeting at Zelena Hora (Grunberg) in 1465. In 1466, Pope Paul II provided moral support to these confederates by releasing all Bohemians from their oath of allegiance to George and pronouncing a sentence of excommunication against him.

## War with Matthias Corvinus
Under papal influence, King Matthias of Hungary deserted his former alliance with George and supported the league of Zelena Hora. This led to a period of desultory warfare. While George was initially successful, the tide turned when Matthias invaded Moravia and captured its capital, Brunn. On May 3, 1469, at a meeting of Catholic nobles in Olmutz, Moravia, Matthias was proclaimed king of Bohemia.

Despite some subsequent successes against his rival, George of Podebrad died in 1471, which temporarily halted the war.

## Succession and Legacy
In his later years, the struggle against Matthias Corvinus led George to seek aid from Poland. He offered the succession of the Bohemian throne to Vladislav (also known as Wladislaus or Ladislaus), the son of King Casimir of Poland, though no formal agreement was finalized.

Following George's death, the Bohemian nobility was split; many supported the claim of Matthias of Hungary, who had already been proclaimed king. However, the national party, driven by strong anti-clerical sentiment, sought a candidate who would maintain the Compacts. On May 27, 1471, Prince Vladislav of Poland was elected king at Kutna Hora.

George's military legacy includes the defeat of a German army, similar to the victory the Hussites had achieved at the pass of Neumark in 1431. Additionally, his lineage continued to play a role in Bohemian governance; in 1522, King Louis appointed Charles of Munsterberg, a grandson of King George, as the regent of Bohemia.
