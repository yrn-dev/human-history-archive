# Walter VI, Count of Brienne

## Early Life and Military Service
Walter VI was the son of Walter V, who served as the duke of Athens and count of Brienne and of Lecce (Apulia). Walter V had previously fought against the Greeks, initially driving them from Thessaly, though he was eventually killed near Lake Copais in 1311. Following in his father's footsteps, Walter VI attempted to reconquer Athens in 1331, but this effort proved vain. Subsequently, he entered the service of Philip of Valois, fighting against the English.

## Political Career in Florence
Walter VI's career extended into the Italian republics, specifically Florence. After successfully defending Florence against the Pisans, he managed to obtain dictatorial powers for himself within the republic. However, his tenure was marked by tyrannical conduct, which ultimately led to his expulsion from the city.

## Final Years and Death
Following his time in Italy, Walter VI was appointed as the constable of France by John the Good. His military career ended in 1356, when he was killed during the battle of Poitiers.

## Succession and the House of Brienne
The countship of Brienne passed to Walter VI's sister and heiress, Isabelle. Through her marriage to Walter of Enghien, the house of Enghien acquired Brienne. Later, John of Luxemburg-St Pol (who died around 1397) became the count of Brienne through his marriage to Margaret of Enghien. The house of Luxemburg maintained control of the countship until Margaret Charlotte of Luxemburg sold it to a man named Marpon. In 1640, Marpon ceded the countship to Henri Auguste de Loménie, whose wife, Louise de Béon, was a descendant of the house of Luxemburg-Brienne.

The Limousin house of Loménie subsequently produced several prominent statesmen. Among them was the celebrated cardinal Étienne Charles de Loménie de Brienne (1727-1794), who served as a minister to Louis XV. The final lords of Brienne were members of the Loménie family.
