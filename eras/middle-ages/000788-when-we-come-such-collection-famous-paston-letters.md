# Paston Letters

The Paston Letters are a famous collection of correspondence belonging to the Paston family of Norfolk. Dating from the 15th century, these documents serve as a significant historical resource for the study of that era.

## Historical Significance and Autographs
The collection is particularly noted for its value in the study of historical handwriting. While the autographs of famous men from the early middle ages are rare, difficult to identify, or often merely copies—such as certain charters at Canterbury attributed to Dunstan—the Paston Letters provide a different case. Within this collection, there are numerous autographs of historical personages from the 15th century.

This distinguishes the collection from later traditions of autograph collecting. For instance, in the days of Shakespeare, the practice of collecting autographs was not yet in vogue in England. Later collections, such as those gathered by Sir Robert Cotton (now in the British Museum) or Philippe de Bethune, Comte de Selles et Charost, were assembled primarily for their literary or historic interest rather than as mere specimens of handwriting. The Paston Letters align with this historical utility, providing authentic primary evidence of the period.

## Scholarly Use and Bibliography
The Paston Letters are cited as a key source for various historical inquiries, including those related to the peerage and legal records. They are listed alongside other primary materials such as the Rolls of Parliament, pipe rolls, liberate rolls, and fine rolls, as well as the chronicles of Roger of Hoveden, Giraldus Cambrensis, and Matthew Paris.

Modern scholarship has further analyzed the collection through detailed introductions. Dr. Gairdner published an introduction to the *Paston Letters* in 1904. His work is regarded as one of the best modern accounts for those researching the period, often cited alongside other authorities such as Sir James Ramsay's *Lancaster and York* (1892) and Professor C. Oman's *The Political History of England* (1906).
