# Villeneuve-les-Avignon and Avignon

## Geography and Infrastructure
Avignon is a city located in south-eastern France and serves as the capital of the department of Vaucluse. It is situated on the left bank of the Rhone river, a few miles above the point where the Rhone meets the Durance. The city is positioned 143 miles south of Lyons on the railway line connecting Lyons and Marseilles. As of 1906, the population of the city was 35,356.

The city occupies a large, oval-shaped area that is not fully populated, as much of the land is covered by gardens and parks. Connectivity to the opposite bank of the river is provided by a suspension bridge that leads to Villeneuve-les-Avignon. Further up the river, there is the Pont Saint-Benezet, a picturesque ruined bridge dating from the 12th century. Of the original eighteen piles of this bridge, only four remain; one of these piles supports the chapel of Saint-Benezet, which is a small Romanesque building.

## Fortifications and Urban Layout
Avignon is encircled by ramparts constructed by the popes during the 14th century. These walls are considered one of the finest examples of medieval fortification currently in existence. The strong walls feature machicolated battlements and are flanked at intervals by thirty-nine massive towers. There are several gateways piercing the walls, three of which were built in the 14th century. A line of pleasant boulevards surrounds the entire fortification.

The primary activity of the town is concentrated in the Cours de la Republique and the Place de l'Hotel de Ville. The Cours de la Republique extends from the Place de l'Hotel de Ville to the ramparts. Outside of these areas, the streets are generally quiet and narrow, and for the most part, they are badly paved.

## Landmarks and Architecture
At the northern edge of the town, the Rocher des Doms is a precipitous rock rising from the river's edge, forming a plateau that extends south toward the Place de l'Hotel de Ville. The summit of this plateau houses a public garden.

Located south of the public garden are the Palace of the Popes and the cathedral of Notre-Dame des Doms. The cathedral is a Romanesque structure dating primarily from the 12th century. Its most notable external feature is a gilded statue of the Virgin located on the western tower. Inside the cathedral, the most beautiful work of art is the mausoleum of Pope John XXII, which is a 14th-century masterpiece of Gothic carving.

The Palace of the Popes is a sombre collection of buildings that dwarfs the cathedral. The palace covers an area of more than 1 1/4 acres.

## Regional History and Figures
The region has been associated with various religious and intellectual figures. Jacques Bridaine (1701-1767), a French Roman Catholic preacher, was educated in Avignon at the Sulpician seminary of St Charles and the Jesuit college. After his ordination in 1725, he served as a missionary preacher for over forty years across southern and central France. He died on December 22, 1767, at Roquemaure, near Avignon. His sermons were published in five volumes at Avignon in 1823.

Additionally, the city has been the subject of various historical texts, including works by J. B. Joudou, J. F. Andre, and A. Canron, as well as the *Istoria della citta d'Avignone e del Contado Venesino* by Fantoni Castrucci.
