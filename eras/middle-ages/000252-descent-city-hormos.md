# OF THE DESCENT TO THE CITY OF HORMOS

The journey to the city of Hormos involves a transition from high plains through perilous terrain to a coastal trade center. The route is characterized by extreme environmental shifts, ranging from fertile plains to dangerous descents and intense heat.

## The Route to Hormos
The approach to the city begins after a five-day journey across a plain. Following this, travelers encounter a descent approximately twenty miles in length. This specific stretch of road is described as very bad and full of peril due to the presence of many bad characters and robbers. 

At the foot of this descent lies the Plain of Formosa, a beautiful region that extends for two days' journey. This plain is noted for its fine streams of water and an abundance of fruit-trees, specifically date-palms. The area is also home to various beautiful birds, including popinjays and francolins, species not found in the author's own country. After two days of riding across this plain, travelers reach the Ocean Sea, where the city of Hormos is situated on the shore.

## The City of Hormos
Hormos is a capital city featuring a harbor and serves as a center of immense trade. It is a primary destination for merchants from India, who arrive in ships loaded with a variety of precious wares, including:
* Spicery and precious stones
* Pearls
* Gold and silk cloths
* Elephants' teeth

The merchants of Hormos purchase these goods and subsequently distribute them throughout the world. While the city is the capital, there are numerous villages and towns located under its jurisdiction. The city is ruled by King Ruomedam Ahomet, who is a liegeman of the King of Kerman.

## Climate and Environment
Hormos is described as a very sickly place characterized by tremendous solar heat. The summer heat is so extreme that residents avoid living within the city, preferring to sleep in country gardens where there are streams and plenty of water.

A particularly dangerous phenomenon is a wind that blows across the sands encompassing the plain during the summer. This wind is so intolerably hot that it is considered lethal. To survive its arrival, residents plunge into water up to their necks and remain there until the wind ceases. 

The severity of this heat is illustrated by an account of a force of 5,000 foot soldiers and 1,600 horsemen sent by the King of Kerman to collect unpaid tribute from the Lord of Hormos. Due to a guide's error, the army was forced to bivouac in a wilderness near Hormos. They were caught by the hot wind and suffocated; not one man survived. When the people of Hormos later attempted to bury the bodies, they found the corpses so "baked" by the heat that the arms parted from the trunks when they were dragged.

## Local Customs and Agriculture
The inhabitants of the region are black and are worshippers of Mahommet. Their agricultural cycle is dictated by the climate: wheat, barley, and other corn are sown in November and reaped in March. Dates are gathered in May. Outside of these periods, the excessive heat dries up all grass and greenery.

The people observe elaborate mourning rituals. When a person dies, women mourn their husbands for four years. This involves gathering friends, kinsfolk, and neighbors at least once a day for weeping and wailing. There are also professional mourners who perform these services for hire.

## Return to Kerman
The return journey from Hormos to the city of Kerman passes through fine plains and features many natural hot baths. These baths are said to possess excellent virtues, including the ability to cure several diseases and the itch. The road is populated with partridges and towns where dates, other fruits, and victuals are cheap and abundant. However, the wheaten bread in this region is noted for being very bitter due to the bitterness of the water, making it difficult for those unaccustomed to it to eat.
