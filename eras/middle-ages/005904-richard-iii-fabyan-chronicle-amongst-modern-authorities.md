# Richard III and Fabyan's Chronicle

## Historical Authorities on Richard III
The study of the life of the 2nd duke of Buckingham relies upon several primary historical sources. The chief original authorities include Fabyan's *Chronicle*, Sir Thomas More's *Richard III*, and the *Continuation of the Croyland Chronicle*. For those seeking modern authorities on the subject, the archive recommends consulting Sir J. Ramsay's *Lancaster and York* and J. Gairdner's *Richard III*.

## Fabyan's Chronicle
Robert Fabyan authored *The New Chronicles of England and France*, which was edited by H. Ellis and published in London in 1811. This work serves as a source for various historical figures and events. For instance, Fabyan's *Chronicle* preserves a couplet by Collingbourne on page 672. 

Beyond its use in the study of the 2nd duke of Buckingham, Fabyan's *Chronicles* (1516) served as one of the main sources for Christopher Marlowe's play *Edward II*, alongside the *Chronicles* of Stow (1580) and Holinshed (1577).

## Related Figures and Events
The period surrounding Richard III involved various political maneuvers and conflicts. Francis Lovell, for example, fought for Richard at the battle of Bosworth. Lovell had previously been in command of the fleet intended to prevent Henry Tudor from landing in 1485. Following the battle, Lovell fled to sanctuary at Colchester and later escaped the following year to organize a revolt in Yorkshire. 

After the failure of the Yorkshire revolt, Lovell fled to Flanders to join Margaret of Burgundy. As a primary leader of the Yorkist party, he participated in the enterprise of Lambert Simnel, accompanying the pretender to Ireland with John de la Pole, earl of Lincoln. Lovell fought for Simnel at Stoke on June 16, 1487. While he was seen escaping that battle, his ultimate fate remained uncertain for centuries; one report mentioned he lived in a vault or cave. However, in 1708, a skeleton was discovered in a secret chamber at the family mansion in Minster Lovell, Oxfordshire, leading to the supposition that Lovell died there of starvation.

## Literary Influence
The figure of Richard III is also noted in the context of English drama. Christopher Marlowe's work established a type of "gigantic" cartoon-figure that recurred through subsequent drama, extending from *Richard III* to the heroes of Dryden. While Marlowe's influence on form and diction was significant—impacting writers such as Milton—Shakespeare later modulated Marlowe's type of chronicle tragedy in his own work, *Richard II*.
