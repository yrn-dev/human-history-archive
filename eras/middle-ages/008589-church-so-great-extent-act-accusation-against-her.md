## Ecclesiastical Conflicts and Religious Trials

In the early Christian era, the Church experienced significant internal divisions, particularly during the Arian controversy. Athanasius, who became the successor to Bishop Alexander on February 8, 326, faced numerous accusations after the council of Nicaea failed to compose the divisions in the Church. Because he opposed the emperor Constantine's demand that Arius be readmitted to communion, Athanasius was charged with murder, sorcery, and cruelty. Specifically, it was reported that he had unlawfully put to death Arsenius, a bishop of the Meletian party in the Thebaid. While Athanasius cleared himself of these charges, he was later summoned to a council at Tyre in 335. When he appealed to the emperor in Constantinople, his accusers—including Eusebius of Nicomedia and Eusebius of Caesarea—shifted to a political charge, claiming Athanasius had threatened to stop Alexandrian corn-ships bound for Constantinople.

In later periods, the Church of Rome faced conflict in Bohemia during the reign of Wenceslas IV. The hold of the Church was weakened by priests like Waldhauser and Milic, who attacked the immorality of the clergy. This tension escalated during a church schism where rival pontiffs exchanged theological threats. Consequently, much of the Bohemian nation supported Huss. After being given a letter of safe conduct by Sigismund, Huss was tried before the council of Constance, declared a heretic, and burnt on July 6, 1415. This led to a civil war and the *Protestatio Bohemorum* on September 2, 1415, in which Bohemian and Moravian nobles defended Huss as a "good, just and catholic man" and declared those who claimed heresy existed in Bohemia to be "sons of the devil" and "liars."

Other religious accusations involved issues of apostasy and morality. In the East, King George, a Nestorian Christian who converted to the Catholic faith under John of Monte Corvino, was accused of apostasy by other Nestorians. In another instance, a man named Felicissimus was condemned by a synod of bishops for embezzlement of public money, adultery, and the debauching of virgins.

## Political and State Accusations

The archive records instances where accusations were used as tools of state power or political survival. In the Roman era, the ruler Caius encouraged slaves to accuse their masters of any crimes they pleased, often at his own suggestion. This led to widespread misery and included an accusation by a slave named Pollux against his own master, Claudius.

In ancient Greece, legal disputes often centered on the reliability of testimony. Theramenês and the generals engaged in mutual accusations; the generals claimed they had given specific orders to Theramenês and Thrasybulus, while Theramenês argued in his defense before the Thirty that the generals had falsely asserted this to discredit his own accusations against them. He maintained that a violent storm had made the rescue of drowning men impossible. Additionally, an Athenian named Andokides was accused of selling his cousin—the daughter of Aristeides—to the despot of Kitium for a cargo of wheat. Though he later brought her back to Athens to avoid prosecution by the Athenian Dikastery, he was detected by the prince and imprisoned.

## Private Legal and Financial Disputes

Civil disputes over inheritance and theft are also documented. In March 1328, Marcolino (Marco Polo of St. John Chrysostom) represented a claim before the *Domini Advocatores* of the Republic. He alleged that *imprestita* belonging to the late Maffeo Polo the Elder had been alienated in May 1318 by the late Marco Polo and his heirs, ignoring a will from February 6, 1308 (or 1309) that bequeathed Marcolino 1000 *lire*. The *Advocatores* ruled the transfer was unjust and ordered it revoked.

In a separate matter, Lady Donata appeared before the Council of Forty regarding a mandate of sequestration. Two sealed bags of money, totaling about 80 *lire* of grossi (300 *l.* in silver value), were left in her custody. Upon opening, only 45 *lire* and 22 *grossi* (about 170 *l.*) were found. Accused of abstracting the balance *non bono modo*, she was ordered by the Council to provide restitution and pay a fine of 200 *lire* as an example to others.

## International Law and War Crimes

In the context of global conflict, the archive notes that persons accused of violating the laws and customs of war are subject to military tribunals. If the accusations involve nationals of a single state, that state's tribunal handles the trial; if multiple states are involved, joint tribunals are used. Following a major war, Germany was required to hand over all accused persons and incriminating documents to associated Governments to ensure the discovery of offenders. Germany also accepted responsibility for loss and damage caused by the aggression of itself and its allies, with total obligations to be determined by an interallied Reparation Commission by May 1, 1921.
