# Labourt's Edition and Translation of the Exposition of the Liturgy

## Overview
The *Exposition of the Liturgy* is a liturgical treatise written by Bar-Salib (also known as Jacob or Dionysius), a prominent 12th-century writer within the Syrian Jacobite church. The systematic editing of Bar-Salib's collective works began in 1903 with the publication of the edition and translation of this specific text by H. Labourt in Paris.

## Author Background
Bar-Salib was a native of Malatia on the Upper Euphrates. His ecclesiastical career included several significant appointments:
* **1154:** Created bishop of Mar'ash by patriarch Athanasius VIII.
* **1155:** Added the diocese of Mabbog to his responsibilities.
* **1166:** Transferred to the metropolitan see of Amid in Mesopotamia by Michael I, the successor to Athanasius.

Bar-Salib remained at the see of Amid until his death in 1171. He is recognized as an able theologian characterized by a combination of vigour and terseness in his arguments.

## Literary Contributions and Style
Bar-Salib was a voluminous writer whose works include epistles, homilies, liturgical treatises, and a treatise against heretics (which contains polemics against the Mahommedans and the Bani Israel). He is perhaps most noted for his exhaustive commentaries on the New and Old Testaments. In these commentaries, he summarized and interwoven interpretations from previous writers, including:
* Ephrem
* Chrysostom
* Cyril
* John of Dara
* Moses Bar-Kepha

Regarding his originality, Bar-Salib did not typically provide detailed indications of the sources for his interpretations and claimed to be a compiler, particularly within his commentaries. His work on the Gospels was utilized to some extent by Dudley Loftus during the 17th century.

## Related Scholarly Editions
Following the 1903 publication of the *Exposition of the Liturgy* by H. Labourt, other works by Bar-Salib were edited and translated by other scholars:
* **Commentaries on the Gospels:** Edited and translated by J. B. Chabot and J. Sedlavek (Fasc. I, Paris, 1906).
* **Treatise against the Bani Israel:** The Syriac text was edited by J. de Zwaan in Leiden (1906).

Further details regarding the manuscripts of his works have been provided by W. Wright in *Syriac Literature*, and copious extracts and accounts of his writings were documented by Assemani in *Bibl. Orient*.
