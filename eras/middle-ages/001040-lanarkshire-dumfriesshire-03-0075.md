# South-Western Scotland: Ayrshire, Dumfriesshire, and Kirkcudbrightshire

The south-western region of Scotland comprises several counties, including Ayrshire, Dumfriesshire, and Kirkcudbrightshire. These areas are characterized by diverse landscapes ranging from rugged mountains and desolate highlands to fertile holms and undulating plains.

## Ayrshire
Ayrshire is a south-western county bounded by Renfrewshire to the north, Lanarkshire and Dumfriesshire to the east, Kirkcudbrightshire to the south-east, Wigtownshire to the south, and the Firth of Clyde to the west. The county covers an area of 1,142 square miles (724,523 acres) with a coastline extending 70 miles. Off its coast lie Horse Island (off Ardrossan), Lady Island (3 miles south-west of Troon), and the rock of Ailsa Craig (10 miles west of Girvan).

Historically, the shire was divided into three districts: Cunninghame (north of the Irvine), Kyle (between the Irvine and the Doon), and Carrick (south of the Doon). Kyle was further split by the Ayr river into Kyle Stewart and King's Kyle. Politically, the county is divided into North Ayrshire (Cunninghame) and South Ayrshire (Kyle and Carrick). Robert Bruce once held the title of earl of Carrick, a title currently held by the prince of Wales.

The terrain is generally undulating, featuring a small mountainous tract in the north and a larger one in the south and south-east. Notable peaks include Black Craig (2,298 ft.), Enoch (1,865 ft.), Polmaddie (1,750 ft.), Corsancone (1,547 ft.), and Stake, located on the border with Renfrewshire. The county's six most noted rivers are the Garnock, Irvine, Ayr, Doon, Girvan, and Stinchar. The Ayr is the longest, rising at Glenbuck on the Lanarkshire border and flowing 38 miles to the Firth of Clyde at the county town. Other significant water bodies include Loch Doon, which is 5.5 miles long and serves as the source of the river Doon, and Loch Finlas.

Communications in the shire are primarily managed by the Glasgow & South-Western railway, which connects industrial towns, ports, and resorts. Its main line via Kilmarnock links to Carlisle and Dumfries, while its trunk line via Girvan provides the shortest sea passage to the north of Ireland and Belfast via Stranraer. Ardrossan serves as the principal port for passenger steamers to Belfast, Arran, and Douglas in the Isle of Man.

## Dumfriesshire
Dumfriesshire is a border county bounded by the Solway Firth to the south, Cumberland to the south-east, Roxburghshire to the east, Lanark, Peebles, and Selkirk to the north, and Ayrshire and Kirkcudbrightshire to the west. It has an area of 1,072 square miles (686,302 acres) and a 21-mile coastline. The landscape slopes from northern mountains down to the sea, featuring tableland and fertile holms. Reclaimed moss lands, such as Nutberry Moss in the east and Lochar Moss, Longbridge Moor, and Craigs Moss in the west, are found near the Solway.

The highest mountains are located near the northern boundaries, including White Coomb (2,695 ft.), Hart Fell (2,651 ft.), Saddle Yoke (2,412 ft.), Swatte Fell (2,389 ft.), Lowther Hills (2,377 ft.), Queensberry (2,285 ft.), and Ettrick Pen (2,269 ft.). The county is cleft from north to south by three great dales: Eskdale, Annandale, and Nithsdale. The three longest rivers are the Esk, the Annan, and the Nith. The Nith, totaling 65 miles in length, rises near Enoch Hill in Ayrshire and flows south-easterly into the Solway. The Annan rises near the Devil's Beef Tub and flows 40 miles to Barnkirk headland. The Esk is formed by the confluence of the Black Esk (rising near Jock's Shoulder) and the White Esk (rising near Ettrick Pen).

Historically, Dumfriesshire was a site of conflict during the era of Robert Bruce, who was lord of Annandale. Factions involving John Baliol and John Comyn were in constant feud. In 1593, a bloody fight occurred in Dryfesdale where the Johnstones killed 700 Maxwells. In 1680, persecuted Covenanters published a declaration against the king at Sanquhar. The county is also associated with Robert Burns, who farmed at Ellisland and spent his final five years in Dumfries, and Thomas Carlyle, who was born at Ecclefechan.

## Kirkcudbrightshire
Also known as East Galloway or the Stewartry of Kirkcudbright, this south-western county is bounded by Ayrshire to the north and north-west, Wigtownshire to the west and south-west, the Solway Firth and Irish Sea to the south and south-east, and Dumfriesshire to the east and north-east. It encompasses 899 square miles (575,565 acres) and includes the lighthouse stations of Little Ross and Hestan.

The north-western region is wild and rugged, containing the highest peak in southern Scotland, Merrick (2,764 ft.), as well as the Rinns of Kells (including Corscrine, Carlins Cairn, Meikle Millyea, and Millfire). Other heights include Cairnsmuir of Carsphairn (2,612 ft.), the Windy Standard (2,287 ft.), Lamachan (2,349 ft.), Larg (2,216 ft.), Cairnsmore of Fleet (2,331 ft.), and Criffel (1,866 ft.) in the south-east. The southern section is mostly undulating or level.

The coastline is rocky with numerous estuaries, though these are often too shallow for commerce. The region is noted for its many "burns" and "waters." Longer rivers include the Dee (or Black Water of Dee), which flows nearly 36 miles from Loch Dee to St Mary's Isle; the Cree, which flows 30 miles from Loch Moan; the Urr, which rises on the Dumfriesshire border and flows 27 miles; and the Ken, which rises on the Ayrshire border.
