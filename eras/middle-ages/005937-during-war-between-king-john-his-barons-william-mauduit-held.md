# William Mauduit and Hanslape Castle

## Conflict with King John
During the period of war between King John and his barons, William Mauduit was involved in the military struggles of the era. Mauduit held Hanslape Castle against the king. This resistance continued until 1216, at which time the castle was captured and subsequently demolished by Falkes de Bréauté.

## Regional Context of Buckinghamshire
Hanslape Castle was located in Buckinghamshire, a county whose proximity to London frequently involved it in major national events. The region had a history of conflict and confiscation dating back to the Norman invasion; at that time, Buckinghamshire was likely part of the earldom of Leofwine, son of Godwin. Because the county supported Leofwine at the battle of Hastings, it suffered sweeping confiscations following the Conquest.

## Later History of Buckinghamshire
In subsequent centuries, Buckinghamshire remained a site of political and military volatility. During the English Civil War, it was among the first counties to join an association for mutual defence on the side of the parliament. This association maintained important garrisons in locations such as Brill and Aylesbury. Conversely, royalist troops briefly garrisoned Newport Pagnell, and the king established his headquarters at Buckingham in 1644. Additionally, the county was severely impacted by the Black Death, which led to the almost entire depopulation of several districts, including Winslow.
