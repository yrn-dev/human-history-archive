# Bonifacio

Bonifacio is a maritime town located at the southern extremity of Corsica, within the arrondissement of Sartene. It is situated 87 miles south-southeast of Ajaccio by road. The town occupies a notable position on the summit of a peninsula composed of white calcareous rock, which extends parallel to the coast and encloses a secure, narrow harbour. The town overlooks the straits of the same name that separate Corsica from Sardinia. The surrounding cliffs and the area below the town feature caverns that are accessible only by boat.

## Foundation and Early History
The town was founded around 828 as a defense against Saracen pirates. Its foundation is attributed to Boniface II, the count of the Tuscan march, who bears the town's name. After conducting a successful expedition against the African Mussulmans, Boniface II returned to Corsica and constructed a fortress in the south of the island, which served as the nucleus for the town of Bonifacio. 

The conflict against the Saracens was continued by Boniface's son, Adalbert, who was restored to his father's dignities in 846. Despite these efforts, the Mussulmans appear to have maintained possession of portions of the island until approximately 930.

## Political Transitions and Sieges
The political control of Bonifacio shifted several times over the following centuries:
* **Pisan and Genoese Rule:** By the end of the 11th century, the town became subject to Pisa. At the end of the 12th century, it was taken and colonized by the Genoese. The influence of the Genoese is still evident in the character of the population.
* **The Siege of 1420:** In 1420, Bonifacio heroically withstood a protracted siege by Alphonso V of Aragon. While Alphonso's fleet succeeded in taking Calvi, Bonifacio held out. This resistance provided the necessary time for Corsicans to organize a revolt against the tyranny and exactions of the Aragonese. Consequently, the siege was raised, and Bonifacio became practically an independent republic under the protection of the Genoese, with its privileges confirmed.
* **Franco-Turkish Occupation:** In 1554, the town fell into the hands of the Franco-Turkish army.

## Architecture and Infrastructure
The town contains several significant historical buildings:
* **Religious Structures:** The cathedral of Santa Maria Maggiore dates primarily from the 12th century. St Dominic, a church built by the Templars, dates from the 13th century.
* **Defenses:** The citadel and fortifications were constructed during the 16th and 17th centuries. Additionally, a massive medieval tower is used as a powder-magazine.

## Economy and Demographics
As of 1906, the population of Bonifacio was 2,940. The town's trade is conducted primarily with Sardinia and focuses on the export of olive-oil of fine quality, cork, wine, and cereals. Local industries include tobacco-manufacture, coral-fishing, and cork-cutting. The surrounding area is characterized by the extensive cultivation of olives, supported by oil-works located within the town.
