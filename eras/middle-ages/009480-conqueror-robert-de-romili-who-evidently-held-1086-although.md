# Robert de Romili and the Manor of Bolton Abbey

## Land Grants and Tenure
The manor of Bolton Abbey, along with the broader district of Craven, was granted by William the Conqueror to Robert de Romili. While the Domesday survey does not mention the manor, it is evident that Robert de Romili held the land in 1086.

## The Foundation of Bolton Priory
The village of Bolton Abbey in the West Riding of Yorkshire takes its name from the foundation of Bolton Priory. The ruins of the priory are situated near the right bank of the upper Wharfe, in a valley characterized by hills and wooded landscapes. The architecture of the church reflects different periods: the earliest section is of transitional Norman date, while the nave is Early English and Decorated. The choir and transepts are currently in ruins, and the remaining domestic buildings are slight.

The priory was originally founded and endowed in 1120 at Emmesay (or Embsay), near Skipton, by William de Meschines and his wife, Cicely de Romili. Cicely was the heiress of Robert de Romili. In 1151, the priory was moved to its current location by Alice de Romili, the daughter of William and Cicely and wife of William FitzDuncan. Alice gave the manor to the monks in exchange for other lands.

## Later Ownership
Following the dissolution of the monasteries, the manor was sold in 1542 to Henry Clifford, the 2nd earl of Cumberland. The manor is currently held by his descendants, the dukes of Devonshire.
