# The Prayer of the One-Eyed Cobbler

The account of the one-eyed cobbler is a narrative concerning a miracle sought by Christians to preserve their faith and lives during a period of conflict with a Caliph. The events center on the spiritual purity of a humble craftsman and the divine intervention resulting from his prayers.

## The Character of the Cobbler
The cobbler is described as a man of exceptional righteousness, chastity, and holiness. His daily life was defined by strict religious devotion: he fasted, avoided all sin, attended Mass daily at church, and gave a portion of his daily earnings to God.

The cobbler's physical condition—having only one eye—was the result of a deliberate act of self-discipline to avoid sin. While taking the measure of a woman's foot and leg for a pair of shoes, the cobbler became conscious of sinful thoughts. Recalling the teaching of the Holy Evangel that one should pluck out an offending eye and cast it away rather than sin, he used his stitching awl to destroy his own eye immediately after the woman departed.

## The Vision and the Request
The necessity for the cobbler's intervention arose during a time of great dismay for the Christians. After a period of eight days and eight nights of collective prayer by men and women, a Holy Angel of Heaven appeared in a vision to a Bishop, who was noted as a very good Christian. The angel revealed that the Bishop should ask a specific Christian cobbler with one eye to pray to God, as God would grant the prayer due to the cobbler's holy life.

The Bishop shared this vision with the Christians, and they collectively called the cobbler before them. They informed him of their wish for him to pray, stating that God had promised to accomplish the matter through his means. Initially, the cobbler made many excuses, claiming he was not as good a man as they believed him to be. However, the Christians persisted with such sweetness that he eventually agreed to fulfill their request.

## The Confrontation and Prayer
The climax of the event occurred on an appointed day when more than 100,000 Christians—including men, women, and people of all ages—gathered for Holy Mass. Following the service, they formed a great procession, carrying a precious cross and singing and weeping as they moved toward a plain in front of a mountain.

Upon arrival, they were met by the Caliph and his armed Saracen host. The Saracens, who did not believe God would grant such a favor to the Christians, were prepared to slay the group if they refused to change their faith. Despite their fear and doubt, the Christians placed their hope in Jesus Christ.

After receiving a benison from the Bishop, the cobbler knelt before the Holy Cross and stretched his hands toward Heaven. He prayed to the Lord God Almighty, beseeching Him in His goodness to grant grace to His people so that they would not perish, and so that their faith would not be flouted, abused, or cast down. In his prayer, the cobbler humbly acknowledged that he was a servant full of sin and not worthy to make such a request, appealing instead to God's great power and mercy.

## The Miracle of the Mountain
The prayer of the one-eyed cobbler resulted in a miracle involving the movement of a mountain. According to the records, the prayer caused a mountain to move, an event that left the Caliph vexed and unsure of how to disprove the faith of the Christians. In related accounts of the event, the mountain of Thir was transported to the valley of Joaquin to the westward. The Caliph and his princes witnessed the mountain moving through the air, an event the Caliph exclaimed upon seeing.
