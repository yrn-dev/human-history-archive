# Baldwin III of Jerusalem

## Origins and Nature of Reign
Baldwin III was a king of Jerusalem who distinguished himself as the first monarch of that kingdom to be a native of the soil of Palestine. This stood in contrast to his three predecessors, all of whom had been emigrants from the West. His ascension marked a significant departure in the style of leadership within the kingdom. While previous kings had been characterized as a blend of the military and clerical—serving as both sound churchmen and hard fighters—Baldwin represented a subtler type of ruler. He possessed the specific capabilities required to guide the kingdom through its middle age, particularly in his ability to manage the problems of law and the various intrigues of the court.

## Intellectual and Personal Character
Like his brother, Amalric I, Baldwin III was a studious and clerkly king. He was well-versed in law and was open to discussing points of dogma. According to the accounts of William of Tyre, Baldwin spent his leisure time reading and maintained a particular affection for history. He was highly skilled in the *jus consuetudinarium* of the kingdom, which was later recorded by Philip of Novara and John of Ibelin as the "assizes of Jerusalem."

Baldwin possessed a royal faculty for remembering faces, which allowed him to address almost anyone he had met by name. This trait contributed to him being more popular with people of all social standings, both high and low, than any of the kings who came before him. He was further noted for his impromptu eloquence and a wit that allowed him to make pleasant jokes at the expense of others while remaining placid when others directed witticisms toward him.

## Moral Development and Conduct
In his youth, Baldwin had been prone to gambling and was considered somewhat lax in his morals prior to his marriage to Theodora. However, upon reaching manhood, he "put away childish things." His married life served as a shining example to his people. He practiced abstemiousness in both drink and food, based on the belief that excess in either area served as an incentive to the worst crimes. Additionally, he was known to be excessively generous without needing to make exactions to fund such generosity, and he maintained a consistent respect for the Church.

## Legacy and External Recognition
The quality of Baldwin III's leadership was recognized even by his enemies. Upon his death, Nureddin remarked that the Franks had lost a prince of such caliber that the world did not then have his like.
