# THE KINGDOM OF KERMAN

The Kingdom of Kerman is a region located within Persia. Historically, it was governed by a hereditary prince; however, following the conquest of the country by the Tartars, the rule ceased to be hereditary. Instead, the Tartar authority appoints whichever lord they please to administer the kingdom.

## Geography and Borders
Kerman is situated in a region characterized by mountains and deserts. To the south, the kingdom is linked to the city of Hormuz. To the north, the territory concludes after a desert passage of four days—noted for the presence of wild asses—where the traveler reaches the city of Cobinan. 

The geography of the region is marked by significant arid stretches. The route from Kerman to Cobinan involves waterless deserts, including the Desert of Lút. In this desert, the only available water is found in a rivulet called Shor-Rúd, which is described as green, salt, bitter, and dirty.

## Natural Resources and Wildlife
The mountains of Kerman are rich in mineral deposits. Turquoises are produced there in great abundance and are extracted directly from the rocks. The region also possesses numerous veins of steel and *Ondanique*.

Kerman is further renowned for its falcons, which are considered the best in the world. While these birds are smaller in size than the Peregrine, they are distinguished by red coloring on their breasts, under the neck, and between the thighs. They are noted for a flight so swift that no bird can escape them.

## Industry and Manufactures
The people of Kerman are highly skilled in the production of military equipment. They manufacture harness of war, including bows, quivers, swords, spurs, bridles, and saddles, all crafted according to local fashions. Historical accounts from the 16th century highlight the fine temper of Kerman steel used in lance-points and scimitars; it was claimed that a Kerman sabre could cleave a European helmet without the edge turning.

The women and daughters of the kingdom are skilled in exquisite needlework. They produce silk embroidery in various colors featuring patterns of flowers, trees, birds, and beasts. Their craftsmanship extends to the creation of cushions, quilts, pillows, and hangings for noblemen.

## Historical Administration and Dynasties
In the period following the death of Malek Shah, the unity of the Turkish empire dissolved, leading to the rise of several dynasties. One of these was the dynasty of Kerman, which commanded an extensive but obscure dominion on the shores of the Indian Ocean. This dynasty was extinguished before the end of the 12th century.

Later, around the year 1300, Kerman and Hormuz were ravaged by large bodies of Turks (Tartars) arriving from Turkestan. Due to the frequency of these visitations, the local population eventually retired to the islands of Kishm and Jerún.

## Nomenclature and Urban Centers
There has been historical confusion regarding the names of the province and its capital. The original name of the entire country was Kerman (ancient Karamania). A province within this region was known as Kúreh-i-Ardeshír (later contracted to Kuwáshír), where the first Sassanian monarch, Ardeshír Bábekán, resided. 

A part of Kúreh-i-Ardeshír was called Bardshír (or Bard-i-Ardeshír), and the city of Kerman was situated at its north-eastern corner. During the Middle Ages, the town was called Bardshír, and it served as the capital and the primary site for striking coins. Over time, the name of the city became Kerman, while the name Bardshír reverted to a district located to the south-west. Another town, Sírján (or Shírján), was also at times regarded as the capital of Kerman.
