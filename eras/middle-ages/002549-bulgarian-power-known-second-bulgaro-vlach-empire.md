# The Second Bulgarian Empire

The Second Bulgarian Empire, also referred to as the "Bulgaro-Vlach" empire, was a sovereign state established in 1186. It emerged following a general insurrection of Bulgars and Vlachs led by the brothers Peter and Ivan Asên of Trnovo. The brothers claimed descent from the Shishmanovtzi dynasty. Upon the recovery of national independence, Ivan Asên assumed the title of "Tsar of the Bulgars and Greeks."

## Capital and Territorial Expansion
The seat of the empire was located at Trnovo, a city regarded by Bulgarians as the historic capital of their race. Under the rule of the Asên monarchs, the empire saw significant territorial growth. Kaloyan, the third monarch of the Asên dynasty, extended the empire's dominions to include Skopïe (Uskub), Nish, and Belgrade. During his reign, Kaloyan acknowledged the spiritual supremacy of the pope and received a royal crown from a papal legate.

The empire reached its zenith under Ivan Asên II, who ruled from 1218 to 1241. Described as a ruler of enlightened and humane character, Ivan Asên II conducted a series of victorious campaigns that established Bulgarian sway over Thrace, Macedonia, Epirus, and Albania.

## Prosperity and Culture
Under the governance of Ivan Asên II, the nation experienced an unprecedented period of prosperity. This era was marked by the flourishing of literature, the arts, and commerce. The capital city of Trnovo was embellished and enlarged during this time. Furthermore, the period saw the endowment and foundation of numerous monasteries and churches. Ivan Asên II is noted for governing his extensive territories with wisdom, moderation, and justice.

## Dynastic Succession and Decline
The Asên dynasty became extinct in 1257, marking the beginning of a period of decadence. Following the Asêns, two other dynasties of Kuman origin held power:
* **The Terterovtzi:** This dynasty ruled from the capital at Trnovo.
* **The Shishmanovtzi:** This dynasty initially founded an independent state at Vidin before eventually reigning in the national capital.

## Fall of the Empire
The decline of Bulgarian power culminated on June 28, 1330, at the battle of Velbûzhd (Kiustendil). On this day, Tsar Michael Shishman was defeated and slain by the Servians under the leadership of Stephen Urosh III. While Bulgaria maintained its native rulers, it became subject to Servia and was incorporated into the empire of Stephen Dushan from 1331 to 1355.

Following the death of Dushan, Servian hegemony ended. The Christian races of the Peninsula, weakened by the conflicts of petty princes, subsequently fell to the advancing power of the Moslem invader.
