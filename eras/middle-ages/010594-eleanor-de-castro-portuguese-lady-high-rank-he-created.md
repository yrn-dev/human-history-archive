# Eleanor de Castro

## Marriage and Titles
Eleanor de Castro was a Portuguese lady of high rank. She married Francis Borgia, who was born in Gandia, Valencia, on October 10, 1510. Following their marriage, Francis Borgia was created marquis of Lombay and received an appointment as the master of the horse to the empress.

## Life with Francis Borgia
During their marriage, Eleanor's husband served in the court of Charles V, where he had been sent by his father in 1528. He accompanied Charles on an expedition to Africa in 1535 and traveled into Provence in 1536. In 1539, Francis was tasked with the duty of convoying the body of the empress to her burial-place in Granada. This experience reinforced Francis's determination to leave the court and his resolve to embrace a monastic life should he survive his consort.

Despite his personal inclinations, Francis was later granted further honors against his will, serving as the commander of the order of St James and the viceroy of Catalonia. He governed from Barcelona with energy and efficiency until 1543, at which point he succeeded his father in the dukedom and retired to Gandia.

## Death and Legacy
Eleanor de Castro died in 1546. Her death prompted Francis Borgia to resolve to become a member of the society of Jesuits, an order he had previously encouraged. Due to political and family circumstances, a papal dispensation was granted to Francis, allowing him to retain his worldly possessions and dignities for four years after taking his vows for the benefit of his and Eleanor's young children.
