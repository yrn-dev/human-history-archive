# Peter Chelcicky

## Overview and Historical Context
Peter Chelcicky was a religious writer who lived during the period of the Hussite wars. Born in the final years of the 14th century and dying in 1460, he is described as a quite independent figure of his era, sometimes compared to the 15th-century equivalent of Tolstoy.

## Religious and Political Philosophy
The dominant ideas of Chelcicky were centered on a horror of bloodshed. He maintained a firm determination to accept all decrees of worldly authorities without resistance, even in instances where those decrees were unjust. While he was a strenuous enemy of the Church of Rome, he remained independent of the various Hussite parties, joining none of them.

## Literary Works
Chelcicky's masterpiece is titled *Sit viry* (the net of faith). In addition to this primary work, his contributions to religious and polemical literature include his *Postilla* and a series of polemical writings composed as letters. These letters were addressed to Bishop Nicolas of Pelhrimov and Archbishop John of Rokycan.

## Bohemian Historical Records
The period of the Hussite wars is noted for being rather poor in historical works written in the native language of the country. However, some chroniclers who were contemporaries or eye-witnesses of the wars did produce writings. These accounts were later collected and published by Frantisek Palacky under the title *Stare ceske letopisy*.
