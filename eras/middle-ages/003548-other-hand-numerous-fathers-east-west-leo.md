# Leo

The name Leo appears across various historical contexts, including the religious traditions of the early Christian church, the imperial history of the East and West, and ancient Greek accounts.

## Religious Figures and Theological Perspectives
In the early Christian church, Leo of Rome is identified as one of the numerous fathers of the East and West who interpreted the practice of trine immersion in baptism. While some figures, such as Jerome and Tertullian, viewed the triple dipping as a representation of the Trinity, Leo of Rome, along with Athanasius, Cyril of Jerusalem, Gregory of Nyssa, and Theophylactus, regarded trine immersion as symbolic of the three days of Christ's entombment.

Additionally, Leo the Great provided a specific designation for the feast of Epiphany, referring to it as the "Feast of the Declaration." This feast, which in the East associated the Baptism of Christ with the miracles of Cana, the feeding of the five thousand, the Star of the Magi, and the physical birth, was known by other figures as the "Manifestation" (Fulgentius) or the "Apparition" of Christ.

## Imperial Leo of Thrace (Leo the Great)
Leo of Thrace was a military tribune and the principal steward to the household of the patrician Aspar. Aspar, whose family had commanded the armies of the East for three generations, recommended Leo for the imperial crown. This nomination was ratified by the senate, and Leo received the crown in a ceremony involving the bishop or patriarch. 

Known as Leo the Great, he is distinguished by the Greeks for his temperate firmness in resisting the oppression of his benefactor, Aspar, demonstrating a consciousness of his royal prerogative and duty. During his reign, the Eastern empire provided compensation to the king of the Vandals to secure peace and the restoration of Eudoxia and her daughter Placidia.

## Conflict with the Papacy and Iconoclasm
A later Leo (referred to as "impudent and inhuman" by Pope Gregory the Second) entered into a severe conflict with the Catholic church. Gregory the Second wrote two extant epistles to this emperor, accusing him of impiety and ignorance for accusing Catholics of idolatry. The Pope defended the use of images of Christ, his mother, and the saints, asserting that such worship was innocent and had been present since the apostolic age.

This Leo is associated with Iconoclastic persecutions. Under his rule, the patriarch Anastasius—who was an Iconoclast under Leo but an image worshipper under Artavasdes—was subjected to public humiliation, including being scourged and led through the streets on an ass. The "Draco of Leo" is mentioned in narratives of these persecutions, a force compared by some historians to the dragoons of Louis XIV. While the East reluctantly abjured sacred images, they continued to be defended by Italians.

## Other Historical Figures
In the history of the papacy, Leo III served as Pope from 795 to 816. In the year 800, Leo III crowned Charlemagne as the Emperor of the West.

In ancient Greek history, a herald named Leos from the township of Agnus is noted for his treachery. During a conflict between Theseus and the sons of Pallas, Leos betrayed the plans of the sons of Pallas, who had laid an ambush at Gargettus. This betrayal allowed Theseus to attack and kill those in ambush. Consequently, the township of Pallene ceased intermarrying with Agnus, and it became customary for heralds in that region to avoid beginning proclamations with the words "Acouete Leo" (Oyez) due to the hatred of the name.
