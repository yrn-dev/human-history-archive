# St Boniface

St Boniface was a prominent missionary and organizer of the early German church, often described as the "proconsul of the papacy." His career was characterized by a commitment to bringing the scattered centers of the missionary movement under the direct control and direction of Rome, motivated by a zeal for unity of the faith rather than political objectives.

## Missionary Work and Church Organization
With the protection of Charles, Boniface entered heathen Germany to conduct a systematic crusade. His activities included baptizing converts, overturning idols, and establishing monasteries and churches. He recruited a group of missionary helpers from England, including monks and nuns such as St Lull, St Burchard, St Gregory, St Lioba, St Walburge, and St Thecla, as well as his biographer, Willibald.

Boniface's organizational efforts were extensive:
* **Bavaria:** Following his third visit to Rome in 738, he organized the church in Bavaria into four bishoprics: Passau, Salzburg, Freising, and Regensburg.
* **East Frankish Church:** In 741, Pope Zacharias appointed Boniface as a legate and charged him with the reformation of the entire Frankish church. With the support of Pippin and Carloman (the mayors of the palace who succeeded Charles Martel), he organized the east Frankish church into four bishoprics—Eichstadt, Buraburg, Wurzburg, and Erfurt—and installed his own monks to lead them.
* **Mainz:** Between 746 and 748, Boniface was appointed bishop of Mainz. He became the metropolitan over Utrecht and the Rhine bishoprics, as well as those he had established in Germany, thereby founding the pre-eminence of the see of Mainz.

In 742, Boniface presided over what is generally considered the first German council. He also founded the abbey of Fulda to serve as a center for German monastic culture, placing it under the Bavarian Sturm and implementing a rule stricter than the Benedictine rule.

## Conflicts and Controversies
Boniface encountered theological and disciplinary opposition during his tenure. In Salzburg, he engaged in a controversy with the Irish bishop Virgil, who held the heresy that worlds other than our own existed. Despite this conflict, Virgil was later canonized.

In France, Boniface opposed a Frankish bishop of Neustria named Adalbert (or Aldebert). Adalbert had gained a significant following by claiming to receive relics directly from angels rather than from Rome, and he encouraged people to desert churches by planting crosses in open fields.

## Later Years and Death
In 747, a synod of Frankish bishops sent a formal statement of submission to papal authority to Rome, an act of significance given the previous Merovingian tendencies toward forming national churches. Boniface did not appear to participate in the anointing of Pippin as king of the Franks in 752.

In 754, Boniface resigned his archbishopric in favor of Lull to pursue his original plan of a mission to Frisia. However, on June 5, 754, Boniface and his companions were massacred by heathens near Dockum. His remains were later transferred to Fulda.

## Literary Contributions
Despite being primarily a man of action, Boniface left several literary works. These include his *Epistolae* (Letters), which are considered historically, dogmatically, and literarily important. His other writings include a grammar titled *De octo partibus orationibus*, poems known as *Aenigmata*, a penitential, and the *Dicta Bonifacii*, though the authenticity of the latter is debated.
