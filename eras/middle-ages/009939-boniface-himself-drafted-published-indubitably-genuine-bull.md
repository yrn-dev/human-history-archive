# Boniface VIII

Boniface VIII, born Benedetto Gaetano into a noble family at Anagni, served as pope from 1294 to 1303. Before his ascension to the papacy, he studied civil and canon law in Italy and potentially in Paris. His early career included appointments to canonicates in France and Todi in June 1260, and he served as a notary and advocate at the papal court. 

## Early Career and Rise to Power
During his time as a papal official, Boniface VIII was involved in diplomatic missions and became an influential member of the Sacred College. In 1267, while accompanying Cardinal Ottoboni to assist King Henry III of England against the baronial party's bishops, he was besieged in the Tower of London by the earl of Gloucester; he was subsequently rescued on April 27 by the future Edward I. He was created a cardinal deacon in 1281 and a cardinal priest (SS. Sylvestri et Martini) in 1291. 

Boniface VIII assisted the ineffective Pope Celestine V in his abdication. On December 24, 1294, Boniface was chosen pope at Naples. The election was not unanimous, a result likely caused by the hostility of certain French cardinals.

## Papal Governance and Ideology
In contrast to the monastic poverty and humility of his predecessor, Boniface VIII asserted the lordship of the papacy over all the world's kingdoms. He was crowned in Rome in January 1295 with great pomp. While he initially planned to recover the Holy Land from the infidel and pacify the West, his nine-year reign was characterized by controversies with major European powers. He was noted for his arrogance, lofty claims, and avarice.

## Political Conflicts and Diplomatic Relations
Boniface VIII engaged in several unsuccessful or contentious political efforts:
* **France:** His most significant conflict was with Philip IV of France.
* **England:** He attempted to try Edward I on charges of interfering with a papal fief, but the English parliament declared in 1301 that Scotland had never been a fief of Rome.
* **Germany and Hungary:** He refused to recognize Albert of Austria as the rightful German king until 1303 and declared that the crown of Hungary should fall to the house of Anjou, assuming the role of overlord.
* **Denmark:** He humbled Eric VI of Denmark.
* **Sicily:** His policy of supporting the house of Anjou in Sicily ended in failure.

Additionally, his attempts to establish large estates for his own family created enmity with most of the Colonna family.

## Legal and Ecclesiastical Contributions
Boniface VIII was involved in the promulgation and preservation of several ecclesiastical documents. In 1298, he promulgated the *Sexts* of the Canon Law at Rome and addressed them to all European universities; this included the bull of Gregory X *approbante sacro concilio*. He also inserted the bull of Nicholas III—which based temporal sovereignty on the donation of Constantine—into the *Sexte* of the Decretals. Furthermore, the *Corpus Juris Canonici* contains two bulls attributed to Boniface VIII and Clement VI.
