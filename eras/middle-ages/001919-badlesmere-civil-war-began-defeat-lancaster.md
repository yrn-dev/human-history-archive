# Bartholomew, Baron Badlesmere

## Early Life and Career
Bartholomew, Baron Badlesmere (1275–1322), was an English nobleman and the heir of Gunselm de Badlesmere, who died in 1301. During the final years of the reign of Edward I, Badlesmere served in the English army, fighting in both Scotland and France. His rise in royal favor continued under Edward II, who appointed him as the steward of his household and the governor of Bristol Castle in 1307.

## Political Alignments and Conflict
Despite the favors bestowed upon him by the king, Badlesmere entered into a compact with other noblemen to secure supreme influence within the royal council. He was initially very hostile toward Earl Thomas of Lancaster; however, in 1318, Badlesmere assisted in brokering peace between the earl and the king. During this period, he was associated with a "middle party" that held a mutual detestation for the violent enemies of the king, such as Lancaster, as well as the king's own minions, including the Despensers.

Eventually, the conduct of Edward II drove Badlesmere to align himself with the earl and the king's other enemies. This political shift culminated in a direct confrontation in October 1321, when Badlesmere's wife, Margaret de Clare, refused to allow Queen Isabella entry into her husband's castle at Leeds in Kent.

## Civil War and Execution
The refusal of entry to the queen led to the king capturing Leeds Castle and the subsequent seizure and imprisonment of Lady Badlesmere. These events marked the beginning of a civil war. Following the defeat of Lancaster at Boroughbridge, Badlesmere was captured. He was executed by hanging at Canterbury on April 14, 1322. He was succeeded by his son and heir, Giles, who died without children in 1338.

## Influence and Family Connections
Badlesmere's influence extended to the ecclesiastical appointments of his relatives. His nephew, Henry Burghersh, was appointed bishop of Lincoln in 1320 by Pope John XXII due to Badlesmere's influence, despite the fact that the chapter had already conducted an election for the vacant bishopric. 

Badlesmere also had a professional relationship with Bartholomew Burghersh (d. 1355), the brother of Bishop Henry. Bartholomew Burghersh served as Badlesmere's assistant until the Baron's execution in 1322. Following Badlesmere's death, Bartholomew Burghersh maintained the confidence of Edward III, serving as the warden of the Cinque Ports and constable of Dover Castle, and later fighting at the battle of Crecy in 1346.
