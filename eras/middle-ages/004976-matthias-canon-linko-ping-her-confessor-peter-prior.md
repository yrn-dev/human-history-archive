# Matthias, Canon of Linköping, and Peter, Prior of Alvastra

The figures of Matthias, a canon of Linköping, and Peter, the prior of Alvastra, are recorded in historical archives primarily through their association with Saint Bridget of Sweden (c. 1302–1373). They served as the translators and recorders of her spiritual experiences during the 14th century.

## Association with Saint Bridget of Sweden
Saint Bridget was the daughter of Birger Persson, a provincial judge (*lagman*) and governor of Uppland, and was among the wealthiest landowners in her country. After the death of her husband, Ulf Gudmarson, lord of Nericia, in 1344 at the Cistercian monastery of Alvastra in East Gothland, Bridget devoted herself entirely to religious life.

Bridget had believed herself to have visions since childhood, but these revelations became more frequent following her husband's death. Matthias, a canon of Linköping, and Peter, who served as the prior of Alvastra and was Bridget's confessor, were responsible for translating the records of these revelations into Latin. These translated works achieved a great vogue during the middle ages.

## The Order of St Saviour
During the period associated with these revelations, Bridget founded the order of St Saviour, also known as the Bridgittines. The principal house of this order was established at Vadstena and received rich endowments from the queen and King Magnus II. 

Bridget traveled to Rome around 1350 to pursue a self-imposed mission to elevate the moral tone of the era and to seek authorization for her new order from the pope. While she became universally beloved in Rome for her good works and kindness, the rule of her order was not formally confirmed by Pope Urban V until 1370. Bridget remained in Rome until her death on July 23, 1373, though she did undertake occasional pilgrimages, including one to Jerusalem in 1373. She was later canonized in 1391 by Pope Boniface IX.
