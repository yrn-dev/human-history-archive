# Croats

## Origins and Settlement
The Croats are a Slavonic people who, along with the Serbs, formed the main body of immigrants that occupied Illyria between 600 and 650. These two groups of tribes spoke a single language and were so closely related that the origin of the distinction between them is obscure. The majority of these Serbo-Croatian immigrants left their homes among the Carpathians to settle in the Balkan Peninsula during the 7th century.

Upon their arrival, the Croats settled in the western half of Illyria. They rapidly absorbed most of the Latinized Illyrians, though they did not absorb the wealthy city-states on the coast, which maintained an Italian character and independence, nor the Mavrovlachi who took refuge in the interior mountains. Consequently, with few exceptions, Illyria became entirely Serbo-Croatian in its culture, language, and population.

## Religious and Cultural Development
The distinction between the Croats and the Serbs was first drawn by early Byzantine chroniclers and was well established by the 12th century. While there is no valid racial or linguistic difference between the two groups, a strong religious divide emerged based on their geographic settlements. 

Because the Croats settled in the west, they came under the influence of Italy and Roman Catholicism. This Latin civilization was sustained by constant intercourse with Italy along the Croatian and Dalmatian coast, leading the immigrants to convert to the Roman Catholic Church. In contrast, the Serbs in the east were influenced by Byzantium and the Greek Church, becoming Orthodox. This difference in creed and civilization has historically tended to keep the Illyrian Slavs politically disunited. In Dalmatia, this division is further marked by the use of different alphabets, with Roman Catholic Croats preferring the Latin alphabet while Orthodox Serbs use the Cyrillic.

## Political Aspirations and Movements
In later periods, the Croats were part of a broader Southern Slav identity. Some Slovenes pursued a political ideal of an "Illyrian" kingdom that would include Croatia and all Southern Slavs in the coast district. As part of this movement, an attempt was made to establish a so-called "Illyrian language" intended for acceptance by both the Slovenes and the Croats.

The archive records the presence of Croats in various political contexts. In one instance, Croats and Serbs are listed together as part of a "Southern Slav Club," which included 37 members in total. Another record indicates that Southern Slavs (including Croats, Serbs, and Slovenes) numbered 71 in a specific political grouping, with Croats and Serbs specifically accounting for 20 of those members.

## Historical Events and Geography
The Croats are primarily associated with the north-west of the Balkan Peninsula and predominate in Dalmatia and north-western Bosnia. Historical records mention various interactions involving the Croats, including a tradition that a sultan was stabbed in his tent by a Croat. Additionally, in 1634, the town of Furth was pillaged and burnt by Croats.
