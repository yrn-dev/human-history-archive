# Don Pedro Lopez de Ayala

Don Pedro Lopez de Ayala (1332–1407) was a Spanish poet, historian, and statesman. Born in Vittoria in 1332, he became one of the most cultivated Spaniards of his era, distinguished as a translator, historian, and poet.

## Political and Military Career
Ayala first rose to prominence within the court of Peter the Cruel, though he eventually deserted Peter's cause. He later became a favorite of both John I and Henry II of Castile. His military service was marked by bravery, although it resulted in him being taken prisoner on two separate occasions: first by the Black Prince at Najera in 1367, and later by the Portuguese at Aljubarrota in 1385. In 1398, Henry III appointed him to the position of grand chancellor of the realm. In addition to his military and courtly roles, Ayala served as an able diplomat.

## Literary Works
Ayala's literary contributions are diverse, spanning historical chronicles and poetry. His most significant works are his chronicles concerning the four kings of Castile who reigned during his lifetime. These chronicles are noted for providing generally accurate accounts of events and scenes that Ayala had personally witnessed. The first portion of his chronicle, which focuses exclusively on the reign of Peter the Cruel, was printed in Seville in 1495. A complete edition of his chronicles was later printed between 1779 and 1780 as part of the *Cronicas Espanolas*, a collection produced under the auspices of the Spanish Royal Academy of History.

Beyond his historical writing, Ayala authored a long didactic and satirical poem. This work serves as a record of contemporary morality and a reflection of his own personal experiences.

## Death and Legacy
Don Pedro Lopez de Ayala died in Calahorra in 1407. His life and literary output have been the subject of further study, including work by Rafael Floranes titled "Vida literaria de Pedro Lopez de Ayala," published in *Documentos ineditos para la historia de Espana* (vols. xix and xx), and F. W. Schirrmacher's "Ueber die Glaubwurdigkeit der Chronik Ayalas," found in *Geschichte von Spanien* (1902).
