# Cathedral

A cathedral is a significant ecclesiastical building, often serving as the seat of a bishop. These structures vary widely in architectural style, size, and historical origin, reflecting the regional influences and periods in which they were constructed.

## Architectural Features and Styles
Cathedrals often employ specific architectural plans and elements. Many are cruciform in design, as seen in the 13th-century cathedral at the Rock of Cashel, the cathedral of St Canice in Kilkenny, and the cathedral in Bangor. Common features include naves, transepts, choirs, and towers. For example, the cathedral in Bristol possesses two western towers, a central tower, a nave, short transepts, a choir with aisles, and an eastern Lady chapel.

Architectural styles vary by region and era:
*   **Norman and Early English:** The best Norman remains are found in Durham cathedral. The eastern portion of Durham cathedral is attributed to the Early English period. In Kilkenny, the cathedral of St Canice is mainly in the Early English style, featuring a low massive tower supported on clustered columns of local black marble.
*   **Pointed and Lancet:** The cathedral at the Rock of Cashel features lancet windows and pointed arches. Similarly, the cathedral in Dunblane is built in the Early Pointed style, though its tower is Early Norman.
*   **Byzantine and Orthodox:** In Bucharest, the metropolitan cathedral is shaped like a Greek cross, surrounded by a broad cloister with four main entrances and three brick towers. In Kiev, the cathedral of St Sophia—the oldest in the Russian empire—features ten cupolas (four spangled with stars and six with crosses) and a four-storeyed campanile. While its plan does not imitate the Byzantine church of Constantinople, its interior mosaics and frescoes show direct Byzantine influence.
*   **Domes:** The term "dome" (or *duomo* in Italian) refers to a spherical or spheroidal vault with a circular horizontal plan. These may be supported by a circular wall, a drum, or carried over square or polygonal areas using squinches, corbels, or pendentives.

## Notable Cathedrals and Their Histories

### British Isles
*   **Durham:** This cathedral contains significant remains from the Norman period.
*   **Bristol:** The cathedral was originally the church of Fitz-Harding's abbey of St Augustine, founded in 1142. It features a chapter-house described as one of the finest Norman chambers in Europe, carved with Byzantine exuberance. The nave was modernized in 1877 by Street to imitate the 14th-century choir.
*   **Bangor:** Located in North Wales, this cruciform cathedral stands on the site of a church founded around 525 by St Deiniol. It has a history of destruction, having been burned during the time of Owen Glendower and destroyed in 1211. It was restored by Sir G. Scott between 1866 and 1875.
*   **Dunblane:** Founded by David I in 1141 and rebuilt around 1240 by Bishop Clemens, this cathedral escaped injury during the Reformation. Its west window is noted for a design resembling a leaf.
*   **Kilkenny:** The cathedral of St Canice, dating from approximately 1255, is the largest ecclesiastical building in Ireland with the exception of St Patrick's in Dublin. It measures 226 ft. from east to west and 123 ft. from north to south.
*   **Rock of Cashel:** This 13th-century cruciform cathedral is part of an assemblage of ruins on a limestone elevation, alongside a round tower and Cormac's chapel.

### Continental Europe
*   **Kiev:** The cathedral of St Sophia was founded in 1037 by Prince Yaroslav to commemorate a victory over the Petchenegs. It contains a colossal 15 ft. representation of the Virgin and the sarcophagus of Prince Yaroslav.
*   **Bucharest:** The city contains a metropolitan cathedral built between 1656 and 1665, as well as the cathedral of St Joseph for Roman Catholics.

## Interior Elements and Memorials
Cathedrals often serve as sites for significant memorials and art. The Bristol cathedral contains canopied tombs of the Berkeleys with effigies in chain mail, as well as memorials to Bishop Butler and Lady Hesketh. In Dunblane, the choir contains 16th-century carved oak stalls and blue marble stones believed to mark the graves of Lady Margaret Drummond and her two sisters.

Artistic treasures are also common; the cathedral of St Sophia in Kiev is known for its mosaics and frescoes, while some cathedrals have housed works by masters such as van Dyck and Verhaghen.

## Impact of Conflict
Cathedrals have frequently been affected by war. In the 14th and 15th centuries, incessant wars between England and Scotland left the Decorated and Perpendicular periods scantily represented in certain regions. More recently, some cathedrals have suffered from artillery; one account describes a cathedral where shells penetrated the south portal and fired from the north, leaving blocks of fallen stone and limestone dust over archiepiscopal tombs, though some statues and paintings were saved.
