# Charles du Fresne, Sieur Du Cange

Charles du Fresne, Sieur Du Cange (1610–1688), was a prominent 17th-century French scholar and critic. He is recognized as one of the lay members of a group of scholars who established the foundations of modern historical criticism.

## Life and Career
Born in Amiens on December 18, 1610, Du Cange was educated at the Jesuits' college in his hometown, where he distinguished himself. He subsequently pursued the study of law at Orleans and moved to Paris, where he was received as an advocate before the parliament in 1631. 

After experiencing limited success in his legal profession, Du Cange returned to Amiens. In July 1638, he married Catherine Dubois, the daughter of the treasurer in Amiens, who was a royal official. In 1647, Du Cange purchased the office of treasurer from his father-in-law. The duties associated with this office did not hinder his devotion to historical and literary work.

In 1668, a plague forced Du Cange to leave Amiens and settle in Paris, where he remained until his death on October 23, 1688. While residing in Paris, he utilized the city's archives to consult a vast array of manuscripts, diplomas, charters, and printed documents that were unavailable elsewhere.

## Literary Contributions and Methodology
Du Cange was noted for his critical sagacity, accurate and varied knowledge, and his linguistic acquirements, which distinguished him from nearly all other writers of his era. His industry was described as unremitting and exemplary; the extensive number of his literary works is evidenced by the original manuscripts, all written in his own hand, which have survived.

His most significant contributions are two glossaries:
*   ***Glossarium ad scriptores mediae et infimae latinitatis*** (Paris, 1678)
*   ***Glossarium ad scriptores mediae et infimae graecitatis*** (Lyons, 1688)

These works are considered indispensable tools for those studying the literature and history of the middle ages. The Latin *Glossarium* originally consisted of three volumes. It was later expanded by the Benedictines of St Maur, who added three supplementary volumes between 1733 and 1736, and further expanded by the Benedictine Pierre Carpentier (1697–1767), who added four volumes in 1766. Other editions and an abridged version with corrections were also produced.

## Applications of the Glossarium
The *Glossarium* serves as a primary reference for various historical, linguistic, and legal inquiries across multiple disciplines:

**Etymology and Linguistics**
The work is cited in the study of various terms and their origins. For example, in the study of the sweetmeat "marchpane" (or marzipan), the *Glossarium* records the Latin form *martiapanis* as appearing in Johann Burchard's *Diarium curiae romanae* (1483–1492). It also provides information on the *matapanus*, a Venetian coin struck by Doge Enrico Dandolo (1192–1205) featuring an enthroned figure of Christ. Additionally, Du Cange defines the medieval Latin *lodium* or *lodarium* as *lugurium*, meaning a small hut.

**Law and Governance**
The *Glossarium* is used to trace titles and legal terms. It is referenced regarding the term "Baro" and "Bannereti." In the context of ecclesiastical and legal terminology, Du Cange notes that *Jurisconsulti* use the term *incumbere* in the sense of *obtinere* or *possidere*.

**History and Culture**
The texts are utilized to research various historical groups and symbols, including the "Beginen" (Beguines) and the "Draco" (dragon).
