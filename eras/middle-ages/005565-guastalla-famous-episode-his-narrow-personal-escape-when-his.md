# Guastalla

## Political Transitions and the War of Polish Succession
Guastalla is a location that has been a subject of territorial disputes and diplomatic agreements among European powers. In 1731, Great Britain and Holland agreed to respect the Pragmatic Sanction of April 19, 1713—an instrument by which Charles, lacking a son, had settled the succession of the indivisible monarchy on his daughter, Maria Theresa. In exchange for this agreement, the territories of Guastalla, Piacenza, and Parma were to be ceded to Don Carlos.

However, the Bourbon powers remained hostile to this arrangement, which led to the War of Polish Succession in 1733. The outcome of this conflict resulted in the strengthening of the Habsburg monarchy's power in northern Italy through the acquisition of Guastalla, Piacenza, and Parma.

The war in Italy during this period involved significant French military engagement. In this capacity, he fought in the battles of Guastalla and Parma.

A famous episode associated with the military actions at Guastalla involves a narrow personal escape by the Duc de Broglie. On the night of September 14, 1734, the enemy raided his quarters, which were located on the Secchia, forcing him to make a hasty escape.
