# Altengl. Bibliothek, vol. i.

The *Altengl. Bibliothek*, vol. i., is a scholarly publication edited by E. Kolbing. One of the notable works contained within this volume is the *Osbern Bokenams Legenden*, edited by Dr. Carl Horstmann in 1883.

## Osbern Bokenam
Osbern Bokenam was an English author who, according to his own records, was born on October 6, 1393. There are differing accounts regarding his origins: Dr. Horstmann suggests he may have been a native of Bokeham (currently Bookham) in Surrey, deriving his name from that location. However, a concluding note in his *Lives of the Saints* describes him as "frere Austyn of Stoke Clare," a "Suffolke man."

Bokenam was a follower of Lydgate and Chaucer, and he likely drew inspiration from Chaucer's *Legend of Good Women*. He wrote in the Suffolk speech and adopted a plain style, justifying his lack of poetic decoration by stating that the "finest flowers" had already been plucked by Gower, Lydgate, and Chaucer.

## Literary Works and Sources
Bokenam authored a series of thirteen legends focusing on women and holy maidens. These works were primarily composed in stanzas of seven or eight lines, and nine of these legends include prologues. His primary source for these works was the *Legenda Aurea* written by Jacobus de Voragine, the archbishop of Genoa, whom Bokenam refers to as "Januence."

Specific dedications were made for these works; for example, the *Vita Scae Margaretae, virginis et martiris* (the first of the legends) was written for Thomas Burgh, a monk at Cambridge. Other legends were dedicated to pious ladies who wished to read the history of the saints who shared their names.

## Manuscripts and Editions
A unique copy of Bokenam's work is found in the Arundel MS. 327 at the British Museum. This copy was completed in 1447 and was presented by the scribe, Thomas Burgh, to an unnamed convent so that the nuns might remember him and his sister, Dame Betrice Burgh.

In addition to the edition found in E. Kolbing's *Altengl. Bibliothek*, vol. i., the poems were edited in 1835 for the Roxburghe Club under the title *Lyvys of Seyntys*.

## The Latin and English Dialogue
Both the Roxburghe Club edition and the edition in *Altengl. Bibliothek*, vol. i., include a dialogue written in both Latin and English. This text is taken from volume vi (page 1600) of the 1846 edition of Dugdale's *Monasticon Anglicanum*. 

The dialogue features a conversation between a "Frere answerynge" and a "Secular asking" taking place at the grave of Dame Johan of Acres. The purpose of this dialogue is to demonstrate the lineal descent of the lords of the honoure of Clare, covering the period from 1248 (MCCXLVIII) to 1356 (MCCCLVI).
