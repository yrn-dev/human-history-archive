# Humphrey V de Bohun and the Events of 1258

## The Bohun Family Origins
The Bohuns were a family of significance in English history during the 13th and 14th centuries. The family name originated from a village located in the Cotentin, situated between the estuary of the Vire and Coutances. While their early history in England following the Norman Conquest is obscure, the family's prominence was established by Humphrey III. During the later years of Henry I, Humphrey III served as a *dapifer*, or steward, within the royal household. He married the daughter of Milo of Gloucester and maintained an ambiguous position during the reign of Stephen, shifting his support from the king to the empress. Humphrey III remained loyal to Henry II and fought against the rebels of East Anglia at Farnham in 1173.

## Succession and the Earldom of Hereford
Humphrey III lived until 1187, outliving his eldest son, Humphrey IV. The family estates were subsequently inherited by his grandson, Henry. Henry's mother, Margaret, was the sister of William the Lion, providing a connection to the royal house of Scotland that likely aided Henry in obtaining the earldom of Hereford from John in 1199. Under Henry, the Bohuns became leading Marcher barons, as their lands were primarily located on the Welsh Marches. Henry was one of the twenty-five barons elected to enforce the terms of the Great Charter. However, during the following civil war, he fought for Louis and was captured at the battle of Lincoln in 1217. He died on a pilgrimage on June 1, 1220, after taking the cross.

## Humphrey V and the Crisis of 1258
Humphrey V, the son and heir of Henry, initially returned to a path of loyalty. Before 1239, he was permitted to inherit the earldom of Essex from his maternal uncle, William de Mandeville. However, in 1258, Humphrey V shifted his allegiance from the royal cause to the baronial cause, mirroring the actions of his father. 

During the Oxford parliament of 1258, Humphrey V served as a nominee of the opposition. He was appointed to a committee of twenty-four tasked with the reform of the administration. His return to royal allegiance occurred only after Montfort formed an alliance with Llewelyn of North Wales.

## Later Political Activity and Legacy
Humphrey Bohun, the earl of Hereford, later collaborated with the 5th earl of Norfolk, Roger Bigod. Following a dispute between Bigod and Edward I regarding military service in Gascony and Flanders, Bohun and Bigod worked together to prevent the collection of an aid for the war. Their joint efforts forced Edward I to confirm the charters in 1297 and again in 1301. Regarding these figures, the historian Stubbs characterized Bigod and Bohun as "degenerate sons of mighty fathers," suggesting they were greater in their opportunities than in their patriotism.
