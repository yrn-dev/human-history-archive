# The Battle of Castillon and the Submission of Bordeaux

## The Battle of Castillon and its Aftermath
The battle of Castillon took place in 1453, resulting in a defeat for the English. This military failure was followed by a siege lasting three months, which ultimately led to the submission of the city of Bordeaux to Charles VII.

## History of Bordeaux
Bordeaux, known originally as *Burdigala*, served as the chief town of the Bituriges Vivisci. During the Roman Empire, it flourished as a commercial city and was designated the capital of Aquitania Secunda in the 4th century. The 4th-century writer Ausonius, a native of the city, described Bordeaux as being surrounded by walls and lofty towers, and noted its status as one of the greatest educational centres in Gaul.

Following the disintegration of the Roman Empire, the city experienced a period of decline, not recovering its prosperity until the beginning of the 10th century.

## English Rule in Bordeaux
For nearly three hundred years, from 1154 to 1453, Bordeaux and Guienne belonged to the English kings. During this period, the city served as the seat for the court of Edward the Black Prince, and his son, Richard, was born there.

The English administration supported the trade and liberties of the city. In 1235, Bordeaux was granted the right to elect its own mayors, who were assisted in the city's administration by a municipal council known as a "jurade." The city's regional influence grew as it led a federation that included other important towns, such as Libourne and St Emilion.

Economically, Bordeaux merchants developed extensive commerce with fellow subjects in England. The primary English ports involved in this trade were Chester, Bristol, Dartmouth, Exeter, Hull, and London.

## Transition to French Control
The English presence in the region was challenged in 1451 when Jean Dunois, the Count of Orleans (also known as the "Bastard of Orleans"), attacked Guienne. During this campaign, Dunois took Bayonne and Bordeaux, the latter of which the English had held for three hundred years.

Following the final defeat of the English at the battle of Castillon in 1453 and the subsequent three-month siege, Bordeaux submitted to Charles VII. This transition resulted in an immediate curtailment of the city's privileges. These privileges were only partially restored later under Louis XI, who established the parlement of Guienne in the city.

## Later Unrest
In 1548, the inhabitants of Bordeaux rose up by force of arms to resist the imposition of a salt-tax. This rebellion was suppressed with merciless severity by the constable Anne de Montmorency.
