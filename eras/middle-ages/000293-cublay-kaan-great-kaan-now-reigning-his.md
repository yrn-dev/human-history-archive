# Cublay Kaan

Cublay Kaan is the sixth sovereign in the imperial lineage descending from Chinghis Kaan, the first Lord of the Tartars. He ascended to the throne in the year of Christ 1256, a succession attributed to his valour, ability, and great worth. Although his claim was disputed by kinsmen and brothers, he maintained his position through his personal valour and his direct descent from the imperial line. By the year 1298, Cublay Kaan had reigned for forty-two years. At that time, he was approximately eighty-five years of age, meaning he was about forty-three when he first became sovereign.

## Power and Dominion
Cublay Kaan is described as the most potent man to exist in the world in terms of treasure, lands, and forces, from the time of the First Father Adam until the present day. His power is said to exceed that of the five Kaans who preceded him combined. Furthermore, it is asserted that the combined power of all the Christians in the world, including their Kings and Emperors, along with the Saracens, would not equal the power of Cublay Kaan. As the Lord of all the Tartars in the world, including those of the Ponent and the Levant, all such peoples are his subjects and liegemen.

## Governance and Military Strategy
The Great Kaan is characterized as the wisest and most accomplished man among the Tartar tribes, noted for his skill in ruling an empire and governing men. To maintain order across the provinces of Mangi and Cathay, where disloyal populations may be prone to rebellion, the Kaan employs a system of garrisons. These troops are stationed four or five miles from large cities, which are forbidden from having gates or walls that could obstruct the entrance of troops. 

To prevent instability, the Kaan relieves both the garrisons and their commanders every two years. These troops are maintained through regular pay from provincial revenues and by keeping vast quantities of cattle and selling milk in the cities. These stations are scattered at distances of 30, 40, or 60 days from the capital.

## The Revolt of Nayan
Although Cublay Kaan was a gallant soldier and excellent captain before his ascension, he only went to war in person once after becoming sovereign, in the year of Christ 1286. This campaign was necessitated by the revolt of his uncle, Nayan, a young man of thirty who was the Lord of four great provinces: Chorcha, Cauly, Barscol, and Sikintinju.

Nayan, possessing a force of 300,000 horsemen, sought to wrest the empire from his nephew. He conspired with another Tartar Prince and kinsman of the Kaan named Caidu. Caidu, who was also a nephew of the Great Kaan but was already in rebellion and bitter enmity with his sovereign, agreed to the plot and mustered 100,000 horsemen to attack Cublay Kaan from a second side.

Following the battle, Nayan was captured. To avoid spilling the blood of the Imperial Line upon the ground or exposing it to the Sun and Heaven, Cublay Kaan ordered that Nayan be put to death in secret. Nayan was wrapped in a carpet and tossed to and fro until he died. Following this victory, the people and Barons of Nayan's provinces renewed their fealty to the Great Kaan.

## Family and Succession
Cublay Kaan has four lawful wives, with whom he has twenty-two male children. The eldest son, Chinkin, was named in honor of Chinghis Kaan but died before he could succeed his father. Consequently, the succession passes to Chinkin's son, Temur, who is described as a brave and able man. Additionally, the Kaan has twenty-five sons by concubines, all of whom are great chiefs and valiant soldiers. Seven of the children born to his four lawful wives serve as governors or kings of vast provinces.

## Customs and Burial
According to the customs of the Tartars, all Grand Kaans and descendants of Chinghis Kaan are interred at a mountain called Altay. Regardless of where a sovereign dies—even if the location is a 100 days' journey away—the body must be carried to this mountain to be buried with his predecessors.
