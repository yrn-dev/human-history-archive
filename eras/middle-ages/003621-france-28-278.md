# France

## Early History and the Merovingians
The early history of the French monarchy is associated with the Merovingians, a royal family whose distinctive appellation can be traced to the beginning of the seventh century. According to Gregory of Tours, the family created "Reges Crinitos" (long-haired kings) from the noblest of their lineage. This peculiar fashion of wearing long hair was noted by both natives and foreigners, including Agathias, Priscus, and Gregory of Tours. This German custom was eventually adopted by the emperors of Constantinople. Some critics have suggested a connection between the Merovingians and the prince Maroboduus, asserting that the prince who named the first race predated the father of Childeric.

In the Roman era, the Franks were described by the Greek orator Libanius, who provided a lively picture of their manners. However, Libanius misapprehended a passage by Julian, leading him to represent the Franks as consisting of only a thousand men and comparing them to the Lacedæmonians who were besieged on the Island of Sphatoria.

## Territorial Changes and the Spanish March
The territorial boundaries of France shifted over several centuries. Around the year 900, the counts or governors of the Spanish march revolted from Charles the Simple. In 1642, the kings of France recovered a small portion of this territory known as the Rousillon. Despite being described as a "poor pittance," the Rousillon contained 188,900 subjects and provided an annual payment of 2,600,000 livres.

## Political Revolutions and the Napoleonic Era
France underwent significant political transformations starting in the late 18th century. The year 1789 marked the beginning of a revolution driven by revolutionary ideas. This period saw the establishment of a "crowned republic" between 1789 and 1791, followed by the Revolution of the Jacobins. The Jacobin republic lasted from 1792 to 1794, after which the government transitioned to the Directory.

Following this period of reconstruction, Napoleon Bonaparte rose to power. Originally a republican general from a family in Corsica, Bonaparte served as First Consul from 1799 to 1804. In 1804, he became Napoleon I, Emperor, a position he held until 1814. His career later included a period known as the Hundred Days.

## Trade and International Presence
France has maintained a global diplomatic and mercantile presence. In the port of Bushire, France established a consulate alongside other nations such as Great Britain, Russia, Germany, and Turkey.

In terms of trade, data from the late 19th and early 20th centuries shows a decline in the percentage of specific trade weights (Cwt) attributed to France. In 1897, France accounted for 448,128 Cwt (13.9% of the total), which decreased to 416,821 Cwt (13.0%) in 1898, 353,942 Cwt (10.5%) in 1899, and 322,048 Cwt (9.5%) in 1900.
