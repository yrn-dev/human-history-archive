# Harbour Cities and Their Religious Architecture

The relationship between maritime infrastructure and religious architecture is evident across various global cities, where harbours often serve as the nucleus for urban development and the placement of significant churches and temples.

## Bristol and the Floating Harbour
In Bristol, the city's nucleus is located north of the river, with a business centre radiating from a point close to the Floating Harbour. To the south of this centre, an island is formed between the Floating Harbour and the New Course of the Avon. This area contains several public buildings, including the general hospital, Temple Meads station, and two of the city's finest churches: the Temple and St Mary Redcliffe.

The city's centre contains a remarkable collection of ecclesiastical architectural antiquities, attributed to the influence of great merchants—such as the Framptons, Shipwards, and Canyngs—and baronial families, including the Berkeleys and the earls of Gloucester. The cathedral is situated on the south side of College Green, bordering the Floating Harbour above the area known as Canons' Marsh. The cathedral features a nave (modernized by Street in 1877), a choir from the 14th century with skeleton-vaulting in the aisles, two western towers, a central tower, a choir with aisles, and an eastern Lady chapel. It also contains a cloister court and a chapter-house carved with Byzantine exuberance, regarded as one of Europe's finest Norman chambers. The site was originally the abbey of St Augustine, founded by Fitz-Harding in 1142. Nearby, on the north side of College Green, is the Mayor's chapel (originally St Mark's), which is primarily Perpendicular and Decorated in style.

## Maritime Cities of New Zealand and Holland
In Dunedin, New Zealand, the city's location was chosen specifically for the Otago harbour, a narrow inlet on the south-eastern coast of the South Island. The city features numerous handsome churches, including St Paul's, Knox church, and the Roman Catholic cathedral of St Joseph. The First church is particularly notable for overlooking the harbour and occupying the site of the original settlers' church.

In Enkhuizen, a seaport of Holland on the Zuider Zee, the harbour is overlooked by the Dromedary tower-gateway, dating from 1540. The city's churches include the Zuiderkerk (South church), which has a conspicuous tower dating from 1450-1525, and the Westerkerk (West church), which features a wooden bell-house from 1519 and a Renaissance pulpit and screen from the middle of the 16th century. The city also contains a synagogue and a Roman Catholic church.

## Temples and Churches in Asia and the Mediterranean
In Bangkok, the capital of Siam, the harbour, docks, warehouses, and rice-mills are located to the south of the city. The urban landscape is characterized by beautiful Buddhist temples with gilded spires and coloured tile roofs. Additionally, the city contains Christian churches of various denominations, primarily Roman Catholic.

In Caesarea Palaestina, a town built by Herod between 25 and 13 B.C., a gigantic mole protecting the harbour on the west and south was constructed from stones 50 feet long. The ruins of the city include a temple dedicated to Caesar, as well as a cathedral (now covered by modern houses) and a church.

In Epidaurus, a maritime city on the eastern coast of Argolis, the city was situated on a rocky peninsula with a natural harbour to the north. Its importance was driven in part by the fame of its temple of Asclepius.

In Damascus, the church of St John the Baptist was constructed by Arcadius on the site of a temple. This structure was later converted into a mosque by Caliph Walid I between 705 and 717.

## Other Maritime Religious Sites
In Cartagena, the city occupies a peninsula facing the northern end of the harbour. Among its numerous churches, the Jesuit church of San Juan de Dios is the most imposing, featuring double towers and a celebrated marble pulpit. The city is an episcopal see with a cathedral dating from colonial times.

In Bergen, King Eystein established a monastery at Nordnes and built a harbour where there had previously been only a barren spot. He also constructed Michael's church, described as a splendid stone temple, and the Church of the Apostles within the king's house.
