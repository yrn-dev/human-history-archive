# BARMAKIDS

The Barmakids (also referred to as Barmecides) were a noble Persian family that rose to significant power and influence during the era of the Abbasid caliphs.

## Origins and Early History
The family was founded by Barmak, a Persian fire-worshipper believed to have been a native of Khorasan. According to tradition, Barmak's wife was briefly taken into the harem of Abdallah, who was the brother of Kotaiba, the conqueror of Balkh; from this union, Khalid b. Barmak the Barmecide was born. Around A.D. 736, following the rebellion of Harith, Barmak rebuilt and adorned his native city of Balkh.

## Rise to Power
The family's prosperity continued into subsequent generations. Yahya b. Khalid, the grandson of Barmak, served as the vizier to the caliph Mahdi and acted as the tutor for Harun al-Rashid. The influence of the family further expanded under Harun al-Rashid, as Yahya's sons, Ja'far and Fadl, both held high offices within the caliphate. Ja'far is noted as the "Giafar" appearing in the *Arabian Nights*.

## Disgrace and Fall
The downfall of the Barmakids is associated with a romantic but potentially improbable account involving the caliph Harun al-Rashid. It is said that Harun enjoyed the company of Ja'far and his sister, 'Abbasa. To allow them to remain in his society continuously without violating etiquette, Harun persuaded the two to enter into a purely formal marriage. However, the conditions of this arrangement were not observed. Upon discovering that 'Abbasa had borne a son, Harun ordered the sudden arrest and beheading of Ja'far. 

Following this event, the rest of the family was imprisoned and stripped of their property, with the exception of Mahommed, the brother of Yahya. While this narrative exists, it is probable that Harun's anger was driven largely by the insinuations of courtiers who suggested that the caliph had become a mere puppet in the hands of the powerful Barmakid family.

## Cultural Legacy
The Barmakids are the source of the expression "Barmecide Feast," which refers to an imaginary banquet. This term is derived from a tale in the *Arabian Nights* titled "The Barber's Tale of his Sixth Brother." In the story, one of the Barmecides serves a series of empty dishes to a hungry man as a means of testing his sense of humor.
