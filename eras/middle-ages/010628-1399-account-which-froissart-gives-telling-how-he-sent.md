# Events of 1399 and the Works of Froissart

## Political Transitions in England
The year 1399 was marked by significant political shifts in England involving the supporters of King Richard II. Thomas le Despenser, who had previously supported Richard II against the lords appellant—including Thomas of Woodstock, duke of Gloucester—in 1397, deserted the king in 1399. Following this desertion, Despenser was degraded from his earldom due to his participation in the death of Gloucester. Subsequently, Despenser joined a conspiracy directed against Henry IV, which led to his seizure and execution by a mob in Bristol in January 1400.

## The Career of Thomas Arundel
Thomas Arundel (1353–1414), the archbishop of Canterbury and son of Richard Fitzalan, earl of Arundel and Warenne, was a prominent figure in the ecclesiastical and political landscape of the era. His rise within the church was rapid; he served as archdeacon of Taunton in 1373 and was consecrated bishop of Ely in April 1374. 

Arundel was aligned with a political party that included Henry, earl of Derby (later King Henry IV), Thomas, duke of Gloucester, and his own brother, Richard, earl of Arundel. In 1386, Arundel and Gloucester were sent to Eltham to persuade King Richard II to return to parliament. The success of this mission resulted in Arundel being appointed lord chancellor, replacing Michael de la Pole, duke of Suffolk. 

Arundel's tenure as chancellor was interrupted by his appointment as archbishop of York in April 1388. He returned to the office of chancellor in 1391, during which time he attempted to move the courts of justice from London to York, though they were eventually returned to the metropolis. In September 1396, he was translated to Canterbury and resigned the chancellorship. His leadership in Canterbury was characterized by a vigorous assertion of his rights, including a thorough visitation of his province and warnings to London citizens regarding the withholding of tithes. In November 1396, he officiated at the marriage of Richard II and Isabella, daughter of Charles VI of France.

Arundel's downfall occurred in 1397 following the king's attack on the lords appellant. Despite Richard II offering hopes of safety, Arundel was arrested and impeached by the Commons. He was charged with assisting in the procurement of the commission of regency in derogation of royal authority and was sentenced to banishment, receiving forty days to leave the realm.

## The Chronicles of Froissart
The writer Froissart is noted for his historical accounts and his relationships with various figures of his time. He was patronized by Edward le Despenser (d. 1375), whom Froissart referred to as *le grand sire Despensier*. 

In his writings, Froissart is contrasted with the poet Deschamps. While Deschamps provided a faithful picture of the anarchy in France and criticized the vices of the clergy and heavy taxation, Froissart is described as casting a "glamour" over the miserable wars of the period. Additionally, Froissart's work includes the depiction of the Khalif of Baudas, referred to as "*Le Galifre de Baudas*," bringing the figure to life approximately one hundred and twenty years after the events.
