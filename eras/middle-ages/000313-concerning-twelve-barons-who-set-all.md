# Concerning the Twelve Barons of the Great Kaan

The Twelve Barons constitute a high-ranking administrative body appointed by the Great Kaan to oversee the essential affairs of the empire. These officials exercise significant authority over the governance of thirty-four great provinces and operate from the capital city of Cambaluc.

## Administrative Role and Authority
The Great Kaan has committed all necessary affairs of thirty-four great provinces to these twelve Barons. Their power is extensive, particularly regarding the appointment of provincial leadership; the Barons choose the governors for all thirty-four provinces and subsequently inform the Emperor of their selections. Upon receiving this information, the Emperor confirms the choice and grants the nominated governor a tablet of gold appropriate to the rank of his government.

While the Barons manage the general administration, the Great Kaan retains final authority over matters of very great importance. In such instances, the twelve Barons present the affair to the Emperor, who decides the outcome as he deems best.

## The Palace and Provincial Administration
The twelve Barons reside together in a handsome and rich palace located within the city of Cambaluc. This palace is composed of various edifices and numerous suites of apartments. The administrative structure extends to the provincial level through the assignment of a judge and several clerks to every province. These judges and clerks also reside within the palace in separate quarters, where they administer the affairs of their respective provinces under the direct supervision of the twelve Barons.

## Terminology and the Great Council
Historical records and notes regarding the administration of the Mongol Dynasty mention the term *Sing* (or *Sheng*). Some sources identify the *Sing* as a place for the sittings of the Great Council, where dignitaries are expected to attend daily to remain acquainted with current events. There is scholarly discussion regarding whether the term refers to the office of a minister (*Siang*) or to the chiefs of twelve great provincial governments. Some interpretations suggest a distribution of the empire into twelve governments, noting that certain cities, such as Yangchau, were elevated as one of the twelve seats.

## Distinction from the Keshican
The twelve Barons of the high council are distinct from a larger group of 12,000 men known as *Keshican*, who are also referred to as Barons. These 12,000 men receive thirteen changes of raiment from the Emperor, each set in a different color. These robes are richly garnished with pearls, gems, and other precious materials. Along with these robes, the Emperor bestows a fine golden girdle and boots of *Borgal* (or *Camut*) wrought with silver thread thirteen times a year. The Emperor wears suits that correspond in color to those of these 12,000 Barons, acting as their comrade.
