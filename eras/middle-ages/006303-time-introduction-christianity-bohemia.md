# The Introduction and Development of Christianity in Bohemia

The introduction of Christianity into Bohemia marked a transition in the region's history, moving it from a period of obscurity into a more documented era. While the earliest attempts to establish the faith originated in Germany, these efforts saw little success. This failure is attributed to an innate distrust the Bohemians held toward the Germans, which made them unfavorable to a creed arriving from the realm of their western neighbors.

## Adoption of the Faith
The successful introduction of Christianity occurred when the religion approached from Moravia. In Moravia, the doctrine had been taught by Cyrillus and Methodius, who were Greek monks from Thessalonica. The adoption of the faith accelerated around the year 873, when the Bohemian prince Borivoj was baptized by Methodius. Following this event, the Bohemians rapidly adopted the Christian faith.

## Early Christian Rulers and Political Conflict
Among the most famous rulers of this period was Wenceslas, known as Wenceslas the Holy. He served as an ally of Germany, but in 935, he was murdered by his brother, Boleslav. Wenceslas was subsequently canonized by the Church of Rome. 

The murder of Wenceslas triggered a war with Germany. Boleslav, who succeeded Wenceslas, was generally successful in this conflict. Under the reigns of Boleslav and his son, Boleslav II, Bohemia expanded its frontiers. Boleslav II established rule over Moravia, Bohemia, a large portion of Silesia, and the part of Poland that is currently the Austrian province of Galicia. However, this empire did not endure long. Boleslav III, the son of Boleslav II, lost all foreign possessions to Boleslav the Great, the king of Poland. 

The reign of Boleslav III was characterized by constant civil war stemming from dissensions between him and his brothers, Ulrick and Jaromir. Although Boleslav III managed to expel his brothers, his cruelty led the Bohemians to dethrone him. They chose the Polish prince Vladivoj—the son of the Bohemian princess Dubravka and brother of Boleslav the Great—as their ruler. To strengthen his position, Vladivoj sought aid from Germany and agreed to continue paying tribute.

## Intellectual and Religious Writing
By the 14th century, Bohemia had attained a high intellectual level, leading to the emergence of writers who used the national language to discuss philosophical and religious subjects. A primary figure in this movement was Thomas of Stitny (c. 1331-1401), whose works *Besedni reci* ("learned entertainments") and *O obecnych vecech Krestanskych* (on general Christian matters) are considered highly valuable. Stitny and other contemporaries whose writings have been lost are recognized as the forerunners of Huss.

Huss wrote in both Latin and Bohemian, contributing significantly to the development of the native language. His most important Bohemian work, written in 1412, is the *Vyklad viry, desatera Boziho prikazani, a patere* (exposition of the creed, the ten commandments and the Lord's Prayer). Other notable works by Huss include the *Postilla* (a collection of sermons), treatises on simony (*O svatokupectvi*) and the true road to salvation (*O poznani cesty prave k spaseni*), as well as a collection of letters, including those written while he was in prison.

## Historical Documentation
Much of the early history and traditions of Bohemia were recorded by Cosmas of Prague (1045-1125), the dean of the cathedral and the earliest Bohemian historian. His work, *Chronicae Bohemorum libri iii.*, covers the history of the country up to nearly the time of his death and earned him the title of the "Herodotus of his country." This historical record was later extended by continuators to the year 1283.
