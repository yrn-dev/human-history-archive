# Asrār ut-tanzīl wa Asrār ut-ta'wīl

*Asrār ut-tanzīl wa Asrār ut-ta'wīl* (translated as *The Secrets of Revelation and The Secrets of Interpretation*) is a prominent commentary on the Koran. It was authored by the Mahommedan critic 'Abdallah ibn 'Umar al-Baiḍāwī.

## Author and Historical Context
'Abdallah ibn 'Umar al-Baiḍāwī was born in Fars during the reign of the Atabek ruler Abu Bakr ibn Sa'd (1226-1260). His father served as the chief judge in Fars. Baiḍāwī followed a judicial path, becoming a judge in Shiraz. He died in Tabriz approximately in the year 1286. In addition to his major commentary on the Koran, Baiḍāwī wrote several treatises on theology.

## Nature and Composition of the Work
The work is primarily a digest of *al-Kashshāf*, a great Mu'tazalite commentary written by Zamakhsharī. In creating this digest, Baiḍāwī included additional notes and made certain omissions. 

While the work is regarded by orthodox Moslems as the standard commentary and is viewed as almost holy, it has been noted by some critics, such as Th. Noeldeke, that the text is not always accurate. Furthermore, it is not considered complete in its treatment of the linguistic or theological knowledge it addresses.

## Reception and Academic Study
The commentary has seen widespread distribution and study:
* **Editions:** Numerous editions have been published in the East. H. O. Fleischer edited a two-volume version in Leipzig between 1846 and 1848, with indices later edited by W. Fell in 1878.
* **Selections:** D. S. Margoliouth edited a selection of the work titled *Chrestomathia Beidawiana*, published in London in 1894.
* **Further Scholarship:** The influence of the work is evidenced by the many supercommentaries that have been written based on Baiḍāwī's original text.
