# Regesta chronologico-diplomatica regum atque imperatorum Romanorum 911-1313

The *Regesta chronologico-diplomatica regum atque imperatorum Romanorum 911-1313* is a historical abstract published in Frankfort in 1831. It was authored by the German historian Johann Friedrich Bohmer.

## Author and Context
Johann Friedrich Bohmer (1795–1863) was born in Frankfort-on-Main on April 22, 1795. He received his education at the universities of Gottingen and Heidelberg. While he initially showed an interest in art and traveled to Italy, he eventually dedicated himself to the study of history upon returning to Frankfort. Bohmer held several professional roles in his home city, serving as the librarian, the archivist, and the secretary of the *Gesellschaft fur altere deutsche Geschichtskunde*.

Bohmer was known for his personal convictions, possessing a strong affection for the Roman Catholic Church and Austria, while maintaining a great dislike for the Protestant faith and Prussia. Although he did not belong to the Roman Catholic Church himself, these leanings were noted in relation to his critical sense.

## Scope and Methodology
Bohmer's primary contribution to historical scholarship focused on the collection and tabulation of imperial documents from the middle ages, as well as charters. The *Regesta chronologico-diplomatica regum atque imperatorum Romanorum 911-1313* served as the first appearance of his work in this vein. These collections are characterized by the inclusion of explanatory passages and introductions written by Bohmer.

## Subsequent Works and Related Collections
Following the 1831 publication, Bohmer expanded his research into other periods and types of documentation:

*   **Regesta chronologico-diplomatica Karolorum**: Published in Frankfort in 1833, this work provided short abstracts of the documents of all the Carolingians.
*   **Regesta imperii**: Bohmer produced a series of these works, including a volume for the period 1314-1347 (Frankfort, 1839), which was followed by three additional volumes. He also produced two supplementary volumes for the period 1246-1313 (Frankfort, 1844). A further period, 1198-1254, was edited by Bohmer and published in Stuttgart in 1849.
*   **Fontes rerum Germanicarum**: Published in Stuttgart between 1843 and 1868, this collection gathered original authorities for German history during the 14th and 13th centuries. The final fourth volume was edited by A. Huber after Bohmer's death.
*   **Other Edited Collections**: Bohmer edited *Die Reichsgesetze 900-1400* (Frankfort, 1832), the *Codex diplomaticus Moeno-Francofurtanus. Urkundenbuch der Reichsstadt Frankfurt* (Frankfort, 1836), and the *Wittelsbachische Regesten von der Erwerbung des Herzogtums Bayern bis zu 1340* (Stuttgart, 1854).

## Legacy
Johann Friedrich Bohmer died unmarried in Frankfort on October 22, 1863. Despite notes that his critical sense may have been somewhat warped, his researches are considered of great value to students of history.
