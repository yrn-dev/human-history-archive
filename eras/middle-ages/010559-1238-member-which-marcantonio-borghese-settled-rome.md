# Borghese

The Borghese are a noble Italian family of Sienese origin. The family is first mentioned in historical records in 1238. A member of the family, Marcantonio Borghese, settled in Rome; he was the father of Camillo Borghese (1550–1620), who was elected pope in 1605 under the title of Paul V.

## Rise to Roman Nobility
Under the papacy of Paul V, the family's status and titles expanded. On November 17, 1609, Paul created his nephew a prince of Vivero. In 1610, Philip III of Spain conferred upon him the title of prince of Sulmona. The family further solidified its position among the higher Roman nobility in 1614 through the marriage of the prince's son, Paolo, to Olimpia, who was the heiress of the Aldobrandini family.

## Later History and Napoleonic Ties
In 1803, Camillo Filippo Ludovico, Prince Borghese (born 1775), married Pauline, the sister of Emperor Napoleon and widow of General Leclerc. Pauline was born in Ajaccio on October 20, 1780, and had previously accompanied General Leclerc to St Domingo, where he died of yellow fever in 1802. Following her marriage to Prince Camillo on August 23, 1803, she resided in Rome, though she later returned to Paris. In 1806, she received the title of duchess of Guastalla, a title also held by Camillo Filippo Ludovico.

Pauline's relationship with the imperial court was strained; her treatment of the new empress, Marie Louise, in 1810 resulted in her removal from court. However, in 1814, she traveled to Elba with "Madame Mere" and reportedly wished to share Napoleon's exile in St Helena. She died of cancer in 1825. Camillo Filippo Ludovico served for several years as the governor of the Genoese and Piedmontese provinces. After the fall of Napoleon, he moved his residence to Florence, where he died in 1832.

## The Borghese Palace and Villa
The Borghese palace in Rome is described as one of the city's most magnificent buildings. It once housed a splendid gallery of pictures. Most of these works were transferred to the Villa Borghese, located outside the Porto del Popolo. This site, now known as Villa Umberto I, is the property of the Italian government. The villa also contains a statue in a sitting posture with an open hand; while vulgarly attributed to Belisarius, it has been ascribed to Augustus propitiating Nemesis.

## Patronage of the Arts
The Borghese family acted as patrons for various artists in Rome. The painter Domenichino obtained employment from the Cardinals Borghese, as well as the Aldobrandini and Farnese families, for whom he painted works in fresco.
