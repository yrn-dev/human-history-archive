# Continuation of Theophanes, Genesius, and Vita

The *Continuation of Theophanes* and the works of Genesius, along with various *Vitae* (biographies), serve as primary historical sources for the study of the Byzantine Empire and related ecclesiastical figures. These texts provide diverse perspectives on the political and religious landscape of their respective eras, ranging from the exploits of military rebels to the glorification of imperial figures.

## The Continuation of Theophanes and Genesius
The *Continuation of Theophanes* is a historical record that includes the work of Petrus Siculus. This source is utilized to describe the exploits and revolt of Carbeas and the Paulicians. 

Similarly, the writer Genesius provides historical accounts in his *Chronicle* (edit. Venet.). In his writings regarding Chrysocheir, Genesius is noted for exposing the "nakedness of the empire." These accounts contrast with other historians of the period, such as Cedrenus, who is described as lacking the passions or knowledge found in the works of Genesius and Constantine Porphyrogenitus.

## The Vita Basilii
The *Vita Basilii* is a biographical work written by Constantine VII, who was the grandson of the subject. This text is preserved as book v. of the *Continuation of Theophanes* (ed. Bonn). In this work, Constantine Porphyrogenitus displays the glory of his grandfather, providing a contrasting imperial perspective to the more critical accounts found in the works of Genesius.

## Other Biographical and Historical Sources
The archive identifies several other *Vitae* and historical editions associated with these scholarly traditions:

*   **Vita Euthymii**: An edition of this work was published by De Boor in Berlin in 1888.
*   **Vitae of Cardinals**: The lives of Cardinals Pandulf and Boso are found in J. M. Watterich's *Pontif. Roman. vitae* (vol. 2, Leipzig, 1862).
*   **Vita Lysandri**: A work by C. A. Gehlert published in Bautzen in 1874.
*   **Vita del cardinale Bellarmino**: A biography of Cardinal Bellarmine written by J. Fuligatti and published in Rome in 1624. Other accounts of Bellarmine include D. Bartoli's *Della vita di Roberto cardinal Bellarmino* (Rome, 1678) and M. Cervin's *Imago virtutum Roberti card. Bellarmini Politiani* (Siena, 1622), though these are characterized as panegyrics of limited historical value.

## Scholarly Editions and Archives
Many of these texts have been preserved through specific academic editions, particularly those produced in Bonn. The Bonn *Corpus scriptorum Hist. Byz.*, edited by I. Bekker in 1834, contains a history preserved in a single Paris manuscript. This specific Bonn edition is notable for including a 15th-century Italian translation of the text, which was discovered in a Venice library by Leopold Ranke and subsequently sent to Bekker.
