# Gallus

The name Gallus appears in the archive's records in several distinct historical and literary contexts, most notably as a Roman emperor, a military commander, and as a reference in historical chronicles.

## Gallus as Roman Emperor
Gallus is identified as one of the Roman emperors, appearing in historical records alongside Decius, Æmilianus, Valerian, and Gallienus. His character and reign are described with significant severity in the writings of Eutropius, who wrote an abridgment approximately fifteen years after Gallus's death. Eutropius characterizes Gallus Cæsar as a man who was naturally fierce and more prone to tyranny, suggesting that he would have acted as such had it been within his right to rule.

The records of Ammianus Marcellin provide ample detail regarding the cruelties committed by Gallus. One specific instance of his severity involved a man named Clematius of Alexandria; Clematius was executed for refusing to satisfy the desires of his mother-in-law, who sought his death after being disappointed of his love. Furthermore, the brother of Gallus, Julian, suggests that a secret conspiracy had been formed against him. Zosimus identifies the participants in this conspiracy as two obscure agents seeking to make their fortunes and a minister of considerable rank.

## Ælius Gallus and the Sabæan Campaign
Separate from the emperor, the records mention Ælius Gallus in the context of an expedition to Sabæa. There is a scholarly dispute regarding the geography of his campaign. While Pliny reckons Mariaba among the conquests of Gallus, other sources, such as Strabo, suggest that the invaders failed before a place called Marsuabæ. It is noted that Mariaba and Marsuabæ are distinct locations, and some observations suggest that Ælius Gallus would not have failed due to a lack of water before reaching Mariaba. Consequently, it is concluded that Gallus did not approach the capital of Sabæa.
