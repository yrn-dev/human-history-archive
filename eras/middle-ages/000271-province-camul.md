# THE PROVINCE OF CAMUL

## Geography and Administration
Camul is a province that formerly functioned as a kingdom. It is characterized by the presence of numerous villages and towns, with the chief city of the region bearing the name of Camul. Geographically, the province is situated between two deserts: the Great Desert of Lop on one side, and a smaller desert on the other, which is three days' journey in extent.

## Culture and Society
The inhabitants of Camul possess a peculiar language and are described as Idolaters. The people are noted for a relaxed disposition, dedicating much of their time to singing, dancing, playing, and general enjoyment. Their economy is based on the fruits of the earth, which are produced in abundance and sold to travelers.

The women of the province are described as fair and wanton. The society is noted for a specific domestic custom regarding the treatment of foreigners. When a stranger comes to lodge in a home, the host is delighted and arranges for his wife to put herself entirely at the guest's disposal. During this time, the husband removes himself from the house and does not return until the guest has departed. The guest may enjoy the society of the wife for as long as he desires. The husbands of Camul do not feel shame regarding this practice; rather, they consider it an honor. Consequently, all men in the province are made wittols by their wives in this manner.

## Legal Conflict and Tradition
During the reign of Mangu Kaan, the lord of the province became aware of this custom and issued an order forbidding the practice under threat of grievous penalties. In place of this custom, the order suggested the provision of public hostelries for travelers.

The people of Camul were much vexed by this decree. Although they adhered to the order for approximately three years, they subsequently observed that their lands had ceased to be fruitful and that many mishaps had occurred. Believing that their gods bestowed the good things they possessed specifically because of this inherited usage, the people concluded they could not continue to exist without it.

The inhabitants collected a grand present and sent a petition to Mangu Kaan, praying for the right to retain the custom of their ancestors. Upon hearing the petition, the Prince replied, “Since ye must needs keep your shame, keep it then,” and granted them the liberty to maintain the practice. The people of Camul have continued this custom since that time.
