# BOMBARDIER

## Definition and Evolution
The term bombardier originally referred to an artilleryman who was in charge of a bombard. In the modern British army, a bombardier is a non-commissioned officer within the artillery, holding a rank that is below that of a corporal.

## The Bombard
The bombard was a type of cannon used during the Middle Ages, primarily employed during sieges to throw heavy stone balls. The name is derived from the Greek word *bombein*, which means to make a humming noise, passing through French and Medieval Latin forms. The use of the term later evolved into a verb, resulting in the word bombardment.

## Related Musical Instruments
The name "bombard" was also applied to various medieval musical instruments, including the "pumhart," "pommer," and "bumhart." These instruments served as forerunners to the schalmey or the bass oboe. In the 18th century, German ancestors used the terms *Bombardi* or *Fagotte* to describe the *Deutscher Basson* before the influence of French and Italian styles. These were extraordinarily long instruments, such as the bass pommer.

In contemporary times, Breton peasants use a small, primitive oboe known as a *bombarde*, which features eight holes and no keys.

## Bombardment
Bombardment is defined as an attack by artillery fire directed at troops in position, fortifications, buildings, or towns. In a strict sense, the term applies to the targeting of undefended or defenceless objects, such as public buildings and houses. The objective of such an attack is to dishearten an opponent and coerce the civil authorities and population of a besieged location into persuading their military commandant to capitulate before the actual defences are rendered impotent.

Such bombardments generally fail if the commandant is determined, and they typically only succeed if the suffering of non-combatants is sufficient to break their resolution. Unlike "artillery attacks," which are slow, steady, and minutely accurate strikes against fortifications, a bombardment is an intentionally terrific fire at a large target. This method wears out the attacking guns and requires the expenditure of large quantities of ammunition. Despite this, bombardments are frequently used to test the temper of a civil population and garrison, as seen in Strassburg in 1870. The term is also loosely used to describe artillery attacks on fortified positions to prepare for infantry assaults.

## Historical Instances of Bombardment
Historical records document the severe impact of bombardments on various cities and structures:

*   **Antwerp:** The city suffered damage from bombardment, including a two-hour bombardment of the St. Anne’s quarter. Reports indicate that shells set the town on fire in several places, creating a "terrific din" of crashing walls and roaring guns.
*   **Belfort:** In December 1870, the town was subjected to bombardment from batteries constructed by von Tresckow. While some houses were damaged, the garrison remained unintimidated and replied with their own artillery.
*   **Ypres:** German bombardments destroyed the Cathedral, the Hôtel de Ville, and the Cloth Hall.
*   **Caix:** The village suffered some damage, but the local church was severely affected; the stained glass was destroyed and the upper part of the belfry collapsed.
