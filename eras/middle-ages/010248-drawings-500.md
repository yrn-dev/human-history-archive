# Drawings

In the context of historical records and artistic archives, drawings serve various functions, ranging from scientific observation and religious representation to the reproduction of architectural sites and the illustration of ancient manuscripts.

## Botanical and Religious Illustrations
Historical manuscripts have utilized drawings to document the natural world and religious narratives. In the *Natural History* of Dioscorides of Vienna, produced around 500, plants are drawn with a level of observational accuracy that the archive notes disappeared for a thousand years. Similarly, the Joshua Roll of the Vatican features a series of drawings that are delicately tinted in blue and pink. These works often incorporate classical survivals, such as the personification of rivers. Later artistic schools showed a marked classical revival in the 10th century, while others integrated Persian influences, specifically intricate arabesques resembling eastern rugs.

## Architectural and Cultural Reproductions
Drawings have also been used to preserve and share images of distant locations. In the records regarding the Travels of Marco Polo, reproductions of the Temple of 500 Genii (*Fa Lum Sze*) at Canton were produced. These were based on drawings by Félix Régamey, who created them using photographs provided by M. Camille Imbault Huart, the French Consul at Canton. These drawings included a reproduction of the temple and a specific figure within the temple—one of 500 figures of Buddhist saints or *Arhans*. This particular figure attracted attention due to its hat, which resembles a sailor's straw hat. A model of this figure was exhibited at the international Geographical Congress in Venice in 1881.
