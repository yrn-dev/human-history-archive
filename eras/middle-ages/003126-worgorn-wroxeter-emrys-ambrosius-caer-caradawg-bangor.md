# Bangor and Associated Institutions

The term "bangor" refers to various locations and institutions across different geographical regions and historical contexts, ranging from ancient collegiate institutions in Britain to modern cities in North America and Ireland.

## Ancient Bangor Institutions
According to the *Myvyrian Archaeology*, there were three principal *bangor* (college) institutions. These included the *bangor* of Illtud Farchawg, located at Caer Worgorn (Wroxeter), and the *bangor* of Emrys (Ambrosius), located at Caer Caradawg. A third institution, the *bangor wydrin* (glass), was situated in the "glass isle" of Afallach. Additionally, a fourth institution is noted as *bangor Illtud*, also referred to as Llanilltud or Llantwit major. In each of the first three colleges, 420 saints served in their pious offices, succeeding one another by hundreds, day and night.

## Bangor in North America
Bangor, Maine, is a city, port of entry, and the county-seat of Penobscot county, located at the confluence of the Penobscot river and the Kenduskeag stream. It is situated approximately 75 miles northeast of Augusta and 60 miles from the ocean. The city's population grew from 19,103 in 1890 to 24,803 by 1910.

The authentic history of the settlement began in 1769. Originally called Conduskeag and briefly known as Sunbury, the town was incorporated in 1791. The name was changed to Bangor through the influence of the first pastor, Rev. Seth Noble, after one of his favorite hymn-tunes. The city was chartered in 1834.

Bangor is a major trade center for an agricultural district and one of the largest lumber depots in the United States, also shipping ice. Its infrastructure includes the Maine Central railway and the Bangor & Aroostook railway. Notable institutions in the city include the law school of the University of Maine, the Eastern Maine general hospital, the Eastern Maine insane hospital, and the Bangor Theological Seminary (Congregational), which was incorporated in 1814 and empowered to confer degrees in divinity in 1905. Some antiquarians have identified the site as the mythical city of Norumbega, reporting that a French-built Fort Norombega stood there in 1656.

## Bangor in the United Kingdom
### North Wales
Bangor (formerly Bangor Fawr) is a city, seaport, and market-town in Carnarvonshire, North Wales. It is located near the northern entrance of the Menai Straits. The city features a cruciform cathedral on the site of a church possibly founded by St Deiniol around 525, which was destroyed by the English in 1071. The cathedral underwent various reconstructions, including work by Bishop Dean during the time of Henry VII and Bishop Skevyngton in 1532, and a restoration by Sir G. Scott between 1866 and 1875. Other notable buildings include the University College of N. Wales (1883) and several other training and religious colleges.

### Northern Ireland
Bangor, Co. Down, is a seaport and market-town located on the south side of Belfast Lough. It is known as a watering-place and a center for trade in embroidered muslin, linen, and cotton. The town contains slight remains of an abbey of Canons Regular founded by St Comgall in the middle of the 6th century, which was later rebuilt in the first half of the 12th century by St Malachy O'Morgair.

## Liturgical and Architectural Contexts
In ecclesiastical history, the "use of Bangor" refers to specific liturgical rubrics. Under this use, the priest censed the altar before the Introit and the middle of the altar before the reading of the Gospel. According to the Bangor rubric, the thurible and lights attended the Gospel to the lectern. Following the offering of oblations, the priest censed the oblations and the space between himself and the altar; specifically at Bangor, the relics and the *Sinistrum Cornu* of the altar were censed.

Regarding architecture, the site of Uriconium (Wroxeter), where the *bangor* of Illtud Farchawg was located, is noted for containing remains of a basilica.
