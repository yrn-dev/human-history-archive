# Bridlington

Bridlington is a seaside resort, municipal borough, and market town located in the East Riding of Yorkshire, England, within the Buckrose parliamentary division. It is situated 31 miles north-northeast of Hull and is accessible via a branch of the North Eastern railway. The town is divided into two distinct areas: the ancient market town, located approximately one mile from the coast, and Bridlington Quay, a watering-place consisting of modern houses that fringe the shore of Bridlington Bay. The coastline to the north is steep and fine, featuring the eastward projection of the great spur of Flamborough Head, while the coast to the south is low.

## Early History and Norman Conquest
Evidence from the Domesday survey, which mentions four burgesses at Bridlington (also referred to as Brellington or Burlington), indicates that the settlement was a borough prior to the Norman Conquest. During the reign of Edward the Confessor, Bridlington was part of the possessions of Earl Morcar and had a value of £32. However, along with the rest of the north of England, Bridlington suffered from the ravages of the Normans. By the time of the Domesday survey, the town had decreased in value to 8s. and had passed into the hands of the king following the forfeiture of Earl Morcar.

## The Priory and Medieval Development
William II granted the town to Gilbert de Gaunt. Gilbert's son and heir, Walter de Gaunt, founded an Augustinian priory around 1114, endowing it with other lands and the manor of Bridlington. This foundation marked the beginning of a steady increase in the town's importance. The gift made by Walter de Gaunt was confirmed by Henry I and several subsequent kings. King Stephen further expanded the town's privileges by granting the right to have a port.

In 1200, King John granted the prior of Bridlington an annual fair on the vigil, feast, and morrow of the Assumption of the Virgin Mary, as well as a weekly market held on Saturdays. Later, in 1446, Henry VI granted the prior three additional annual fairs to commemorate the Translation and Deposition of St John (a former prior of Bridlington) and the vigil, day, and morrow of the Nativity of the Virgin Mary.

The priory flourished until 1537, when the last prior was executed for his involvement in the Pilgrimage of Grace. The church of St Mary and St Nicholas, which remains in the old town, consists of the priory church's Early English portions and a fine nave in the Perpendicular and Decorated styles. The Perpendicular gateway of the priory now serves as the town hall.

## Post-Dissolution and Governance
Following the Dissolution, the manor of Bridlington remained with the crown until 1624, when Charles I granted it to Sir John Ramsey. In 1633, Sir George Ramsey (brother and heir to Sir John) sold the manor to thirteen inhabitants of the town on behalf of all the manor's tenants. These thirteen lords were assisted by twelve other inhabitants chosen by freeholders. When the number of lords eventually dropped to six, seven replacements were chosen from the assistants, and a chief lord was elected annually. This system of governance continues to hold good.

While the fact that thirteen inhabitants were permitted to hold the manor suggests a form of incorporation existed in the 17th century, the official incorporation charter was not granted until 1899, when Bridlington was created a municipal borough. The borough covers an area of 2,751 acres and is governed by a mayor, 18 councillors, and 6 aldermen.

## Local Economy and Geography
Bridlington Quay is noted for its ornamental gardens, parade, and excellent sea-bathing. Extensive works have been conducted along the sea front, and the area features a chalybeate spring. The harbor is protected by two stone piers, providing good anchorage within the bay.

## Notable Figures and Associations
The town is associated with several historical figures and works. Peter Langtoft, a canon of Bridlington, wrote an Anglo-French chronicle that served as a source for the later chronicle of Robert Mannyng of Brunne. Additionally, a canon of Bridlington authored the *Gesta Edwardi de Carnarvan*. In religious history, a Congregational society was established in 1662, with a church built in 1702 that remained until 1906.
