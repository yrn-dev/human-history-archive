# HOW THE OLD MAN USED TO TRAIN HIS ASSASSINS

## Overview of the Sect
The "Old Man of the Mountain" was the title given to the chief of a sect known as the Assassins, also referred to as the *Hashíshîn* or *Muláhidah*. While Crusaders applied this title to the leader of the branch settled in the mountains north of Lebanon (a translation of the Arabic *Shaikh-ul-Jibal*), it is probable that the title properly belonged to the Prince of Alamút. The territory of this leader was known as the *Balad-ul-Jibal*, and he notably never referred to himself as an Amir, Malik, or Sultan.

The sect's kingdom is mentioned in Armenian history as *Mulehet* and was referred to as the kingdom of *Mulahi* in Chinese narratives concerning the expedition of Hulaku.

## Organization and Hierarchy
The Assassins were organized into several divisions scattered across various regions, including Asia Minor, Syria, and Kurdistan (specifically near the Lake of Wan). Despite this geographic dispersion, all branches acknowledged a single High Priest or Imaum: the Chief residing at Alamut.

Under the authority of the "Grand Master" or Shaikh at Alamut, there were "Grand Priors" who managed specific regions. These included chiefs located in:
* ’Irák
* Kumis (the region surrounding Bostam and Damghan)
* The Kuhistan of Eastern Persia (Tun-o-Kaïn)
* Syria

## Training and Operation of the Assassins
The adepts of the sect, known as *Fidáwí*, were trained to carry out notable murders. In later years, the nature of their service evolved, and the *Fidáwí* appeared to operate as hired assassins, offering their services for payment. This shift is evidenced by records from Bibars, who boasted in a letter to his court in Cairo about utilizing these assassins when necessary.

The influence and reach of the Old Man's training were such that the sect was linked to high-profile political attempts, including an attempt on Prince Edward, which some authors ascribe to the instigation of Bibars. Additionally, some accounts from the perspective of the Moslems include confessions from assassins claiming they were sent by the King of England. In response to such claims, a letter attributed to the "Sheich" or Old Man of the Mountain emerged, which sought to justify Richard by assuming the guilt or merit of the murder himself.

## Decline of the Sect
The sect faced apparent destruction through the actions of Hulákú, though sources indicate it survived beyond that period. The primary strongholds associated with the Old Man and his followers were the castles of Girdkuh and Alamut.
