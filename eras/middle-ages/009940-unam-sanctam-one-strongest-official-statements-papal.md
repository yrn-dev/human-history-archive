## Overview of Papal Authority and Conflict
The history of the papacy is marked by recurring tensions between the pretensions of the Pope to act as the absolute ruler of all Christendom and the claims of secular rulers. This conflict, which had been smouldering over time, was articulated in a document of extreme importance by Frederick II in response to Pope Gregory IX (1227). Frederick's document addressed the princes of Europe, launching a shattering attack on the ambition of the Pope to become the absolute ruler of all Europe and suggesting a union of princes to oppose such usurpation.

## The Pontificate of Boniface VIII
Benedetto Gaetano, known as Pope Boniface VIII, reigned from 1294 to 1303. Born into a noble family in Anagni, he was educated in civil and canon law in Italy and possibly Paris. Before his ascension to the papacy, he served as a notary and advocate at the papal court and was created a cardinal deacon in 1281 and a cardinal priest in 1291. He was chosen as pope at Naples on December 24, 1294, following the abdication of Celestine V.

Unlike his predecessor, who ruled with humility and monastic poverty, Boniface VIII ardently asserted the lordship of the papacy over all the kingdoms of the world. His reign was characterized by great pomp, beginning with his coronation in Rome in January 1295.

## Political Controversies and Diplomatic Failures
Although Boniface VIII initially planned to pacify the West and recover the Holy Land from the infidel, his nine-year reign was defined by controversies with leading European powers. His tenure was marked by arrogance, lofty claims, and avarice, which created numerous enemies.

His political efforts included several unsuccessful or failed ventures:
* **France:** His most noted conflict was with Philip IV of France.
* **England:** He attempted to try Edward I on charges of interfering with a papal fief, but the English parliament declared in 1301 that Scotland had never been a fief of Rome.
* **Germany and Hungary:** He refused to recognize Albert of Austria as the rightful German king until 1303 and declared that the crown of Hungary should fall to the house of Anjou, assuming he was the overlord of that nation.
* **Denmark:** He succeeded in humbling Eric VI of Denmark.
* **Sicily:** His policy of supporting the house of Anjou in Sicily resulted in a grand failure.

Additionally, his attempts to establish great estates for his own family alienated the Colonna.

## Other Papal Instruments and Jurisdictions
The archive records other forms of papal communication and censure. The *In Coena Domini* was a papal bull first published in 1363 and issued annually on Holy Thursday or Easter Monday. It served as a statement of ecclesiastical censure against crimes such as piracy, forgery, sacrilege, schisms, heresies, and infringements of papal privileges. Due to opposition from both Catholic and Protestant sovereigns who viewed the bull as an infringement of their rights, Pope Clement XIV discontinued its publication in 1770.

Furthermore, the archive notes the use of broadsides (or broadsheets) for the dissemination of papal indulgences and royal proclamations from the beginning of printing.
