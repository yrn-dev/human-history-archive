# THE FASHION OF THE GREAT KAAN’S TABLE AT HIS HIGH FEASTS

The high feasts of the Great Kaan are characterized by a rigid social hierarchy, specific spatial arrangements within the Great Hall, and elaborate rituals of adoration. These gatherings serve as significant court occasions where the Emperor's authority is visually and ceremonially reinforced.

## Seating and Spatial Arrangement
The layout of the tables is designed so that the Emperor can see all the guests from end to end, regardless of how many tables are present. The Great Kaan sits at the north end of the hall, facing south. His table is elevated significantly above all others.

The seating order follows a strict hierarchy of blood and rank:
*   **The Imperial Family:** The Kaan's chief wife sits to his left. To his right are his sons, nephews, and other kinsmen of the Blood Imperial; however, they sit lower than the Emperor, such that their heads are level with his feet.
*   **The Nobility:** Other Barons are seated at tables that are lower still than those of the Imperial kinsmen.
*   **The Women:** The wives of the Kaan's sons, nephews, and other kinsmen sit at a lower table to the Emperor's right. Below them are the ladies of the Knights and other Barons, each placed according to the Lord's specific orders.

It is noted that not all attendees sit at tables. A large portion of the officers and soldiers take their meals on carpets within the hall. Outside the hall, crowds often exceed 40,000 people, consisting of foreign visitors bringing curiosities or people bringing presents to the Lord.

## Pre-Feast Rituals and Adoration
On the morning of a feast, before the tables are set, a wide array of officials and specialists present themselves in the Great Hall. This group includes Kings, Dukes, Marquesses, Counts, Barons, Knights, Astrologers, Philosophers, Leeches, and Falconers. Those unable to fit inside the hall stand outside in positions where the Emperor can see them clearly.

The company is marshalled by rank: first the sons, nephews, and Princes of the Blood Imperial, followed by Kings, then Dukes, and then all others according to their degree. Once seated, a great prelate commands the company to "Bow and adore!" The attendees bow until their foreheads touch the earth, repeating this act of adoration four times toward the Emperor as if he were a god. Following this, they proceed to a highly decorated altar featuring a gold censer and a vermilion tablet inscribed with the Grand Kaan's name, which they incense with great reverence before returning to their seats.

## Major Annual Festivals
The Great Kaan holds several significant feasts throughout the year:
*   **The Birthday Feast:** The greatest feast of the year (excluding the New Year) is held on the 28th day of the September moon. On this day, all Tartars and governments owing allegiance to the Kaan offer presents based on fixed prescriptions or their own ability. Others bring presents to seek employment, and twelve Barons are tasked with providing answers to these supplicants. This day is marked by solemn devotions from Christians, Saracens, Idolaters, and other peoples, who burn incense and light lamps to pray for the Emperor's health, happiness, and long life.
*   **The New Year Feast:** This is referred to as the "White Feast."
