# Francis Borgia

## Early Life and Court Career
Francis Borgia was born on October 10, 1510, in Gandia, Valencia. From his youth, he was noted for his piety. At the age of twelve, he began his education at Saragossa under the guidance of his uncle, who served as the archbishop. During this period, Borgia developed a strong inclination toward monastic life.

However, in 1528, his father sent him to the court of Charles V. Borgia distinguished himself in this environment and married a high-ranking Portuguese lady named Eleanor de Castro. Following this marriage, he was appointed as the master of the horse to the empress and was created the marquis of Lombay. His service to Charles V included accompanying him on an expedition to Africa in 1535 and a trip to Provence in 1536.

## Transition to Religious Life
A turning point occurred in 1539 upon the death of the empress. Borgia was tasked with convoying her body to the burial-place in Granada. This experience reinforced his resolve to embrace a monastic life, provided he survived his wife. Despite this determination, he was granted further honors upon his return to Toledo, though these were thrust upon him against his will. He was appointed as the commander of the order of St James and the viceroy of Catalonia.

While serving as viceroy, Borgia established his government at Barcelona. During his tenure there, he lived a life of great austerity, though he discharged his official duties with efficiency and energy. He remained in this position until 1543, at which point he succeeded his father in the dukedom. Having obtained permission to resign his viceroyalty, he retired to Gandia to pursue a more congenial mode of life.

## The Society of Jesus
Borgia had maintained correspondence with Ignatius Loyola and became a powerful encourager of the recently founded order of Jesus. Upon his return to Gandia, one of his primary objectives was the construction of a Jesuit college. Following the death of Eleanor in 1546, Borgia resolved to join the society himself. 

Due to family and political circumstances, he obtained a papal dispensation that allowed him to retain his worldly possessions and dignities for four years after taking his vows for the benefit of his young children. In 1550, he visited Rome, where he was received with distinction and provided the funds necessary to build the Collegium Romanum. He returned to Spain the following year and formally resigned his estate and rank. Francis Borgia eventually served as the general of the order of Jesuits and is recognized as a Roman Catholic saint. He died in 1572.
