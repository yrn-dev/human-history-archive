# How the Two Brothers Went on Beyond Soldaia

The journey of the two brothers, Messer Nicolas Polo and his brother Messer Maffeo Polo, beyond the port of Soldaia represents a significant venture of trade and exploration. Having previously traveled from Venice to Constantinople with merchants' wares, the two brothers—described as noble, wise, and provident—decided to cross the Greater Sea (also known as the Euxine or the Black Sea) to Soldaia, carrying a store of jewels.

## Departure from Soldaia and the Court of Barca Kaan
After staying for a period in Soldaia, the brothers determined to extend their journey further. They traveled from the port until they reached the court of a Tartar Prince named Barca Kaan. The residences of Barca Kaan were located at Bolgara and Sara (also referred to as Sarai). 

Barca Kaan, who was regarded as one of the most liberal and courteous Princes among the Tartars, received the two brothers with great honor. In return, the brothers presented the Prince with all the jewels they had brought with them. Barca Kaan accepted this offering graciously and provided the brothers with a return value at least twice that of the jewels.

## Conflict and Further Travel
The brothers remained at the court of Barca Kaan for one year. During this time, a great war broke out between Barca Kaan and Aláu, the Lord of the Tartars of the Levant, resulting in the mustering of great hosts on both sides.

Following their time at the court, the brothers made arrangements to continue their travels. They joined company with certain envoys and journeyed for a full year, traveling in a northward and north-eastward direction. During this year of travel, they witnessed many marvels of various kinds.

## Arrival at the Court of the Great Kaan
The journey eventually led the two brothers to the court of the Great Kaan. Upon their arrival, the Great Kaan received them with hospitality and great honor. The Kaan expressed significant interest in their origins and the world they came from, posing a series of detailed questions.

The Great Kaan first inquired about the emperors, specifically regarding how they administered justice in their dominions, how they maintained their dignity, and their conduct when going forth to battle. He sought similar information regarding other kings, princes, and potentates.

## Discussions on Christianity and the Papacy
The Great Kaan further questioned the brothers about the Church, the Pope, the customs of the Latins, and the activities occurring in Rome. Because the two brothers were proficient in the Tartar language, they were able to provide the Great Kaan with a truthful, orderly, and sensible account of these matters.
