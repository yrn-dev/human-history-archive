# The History of the Ottoman Empire by Joseph von Hammer-Purgstall

The work *The History of the Ottoman Empire* by Joseph von Hammer-Purgstall (with a French translation by J. J. Hellert published between 1835 and 1843) serves as a significant authority on the Ottoman dynasty. It is cited as a source that can enlighten those seeking knowledge of the history and geography of the Orient.

## Historical Accounts and Corrections
While von Hammer-Purgstall provides accounts of the Barbarossas, these records are noted to require correction by other authorities. Alternative perspectives include a 16th-century Arabic chronicle, *La Fondation de la regence d'Alger, histoire des Barberousse*, published in 1837 by Sander Rang and Ferdinand Denis. Further evidence regarding the Barbarossas was collected by H. D. de Grammont in *Histoire d'Alger* (1887), who also examined the origin of the name in the *Revue Africaine*. Additionally, Ad. Jurien de la Graviere provides technical knowledge of their campaigns in *Les Corsaires barbaresques et la marine de Soliman le Grand* (1887) and *Doria et Barberousse* (1886).

## Ottoman Historiography and Sources
There has been historical debate regarding the existence of Turkish writers prior to Mahomet II. While some sources suggested a lack of early historians, von Hammer-Purgstall demonstrated that the Turks possessed learned lawyers, poets, authors on medicine, and sheiks (religious writers). 

The oldest historical work utilized by von Hammer-Purgstall is the *Tarichi Aaschik Paschasade* (the History of the Great Grandson of Aaschik Pasha). Aaschik Pasha was a celebrated ascetic poet and dervis during the reign of Murad I. The author of this history, Ahmed, lived during the reign of Bajazet II and drew information from the book of Scheik Jachshi. Jachshi was the son of Elias, who served as Imaum to Sultan Orchan (the second Ottoman king) and recounted the earliest Ottoman history from his father.

## Analysis of Early Ottoman History
Von Hammer-Purgstall has addressed the silence of Turkish historians regarding early interactions between the Ottomans and the European continent. He enumerates sixteen different occasions of such intercourse, suggesting that Turkish historians may have disdained these "peaceful incursions" because they did not result in permanent footings on Byzantine territory or direct conquest. Regarding the first expedition of Soliman, von Hammer-Purgstall characterizes the account as one where the "poetry of tradition" had not yet been superseded by the "prose of history."

## Other Contributions and Observations
The works of von Hammer-Purgstall are used to document various aspects of Ottoman life and leadership:
* **Social Customs:** His writings are referenced regarding the "common delicacy" of Turkish nations in avoiding speech about their women.
* **Leadership and Conduct:** He provides records of "barbarities" perpetrated against brave men (vol. i, p. 295).
* **Personal Life of Rulers:** Von Hammer-Purgstall clarifies the nature of the retreat of Amurath, arguing it was the retreat of an "epicurean" focused on profane love rather than that of a dervis.
* **Chronology:** His work is used to verify the age and reign of certain figures, such as a ruler who reached the 75th year of his age and the 35th year of his reign.

## Related Maritime and Dynastic Records
Other authorities on the empire include Hajji Khalifa's *The History of the Maritime Wars of the Turks* (translated by J. Mitchell, 1831), which is said to be based on evidence collected by order of Sultan Suleiman. Other historical perspectives on the growth and decay of the empire (1300–1683) were provided by Demetrius Cantemir, prince of Moldavia, who utilized the *Synopsis* of Saadi Effendi of Larissa (dedicated to Sultan Mustapha in 1696).
