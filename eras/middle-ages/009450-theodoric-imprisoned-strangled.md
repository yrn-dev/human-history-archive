# Theodoric and Amalasuntha

## The Reign of Theodoric
Theodoric was a ruler of the Amali regal succession, representing the eleventh generation of his line. Described as "the great Theodoric," his personal merit was such that it might have ennobled a plebeian origin. During his reign, he is noted for having issued a Latin edict consisting of one hundred and fifty-four articles, although Procopius affirms that no laws were promulgated by Theodoric and subsequent kings of Italy in the Gothic language. The image of Theodoric was engraved upon his coins, a practice his more modest successors did not follow, as they instead added their own names to the head of the reigning emperor.

Theodoric maintained a relationship with the emperor Anastasius, a friendship celebrated by Procopius and Cassiodorus, though the figurative style of these compliments was interpreted differently in Ravenna and Constantinople. In the administration of justice during this era, causes between Romans were judged by old Roman courts. Disputes between Goths were handled by the *comes Gothorum*. In cases involving both a Goth and a Roman, a mixed jurisdiction was used involving the *comes Gothorum* and a Roman jurist as an assessor, though this system naturally leaned toward the Goth.

## Amalasuntha
Amalasuntha was the daughter of Theodoric. Her birth served to unite two illustrious Barbarian families, as her mother was the sister of Clovis and descended from the Merovingian race of long-haired kings. Due to her sex, Amalasuntha was excluded from the Gothic throne. However, Theodoric's tenderness for his people and family led him to find the last heir of the royal line, Eutharic, who had ancestors that had taken refuge in Spain. Eutharic was elevated to the rank of prince and consul.

Following the deaths of her husband and father, Amalasuntha became the queen and regent of Italy and the guardian of her son, Athalaric. By the age of twenty-eight, she was recognized for her beauty—which was thought by Theodora to be capable of disputing the conquest of an emperor—as well as her resolution, activity, and "manly sense." She was highly educated, engaging in philosophic studies and possessing the ability to express herself with ease and elegance in the Gothic, Latin, and Greek tongues.

## Governance and Legacy
As regent, Amalasuntha sought to revive the prosperity of her father's reign through the imitation of his virtues. She worked to expiate the faults and obliterate the darker memories associated with the declining age of Theodoric. Her administration was characterized by extreme lenity toward her Roman subjects, refusing to inflict pecuniary or corporal penalties upon them. She also ignored the demands of the Goths, who, after forty years, continued to view the people of Italy as enemies or slaves.

Amalasuntha's governance was guided by the wisdom and eloquence of Cassiodorus. During her regency, she restored the paternal inheritance of the children of Symmachus and Boethius and sought the friendship of the emperor.

## Imprisonment and Death
The life of Amalasuntha ended in violence. She was imprisoned and strangled at a castle located on the island of Martana. This island is one of two islands found within the Lake of Bolsena (also known as *Lacus Volsiniensis*), a nearly circular body of water 71 square miles in area and 480 feet deep, situated 1,000 feet above sea-level in a large volcanic district.
