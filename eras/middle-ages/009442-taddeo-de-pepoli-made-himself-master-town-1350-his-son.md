# Taddeo de' Pepoli and the Governance of Bologna

## Rise to Power in Bologna
During the 12th through the 14th centuries, the city of Bologna was characterized by frequent warfare. The city strongly supported the Guelph cause in its conflicts against the neighboring cities of Emilia and Romagna, as well as against Frederick II. A notable event of this period occurred in 1249, when the Bolognese captured and imprisoned Enzio, the son of the emperor, for the remainder of his life.

Following these external conflicts, Bologna was plagued by internal struggles between the Ghibellines and the Guelphs. These domestic disputes weakened the commune to such an extent that in 1337, Taddeo de' Pepoli established himself as the master of the town.

## Transition of Control
The rule established by Taddeo de' Pepoli did not remain within the family for long. In 1350, his son sold the town to Giovanni Visconti of Milan. This transfer of power marked a shift in the city's administration, though the Visconti control was temporary. Ten years after the sale to Visconti, Bologna was given to the papacy.

## Subsequent Political Shifts
The city's relationship with the papacy was volatile. Shortly after being given to the papacy, Bologna revolted and regained its liberty. In 1401, Giovanni Bentivoglio seized power as the lord of Bologna, but his tenure was brief; he was killed during a rebellion in 1402.

Following Bentivoglio's death, the city returned to the control of the Visconti. After further struggles with the papacy, the Bentivoglio family secured the city again in 1438. They maintained control until 1506, when Pope Julius II drove them out and brought Bologna back under papal sway.

## Long-term Papal Administration
Following the expulsion of the Bentivoglio in 1506, Bologna remained under the authority of the papacy for several centuries. There were specific exceptions to this rule, including the Napoleonic period between 1796 and 1815, as well as the revolutions of 1831 and 1821. This period of papal influence ended in 1860, when Bologna became a part of the kingdom of Italy.

## Historical Context of the City
Bologna's early history includes a brief period of subjection to the Lombards. For much of its early existence, it remained under the rule of the exarchate of Ravenna until Pippin gave it to the papacy in 756. The city suffered a sacking by the Hungarians in 902. While the exact date it acquired its freedom and its motto, *Libertas*, is uncertain, the first constitution of the commune of Bologna is dated to approximately 1123, at which point the city was free and independent.
