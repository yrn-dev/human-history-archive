# The Return of the Great Kaan to Cambaluc

The return of the Great Kaan to his capital city of Cambaluc followed his military victory over Nayan. This specific campaign was notable because the Great Kaan typically did not lead campaigns himself, preferring to send his barons or his sons into the field. However, he viewed the rebellion of Nayan as a matter too perilous and serious to be handled by anyone other than himself.

## Return and Aftermath of the Rebellion
After defeating Nayan, the Great Kaan returned to Cambaluc, where he resided in a state of ease and festivity. The defeat and subsequent death of Nayan caused great trouble for another Tartar Lord named Caydu. While Caydu held himself ready for war, he remained in significant fear of being treated in the same manner as Nayan.

## The Administration of Justice and Religious Reforms
Upon his return to Cambaluc, the Great Kaan sought to investigate the causes of certain affairs, leading to the discovery of the "endless iniquities" of Achmath and his sons. It was revealed that Achmath and seven of his sons had ravished and forced numerous women to be their wives. In response, the Kaan ordered that the enormous amount of treasure Achmath had accumulated in the Old City be transferred to the Kaan's own treasury in the New City. The Kaan further ordered that Achmath's body be exhumed and cast into the streets to be torn by dogs, and those sons who had followed their father's evil example were ordered to be flayed alive.

These events led the Great Kaan to develop a strong disgust and abomination for the doctrines of the Sect of the Saracens, which he believed excused crimes, including murder, when committed against those outside their religion. Consequently, the Kaan summoned the Saracens and prohibited various religious practices. He commanded that they regulate their marriages according to Tartar Law and forbade the cutting of animals' throats for food, requiring instead that they rip the stomach in the Tartar way.

## Life and Residence in Cambaluc
Cambaluc serves as the capital city of Cathay and the site of the Great Kaan's great palace. The Kaan's annual schedule is divided to maximize enjoyment and avoid seasonal heat:
* **September through February:** Six months are spent at the chief palace in the royal city of Cambaluc.
* **March through May:** Three months are spent on a great hunting expedition toward the sea.
* **June through August:** Three months are spent at the city of Chandu in the Cane Palace to avoid the heat.

Between these periods, the Kaan returns to Cambaluc, including a brief three-day stay after his hunting expeditions.

## Imperial Charity and Social Support
The Great Kaan is noted for his great charity toward the poor within the city of Cambaluc. He establishes a system of annual support for a vast number of indigent families, regardless of their size (ranging from six to ten members or more), providing them with sufficient wheat and other corn for the entire year. Additionally, a daily dole is provided at the Court, where approximately 30,000 people receive a hot loaf of bread every day.

Beyond the poor, the Great Kaan also provides annual maintenance and clothing for approximately five thousand astrologers and soothsayers in Cambaluc, who are comprised of Cathayans, Saracens, and Christians.
