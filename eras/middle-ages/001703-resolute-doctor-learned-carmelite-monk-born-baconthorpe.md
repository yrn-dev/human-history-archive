# John Baconthorpe

John Baconthorpe, known by the title "the Resolute Doctor," was a learned Carmelite monk who died in 1346. He was born in Norfolk at Baconthorpe and is believed to have been the grandnephew of Roger Bacon.

## Education and Academic Career
Baconthorpe was raised in the Carmelite monastery of Blakeney, located near Walsingham. He pursued higher education at the universities of Paris and Oxford. During his time in Paris, he earned the reputation of being the "Princeps" of the Averroists. However, the scholar Renan suggests that Baconthorpe's efforts were not to promote Averroism as such, but rather to justify it against accusations of heterodoxy.

## Ecclesiastical and Political Life
In 1329, Baconthorpe was appointed as the twelfth provincial of the English Carmelites. He is noted for having advocated for the subordination of the clergy to the king, a position that appears to have anticipated the views of Wycliffe.

In 1333, he was summoned to Rome. Records indicate that he initially maintained the authority of the pope regarding cases of divorce, though he later retracted this opinion. He eventually died in London in 1346.

## Literary Works and Legacy
Baconthorpe's primary work is titled *Doctoris resoluti Joannis Bacconis Anglici Carmelitae radiantissimi opus super quattuor sententiarum libris*. First published in 1510, this work went through several editions. Its influence persisted for nearly three centuries, remaining a subject of study at Padua, which was the final home of Averroism. Additionally, Lucilio Vanini expressed great veneration for Baconthorpe.
