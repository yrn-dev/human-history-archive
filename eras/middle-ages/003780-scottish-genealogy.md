# Scottish Genealogy

## The Works of Barbour
In the records of Scottish genealogical writing, Wyntoun's *Chronicle* mentions a "Treteis" authored by Barbour. This work is described as a "genealogy" of the "Brutus lynagis." Additionally, Wyntoun's poem contains references to a work by the archdeacon titled "Stewartis Oryginale."

## The Trojan Connection and Critical Debate
The "Brut" mentioned by Wyntoun is currently unknown. Some scholars have suggested that this reference refers to a Troy-book based on Guido da Colonna's *Historia Destructionis Troiae*. This ascription was first proposed by Henry Bradshaw, the librarian of Cambridge University.

Evidence for this theory consists of two fragments of a work preserved in texts of Lydgate's *Troy-book*. The first fragment is found in MS. Camb. Univ. Lib. Kk. v. 30, and the second is located in the same manuscript as well as in MS. Douce 148 in the Bodleian library, Oxford.

However, the current consensus of critical opinion opposes this ascription. Even if these Troy fragments were proven to be the work of Barbour, there is significant doubt regarding their identification as the book on the Stewart line. This doubt is based on the scale of the narrative; the fragments consist of 3,713 lines (divided into 595 and 3,118 lines) and focus entirely on "Trojan" matters. Such an extensive focus on Trojan subjects would be considered an undue allowance for a Scottish "genealogy."
