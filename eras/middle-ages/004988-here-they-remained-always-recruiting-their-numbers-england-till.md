# The Bridgittine Order and the Convent of Syon

## Origins and Structure
The Bridgittines are an order of Augustinian canonesses established around 1350 by St Bridget of Sweden. The order received approval from Urban V. in 1370. It was organized as a "double order," meaning that each convent included a small community of canons who served as chaplains. Despite the presence of these canons, the government of the convent remained under the authority of the abbess.

The order experienced wide expansion across Norway and Sweden, where it played a significant role in the promotion of literature and culture in Scandinavia. This influence is evidenced by the head house at Vastein, located by Lake Vetter, which continued to operate until its suppression in 1595. In total, the order established 80 houses across various lands. In the modern era, the order consists only of women.

## The Convent of Syon
In England, the Bridgittine order was represented by the famous convent of Syon at Isleworth, Middlesex. Founded in 1415, the convent was royally endowed by Henry V and grew to become one of the most influential, fashionable, and richest nunneries in England. 

During the reign of Mary, Syon was among the few religious houses to be restored, with nearly twenty members of the old community being re-established there. However, upon the accession of Elizabeth, the community migrated to the Low Countries. Following a period of many vicissitudes, they moved to Rouen, and finally settled in Lisbon in 1594.

## Exile and Return
While based in Lisbon, the community continued to recruit its members from England. They remained in Lisbon until 1861, at which point they returned to England. The community of Syon House is currently established at Chudleigh in Devon. It is the only English community that can claim an unbroken conventual existence dating back to pre-Reformation times.
