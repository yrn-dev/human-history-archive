# CONCERNING THE CITY OF CAMBALUC

## Overview and Geography
Cambaluc serves as the capital city of Cathay and is situated at the north-eastern extremity of the country. It is characterized by a vast population and a multitude of houses located both inside and outside its walls. The city is a major center of trade, acting as a mart that attracts numerous traders, foreign merchants, and travelers who arrive to sell articles at Court or bring presents to the Emperor.

## City Structure and Suburbs
The city is enclosed by walls and features twelve gates. Outside each of these gates is a suburb. These suburbs are so extensive that they are described as containing more people than the city itself; the width of one suburb extends until it meets the next, and they reach lengths of some three or four miles. 

Within these suburbs, specifically within a mile of the city, there are numerous fine hostelries used for the lodgment of merchants from various parts of the world. These hostelries are organized by the origin of the people, with special houses assigned to different groups, such as the Frenchmen, Germans, and Lombards. Additionally, the suburbs contain numerous houses belonging to barons and great lords.

## The Imperial Palace and Residency
The Great Kaan maintains a great palace within Cambaluc. The Emperor's residency in the capital follows a specific annual cycle:
* **September through February:** The Kaan resides at his chief palace in Cambaluc. During September, he observes his Birthday Feast, and in February, he celebrates the New Year, known as the White Feast.
* **March through May:** The Kaan departs for a hunting and hawking expedition toward the Ocean Sea.
* **May to August:** After returning to Cambaluc for a brief three-day stay of grand entertainments and rejoicings with his wives, he proceeds to the city of Chandu. There, he stays at a palace of cane to escape the heat.
* **Late August:** Following his stay in Chandu, he returns to his capital, Cambaluc.

## Administration and Infrastructure
Cambaluc serves as the hub for a sophisticated network of roads and highways. Each road is named after the province to which it leads. For the Emperor's messengers, a system of stations called *Yamb* (or "Horse-Post-House") is established every twenty-five miles. These stations consist of large, handsome buildings with rooms furnished with rich silk and fine beds, providing accommodations sufficient for even a king.

The city is also home to a significant number of astrologers and soothsayers—approximately five thousand, including Cathayans, Saracens, and Christians. These individuals are provided with annual clothing and maintenance by the Great Kaan, allowing them to constantly exercise their art within the city.
