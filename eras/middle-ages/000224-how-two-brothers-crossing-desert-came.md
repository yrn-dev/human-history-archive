# The Journey of the Polo Brothers to Bocara

## Departure and Initial Travels
In the year of Christ 1260, during the reign of Baldwin in Constantinople, two brothers, Messer Nicolas Polo and Messer Maffeo Polo, were present in the city. Having traveled from Venice with merchants' wares, the two brothers—described as wise, provident, and singularly noble—decided to embark on a venture of trade. After securing a store of jewels, they departed from Constantinople and crossed the Greater Sea to reach the port of Soldaia.

## The Crossing of the Desert
Following their time beyond Soldaia, the two brothers undertook a journey that required them to traverse a desert. According to archival notes, the stretch of desert specifically mentioned in the context of their travel applies to a period of 17 days. However, the total journey from Ukek to Bokhara is estimated to have taken at least 60 days.

Upon successfully crossing this desert, the brothers arrived at a city called Bocara.

## The City of Bocara
Bocara is described as a very great and noble city, characterized as the best city in all Persia. At the time of the brothers' arrival, the territory of the city belonged to a king also named Barac (or Bocara). 

Upon reaching the city, the Polo brothers found themselves in a position where they could neither proceed further forward in their journey nor turn back. Consequently, they remained in Bocara for a period of three years.

## Encounter with the Envoys
During their three-year sojourn in Bocara, the brothers encountered envoys who had come from Alau, the Lord of the Levant. These envoys were traveling toward the court of the Great Kaan, who was the Lord of all the Tartars in the world.

The envoys were amazed to see the Polo brothers, as they had never previously encountered Latins in that region of the world. The envoys offered the brothers their counsel, suggesting that accompanying them to the court of the Great Kaan would bring them great profit and honor. They explained that the Great Kaan had a great desire to see Latins, as he had never seen any before. The envoys assured the brothers that the Great Kaan would treat them with liberality and honor, and that by traveling in the company of the envoys, the brothers would have perfect security and need not fear being molested.
