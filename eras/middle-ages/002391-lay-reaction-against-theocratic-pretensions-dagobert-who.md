# The Lay Reaction Against Dagobert

## The Conflict in Jerusalem
In the early 12th century, a political and religious struggle emerged in Jerusalem following the death of Godfrey of Bouillon. This conflict centered on the "theocratic pretensions" of Dagobert, who sought to maintain influence and was relying on support from the Normans. In response to these pretensions, a lay reaction developed.

This movement among the laity was responsible for summoning Baldwin I to Jerusalem to succeed his brother, Godfrey. Baldwin I had previously served as a clerk in orders and held several prebends before joining the first crusade in 1096. He had spent the years between 1098 and 1100 ruling in Edessa, where he acted as an intermediary between the Armenians and the crusaders and successfully defended his territory against conspiracies and Mahommedan powers.

## The Accession of Baldwin I
Due to the strength of the lay reaction against Dagobert, Baldwin I was able to establish himself as the first king of Jerusalem. He was crowned by the patriarch on Christmas Day in 1100. However, the coronation did not resolve the underlying struggle between church and state.

The tension between Baldwin I and Dagobert continued into 1101. In the spring of that year, Baldwin I utilized a papal legate to have Dagobert suspended. Later in 1101, the two men clashed again over a specific financial dispute regarding the contribution the patriarch should provide for the defense of the Holy Land. This ongoing struggle concluded in 1102 with the deposition of Dagobert and the ultimate triumph of Baldwin I.
