# The Departure of the Polo Brothers from Venice

## Arrival in Venice and Family Discoveries
The two brothers, Nicolo and Maffeo, arrived in Venice after traveling from the port of Ayas to the city of Acre. They reached Acre in April of the year of Christ 1269, where they discovered that the Pope had died. Upon this discovery, they consulted Theobald of Piacenza, a wise Churchman and Legate for the kingdom of Egypt. The Legate informed the brothers that they must wait for the election of a new Pope before they could execute their charge.

Agreeing that it was a just assessment, the brothers decided to use the intervening time to visit their households in Venice. They traveled from Acre to Negropont, and from there continued to Venice. Upon his arrival, Messer Nicolas discovered that his wife had died. She had left behind a son named Marco, who was fifteen years of age at the time.

## The Period of Waiting
The two brothers remained in Venice for a couple of years. Their stay was intended to last until a new Pope was elected, which would allow them to fulfill the mission they had been assigned. During this period, the brothers tarried in the city, awaiting news of the papacy.

## Decision to Return to the Great Kaan
After the period of waiting, the brothers observed that no Pope had been made. Determining that their return to the Great Kaan could be delayed no longer, they departed from Venice. For this return journey, they took Mark, the son of Messer Nicolo, along with them.

The group traveled straight back to Acre, where they again sought out the Legate. The brothers engaged in discourse with him regarding their situation and requested his permission to travel to Jerusalem. Their objective was to obtain oil from the lamp on the Sepulchre to bring back to the Great Kaan, as the Kaan had enjoined them to do.

## Final Preparations and Departure from Acre
After receiving leave from the Legate, the brothers and Mark traveled from Acre to Jerusalem, secured the oil, and returned to Acre. Upon their return, they informed the Legate that since there was still no sign of a new Pope being elected, they desired to return to the Great Kaan, noting that they had already experienced more than enough delay.

The Legate expressed his contentment with their decision to go back. To assist them, he arranged for letters to be written for delivery to the Great Kaan. These letters served as testimony that the two brothers had come in good faith to accomplish their charge, but were unable to do so because there was no Pope.
