# BONIFACE IX

## Early Life and Election
Boniface IX, born Piero Tomacelli, was born in Naples to a family that was ancient but poor. Before ascending to the papacy, he was created a cardinal by Urban VI. He was elected as the successor to Urban VI on November 2, 1389.

## Political and Territorial Administration
During his pontificate, which lasted from 1389 to 1404, Boniface IX worked to restore Roman authority across the majority of the papal states. In 1398, he successfully put an end to the republican liberties of the city of Rome. His influence extended to Naples, which he brought into Roman obedience after the city had previously owed spiritual allegiance to the antipopes Benedict XIII and Clement VII of Avignon.

In the realm of imperial politics, Boniface IX waited until 1403 to confirm the election of Rupert and the deposition of Emperor Wenceslaus. Despite these political maneuvers, his efforts to negotiate a healing of the Great Schism were without result.

## Ecclesiastical Actions and Finance
In 1391, Boniface IX canonized Birgitta of Sweden. He also oversaw the celebration of a regular jubilee in 1390, followed by a more informal jubilee in 1400 that proved to be more profitable.

Regarding his administration of church finances, Boniface IX transformed annates into a permanent tax in 1399. He was also known to sell offices when he faced financial straits. While contemporaries trusted his moral character and prudence despite his inferior education, he was justly accused of nepotism, although he was probably not personally avaricious.

## Death
Boniface IX died on October 1, 1404. At the time of his death, he was still under sixty years of age.
