# ASHBY-DE-LA-ZOUCH AND ASTI

## Ashby-de-la-Zouch: History and Development
Ashby-de-la-Zouch is a market town located in the Bosworth parliamentary division of Leicestershire, England. It is situated 118 miles north-west by north from London, accessible via the Leicester-Burton branch of the Midland railway. As of 1901, the population of the urban district was 4,726.

The town was known as Ashby or Essebi before the addition of "de-la-Zouch." At the time of the Domesday survey, it was part of the estates of Hugh de Grentmaisnel. It was later held by Robert Beaumeis, and subsequently passed through female descent to the la Zouch family. In 1219, Roger la Zouch was granted a two-day fair at the feast of St Helen and a weekly market in exchange for a fine of one palfrey.

In the 15th century, the manor was held by James Butler, earl of Ormond. Following his attainder in 1461, the manor was granted to Lord Hastings. In 1474, Hastings received a royal licence to fortify and build a castle and to empark 3,000 acres. This castle later served as the place of detention for Mary, queen of Scots, in 1569, while she was under the custody of the earls of Shrewsbury and Huntingdon. During the Civil War, the castle was held and fortified for the king by Colonel Henry Hastings and was visited by Charles in 1645. The structure was dismantled by order of parliament in 1648. Extensive remains of the castle still exist to the south of the town.

## Local Economy and Infrastructure
During the 18th century, Ashby was recognized as one of England's premier markets for horses. The town also hosted prosperous factories producing hats as well as cotton and woollen stockings. In the neighboring district, there are extensive coal mines, including those at Moira. The Ashby-de-la-Zouch canal runs south from Moira to the Coventry canal.

The town is also known for the Ivanhoe baths, established in 1826. These baths utilize saline waters from springs located three miles west at Moira; because these waters contain bromine, they are used to treat rheumatic and scrofulous complaints.

## Landmarks and Notable Figures
The church of St Helen is a Perpendicular building that was enlarged and restored in 1880. It houses monuments to the Huntingdon family and an old finger-pillory used to punish those who misbehaved in church. Other landmarks include a Queen Eleanor cross, created by Sir Gilbert Scott to commemorate the countess of Loudoun. The town also features in Sir Walter Scott's novel *Ivanhoe*.

Several notable individuals are associated with the town. John Bainbridge (1582–1643), an astronomer and physician who later held the Savilian chair of astronomy at Oxford, was born in Ashby-de-la-Zouch. Alexander Henry Green (1832–1896), an English geologist and professor at the university of Oxford, was educated at the Ashby Grammar School, where his father, the Rev. Thomas Sheldon Green, served as master.

## Asti
Asti (known in ancient times as *Hasta*) is a town and episcopal see located in the province of Alessandria in Piedmont, Italy. It is situated on the Tanaro, 22 miles west of Alessandria by rail. As of 1901, the town population was 19,787, with a total commune population of 41,047.

The town is a significant center of trade and is currently famous for its sparkling wine, *Asti spumante*. In ancient times, Asti was a manufacturer of pottery. It is also the birthplace of the poet Vittorio Alfieri.

Architecturally, Asti retains numerous medieval towers. Its religious landmarks include a 14th-century Gothic cathedral, an 11th-century octagonal baptistery of S. Pietro, and the remains of a Christian basilica dating from the 6th century.
