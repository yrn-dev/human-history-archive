# Saint Bonaventura

## Early Life and Education
Saint Bonaventura, also known as John of Fidanza, was a Franciscan theologian born in 1221 at Bagnarea in Tuscany. His mother destined him for a life in the church. According to tradition, he received the name Bonaventura from St Francis of Assisi, who is said to have performed a miraculous cure upon him.

Bonaventura entered the Franciscan order in 1243. He pursued his studies in Paris, where he may have studied under Alexander of Hales; he certainly studied under Alexander's successor, John of Rochelle. In 1253, Bonaventura succeeded John of Rochelle in his chair. His academic ascent was marked by his permission to read upon the *Sentences* three years prior to 1253, and he eventually received the degree of doctor in 1255.

## Leadership and Influence
Due to his high reputation, Bonaventura was elected general of the Franciscan order in 1256. During his leadership, he issued orders that resulted in Roger Bacon being interdicted from lecturing at Oxford and being forced to remain under the surveillance of the order in Paris.

Bonaventura also played a significant role in ecclesiastical politics, as he was instrumental in procuring the election of Gregory X. In recognition of his efforts, Gregory X granted him the titles of bishop of Albano and cardinal.

## Death
The Pope insisted that Bonaventura be present at the great council of Lyons in 1274. It was during this meeting that he died.
