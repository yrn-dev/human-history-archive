# The Great Mortality: How the Black Death Reshaped Europe

## Overview
The Black Death, which ravaged Europe between 1347 and 1351, was more than a medical catastrophe; it was a seismic shift that dismantled the foundations of the medieval world. Caused by the bacterium *Yersinia pestis* and transmitted via fleas on black rats, the plague entered Europe through trade routes from Central Asia, arriving in Sicily via Genoese merchant ships.

In a matter of years, the plague wiped out an estimated 30% to 60% of the European population. While the immediate result was unimaginable horror and social chaos, the long-term aftermath triggered a profound socio-economic transformation. The sudden scarcity of labor broke the back of the feudal system. Peasants, once tied to the land in a state of near-slavery (serfdom), suddenly found their labor in high demand. This newfound leverage allowed survivors to demand higher wages and better living conditions, effectively ending the rigid hierarchy of the Middle Ages and paving the way for a nascent middle class.

Furthermore, the plague shook the spiritual monopoly of the Catholic Church. The inability of clergy to stop the disease—and the high mortality rate among priests—led to a rise in mysticism and a questioning of religious authority, sowing the early seeds of the Reformation.

## Key Facts
*   **The Three Strains:** The plague manifested in three forms: **Bubonic** (attacking lymph nodes), **Septicemic** (infecting the blood), and **Pneumonic** (infecting the lungs and spreading through coughing).
*   **The Miasma Theory:** Lacking knowledge of germs, medieval physicians believed the plague was caused by "miasma" (bad air) or planetary alignments.
*   **Labor Shifts:** The "Great Leveling" occurred as land became cheap and labor expensive, leading to the collapse of manorialism.
*   **Artistic Shift:** European art entered a period of obsession with death, characterized by the *Danse Macabre* (Dance of Death) motifs, depicting skeletons leading people of all ranks to the grave.

## Did You Know?
1. **The Plague Doctor's Mask:** The iconic bird-like mask was not actually used during the initial 1347 outbreak; it was developed in the 17th century. The "beak" was stuffed with aromatic herbs to filter out the "bad air."
2. **The Flagellants:** Some groups believed the plague was a divine punishment and traveled from town to town whipping themselves publicly to appease God's anger.
3. **Quarantine Origins:** The word "quarantine" comes from the Italian *quaranta giorni* (forty days), referring to the period ships were forced to wait isolated off the coast of Venice to ensure they weren't carrying the disease.
