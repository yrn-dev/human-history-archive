# Ursinus and Early Baptismal Practices

## Ursinus and the Validity of Baptism
Ursinus was an African monk whose views on the administration of baptism are recorded in the works of Gennadius (*de Scr. Eccl.* xxvii.) and Hilary (*de Synodis*, lxxxv.). Ursinus asserted that baptism performed into the name of Christ alone was valid. This position stands in contrast to the formula utilized in Rome, which is: "I baptize thee in the name of Father and Son and Holy Spirit."

## Regional Variations in Baptismal Formulas
Baptismal practices varied between the Western and Eastern traditions. In the East, the formula used was "so-and-so, the servant of God, is baptized." The Greeks further modified this by adding "Amen" after the mention of each person, concluding the rite with the words, "Now and ever and to aeons of aeons, amen."

## Interpretations of Trine Immersion
The practice of trine immersion—dipping the candidate three times—has been explained through different theological lenses by early church figures:

*   **The Triple Invocation:** Tertullian explained trine immersion as a direct result of the triple invocation, stating that candidates are dipped not once, but thrice, for the several names and into the several persons. Jerome similarly noted that the three plunges were intended to show forth the one sacrament of the Trinity.
*   **Symbolism of Christ's Burial:** Other figures from both the East and West, including Athanasius, Gregory of Nyssa, Theophylactus, Cyril of Jerusalem, and Leo of Rome, viewed trine immersion as symbolic of the three days Christ spent in entombment. This specific interpretation is enjoined in the Armenian baptismal rubric and was detailed in an epistle from Macarius of Jerusalem to the Armenians (circa 330). Armenian writers further associated this practice with the concept of baptism into the death of Christ.
