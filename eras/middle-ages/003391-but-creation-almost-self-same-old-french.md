# Knight Banneret

## Definition and Feudal Status
In the system of feudalism, a banneret (derived from the French *banneret*, from *banniere* meaning banner) was a noble who possessed the right to lead his vassals into battle under his own private banner. Within the feudal hierarchy, bannerets occupied a position between barons and knights bachelors. This specific placement has led to the theory that bannerets were the origin of the order of baronets established by King James I. 

While Selden notes that "the old stories" frequently substitute *baronetti* for *bannereti* and observes that the title became hereditary in France, he explicitly states that the title of banneret has no relation to the later title of baronet.

## Creation and Ceremonies
The title of knight banneret was granted to individuals for distinguished service performed in the field. According to English custom, a knight banneret could only be created in the field, and this creation required either the presence of the king or the display of the royal standard.

The process of creation in England was almost the self-same as the ceremonies used in the old French. The ceremony involved the solemn delivery of a banner that was charged with the arms of the person being created. If the individual already had his arms on a streamer in the field prior to the creation, the end of the pennon or streamer was cut to transform it into a square or into the shape of a banner.

## History and Role
The creation of bannerets can be traced back to the era of Edward I. In the military structure of the time, various esquires and knights bachelors typically served under these bannerets. The wages received by the bannerets were determined by the number of knights bachelors and esquires serving under them.

The last authentic instance of a knight banneret being created occurred during the battle of Edgehill. Charles I created John Smith a banneret for his actions in rescuing the royal standard from the enemy.
