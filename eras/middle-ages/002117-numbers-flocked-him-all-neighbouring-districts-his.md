# Patterns of Population Movement and Assembly

The historical record contains various instances where individuals, groups, or merchants flocked to specific leaders, locations, or events, often driven by political disorder, spiritual attraction, or economic opportunity.

## Military and Political Assemblies
In several historical contexts, leadership and perceived security attracted large numbers of followers. During the disordered era of the War of the Dynasties, Morokiyo—a scion of the illustrious Kitabatake family and successor to Murakami Yoshihiro of Iyo province—saw numbers flock to his standard. Under this leadership, corsairs raided the entire littoral from Amoy and Formosa in the south to Korea in the north.

In Norway, Earl Hakon assembled a matchless number of men for a conflict with King Ragnfred. Hakon gathered forces from the sea-coast from Stad to Bryda, including people from Naumudal and Halogaland, as well as those who flocked to him from Raumsdal and the Throndhjem district. This force included seven earls and men from four great districts. 

Similarly, in A.D. 1174, a young man named Eystein, who claimed to be the son of King Eystein Haraldson, entered Norway. Upon arriving in Viken, people flocked to him in crowds, and he was proclaimed king. However, due to poverty, his followers engaged in robbery, leading lendermen and bondes to raise forces against them. Overpowered by numbers, Eystein's group fled to hill grounds and forests. Because they wound birch-tree bark around their legs due to worn-out clothing, the bondes called them Birkebeins.

## Settlement and Urban Migration
The movement of people toward new settlements has not always resulted in long-term prosperity. In one instance, a town was founded by the Lacedaemonians to annoy Euboea. Despite the foundation causing initial alarm in Athens, the town never troubled them. Although people originally flocked from all quarters to the site, believing it secure of prosperity, the settlement was eventually ruined. This decline was caused by the Thessalians, who feared a powerful neighbor and continually harassed the settlers, and by the Lacedaemonian governors, whose harsh and unfair governance frightened away a majority of the inhabitants.

## Spiritual and Economic Attractions
Individual fame and commercial events have also served as catalysts for the gathering of people. Benedict of Nursia, born into the family of the Anicii in Nursia near Spoleto, sought a life of solitary prayer and austerity in a cave at Subiaco after fleeing the licentiousness of Roman schools. Following a three-year period of formation, his fame spread. Although he briefly served as an abbot for a monastery whose monks were dissolute and attempted to poison him, he returned to his cave, where disciples flocked to him, eventually leading to the formation of twelve monasteries.

In a commercial context, the town of Dorohoi in Rumania, located on the right bank of the river Jijia, serves as a market for farm produce and timber from the north Moldavian highlands. Merchants from neighbouring states flock to the town for its great fair, which is held on the 12th of June.
