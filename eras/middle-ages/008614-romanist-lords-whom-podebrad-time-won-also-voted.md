# George of Podebrad and the Romanist Nobility

## Political Divisions in Bohemia
Following the recognition of the Compacts at Iglau in Moravia, Sigismund was accepted as king of Bohemia. After his death in 1437, he was succeeded by Albert of Austria. Upon Albert's death, which occurred after a reign of less than two years, a succession crisis emerged regarding whether the Bohemian crown was hereditary or elective. This dispute divided the nobility into two primary factions: a national party led by George of Podebrad and a Roman or Austrian division led by Ulrich of Rosenberg.

The two parties eventually reached an agreement known as the "Letter of Peace" (_list mirny_). Under this agreement, signatories pledged to recognize the Compacts and support John of Rokycan as the archbishop of Prague. John of Rokycan had been chosen by the estates according to an agreement made with the Compacts, though the Church of Rome refused to recognize him. In exchange, the national party abandoned the candidacy of Prince Casimir of Poland for the throne, facilitating the eventual succession of Albert's heir.

## The Reign of King George
George of Podebrad, the only Hussite king of Bohemia, initially enjoyed a prosperous reign. His early policy was based on a firm alliance with Matthias Corvinus, the king of Hungary. Through the influence of Matthias, George was crowned by the Romanist bishop of Waitzen. During this period, George's primary support came from the towns and the smaller nobility.

However, this stability shifted as some Romanist nobles became hostile toward the king. Their influence contributed to a protracted struggle between George and the papal see. This conflict hindered several of George's ambitions, including his attempts to obtain supremacy over Germany.

## Conflict with the Romanist Party
In 1452, after negotiations with Rome failed, George assembled the estates at Prague. He declared his commitment to the communion in both kinds, stating he was prepared to risk his crown and his life to defend his faith. This declaration further embittered the Romanist party in Bohemia.

By 1465, the opposition intensified at a meeting in Zelena Hora (Grunberg), where many nobles of the Roman religion formed a confederacy against the king. This league received moral support in the following year from Pope Paul II, who released all Bohemians from their oath of allegiance to George and pronounced a sentence of excommunication against him.

## The Rise of Matthias Corvinus
Papal influence also affected George's international alliances. King Matthias of Hungary deserted George and instead supported the lords of the league of Zelena Hora. This led to desultory warfare between the two factions. While George was initially successful, the tide turned when Matthias invaded Moravia and took possession of Brunn, the capital.

On May 3, 1469, at a meeting of the Catholic nobles of Moravia and Bohemia held at Olmutz, Matthias was proclaimed king of Bohemia. Although George achieved some successes over his rival in the following year, the war ceased upon George's death in 1471. Despite the conflicts of his reign, George of Podebrad remains, along with Charles IV, one of the rulers whose memory is most endeared to the Bohemian people.
