# AVIGNON

Avignon is a city located in south-eastern France and serves as the capital of the department of Vaucluse. It is situated on the left bank of the Rhone river, a few miles above the point where the Rhone meets the Durance. Geographically, the city is positioned 143 miles south of Lyons on the railway line connecting Lyons and Marseilles. As of 1906, the population of the city was 35,356.

## City Layout and Infrastructure
The city occupies a large oval-shaped area that is not fully populated, with significant portions covered by gardens and parks. The urban center's activity is primarily concentrated around the Cours de la Republique and the Place de l'Hotel de Ville; the Cours de la Republique extends from the square to the city's ramparts. In other areas, the streets are characterized as being quiet, narrow, and generally badly paved.

Avignon is connected to Villeneuve-les-Avignon via a suspension bridge. Further up the river is the Pont Saint-Benezet, a picturesque ruined bridge dating from the 12th century. Of the original eighteen piles of this bridge, only four remain, one of which supports the chapel of Saint-Benezet, a small building of Romanesque architecture.

## Fortifications and Ramparts
The city remains encircled by ramparts constructed by the popes during the 14th century. These walls are considered one of the finest existing examples of medieval fortification. They are characterized by their great strength and are surmounted by machicolated battlements. The fortifications are flanked at intervals by thirty-nine massive towers and are pierced by several gateways, three of which were built in the 14th century. A line of pleasant boulevards surrounds the exterior of these walls.

## The Rocher des Doms and Religious Sites
At the northern edge of the city, a precipitous rock known as the Rocher des Doms rises from the edge of the river. This rock forms a plateau that stretches south toward the Place de l'Hotel de Ville. The summit of the plateau is home to a public garden and two major architectural landmarks: the Palace of the Popes and the cathedral of Notre-Dame des Doms.

The cathedral is a Romanesque structure dating primarily from the 12th century. Its most prominent external feature is a gilded statue of the Virgin located on the western tower. Inside the cathedral, the most beautiful work of art is the 14th-century mausoleum of Pope John XXII, which is described as a masterpiece of Gothic carving.

Adjacent to the cathedral is the Palace of the Popes, a sombre complex of buildings that covers an area of more than 1 1/4 acres and dwarfs the cathedral in size.

## Education and Notable Figures
Avignon has served as a center for religious education. Jacques Bridaine (1701-1767), a French Roman Catholic preacher, was educated in the city, attending first the Jesuit college and subsequently the Sulpician seminary of St Charles. Bridaine, who later joined the *Missions Royales* to convert Protestants to the Catholic faith, died at Roquemaure, near Avignon, on December 22, 1767. His sermons were published in five volumes at Avignon in 1823.
