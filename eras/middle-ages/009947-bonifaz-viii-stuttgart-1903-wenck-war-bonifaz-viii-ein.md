# Bonifaz VIII.

## Overview of Historical Scholarship
The study of Bonifaz VIII. is supported by a variety of academic registries and historical analyses. Primary source material includes *Les Registres de Boniface VIII*, published in Paris starting in 1884 by Digard, Faucon, and Thomas. Further comprehensive biographical and historical data can be found in the *Kirchenlexikon* (2nd edition, 1883) and the *Realencyklopadie* (3rd edition, 1897), the latter of which provides an elaborate bibliography for researchers.

## Key Historical Analyses
Several epoch-making and specialized works examine the era and actions of Bonifaz VIII. H. Finke's *Aus den Tagen Bonifaz VIII.* (Munster, 1902) is described as dreary but epoch-making. Other significant contributions include J. Loserth's *Geschichte des spateren Mittelalters* (Munich, 1903) and R. Scholz's 1903 work, *Die Publizistik zur Zeit Philipps des Schonen und Bonifaz VIII.*, published in Stuttgart. Additionally, the *Historische Zeitschrift* (vol. xciv, Munich, 1905) contains a piece by K. Wenck titled "War Bonifaz VIII. ein Ketzer?"

## Specific Papal Documents
Scholarship on Bonifaz VIII. frequently focuses on specific decrees and documents:

*   ***Unam Sanctum***: This document is the subject of exhaustive discussion in the *Kirchenlexikon* (vol. xii, 1901). Other sources for the history of this text include H. Finke (pages 146-190), C. Mirbt's *Quellen zur Geschichte des Papsttums* (2nd edition, 1901), and J.H. Robinson's *Readings in European History* (vol. i, 1904).
*   ***Clericis laicos***: Documentation regarding this decree is found in Gee and Hardy's *Documents Illustrative of English Church History* (London, 1896).
