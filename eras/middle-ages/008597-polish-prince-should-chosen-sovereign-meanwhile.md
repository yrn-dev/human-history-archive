# Political Transitions in Bohemia (1421–1427)

## Deposition of Sigismund and Provisional Governance
In June 1421, royal authority in Bohemia ceased following a meeting of the diet at Caslav on June 1, where Sigismund was deposed. The diet decided that a Polish prince should be selected to serve as the sovereign. During the interim period before a sovereign was established, a provisional government was created, consisting of twenty men drawn from various parties.

## The Regency of Sigismund Korybutovic
In 1422, Sigismund attempted to regain control by invading Bohemia, but he suffered a decisive defeat at Nemecky Brod (also known as Deutschbrod) at the hands of Zizka. Following this, the chosen Polish prince, Sigismund Korybutovic, arrived in Bohemia. He was recognized as the regent by a large majority of the population. However, his first tenure was brief; due to the influence of the papal see, the rulers of Poland recalled him after only a few months.

## Internal Conflict and Reunification
The departure of Prince Korybutovic triggered the first outbreak of civil war between two factions of Hussites: the advanced Taborite party and the moderate Hussites, who were also known as Utraquists or Calixtines. While isolated disturbances had occurred between these groups previously, this period marked the start of open civil war.

The Bohemians were eventually reunited by two factors: the return of Prince Korybutovic and the threat of an invasion by German forces. This unity led to a decisive victory for the Bohemians over the Germans at Aussig in 1426.

## The End of the Slav Dynasty Ambition
Although Prince Korybutovic participated in the victory at Aussig, he subsequently incurred the dislike of the extreme Hussites. This animosity forced him to leave Bohemia, an event that ended all hopes of establishing an independent Slav dynasty within the region.

## The Crusade of 1427
In 1427, several German princes launched a new crusade against the Hussites. This invading force included 1,000 English archers who served as the bodyguard to Henry Beaufort, the bishop of Winchester, who acted as the papal legate. The crusade was unsuccessful; the invaders were seized by sudden panic at Tachau and Mies (Stribro) upon approaching the Hussites, resulting in a hurried flight across the mountains into Bavaria.

Following this success, the Bohemians shifted to the offensive, repeatedly invading the German states and Hungary, despite the recurrence of internal disturbances.
