# The Battle of the White Hill and the Transformation of Bohemia

The Battle of the White Hill, fought on November 8, 1620, represents a pivotal epoch in the history of Bohemia, marking a transition toward the loss of the country's individuality and the establishment of Habsburg dominance.

## Conflict and Defeat
The events leading to the battle began following the death of Matthias, which left the Bohemian crown vacant in March. On July 31, the Bohemian estates formally deposed Ferdinand, and on August 26, they elected Frederick, the elector palatine, as their king. Frederick and his queen, Elizabeth of England, arrived in October and were crowned at St Vitus's cathedral in Prague. 

While warfare with Austria continued through 1619—during which Thurn occupied Moravia and advanced on Vienna before retreating—the situation turned fatal for Bohemia the following year. Ferdinand, now the emperor and successor to Matthias, sought to reconquer the region. He received assistance from Poland, Spain, several Italian states, and the Lutheran elector of Saxony. Additionally, the powerful duke Maximilian of Bavaria joined forces with Ferdinand.

In September 1620, a large imperialist army commanded by Bouquoi, Tilly, and the duke of Bavaria entered Bohemia. After a series of defeats for the Bohemians, the imperial forces reached the outskirts of Prague on November 7. The following morning, they attacked the Bohemian army, which was positioned on a slightly fortified plateau known as "Bila Hora" (White Hill). The Bohemians were defeated within a few hours. By that evening, the imperialists occupied the port of Prague on the left bank of the Vltava (Moldau), and King Frederick fled the city the next morning.

## Political and Social Repressions
Following the victory, Bohemia and the lands of the Bohemian crown submitted to Ferdinand with almost no resistance. On June 21, 1621, the principal leaders of the national movement were executed. This was followed by a system of wholesale confiscation of lands from anyone who had participated in the national movement. This process drove almost the entire ancient nobility of Bohemia into exile, while the spoils were shared among adventurers from various countries, primarily men who had served in the imperial army.

The religious landscape was similarly transformed. Those who refused to recognize the creed of the Roman church were gradually expelled. Through the use of "terrible cruelty," Catholicism was entirely re-established in the country. Furthermore, the Jesuits orchestrated the systematic destruction of Bohemian writings, which resulted in the loss of a large portion of controversial literature from the Hussite period, including all writings of the extreme Taborite party.

## Constitutional Changes
In 1627, Ferdinand published a decree that formally suppressed the ancient free constitution of Bohemia. While a semblance of representative government remained, the new constitution established the following:

*   **Heredity:** The Bohemian crown was proclaimed hereditary within the house of Habsburg.
*   **The Estates:** A new estate for the clergy was added to the three existing ones. This estate, consisting of the Roman archbishop of Prague and ecclesiastics with landed estates, took precedence over all others.
*   **Legislative Power:** The diet was stripped of all legislative power, which was vested exclusively in the sovereign. The diet could only discuss matters presented to it by the king's representatives.
*   **Taxation:** While the estates retained the right to vote on taxes, they were forbidden from attaching any conditions to the grants of money provided to the sovereign.
*   **Language:** The German language was granted equal right with the Bohemian language in all law-courts and government offices. This became a necessity because the vast confiscations of land had placed the majority of the territory in the hands of foreigners who did not know the national language.

## Long-term Impact
Although some autonomy remained, Bohemia gradually lost its individuality from this point until the beginning of the 19th century. Even after the battle of the White Hill, it remained customary to consult the estates on serious constitutional changes, as seen during the 1815 Congress of Vienna when the country was declared part of the Germanic Confederation. However, the influence of the house of Habsburg and the centralization of power continued to shape the region's trajectory through subsequent centuries.
