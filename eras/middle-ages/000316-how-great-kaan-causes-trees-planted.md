# The Planting of Trees under the Great Kaan

The Great Kaan implemented extensive programs for the planting of trees, serving both the practical needs of his subjects and his own personal aesthetic and spiritual preferences. These initiatives ranged from the systematic lining of the empire's highways to the creation of a curated botanical collection on a man-made hill.

## Highway Afforestation and Navigation
The Emperor ordered that all highways used by the general population and his messengers be planted with rows of great trees. These trees were spaced a few paces apart, creating visible markers that could be seen from a long distance. This system ensured that travelers would not lose their way, whether traveling by day or by night.

This planting initiative extended even into uninhabited tracts of land, providing significant solace to those traveling through desolate areas. The Kaan applied this practice to all roads where it was deemed to be of service. In regions where the ground was too sandy or desert-like to support tree growth, the Emperor instead ordered the placement of stones, pillars, or other landmarks to guide travelers.

The motivation for this widespread planting was partly influenced by the Kaan's astrologers and diviners, who informed him that the act of planting trees leads to a long life.

## The Green Mount
In addition to public infrastructure, the Great Kaan commissioned a private botanical project on the north side of his palace. Approximately a bow-shot from the palace, there is a hill created by art from earth excavated from a lake. This hill stands roughly one hundred paces high and one mile in compass.

The hill is entirely covered in evergreen trees. The Emperor maintained a policy that whenever he received news of a beautiful tree existing anywhere, he would have it transported to this hill. Regardless of the tree's size, it was carried by elephants, transported bodily with its roots and attached earth. This process allowed the Kaan to assemble what is described as the most beautiful collection of trees in the world.

To enhance the visual uniformity, the entire hill was covered in a very green ore of azure. Consequently, the hill, the trees, and a large palace situated at the top—which is green both inside and out—created a monochromatic spectacle of green. This site, known as the Green Mount, was designed for the delectation, comfort, and solace of the Kaan's heart.

## Use of Trees in Currency
While not involving the planting of new forests, the Great Kaan utilized the bark of specific trees to establish a national currency. He utilized the Mulberry tree, which was so numerous that entire districts were filled with them. 

Officials extracted a fine white skin or bast located between the thick outer bark and the wood of the Mulberry tree. This material was processed into black sheets resembling paper and cut into various sizes to represent different values, ranging from a half tornesel up to ten Bezants of gold. These paper notes were issued with the same authority as gold or silver, authenticated by the names and seals of officials and a final red vermilion seal from the Kaan's chief officer.
