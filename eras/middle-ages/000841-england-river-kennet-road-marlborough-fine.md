# Avebury

## Overview and Location
Avebury is a village located in the Devizes parliamentary division of Wiltshire, England. It is situated on the river Kennet, approximately 8 miles by road from the town of Marlborough.

## The Village and Architecture
The village features the church of St James, which is described as fine and contains a rich Norman doorway, an early font with Norman carving, a painted reredos, and a well-preserved, beautiful old roodstone. Additionally, the village contains Avebury House, an Elizabethan residence that includes a curious stone dovecot.

## The Stone Circle
The village of Avebury has encroached upon the remains of a massive stone circle, which is the largest specimen of primitive stone monuments in Britain, measuring an average of 1200 feet in diameter. The monument consists of a large circle—which is not perfectly circular—surrounded by a rampart of earth and a ditch. In antiquity, it was approached by two avenues of monoliths.

Within the primary circle, there were two smaller circles located on the north-eastern side rather than on the main axis. Each of these smaller circles consisted of a double concentric ring of stones. One of these had a dolmen (a tablestone resting on two uprights) at its center, while the other featured a menhir or pillar.

The monoliths are composed of native Sarsens found throughout the district and show no evidence of being hewn. The remaining stones vary in height from 5 to 20 feet above the ground and in breadth from 3 to 12 feet. Many of the monoliths have been broken up for use in building, leaving few traces of the original structure.

## Antiquarian Theories and Research
The purpose of the Avebury monument has been a subject of significant debate among antiquaries:
* **Dr. Stukely**, in his 1740 work *Stonehenge a Temple restored to the British Druids*, regarded the site as a Druidical temple.
* **Fergusson**, in *Rude Stone Monuments* (1872), proposed that the site, along with Silbury Hill, marks the graves of those who died in the last Arthurian battle at Badon Hill in A.D. 520.
* **General Consensus**: The majority of antiquaries believe the monument belongs to the same chronological horizon as other analogous monuments in Great Britain, many of which have been identified as Bronze Age burial places.

Excavations were conducted at the site in 1908, though they did not provide any important new light on the monument.
