# Hiberniae

The historical and hagiographical records of Hiberniae (Ireland) include the lives of early saints, the activities of missionary bishops, and the preservation of ancient manuscripts. These records detail the establishment of early religious cells, the spread of the gospel to continental Europe, and the production of significant literary works.

## Early Saints and Religious Foundations

Saint Brigid (also referred to as Bridget), a patron saint of Ireland, lived approximately between 452 and 523. Born at Faughart in county Louth to a prince of Ulster, she refused marriage in favor of a life of seclusion. She established the first cell in Ireland under a large oak tree; this site became known as Kil-dara, meaning "the church of the oak," and is believed to be the origin of the name for the city of Kildare. 

Saint Brigid died in 523 and was initially buried at Kildare. Her remains were later translated to Downpatrick, where she was laid alongside Saint Columba and Saint Patrick. Her feast is observed on February 1st. Her reputation extended beyond Ireland, where she was known as Saint Bride and became a favorite saint in England, with numerous churches dedicated to her in Scotland.

## Missionary Activity in Franconia

During the late 7th century, Saint Kilian (also known as Chilian or Killian), a British missionary bishop and native of Ireland, traveled with companions to eastern Franconia to preach the gospel. According to the martyrology of Hrabanus Maurus, Kilian and his party preached in Würzburg. However, the group was put to death by the orders of an unjust judge or duke named Gozbert. 

Kilian's companions included a priest named Coloman and a deacon named Totman. The relics of the three martyrs were later elevated by Burchard, the first bishop of Würzburg, and continue to be venerated in the cathedral of that town. The festival of Saint Kilian is celebrated on July 8th.

## The Book of Lismore

The *Book of Lismore* is a significant manuscript written around 1460 for Catharine Fitzgerald (daughter of Gerald, Eighth Earl of Desmond) and her husband, Finghin MacCarthy. The volume was written on large vellum and discovered in 1814 inside a wooden box behind a closed doorway in Lismore castle, where it was found alongside a superb crozier.

The contents of the *Book of Lismore* are diverse, including:
* Lives of the Saints.
* The (Romance) History of Charlemagne.
* The History of the Lombards.
* Tales and histories of Irish wars.
* A version of the travels of Marco Polo.

The translation of Marco Polo's work within the book is believed to have been executed around 1460, likely in the Monastery of Lismore in the county of Waterford. This version was translated with "astounding freedom" from the Latin of Friar Francesco Pipino, though both the beginning and the end of the text are missing.
