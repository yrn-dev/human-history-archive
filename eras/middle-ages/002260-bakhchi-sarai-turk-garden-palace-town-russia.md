# BAKHCHI-SARAI

Bakhchi-sarai, the name of which is Turkish for "garden-palace," is a town located in Russia within the government of Taurida. It is situated in the Crimea in a narrow gorge, positioned 20 miles south-southwest of Simferopol by rail.

## History and Governance
From the close of the 15th century until 1783, Bakhchi-sarai served as the residence of the Tatar khans of the Crimea. The town's streets maintain a decidedly oriental appearance. The most significant building in the town is the palace, known as the *Khan-sarai*. This structure was originally built in 1519 by Abdul-Sahal-Ghirai. Although it was destroyed in 1736, the palace was later restored under the command of Potemkin to receive Catherine II. A mausoleum is attached to the palace, which houses the tombs of many of the khans.

## Demographics and Religion
As of 1897, the population of Bakhchi-sarai was 12,955, consisting mostly of Tatars. The town is characterized by a significant presence of Islamic architecture, containing no fewer than thirty-six mosques.

## Industry and Economy
The town is engaged in the manufacture of several goods, including:
* Cutlery and sabres
* Agricultural implements
* Sheepskin cloaks
* Morocco

## Surrounding Landmarks
Several notable sites are located in the vicinity of Bakhchi-sarai:

**Chufut-Kaleh**
Located two and a half miles to the east, Chufut-Kaleh (also referred to as "Jews' city") is situated on lofty cliffs that are almost inaccessible. It formerly served as the chief seat of the Karaite Jews of the Crimea. Currently, the site is deserted, with the exception of the rabbi.

**Uspenskiy Monastery**
Situated between Bakhchi-sarai and Chufut-Kaleh, the Uspenskiy monastery is described as clinging to the face of the cliffs like a swallow's nest. It is the site of a great pilgrimage held annually on August 15th (29th).
