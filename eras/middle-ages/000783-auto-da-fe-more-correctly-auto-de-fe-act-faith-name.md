# AUTO-DE-FE

The auto-de-fe, more correctly termed auto-de-fe (meaning "act of faith"), was a public ceremony used by the Spanish Inquisition for the reading and execution of sentences. This practice was nearly identical to the *sermo generalis* utilized by the medieval inquisition.

## Scheduling and Attendance
An auto-de-fe was never conducted on a church feast day. Instead, these ceremonies were scheduled to coincide with famous anniversaries, such as the birth of an infant, the marriage of a Spanish monarch, or the accession of a monarch. Because the events were public, they were attended by the people, the court, the royal family, the king, and the grand councils of the kingdom.

## Ceremonial Proceedings
The ceremony consisted of several specific components:
* **Procession:** A procession featuring the agents and familiars of the Holy Office, the members of the Holy Office, the penitents, and the condemned persons.
* **Religious and Legal Rites:** The proceedings included a solemn mass and a sermon delivered by the Grand Inquisitor.
* **Oaths:** The king and all lay functionaries took an oath of obedience to the inquisition.
* **Sentencing:** The Holy Office delivered the reading of sentences, which resulted in either acquittal or condemnation.

## Execution of Sentences
The auto-de-fe properly so called did not usually include the punishment or the handing over of relapsed or impenitent persons to the secular power. However, those condemned to the flames were sometimes burned on the night following the ceremony.

## Historical Evolution
The first great auto-de-fes were celebrated under the leadership of Thomas de Torquemada at the head of the Spanish inquisition, including events in Toledo in 1486 and Seville in 1482. 

In later periods, subsequent to the time of Charles III, the ceremonies changed in nature. They were held in secret and dealt with a very small number of sentences, almost none of which were capital. Certain isolated incidents in the 19th century—specifically the torturing of a Quaker and a relapsed Jew in Spain during 1826, and the torturing of a revolutionary priest in Mexico in 1816—are not considered to be auto-de-fes.
