# THE FALL OF THE ASHIKAGA

The Ashikaga period was characterized by a persistent lack of loyalty and a recurring cycle of internecine strife. From the accession of Takauji in 1338 until the death of Yoshiaki in 1597, the Ashikaga family presided over a period of 259 years that lacked even a single decade of efficient government or signal success.

## Internal Discord and Political Instability
A defining feature of the Ashikaga annals was the continuity of family feuds and broken promises. This instability stood in contrast to the preceding Hojo era, which had been conspicuously free of such internal conflict. The debilitating effects of this discord significantly deferred the supremacy of the Ashikaga sept.

Early indications of this instability appeared in 1348. Ko Moronao, an able general, was forced to abandon his victory over the Southern Court in Yamato and hasten back to Kyoto to ensure his own safety. During his absence on campaign, Ashikaga Tadayoshi and Uesugi Shigeyoshi had matured a plot to overthrow him.

## The Concept of Ge-koku-jo
The history of the Ashikaga is summarized by the term "ge-koku-jo," which refers to the overthrow of the upper by the lower. This pattern of violated allegiances permeated every level of the social and political hierarchy:
* Miyoshi vassals plotted against their own chiefs.
* The Miyoshi plotted against the Hosokawa.
* The Hosokawa plotted against the Ashikaga.
* The Ashikaga sacrificed the interests of the Throne for their own ambition.
* Different branches of the Imperial family plotted against one another.

The instability was further evidenced by the fate of the fifteen Ashikaga representatives: two were killed by their own vassals, five died in exile, and one was forced to commit suicide.

## Conflict with the Southern Court
The Ashikaga faced significant challenges from the Southern Court. In January 1338, Kitabatake Akiiye, the youthful governor of Mutsu and son of Chikafusa, seized Kamakura. Despite the distance of five hundred miles from Kyoto and the presence of hostile regions, Akiiye marched toward Yoshino, winning three victories. However, on June 11, 1338, at Ishizu, his forces were shattered by an Ashikaga army led by Ko no Moronao, and Akiiye was killed in the engagement. Following this, his younger brother Akinobu attempted a rally by occupying Otokoyama, which commanded Kyoto.

Earlier struggles involved strategic maneuvers and deception. Kusunoki Masashige once spread rumors of Yoshisada's death to induce Takauji to detach large forces, allowing Masashige to drive Takauji to Hyogo. During a period where the Ashikaga cause seemed hopeless, Akamatsu Norimura provided asylum for Tadayoshi and Takauji, advising them to seek a secret mandate from the senior branch of the Imperial family to justify flying the brocade banner.

## Administrative Shifts and Regional Control
The Ashikaga modified the administrative structures of their predecessors. While the Hojo had utilized the *shikken* (military regent) as the head of the *man-dokoro*, the Ashikaga substituted the second *shitsuji* for this role. Later, the office of *kwanryo* became the equivalent. One of the first individuals to be called *kwanryo* was the *shitsuji* Yoshimasa, who died in 1410.

Geographically, the Ashikaga maintained headquarters in Kamakura, though Takauji eventually removed the primary seat to Kyoto, keeping Kamakura as a secondary basis. Over time, the Ashikaga were driven out of Kamakura and replaced by the Uesugi.
