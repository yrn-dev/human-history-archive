# L'y en a'z une plus belle que le jour

The phrase "L'y en a'z une plus belle que le jour" is associated with old French ballads featuring "Trois belles filles" (three beautiful girls). This motif is part of a broader pattern of primitive poetry and folk-song traditions found across various cultures.

## Comparative Folk Motifs
The presence of "Trois belles filles" in French ballads mirrors similar numerical motifs in other global traditions. Examples include the "Three Sailors" found in Provencal and Lithuanian originals of Thackeray's *Little Billee*, and the "three golden birds" ([Greek: tria poulakia]) that serve as a prelude to Greek folk-songs. Additionally, Scottish ballads contain the recurring phrase "the league, the league, the league, but barely three."

## Characteristics of Primitive Poetry
Primitive poetry is often characterized by a "lavish and reckless" use of precious metals. This tendency is observed in several traditions:
* **General Folk-Song:** Common elements include knives made of fine gold, silver horses' shoes, and birds with gold on their wings, as seen in the works of Aristophanes.
* **Finland:** In the *Kalewala*, a so-called epic composed of juxtaposed popular songs, almost everything is described as gold.
* **Mount Rhodope:** Ballads collected by M. Verkovitch in this region similarly employ gold freely.
* **French Song:** This lavishness is exemplified in a song where a Captain's runaway bride is dressed by her lover "Tout en or et argent" (entirely in gold and silver).

H. F. Tozer, in his study of ballads in the Highlands of Turkey, attributes this frequent use of gold and silver to Eastern influences.

## Transmission and Evolution of Popular Poetry
The nature of epic poetry is shaped by the method of its transmission. In cultures where poetry is transmitted via tradition, public recitations, or chant, there is a constant popular tendency to amalgamate diverse compositions into a single work. This process creates a "common treasure" that the people utilize and adapt without concern for individual authorship. 

This trend of amalgamation is noted by M. Fauriel, who suggests that the *Mahabharat* and the *Shah Nameh* of Ferdusi were likely formed by combining originally separate epic poems. This communal approach to poetry ceases once the works are fixed in books and transitioned to reading, at which point poets begin to seek personal glory and individual ownership of their work.

## The Role of Ambulant Singers in Greece
In Greece, the preservation and dissemination of popular songs are facilitated by blind ambulant singers. These individuals memorize a vast quantity of popular songs from various eras and genres. They travel between villages and cities, performing songs that they believe best suit the specific location or circumstance for a small payment.

These singers typically target the most rural populations, who are described as the most curious and eager for impressions. They perform using a stringed instrument played with a bow, which retains the form and name of the ancient Greek lyre. While widely accepted among the Greek population, these singers are not listened to by the Turks.
