# BACHELOR

The term bachelor, derived from the Medieval Latin *baccalarius* (with a late and rare variant *baccalaris*), entered the English language through the Old French *bacheler*. In its most general sense, the word refers to a young man. However, the term has a complex history with several distinct applications, and its ultimate origin remains somewhat obscure.

## Etymology and Early Origins
While Skeat's *Etymological Dictionary* mentions a possible derivation from the Welsh word *bach*, meaning "little," this theory has been definitely discarded by the *New English Dictionary*. The more probable derivation is from the Medieval Latin *baccalarius*, which referred to the tenant of a *baccalaria*. The term *baccalaria* is derived from *baccalia* (a herd of cows), with *bacca* serving as a Low Latin variant of *vacca*. 

Initially, a *baccalaria* was presumably a grazing farm, making it practically identical to a *vaselleria*, or the fief of a sub-vassal. As the size and character of these farms changed over different eras, the meaning of *baccalarius* shifted. By the 8th century, the term was applied to *rustici*—both men and women (*baccalariae*)—who performed work for the tenant of a *mansus*. Throughout these various evolutions, the word has consistently maintained an underlying idea of subordination.

## Applications in Society and Hierarchy
Due to its association with subordination, the term "bachelor" was applied to several different categories of people across various social and professional hierarchies:

### Ecclesiastical Use
In the church, the term was used to describe ecclesiastics of an inferior grade. This included young monks or canons who had been recently appointed.

### Knighthood and Feudalism
Within the system of knighthood, "knights bachelors" represented the lowest stage of the hierarchy. This category consisted of two types of individuals: poor vassals who lacked the financial means to take the field under their own banner, and knights who were too young to handle the dignity and responsibility associated with knights bannerets. 

In the feudal hierarchy, knights bachelors and esquires typically served under bannerets. The bannerets received wages based on the number of knights bachelors and esquires serving under them.

### Academic Use
In a university setting, a bachelor is one who holds a preliminary degree. This degree enables the holder to proceed toward the degree of master (*magister*), which is the only degree that entitles the holder to teach. The terms *baccalarius* or *baccalaureus* first appeared at the university of Paris during the 13th century. This was part of a system of degrees established under the auspices of Pope Gregory IX and was applied to scholars who were still *in statu pupillari*.
