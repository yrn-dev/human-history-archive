# The History of Bohemia

## Introduction of Christianity
The history of Bohemia became less obscure following the introduction of Christianity. Initial attempts to bring the faith to the region originated in Germany, but these efforts saw little success due to the Bohemians' innate distrust of their western neighbors. The situation changed when Christianity arrived via Moravia, where the doctrine had been taught by Cyrillus and Methodius, Greek monks from Thessalonica. Around the year 873, the Bohemian prince Borivoj was baptized by Methodius, leading to a rapid adoption of the Christian faith among the Bohemians.

## Early Rulers and the Premyslide Dynasty
One of the most famous early rulers was Wenceslas, surnamed the Holy. An ally of Germany, Wenceslas was murdered in 935 by his brother Boleslav, who succeeded him. This murder triggered a war with Germany, though Boleslav was generally successful. Under Boleslav and his son, Boleslav II, Bohemia expanded its frontiers to include Moravia, a large part of Silesia, and the portion of Poland now known as the Austrian province of Galicia. However, this empire did not endure; Boleslav III, son of Boleslav II, lost these foreign possessions to Boleslav the Great, king of Poland. Boleslav III's reign was marked by constant civil war and dissensions with his brothers, Jaromir and Ulrick. Due to his cruelty, the Bohemians eventually dethroned Boleslav III in favor of the Polish prince Vladivoj, the son of the Bohemian princess Dubravka.

In 1197, Premysl Ottakar became the undisputed ruler and was crowned king the following year, beginning an uninterrupted line of royal titles. Wenceslas I (1230–1253) succeeded him, though his final years were troubled by discord with his son, Premysl Ottakar II. Ottakar II is regarded as one of Bohemia's greatest kings; he acquired the archduchies of Austria and the loyalty of the Styrian nobility. After defeating King Bela of Hungary at the battle of Kressenbrunn in 1260, his power reached its peak, extending his possessions to Carinthia, Istria, and parts of northern Italy. Known as "the man of gold" for his wealth and "the man of iron" for his military power, Ottakar II encouraged German immigration to counter the influence of the Bohemian nobility.

The Premyslide dynasty ended with Wenceslas III, who was murdered by unknown assassins in 1306 after ruling for only one year.

## Religious Conflict and the Utraquist Church
Later periods of Bohemian history were defined by intense religious controversy. Following the reign of George of Podebrad, the election of Prince Vladislav of Poland in 1471 represented a victory for the national party and anti-clerical sentiment, as Vladislav promised to maintain the Compacts. While he allowed communion in both kinds, his policy remained reactionary; he appointed government officials in Prague from the section of the Utraquist party closest to Rome and oversaw the persecution of the Bohemian Brethren, extreme Hussites.

By 1575, during the reign of Maximilian, the Bohemian estates demanded the recognition of the "Confession of Augsburg," a summary of Luther's teachings. When Maximilian refused to recognize this confession or allow the estates to appoint members of the consistory (the body ruling the Utraquist church), the Bohemian brotherhood and advanced Utraquists drafted the *Confessio Bohemica*. This profession of faith was largely identical to the Augsburg confession but aligned with Calvin regarding the sacrament of the Lord's Supper. Maximilian verbally approved the *Confessio Bohemica* but refused to make it public or include it in the kingdom's charters.

## The Habsburg Dynasty
The Habsburg dynasty permanently acceded to the Bohemian throne on October 23, 1526, when twenty-four electors chose Archduke Ferdinand of Austria as king. Ferdinand claimed the throne as the husband of Anna, daughter of King Vladislav. Although devoted to Rome, Ferdinand initially showed moderation in religious matters to establish his dynasty's hereditary right. However, after a fire in 1541 destroyed state documents, Ferdinand substituted a charter claiming hereditary right over the original elective one, causing significant dissatisfaction.

Later Habsburg rule was marked by instability. In 1611, Archduke Leopold of Austria invaded Bohemia, occupying part of Prague before being defeated by troops raised by the Bohemian estates. This led Matthias to occupy Bohemia and instigate the formal deposition of King Rudolph, who died on January 20, 1612. While Matthias had previously allied with Bohemian Protestants, he eventually adopted the traditional Habsburg policy favorable to the Church of Rome, straining his relations with the Protestants.

## Language and Nationality
During a general diet convoked by Matthias in 1615, which included representatives from the "lands of the Bohemian crown," the issue of nationality returned to the forefront. Amidst ongoing religious controversies, the diet reaffirmed enactments enforcing the use of the national language and decreed that Bohemian would be the official, or "authorized," language of the country.
