# Val-de-Vire

The Val-de-Vire is a location in Normandy, France, known in the historical record primarily as the birthplace of the poet Olivier Basselin.

## Olivier Basselin
Olivier Basselin was a French poet born in the Val-de-Vire toward the end of the 14th century. By profession, Basselin was a fuller; tradition continues to identify the specific site where his mill was located. He is associated with a body of drinking songs that became famous under the name of Vaux-de-Vire, a term that has been corrupted in modern times into the word "vaudeville."

The circumstances of Basselin's death are subject to various traditions, though it is gathered that he was killed during the English wars around the middle of the 15th century. One possibility cited is that he fell at the battle of Formigny in 1450.

## Literary Attributions and Scholarship
The authenticity of the works attributed to Olivier Basselin has been a subject of academic discussion. At the start of the 17th century, a Norman lawyer named Jean Le Houx published a collection of songs claiming they were the work of Basselin. However, there is very little doubt that Le Houx was the actual author of these songs, including those he admitted were his own.

Conversely, it has been suggested that Basselin may be legitimately connected to certain songs published at Caen in 1866 by M. Armand Gaste, which are preserved in the *Bibliotheque Nationale* in Paris. This scholarly debate regarding the works of Basselin and Jean Le Houx is discussed in the 1897 work by M. V. Patard titled *La Verite dans la question Olivier Basselin et Jean le Houx a propos du Vau-de-Vire*. Additionally, the edition of the *Vaux-de-Vire* produced by A. Gaste in 1875 was translated into English by J. P. Muirhead in 1885.
