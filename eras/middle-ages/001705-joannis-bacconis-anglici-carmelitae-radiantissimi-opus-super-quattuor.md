# Joannis Bacconis Anglici Carmelitae radiantissimi opus super quattuor

The *Doctoris resoluti Joannis Bacconis Anglici Carmelitae radiantissimi opus super quattuor sententiarum libris* is the primary work of John Baconthorpe, a learned Carmelite monk. First published in 1510, the text has been released in several editions. Its academic influence persisted for nearly three centuries after its publication, remaining a subject of study at Padua, which served as the final home of Averroism.

## Author Biography
John Baconthorpe, also referred to as Bacon, Baco, or Bacconius, was born in Baconthorpe, Norfolk. He is known by the title "the Resolute Doctor." Evidence suggests he was the grandnephew of Roger Bacon. 

Baconthorpe received his early upbringing at the Carmelite monastery of Blakeney, located near Walsingham. He pursued higher education at the universities of Oxford and Paris. During his time in Paris, he gained a reputation as the "Princeps" of the Averroists, although the scholar Renan suggests that Baconthorpe's intent was actually to justify Averroism against accusations of heterodoxy.

## Career and Political Views
In 1329, Baconthorpe was appointed as the twelfth provincial of the English Carmelites. He is noted for advocating the subordination of the clergy to the king, a position that appears to have anticipated the views of Wycliffe.

In 1333, he was summoned to Rome. Records indicate that he initially maintained the authority of the pope regarding cases of divorce, though he later retracted this opinion. John Baconthorpe died in London in 1346.

## Legacy and Reception
Beyond the academic study of his work at Padua, Baconthorpe has been spoken of with great veneration by Lucilio Vanini. Biographical details regarding his life are found in J. B. de Lezana's *Annales Sacri*, although the accuracy of these specific records is considered doubtful.
