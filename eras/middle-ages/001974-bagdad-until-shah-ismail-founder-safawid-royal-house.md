# Bagdad until Shah Ismail I.

The city of Bagdad experienced a succession of shifting powers and conquests during the centuries leading up to the early 16th century, characterized by the rule of various Mongol and Turkic factions.

## Mongol and Tatar Rule
The Tatars maintained possession of Bagdad for a period of a century and a half, a tenure that lasted until approximately A.D. 1400. During the period following the sack of the city, Hulaku dispatched 3,000 Moghul horse under the command of Ḳarábúgá and Ilká Noyán to Bagdad. Their mission was to restore order and repair the city's infrastructure, which included rebuilding the bazaars and removing the carcasses of beasts from the streets. To maintain security, these chiefs stationed sentries throughout the different quarters of the town.

## The Transition of Power (1400–1468)
Around A.D. 1400, the city was captured by Timur. This conquest forced the sultan Ahmed Ben Avis to flee Bagdad. Ahmed Ben Avis sought refuge with the Greek emperor, though he later succeeded in contriving a way to repossess the city. His return to power was temporary, as he was eventually expelled in 1417 by Kara Yusuf. Kara Yusuf belonged to the Kara-Kuyunli, also known as the "Black Sheep" Mongols.

## The Ak-Kuyunli Period
The descendants of the Kara-Kuyunli held the city until approximately 1468. At that time, they were driven out by Cassim (also known as Uzun Hasan) of the Ak-Kuyunli, or "White Sheep" Mongols. Uzun Hasan and his descendants continued to reign in Bagdad for several decades.

## The Conquest by Shah Ismail I.
The era of Ak-Kuyunli rule ended when Shah Ismail I., the founder of the Safawid royal house of Persia, established himself as the master of Bagdad. This conquest occurred around either 1502 or 1508. Following the acquisition of the city by Shah Ismail I., Bagdad entered a long period of instability, becoming a frequent object of contention between the Persians and the Turks.
