# Barcelona

## Industrial and Commercial Pre-eminence
Barcelona has served as the industrial and commercial center of eastern Spain for a significant period, with a pre-eminence that dates back to the 12th and 13th centuries. While the disasters of the Spanish-American War of 1898 caused a temporary check to this status, the city recovered quickly. Less than a year after the conflict, Barcelona paid approximately L550,000 in commercial and industrial taxes, a sum representing more than 11% of the total amount collected within the kingdom.

## Shipping and Trade
The city's role as a major port was solidified within five years of the 1898 war, becoming a regular port of call for thirty-five important shipping companies. Furthermore, Barcelona housed the head offices of thirteen other shipping lines. Among these was the Transatlantic Mail Company, which operated a fleet of twenty-five steamships.

The city's trade profile includes the import of combustibles, raw materials, and comestibles, as well as a considerable quantity of foreign manufactured goods. Its principal exports consist of:
* Machinery
* Boots, shoes, and leather goods
* Furniture and barrels
* Cigarette-paper, soap, and cotton goods
* Olive-oil, cereals, and wines

## Manufacturing and Industry
Trades and industries in Barcelona provide employment to more than 150,000 workers of both sexes. The primary industries are the weaving and spinning of silk, cotton, and wool. Driven by an enterprising spirit, the Catalans have attempted to establish almost every industry where native capital could compete with foreign interests. This competitive drive increased following the institution of protectionist tariffs in 1892, and native manufacturers are now fully capable of competing with foreign rivals in peninsular markets.

## Regional Development
The prosperity of Barcelona has been supported in part by the significant development of communication means within the four Catalan provinces and around the city itself.
