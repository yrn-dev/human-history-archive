# Gomes Eannes de Azurara

Gomes Eannes de Azurara (?-1474) was a prominent Portuguese chronicler who entered the field of letters during his middle life. His professional trajectory within the royal administration began during the reign of King Duarte (1433-1438), when he likely served as an assistant to Fernao Lopes in the royal library. By 1452, Azurara had assumed sole charge of the library.

## Appointment as Royal Chronicler
On June 6, 1454, Azurara succeeded Fernao Lopes to become the royal chronicler and the chief keeper of the archives. His relationship with King Alphonso V was characterized by affectionate intimacy. In recognition of his literary services, the king rewarded him with no fewer than three *commendas* of the order of Christ.

## Major Works and Historical Contributions
Azurara authored several significant chronicles, often serving as supplements to earlier works or fulfilling specific royal commissions:

*   **Chronicles of Ceuta:** In 1450, he wrote the *Chronicle of the Siege and Capture of Ceuta*, which acted as a supplement to the *Chronicle of King John I* by Fernao Lopes. Later, in 1456, King Alphonso V commissioned him to write a history of Ceuta—described as "the land-gate of the East"—covering the period from its capture in 1415 until 1437 under the governorship of D. Pedro de Menezes. This work was completed in 1463.
*   **Chronicle of Guinea:** Three years after his 1450 work, Azurara completed the first draft of the *Chronicle of the Discovery and Conquest of Guinea*. This text serves as the primary authority for early Portuguese ocean voyages and discoveries along the African coast, particularly those conducted under Prince Henry the Navigator. The work provides both geographical information and a biographical account of the prince's life work.
*   **Chronicle of D. Duarte de Menezes:** In 1464, the king charged Azurara with recording the deeds of D. Duarte de Menezes, the captain of Alcacer. To ensure accuracy, Azurara traveled to Africa and spent a year in the town studying the scenes of the events and collecting materials, completing the chronicle in 1468.

## Literary Style and Influence
Writing under the influence of the first Renaissance, Azurara's style differed from the "picturesque ingenuousness" of Fernao Lopes. He frequently displayed his erudition through philosophical reflections and quotations. His writing reflects a broad intellectual range, incorporating:
*   Medieval, early Christian, and leading classical writers.
*   Notable European romances and chronicles.
*   The works of the best Spanish and Italian authors.
*   The astrology and geographical systems of the ancients.

As a historian, Azurara is characterized as being accurate, laborious, and conscientious.
