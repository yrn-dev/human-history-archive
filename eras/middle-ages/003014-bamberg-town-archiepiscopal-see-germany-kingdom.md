# BAMBERG

Bamberg is a town and archiepiscopal see located in the kingdom of Bavaria, Germany. Situated on an open plain on the river Regnitz, the town lies two miles above the river's junction with the Main and is 39 miles north of Nuremberg by railway. The population of the town was recorded as 31,521 in 1885 and grew to 45,308 by 1905.

## History and Administration
First mentioned in 902, Bamberg developed around a castle known as Babenberch, which provided the name for the Babenberg family. Following the extinction of that family, the area passed to the Saxon house. In 1007, Emperor Henry II founded the see, and by the middle of the 13th century, the bishops of Bamberg served as princes of the Empire. The see was secularized in 1802 and subsequently assigned to Bavaria in 1803.

## Urban Layout and Architecture
The town is divided into an upper and lower section. The upper town is constructed across seven hills, with each hill crowned by a church. The lower town, which remains partially enclosed by ditches and walls, is split into three districts by the Ludwigskanal and the river. A notable architectural feature of the lower town is the *Obere Bruecke*, a bridge completed in 1455. The Rathaus, rebuilt between 1744 and 1756, is situated on an artificial island halfway across this bridge.

The town's palaces include the Old Palace (*Alte Residenz*), built in 1591 on the site of a former residence of the counts of Babenberg, and the New Palace (1698-1704). The New Palace was previously the residence of the prince-bishops and was occupied by the deposed King Otto of Greece from 1864 to 1867.

## Religious and Educational Institutions
The cathedral of Bamberg is a late Romanesque structure featuring four imposing towers. Founded by Emperor Henry II in 1004 and finished in 1012, it was partially burnt and later rebuilt during the 13th century. The cathedral houses an equestrian statue of Emperor Conrad III and a marble tomb for Emperor Henry II and Empress Cunigunde, which was carved by Tilman Riemenschneider between 1499 and 1513.

Other significant churches include:
* **Jakobskirche**: An 11th-century Romanesque basilica.
* **St Martinskirche**.
* **Marienkirche (Obere Pfarrkirche)**: Built between 1320 and 1387 and restored to a pure Gothic style.
* **Michaelskirche**: A 12th-century Romanesque church on the Michaelsberg. Formerly the church of a Benedictine monastery, it was secularized in 1803 and now houses the municipal art collections, the museum, and the Buergerspital (alms-house).

The town's royal lyceum, which previously served as a Jesuit college, contains a royal library with over 300,000 volumes and other notable collections.

## Monuments and Landmarks
The Maximilian fountain, erected in 1880, features statues of St Otto (bishop of Bamberg), Emperor Henry II and his wife, Conrad III, and Maximilian I of Bavaria. At a short distance from the town is the Altenburg, a castle reaching 1,266 feet.

## Culture and Notable Figures
Bamberg has a history of early printing; around 1461, Albrecht Pfister produced some small books in the town that featured some of the first printed illustrations in the form of rude woodcuts.

The town is also the birthplace of Johann Joseph Ignaz von Dollinger (1799-1890), a German church historian and theologian. Born in Bamberg on February 28, 1799, Dollinger studied at Bamberg before being ordained as a priest on April 5, 1822.
