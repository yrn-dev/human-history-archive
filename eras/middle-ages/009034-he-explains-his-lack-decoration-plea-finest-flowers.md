# Osbern Bokenam

Osbern Bokenam was an English author who, according to his own accounts, was born on October 6, 1393. While his exact origins are debated, Dr. Horstmann suggests he may have been a native of Bokeham (now known as Bookham) in Surrey, taking his name from that location. Other records, specifically a concluding note in his *Lives of the Saints*, describe him as "frere Austyn of Stoke Clare" and a "Suffolke man."

## Literary Works and Style
Bokenam is known for writing a series of thirteen legends focused on holy women and maidens. These works were primarily composed using stanzas of seven or eight lines, and nine of these legends include prologues. In terms of linguistic style, Bokenam stated that he wrote plainly, utilizing the Suffolk speech.

Bokenam was a follower of Lydgate and Chaucer, and he was likely influenced by Chaucer's *Legend of Good Women*. His primary source for his legends was the *Legenda Aurea* written by Jacobus de Voragine, the archbishop of Genoa, whom Bokenam refers to as "Januence."

Regarding the aesthetic quality of his writing, Bokenam addressed his lack of decoration by pleading that the finest flowers had already been plucked by Lydgate, Gower, and Chaucer.

## Dedications and Manuscripts
Bokenam's works were often written for specific individuals. The *Vita Scae Margaretae, virginis et martiris*, the first of his legends, was written for Thomas Burgh, a monk from Cambridge and a friend of the author. Other legends were dedicated to pious ladies who wished to read the history of the saints who shared their names.

A unique copy of Bokenam's work is held in the British Museum as Arundel MS. 327. The concluding note of this manuscript indicates it was finished in 1447. It was presented by the scribe, Thomas Burgh, to an unnamed convent for the purpose of ensuring the nuns remembered him and his sister, Dame Betrice Burgh.

## Other Writings and Travel
Beyond his legends, Bokenam's work includes a dialogue written in both English and Latin. This dialogue, which appears in Dugdale's *Monasticon Anglicanum*, features a "Frere answerynge" and a "Secular asking" at the grave of Dame Johan of Acres. The text details the lineal descent of the lords of the honour of Clare from 1248 to 1356.

Bokenam's life included significant travel. He visited Italy on at least two separate occasions and traveled as a pilgrim to Santiago de Compostela in 1445.

## Modern Editions
Bokenam's poems have been edited for academic study in two notable editions. In 1835, they were edited for the Roxburghe Club under the title *Lyvys of Seyntys*. Later, in 1883, Dr. Carl Horstmann edited them as *Osbern Bokenams Legenden*, published in volume i of E. Kolbing's *Altengl. Bibliothek* in Heilbronn.
