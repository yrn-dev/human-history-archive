# Events and Foundations of 1281

The year 1281 is associated with various significant geopolitical shifts, military conflicts, and urban foundations across Europe and Asia, as recorded in the archive's historical accounts.

## Military Conflicts and Invasions
In East Asia, the year 1281 marked a second attempt by a Mongolian army to invade Japan, an event that occurred during the rule of the Hojo. This followed a previous invasion attempt in 1274. Records indicate that during this period of conflict, the Mongols occupied the island of Hirado and Mongol invaders were present in Hizen.

In the East, historical accounts regarding the capital of the Chinese empire note that in 1281 (or 1282, according to some sources), Kúblái and Prince Chingkim departed for Shangtu. This left Ahmad in charge of the capital at a time when the country was largely in revolt against his oppressions, though Kúblái remained unaware of these sentiments.

## European Urban Foundations and Architecture
Several towns and fortifications in Europe were established or reached significant milestones in 1281:

*   **Sauveterre:** In the department of Aveyron, the town of Sauveterre was founded in 1281. It is noted as a striking example of the *bastide* of that period. (A separate town also named Sauveterre is located on the Gave d'Oloron, featuring a Romanesque and Gothic church and an old bridge, though its founding date is not specified).
*   **Bungay:** In Suffolk, England, the ruins of the castle in the market-town of Bungay date from 1281. These ruins are described as massive, though fragmentary. The castle had previously been a stronghold of the Bigod family, having been granted to Roger Bigod, a follower of the Conqueror, in 1075.

## Political and Territorial Changes
In Germany, the county of Löwenstein underwent a change in ownership in 1281. Prior to this year, the county belonged to a branch of the family of the counts of Calw. In 1281, it was purchased by the German king Rudolph I., who then presented the territory to his natural son, Albert. The Habsburgs subsequently held Löwenstein from 1281 until 1441.
