# CONCERNING THE CUSTOMS OF THE TARTARS

The Tartars are a people characterized by a nomadic lifestyle, specific religious practices, and distinct military traditions. Their customs vary based on their location, with those settled in the Levant adopting the customs of the Saracens and those in Cathay adopting the practices of local Idolaters.

## Seasonal Migration and Domestic Life
The genuine Tartars follow a seasonal migration pattern to optimize the use of their environment. During the winter, they reside in warm plains to secure good pasture for their cattle. In the summer, they move to cooler climates located among valleys and mountains, where they can access woods, pastures, and water.

## Religious Beliefs and Rituals
The Tartar religious system involves the worship of multiple deities. They recognize a Most High God of Heaven, whom they worship daily using incense and a thurible, specifically praying to Him for the health of the body and mind. Additionally, they worship a god of the Earth named Natigay, who is believed to watch over crops, cattle, and children. 

Devotion to Natigay is integrated into the home; every man keeps figures of Natigay, his wife, and his children made of cloth and felt. These figures are arranged with the wife to the left and the children in front. During meals, the Tartars grease the mouths of these figures with the fat of the meat and sprinkle broth before the house door to ensure the god and his family receive their share of the dinner.

## Marriage and Funeral Customs
The Tartars practice specific rituals regarding marriage and death. One notable custom involves the marriage of deceased children. If a man's daughter and another man's son both die before marriage, the parents arrange a formal wedding between the deceased. A regular contract is drawn up and then burned so the parties in the other world are aware of the union. The parents then regard one another as kin. Any agreed-upon dowry is painted on paper and burned so the deceased may receive the items in the afterlife.

Regarding burial, the bodies of Tartar Sovereigns are interred in the Altay. Historical records from the Tartar Dynasty of Liao indicate that by A.D. 692, the practice of killing horses for burial rites was interdicted, along with the placement of helmets, coats of mail, and gold or silver trinkets in tombs.

## Dietary Habits and Social Practices
A primary drink of the Tartars is *Kemiz* (also referred to as *Kumiz*), a beverage made from mare's milk prepared to resemble white wine. Other dietary staples mentioned include *kurút* (Tartar curd).

There are accounts regarding the Tartars' treatment of traitors and enemies. Some sources describe a custom where traitors are executed and their flesh is consumed by the Khan and his barons, or boiled and distributed as mince-meat to an army as an example. Other accounts suggest that enemies are eaten in vengeance for their revolt.

## Customs of War
The Tartars are known for their military rapidity and the use of terrorism. Their troops are organized using a decimal division. The bow is their characteristic weapon, leading Armenian historians to refer to them as "The Archers." Their armor includes light but impenetrable gear made from *cuirbouly*, which is leather softened by boiling to take a specific form and then hardened.

In battle, the Tartars employ the Nakkára, a drum used as a signal for action. It is noted that the host may play and sing in chorus while awaiting the signal from the boom of the Big Drum.

## Related Peoples
The Mescript people, located in the Plain of Bargu, are subject to the Great Kaan and share customs similar to those of the Tartars. They are a wild race that lives on fish and birds—which they catch in lakes and marshes when the birds are moulting and unable to fly—as well as cattle. Notably, the Mescript ride upon stags and possess neither wine nor corn.
