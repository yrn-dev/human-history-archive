# Thomas Hearne in Benedictus Abbas Petroburgensis, vol. ii. (1735)

The publication of *Benedictus Abbas Petroburgensis*, volume ii., in 1735, is associated with the work of Thomas Hearne, specifically regarding the printing of a text titled "The Peregrination of Doctor Board."

## Connection to Andrew Boorde
The text printed by Thomas Hearne in the second volume of *Benedictus Abbas Petroburgensis* (1735) is "The Peregrination of Doctor Board." This work is attributed to Andrew Boorde (also spelled Borde), an English physician and author who lived from approximately 1490 to 1549. 

Boorde was born in Holms Dale, Sussex, at Boord's Hill and received his education at Oxford. His career was marked by a transition from religious life to medicine; he was admitted to the Carthusian order while under age, but was "dispensed from religion" in 1521 to serve as suffragan bishop of Chichester, a role he never actually filled. By 1529, he was freed from his monastic vows, citing an inability to endure the "rugorosite off your relygyon."

## The Works of Andrew Boorde
The "Peregrination" printed by Hearne is one of several authentic works by Boorde. Other known writings include:
*   ***The Fyrst Boke of the Introduction of Knowledge*** (c. 1547): Dedicated to Lady Mary, daughter of King Henry VIII, this work serves as an early continental guide book. It provides instructions on speaking various languages, the customs of different countries, and information on various currencies. It includes a geographical description of England, a model dialogue in the Cornish language, and verse describing the foibles of the Englishman, such as obstinacy and a fondness for new fashions.
*   ***Here foloweth a Compendyous Regimente or Dyetary of health, made in Mountpyllor***: Published by Thomas Colwell in 1562, though earlier undated editions exist.
*   ***The Brevyary of Health*** (c. 1547).
*   ***The Princyples of Astronamy*** (c. 1547).
*   ***A Pronostycacyon or an Almanacke for the yere of our lorde MCCCCCXLV***.

Boorde also authored an *Itinerary of Europe* and a *Treatyse upon Berdes*, both of which are now lost. Additionally, several jest-books, such as *Scogin's Jests* (1626) and *The Merie Tales of the Mad Men of Gotam* (1630), as well as a Latin poem titled *Nos Vagabunduli*, have been attributed to him without authority.

## Context of Boorde's Travels
The "Peregrination" reflects Boorde's extensive travels. After leaving his monastic vows, he studied medicine abroad, visiting the universities of Wittenberg, Toulouse, Poitiers, Orleans, and Montpellier. He also observed surgical practices in Rome and went on a pilgrimage to Compostella in Navarre. 

In 1536, while studying medicine at Glasgow, he recorded observations on the "devellyshe dysposicion" of Scottish men toward Englishmen. Between 1538 and 1542, Boorde undertook his most extensive journey, visiting nearly every European country except Turkey and Russia, and traveling as far as Jerusalem. He originally wrote a full itinerary of these travels, which was sent to Thomas Cromwell but subsequently lost.

## Other Activities of Thomas Hearne
Beyond the 1735 printing of Boorde's work, the archive records Thomas Hearne as an editor of the *Historia vitae et regni Ricardi II* by the Monk of Evesham, published in Oxford in 1729. He is also the author of *Diaries*, which are cited as a source for the life of H. D. (whose son Henry wrote a pamphlet on Christianity).
