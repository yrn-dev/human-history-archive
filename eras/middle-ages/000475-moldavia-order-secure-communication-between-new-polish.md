# Moldavia and Austrian Strategic Interests

## Territorial Context and Administration
Moldavia was a principality under the suzerainty of the Porte. Within this region lay the province of Bukovina, which served as the location for the ancient capital of Moldavia, Suczawa. The territory experienced various shifts in control during the late 18th century; it was occupied by Russian forces in 1769 and subsequently by Austrian forces in 1774. By 1777, the Porte ceded Bukovina to Austria. This province was later incorporated with Galicia into a single province in 1786, before being established as a separate crownland in 1849.

## Austrian Strategic Movements in Moldavia
During the late 18th century, the ambitions of Catherine of Russia and the resulting war with Turkey advanced the empire of the tsars to the Black Sea and threatened its establishment south of the Danube. This created a serious menace for Austria, as Russian control of the Danube was viewed as a more significant threat than the presence of the decadent Ottoman power.

While Kaunitz concluded a defensive alliance with the Porte in July 1771—despite the reluctance of Maria Theresa—Austria was unable to take an offensive position. This was due to the unwillingness of Frederick the Great to break with Russia, as he was negotiating the partition of Poland. Consequently, Maria Theresa agreed to share in the spoliation of Poland to modify Russia's extreme claims in Turkey.

Following the first treaty of partition on August 5, 1772, Austria acquired Galicia and Lodomeria. To secure communication between these new Polish provinces and Transylvania, Austrian troops occupied portions of Moldavia.

## Diplomatic Outcomes
Austria's shift in policy led it to support Russian policy at Constantinople. As a result of this alignment, Austria was rewarded in 1777 with the acquisition of Bukovina from Turkey.
