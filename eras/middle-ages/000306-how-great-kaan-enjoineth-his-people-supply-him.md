## Provisioning and Public Welfare
While the Kaan requires supplies from his people for his sport, he also implements systems to supply his people during times of need. The Emperor provides for the distribution of necessaries during periods of dearth by making stores when goods are cheap.

In the city of Cambaluc, the Kaan exercises great charity toward the poor. He identifies a large number of indigent families—ranging in size from six to ten members per household—and ensures they are supplied annually with wheat and other corn sufficient for the entire year. Additionally, a daily dole is provided at the Court, where approximately 30,000 people receive a hot loaf of bread every day.

## Imperial Administration and Logistics
The Great Kaan's ability to command supplies and maintain order is supported by a robust administrative framework. Twelve barons are set over all the affairs of the Great Kaan. To facilitate communication and the movement of orders, the Kaan utilizes a system of posts and runners known as the Yam, which includes government hostelries and a disciplined corps of posts.

The Emperor also regulates the economy by causing the bark of trees, processed into a paper-like material, to serve as money throughout his country. Furthermore, he invests in public infrastructure, such as the planting of trees along highways to create avenues.
