# The Greek Grammar of R. Bacon and a Fragment of his Hebrew Grammar

The *Greek Grammar of R. Bacon and a Fragment of his Hebrew Grammar* is a scholarly work edited by S. A. Hirsch and E. S. Nolan, published in 1902. This edition includes an introduction and accompanying notes.

## Context within the Works of Roger Bacon
The grammar is one of several printed works attributed to Roger Bacon. His bibliography includes various scientific and philosophical treatises, such as the *Speculum Alchimiae* (1541), *De Mirabili Potestate Artis et Naturae* (1542), and the *Libellus de Retardandis Senectutis Accidentibus* (1590), which was translated as the "Cure of Old Age" in 1683. Other collections of his writings include the *Sanioris Medicinae Magistri D. Rogeri Baconis Anglici de Arte Chymiae Scripta* (1603), which contains small tracts such as *Secretum Secretorum* and *Tractatus Trium Verborum*.

Bacon's larger intellectual output includes the *Opus Majus ad Clementem IV* (edited by S. Jebb in 1733 and J. H. Bridges in 1897), of which the *Perspectiva* (1614) and *Specula Mathematica* serve as the fifth and fourth parts, respectively. Additionally, J. S. Brewer's 1859 *Opera hactenus Inedita* contains the *Opus Minus*, *Opus Tertium*, *De Secretis Operibus Naturae*, and the *Compendium Studii Philosophiae*.

## Relationship to the Liber Sex Scientiarum
Bacon viewed his large-scale works as introductions or preliminaries to a comprehensive project intended to embrace the principles of all sciences. This overarching work is perhaps the *Liber Sex Scientiarum*. While only a few fragments of its outline remain, the structure appears to have begun with the *Compendium Studii Philosophiae*, which provided an account of the causes of error and entered into a detailed discussion of grammar. 

Following the study of grammar, the intended sequence of the *Liber Sex Scientiarum* was to address logic, followed possibly by physics and mathematics, and concluding with experimental science and speculative alchemy. Due to the current state of knowledge regarding the manuscripts, the exact nature and contents of this comprehensive work remain subject to conjecture.
