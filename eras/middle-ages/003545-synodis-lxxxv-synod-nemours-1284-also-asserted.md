# Baptismal Practices and the Synod of Nemours

## The Synod of Nemours and Baptismal Validity
In A.D. 1284, the synod of Nemours asserted that baptism performed into the name of Christ alone was valid. This position was also asserted by Hilary (in *de Synodis*, lxxxv.) and Ursinus, an African monk.

## Comparative Baptismal Formulae
Different traditions have utilized varying formulae for the rite of baptism. The formula used by Rome is, "I baptize thee in the name of Father and Son and Holy Spirit." In contrast, the Eastern tradition uses the phrasing, "so-and-so, the servant of God, is baptized." The Greeks further modify this by adding "Amen" after each person and concluding the rite with the words, "Now and ever and to aeons of aeons, amen."

## Interpretations of Trine Immersion
The practice of trine immersion—being dipped three times—has been explained through different theological lenses by various church fathers.

Tertullian explained trine immersion as being derived from the triple invocation, stating that individuals are dipped thrice for the several names and into the several persons. Similarly, Jerome stated that the three plunges are performed so that the one sacrament of the Trinity may be shown forth.

Conversely, other fathers from both the East and West viewed trine immersion as symbolic of the three days of the entombment of Christ. This group includes Athanasius, Gregory of Nyssa, Theophylactus, Cyril of Jerusalem, and Leo of Rome. This specific interpretation is enjoined in the Armenian baptismal rubric and is also found in an epistle from Macarius of Jerusalem to the Armenians (c. 330). Armenian writers further associate this interpretation with the concept of baptism into the death of Christ.
