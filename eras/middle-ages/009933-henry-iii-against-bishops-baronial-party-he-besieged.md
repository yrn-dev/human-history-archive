# Henry III and the Baronial Conflict

The reign of King Henry III was marked by significant tension between the monarchy and the baronial party, leading to political instability, the redistribution of lands, and open warfare known as the Barons' War.

## The Baronial Party and Government Control
During the reign of Henry III, a prominent baronial party emerged to challenge the king's authority. Among the leaders of this movement was Roger, the 4th earl of Norfolk (d. 1270). Roger, who had obtained the office of marshal of England in 1246, became a leading figure among the barons who wrested control of the government from Henry III. In these efforts, the earl of Norfolk assisted Simon de Montfort.

## Conflict with Foreign Favourites
Popular indignation during this period was concentrated on foreign favourites of the king. A primary target of this resentment was Aymer (or Aethelmar) of Valence, the bishop of Winchester and half-brother to Henry III. Aymer was the son of Isabelle of Angouleme (King John's second wife) and Hugo of Lusignan. 

Aymer's appointment to the see of Winchester in 1250 was achieved through strong pressure from the king upon the electors. The appointment was considered unsuitable as Aymer was illiterate, ignorant of the English language, and lived a wholly secular life. At the parliament of Oxford in 1258, Aymer and his brothers repudiated the new constitution that had been prepared by the barons.

## The Siege of Wolvesey Castle
Following the repudiation of the baronial constitution, Aymer of Valence was pursued to Winchester. He was besieged in Wolvesey castle and was eventually compelled to surrender and leave the kingdom. Although he later gained the support of the pope and attempted to return to England, he died of a fatal illness in Paris. It is noted that Aymer had never been consecrated as bishop; consequently, the chapter of Winchester held a new election in 1259.

## The Barons' War and the Earls of Derby
The conflict escalated into the Barons' War. In Derbyshire, the earl of Derby (Robert, probably the 6th earl) was active in stirring up anti-royalist sentiment within the county. In 1266, the earl assembled a considerable force, but this army was defeated by the king's party at Chesterfield. 

As a result of his prominent role in the baronial rising, the earl of Derby was deprived of his title and his lands in 1266. Henry III subsequently granted these large estates to his own son, Edmund, earl of Lancaster.

## The Role of the Church
The English church was also divided during these conflicts. Boniface of Savoy, the archbishop of Canterbury, had become primate in 1243 through the favour of Henry III, as he was the uncle of the king's queen, Eleanor of Provence. While Boniface had shown some sympathy for the reforming party within the English church and led the council of Merton in 1258 to vindicate the privileges of his order, he ultimately took the royalist side during the Barons' War, though he was not noted for great activity.
