# Baldwin V

Baldwin V was the nominal king of Jerusalem from 1183 to 1186. He was the son of Sibylla, the daughter of Amalric I, and her first husband, William of Montferrat.

## Accession and Regency
Baldwin V ascended to the throne in 1183. He was crowned in the stead of his uncle, Baldwin IV, who had ruled since 1174. The transition occurred after a period of political instability involving Guy of Lusignan. Baldwin IV had previously appointed Guy as regent, but Guy was subsequently deposed from both the regency and his right of succession due to his refusal to engage in battle during an attack by Saladin. Consequently, Sibylla's son by her first marriage was crowned as Baldwin V.

Because the king was a child, the kingdom was governed under a regency. While Baldwin IV remained active for a short time after the coronation, he transferred the regency to Raymund of Tripoli in 1184, prior to his own death in 1185.

## Political Climate and Challenges
The reign of Baldwin V was characterized by external military pressure and internal political strife. The kingdom faced the continued advance of Saladin, whose efforts to seize the inheritance of the sons of Nureddin had been fully realized by 1183. 

Internally, the government was marked by dissensions between the administration and Guy of Lusignan. Guy, the second husband of Sibylla (who had married him in 1180), remained the heir presumptive to the throne should the young Baldwin V die without issue.

## Death and Succession
Baldwin V died in 1186. Following his death, Guy of Lusignan succeeded in obtaining the crown, despite facing opposition from Raymund of Tripoli.
