# Bologna

Bologna is a city and archiepiscopal see located in Emilia, Italy. It serves as the capital of the province of Bologna and is the headquarters of the VI. army corps. Situated at the base of the Apennines, the city sits 180 ft. above sea-level at the edge of the Emilia plain. As of 1901, the population of the town was 102,122, while the commune's population was 153,501.

## Urban Development and Architecture
The city's layout consists of a more or less rectangular Roman city oriented on the points of the compass with streets arranged at right angles. This area is distinct from the outer city, which received its fortifications in 1206. While no Roman buildings remain above ground, several of the oldest churches are located outside the Roman limits. These include S. Giovanni in Monte, SS. Vitale ed Agricola, and S. Stefano; the latter is a group of at least seven buildings of various dates, including the former cathedral of SS. Pietro e Paolo from the middle of the 4th century.

Other notable structures include:
* **S. Pietro:** The present cathedral, erected in 910, is now primarily in the baroque style.
* **S. Petronio:** The largest church in town and dedicated to the patron saint of Bologna, it was begun in 1390 in the Gothic style.
* **S. Domenico:** This church contains the body of the saint who died there in 1221.
* **The Leaning Towers:** The Torre Asinelli (1109) and Torre Garisenda (1110) are square brick towers. The Asinelli stands 320 ft. high and is 4 ft. out of the perpendicular, while the unfinished Garisenda is 163 ft. high and 10 ft. out of the perpendicular.

The city is characterized by arcaded streets and numerous private palaces dating from the 13th century onward.

## Political History and Governance
Bologna's early history is sparsely documented. It was subject to the Lombards for a short period and generally ruled by the exarchate of Ravenna until Pippin gave the exarchate to the papacy in 756. The city was sacked by Hungarians in 902. While the exact date it acquired its freedom and its motto *Libertas* is uncertain, the first "constitution" of the commune of Bologna dates from approximately 1123, at which time the city was free and independent.

Between the 12th and 14th centuries, Bologna was frequently at war, supporting the Guelph cause against the neighbouring cities of Romagna and Emilia and against Frederick II. In 1249, the Bolognese captured and imprisoned the emperor's son, Enzio, for the remainder of his life. Internal conflicts between Guelphs and Ghibellines eventually weakened the commune. In 1337, Taddeo de' Pepoli became master of the town, and in 1350, his son sold the city to Giovanni Visconti of Milan.

The city's subsequent history involved alternating periods of papal rule and local lordship:
* It was given to the papacy ten years after 1350, but later revolted to recover its liberty.
* Giovanni Bentivoglio became lord in 1401 but was killed in a 1402 rebellion.
* The city returned to the Visconti before being secured again by the Bentivoglio in 1438.
* In 1506, Pope Julius II drove out the Bentivoglio, bringing Bologna under the papacy.

Bologna remained under papal sway until 1860, when it became part of the kingdom of Italy, with exceptions during the revolutions of 1821 and 1831 and the Napoleonic period (1796–1815).

## Education and Culture
The University of Bologna, founded in the 11th century, gained a European reputation as a school of jurisprudence. This was driven by the work of Pepo (the first known teacher of Roman law, circa 1076), his successor Irnerius, and the glossators. Between the 12th and 15th centuries, student numbers ranged from three to five thousand, peaking at nearly ten thousand in 1262, including Dante and Petrarch. Anatomy was introduced as a subject in the 14th century.

The university operated as an autonomous corporation without a fixed residence for some time; professors lectured in their own homes or rented rooms. In 1520, law professors were given apartments in a building belonging to the church of S. Petronio. A formal university building was constructed nearby in 1562 by Carlo Borromeo under the orders of Pius IV. This building served as the communal library starting in 1838. Since 1803, the university has been established in the 16th-century Palazzo Poggi. As of the archive's records, it possesses four faculties and approximately 1,700 students, and has included women among its professors.

The city's Museo Civico is one of Italy's most important museums, featuring antiquities and a picture gallery. The gallery showcases 16th and 17th-century Bolognese eclectics such as Guercino, Domenichino, Guido Reni, and the Caracci, as well as 15th-century masters Lorenzo Costa of Ferrara and Francesco Francia. A primary treasure of the collection is Raphael's *S. Cecilia*, painted around 1515.

## Notable Figures
Illustrious natives of the city include Prospero Lambertini (Pope Benedict XIV) and Luigi Galvani (1737–1798), the discoverer of galvanism.
