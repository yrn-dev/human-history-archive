## Historical Context of the Bassoon
References to Harl MS 2034 (specifically fol. 207b) appear in discussions regarding the history of the bassoon and its predecessors. The archive's sources use the manuscript to examine the work of Sigmund Schnitzer, a Nuremberg craftsman. 

In the 18th century, Schnitzer was renowned for producing *fagotte* (bassoons) of pure intonation, easy speech, and extraordinary size. His workmanship was highly regarded in Italy, France, and Germany. However, earlier records from 1549 by Johann Neudorfer describe Schnitzer simply as a *Pfeifenmacher und Stadtpfeifer* (pipe maker and city piper). 

The manuscript's evidence is used to debate whether Schnitzer invented the bassoon by doubling the tube of the bass pommer to reduce its length. Some scholars argue that if he had been the inventor of such a new form, contemporary chroniclers like Neudorfer would have noted it, rather than simply remembering him as a maker of instruments of "extraordinary size."
