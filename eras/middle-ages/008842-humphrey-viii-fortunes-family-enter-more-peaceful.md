## The Bohun Family
The Bohuns were a family of significance in English history during the 14th and 13th centuries, originating from a village in the Cotentin located between the estuary of the Vire and Coutances. They arrived in England at or shortly after the Norman Conquest.

Humphrey III is credited as the founder of the family's greatness. He served as a *dapifer* (steward) in the royal household during the later years of Henry I. During the reign of Stephen, Humphrey III played an ambiguous role, initially siding with the king before switching his allegiance to the empress. He remained loyal to Henry II and fought against the rebels of East Anglia at Farnham in 1173. He lived until 1187.

Humphrey III was succeeded in the family estates by his grandson, Henry, as Humphrey III had outlived his eldest son, Humphrey IV. Henry obtained the earldom of Hereford from John in 1199, aided by his mother Margaret's connection to the royal house of Scotland. The Bohuns became prominent Marcher barons, with their lands primarily located on the Welsh Marches.

Humphrey V, the son and heir of Henry, returned to a path of loyalty and inherited the earldom of Essex from his maternal uncle, William de Mandeville, before 1239. However, by 1258, Humphrey V shifted his allegiance from the royal cause to the baronial cause, serving as a nominee of the opposition on a committee of twenty-four appointed to reform the administration during the Oxford parliament. He eventually returned to royal allegiance following the alliance between Llewelyn of North Wales and Montfort.

## The Earldom of Buckingham
The title of the earldom of Buckingham has a complex history of succession. In 1377, Thomas of "Woodstock," the duke of Gloucester, was created earl of Buckingham during the coronation of Richard II. Following his death, his son Humphrey held the title of earl of Buckingham.

Upon the death of this Humphrey, the title passed to his sister, Anne, who became countess of Buckingham in her own right. Anne married Edmund Stafford, earl of Stafford. After her death in 1438, the title passed to her son, Humphrey Stafford, earl of Stafford. In 1444, Humphrey Stafford was created duke of Buckingham. The title remained within the Stafford family until 1521, when Edward, the 3rd duke, was executed following an attainder.
