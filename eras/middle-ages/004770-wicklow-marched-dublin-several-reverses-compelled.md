# Dublin and Surrounding Regions

## Historical Conquests and Brian Boru
Brian Boru (also known as Brian, Boroma, or Boroimhe), the king of Ireland who lived from 926 to 1014, was the son of Cenneide (or Kennedy), who died in 951. Brian spent his youth fighting the Danes, who frequently ravaged Munster, specifically the northern part of the district where Brian's tribe resided. Following the murder of his brother Mathgamhain (or Mahon)—who had been king of Thomond around 951 and later king of Munster—Brian avenged the act and became king of Munster in 978.

Brian began a career of conquest, forcing the tribes of Leinster and Munster to acknowledge his sovereignty. He defeated the Danes established in Wicklow and around Dublin, eventually marching into Dublin. After several reverses, he compelled Malachy (Maelsechlainn), the chief king of Ireland ruling in Meath, to bow before him in 1002. Brian further extended his success into Ulster and Connaught, receiving tribute and hostages, and was generally recognized as the *ardri*, or chief king of Ireland. After a period of relative quiet, he fought the Danes of Dublin again, achieving a great victory at Clontarf on April 23, 1014. Brian was slain in his tent following this battle and was buried at Armagh. A charter of his is preserved in Trinity College, Dublin.

## Geography of Wicklow and the Vale of Avoca
County Wicklow is located in the province of Leinster and borders County Dublin and County Kildare. The Wicklow range contains a peak that is exceeded in height in Ireland only by the higher reeks in Killarney and one other peak in the Mourne Mountains.

Within the south-eastern part of Wicklow lies the Vale of Avoca (or Ovoca), a densely wooded and narrow mountain glen. This vale is formed by the junction of the Avonbeg and Avonmore rivers, which rise in the county's central highlands. Their combined waters form the Ovoca river, which flows south and south-east to Arklow at the Irish Sea. The area is known for two "meetings of the waters"; the upper meeting, located near the seat of Castle Howard, inspired the poet Thomas Moore in one of his *Irish Melodies*. Charles Stewart Parnell was born at Avondale, situated above this upper meeting by the Avonmore. The beauty of the glen is somewhat marred by the main line of the Dublin & South Eastern railway—with Ovoca station located 42-3/4 miles south of Dublin—as well as copper and lead mines.

## County Kildare
Kildare is a county in the province of Leinster, bounded by Meath to the north, Dublin and Wicklow to the east, Carlow to the south, and King's and Queen's Counties to the west. It covers an area of approximately 654 square miles (418,496 acres). Much of the county is part of Ireland's great central plain, though the east is bounded by the foothills of the Wicklow and Dublin mountains. The center of the county features an elevated plateau with the Hill of Allen (300 ft.) to the north and the hills of Dunmurry to the south.

The county's principal rivers include the Liffey, the Barrow, the Boyne, and the Lesser Barrow. The Liffey enters near Ballymore Eustace and exits at Leixlip. While Kildare has a good supply of water-power, it has few manufactures, consisting mainly of breweries, distilleries, corn mills, and small paper, woollen, and cotton mills. Large quantities of turf are exported from Kildare to Dublin via canal.

## Dublin Infrastructure and Urban Centers
Dublin serves as a major hub for transportation and administration. The city is connected to the Shannon via the Royal Canal (at Cloondara) and the Grand Canal (at Shannon Harbour). In the city, military barracks were historically built in large towns for political reasons to suppress disturbances, often resulting in cramped buildings in slum areas, such as the cavalry barracks at Hulme or the Ship Street barracks and the converted Linen Hall in Dublin.

Kingstown is a seaport in County Dublin located at the south-eastern extremity of Dublin Bay, 6 miles from Dublin. Originally called Dunleary, it was renamed after George IV embarked there in 1821. The port, which features an eastern pier of 3500 ft. and a western pier of 4950 ft., was completed in 1859. Kingstown is a center for sea-fishery and hosts the Royal Alfred, Royal St George, and Royal Irish yacht clubs. It also serves as the station for the City of Dublin Steam Packet Company's mail steamers to Holyhead.
