# FEAST OF THE ASS AND ATHENS, OHIO

## The Feast of the Ass
The Feast of the Ass was formerly a festival celebrated in northern France, typically held on January 14th. The primary purpose of the event was the commemoration of the biblical flight into Egypt. During the ceremony, a girl with a baby at her breast was seated upon a splendidly caparisoned ass and led through the town to the church. Once there, she was placed at the gospel side of the altar while mass was conducted.

Over time, the ceremony degenerated into a burlesque, and the ass associated with the flight into Egypt became confused with Balaam's ass. Due to the scandalous nature of the popular revels that accompanied the event, the church prohibited the celebration in the 15th century.

## Athens, Ohio
Athens is a village and the county-seat of Athens County, Ohio, U.S.A. Located in Athens township on the Hocking river, it is situated approximately 76 miles east-southeast of Columbus. The village is built on rolling ground that rises about 70 feet above the river, which nearly encircles the settlement and provides views of some of the state's most beautiful scenery. Ancient mounds are found in the vicinity.

The population of Athens has grown over the following period:
*   1890: 2,620
*   1900: 3,066
*   1910: 5,463 (with the township population reaching 10,156 in 1910)

The village is served by several railways, including the Hocking Valley, the Toledo & Ohio Central (Ohio Central Lines), and the Baltimore & Ohio Southwestern. The municipality operates its own water-works.

## History and Education in Athens, Ohio
The establishment of Athens is linked to the Ohio Company. Through Manasseh Cutler, the company obtained land from congress with the arrangement that income from two townships—Athens and Alexander—be set aside for the support of a literary institution. These townships were surveyed and located in 1795. In 1800, Rufus Putnam and two other commissioners appointed by the Territorial legislature laid out the town of Athens.

Settlers arrived slowly, and the town became the county-seat in 1805. It was incorporated as a village in 1811 and re-incorporated in 1828.

Athens is the seat of Ohio University, a co-educational state institution established in 1804. By 1908, the university had 1,386 students and 53 instructors. Its facilities included a state preparatory school, a commercial college, a college of music, a state normal college (established 1902), and a college of liberal arts. Additionally, a state hospital for the insane is located in a fine situation south of the village.

## Economy of Athens, Ohio
The region surrounding Athens contains many coal mines. Local manufactures include shirts, veneered doors, furniture, and bricks.
