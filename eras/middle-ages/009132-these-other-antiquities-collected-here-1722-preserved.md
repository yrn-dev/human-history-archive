# Antiquities of Bolgari

## Overview of Bolgari
Bolgari, also referred to as Bolgary, is a ruined town located in Russia within the government of Kazan. Situated at 55 degrees North latitude, the site lies four miles from the left bank of the Volga. It is generally regarded as having served as the capital for the Bulgarians during their establishment in that region of Europe between the 5th and 15th centuries.

## Archaeological Findings and Antiquities
The ruins of Bolgari include surviving towers and old walls. The area is also characterized by numerous burial-mounds, known as *kurgans*. Upon the opening of these tombs, researchers discovered a variety of implements, utensils, weapons, and coins made of copper and silver. 

These coins and other finds feature inscriptions in several languages and scripts:
* **Arabic:** Some inscriptions date from 1222 to 1341, while others are written in Kufic, a form of epigraphic Arabic.
* **Armenian:** Inscriptions have been identified from the years 557, 984, and 986.
* **Turkic:** Additional inscriptions in Turkic were also found.

In 1722, the antiquities collected from this site were gathered for preservation. These items are currently preserved in museums located in St Petersburg, Moscow, and Kazan.

## History and Destruction
The ruins of the city were practically discovered during the reign of Peter the Great and were subsequently described and visited by Humboldt and Pallas. 

The city suffered two major destructions: first by the Mongols in 1238, and later in the early 14th century by Tamerlane. Following these events, Bolgari served as the capital for the sovereign princes, or Khans, of the Mongol Golden Horde. By the second half of the 15th century, the town became part of the principality of Kazan and eventually became part of Russia. According to the Arab geographer Ibn Haukal, the town had a population of 10,000 inhabitants near the end of the 10th century.
