# REHEARSAL OF THE WAY THE YEAR OF THE GREAT KAAN IS DISTRIBUTED

## Annual Festivals and Observances
The Great Kaan maintains a calendar marked by significant annual celebrations. Every year, a great feast is held on the occasion of the Grand Kaan's birthday. Additionally, the Kaan holds a great festival on New Year's Day. These celebrations are associated with specific cultural and symbolic elements, including the "White Month" and the mystic value of the number 9. During these great festivals, twelve thousand barons receive robes made of cloth of gold from the Emperor, with each baron receiving thirteen changes of costume.

## Imperial Hunting and Court Life
The Kaan's yearly activities include extensive hunting expeditions. He employs a variety of animals for the chase, including wolves, leopards, and lions (the latter term used by Polo to describe the tiger). He also utilizes lynxes and the Búrgút Eagle. The management of the Kaan's hounds is entrusted to two brothers who hold a specific title as Masters of the Hounds.

The Emperor's hunting tours involve specialized establishments for hawking and the use of great tents, as well as litters. Upon returning from these expeditions, the Great Kaan holds a great court and entertainment. Furthermore, the Kaan enjoins his people to supply him with game for these purposes.

## Administration and Governance
The Great Kaan governs his dominions, including the provinces of Cathay and Mangi, through a system of strategic military control to prevent rebellion. Garrisons are maintained four or five miles from large cities; these cities are forbidden from having walls or gates that could obstruct the entrance of troops. To ensure loyalty and prevent disturbance, the Kaan relieves the commanders and the garrisons every two years. 

These troops are supported by regular pay from provincial revenues and by the sale of milk from the vast quantities of cattle they keep. These stations are scattered at distances of 30, 40, or 60 days from the capital.

## The Kaan's Relationship with Envoys and Officials
The Great Kaan is noted for his ability to identify and employ capable individuals. Messer Marco Polo served in the Kaan's employment for seventeen years, acting as a trusted agent on various missions. Because Marco took pains to gather knowledge of things that would interest the sovereign and related them in regular order upon his return to court, the Emperor held him in great love and favor. This trust led the Kaan to employ Marco on the most distant and weighty missions, keeping him close to his person, which caused envy among some of the Barons.

The Kaan's trust in his ambassadors was further evidenced when he confided the Queen Cocachin and the daughter of the King of Manzi to the care of three ambassadors to conduct them to Argon, the Lord of all the Levant.

## Military Action and Imperial Law
While the Great Kaan typically sent his barons or sons into the field, he personally led a campaign against the rebellion of Nayan, viewing the insurrection as too perilous for others to command. Following Nayan's capture, the Kaan ordered his secret execution to prevent others from seeking pity or pardon for him, as Nayan was of the Kaan's own flesh and blood. 

Nayan was executed by being wrapped in a carpet and tossed to and fro until he died. This specific method was chosen because the Kaan would not allow the blood of his Line Imperial to be spilt upon the ground or exposed to the Sun and the eye of Heaven. Following this victory, the Barons and people of Nayan's four provinces—Chorcha, Cauly, Barscol, and Sikintinju—renewed their fealty to the Kaan.
