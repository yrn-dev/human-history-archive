# The High Plateaus of Persia, Armenia, and Asia Minor

The high region of Asia west of British India, excluding Syria and Arabia, consists of an extensive plateau. This region is nearly conterminous with Persia (or Iran), Armenia, Afghanistan, Baluchistan, and the provinces of Asia Minor. In size, this plateau is comparable to that of Tibet, though it exists at a lower altitude.

## Geography and Boundaries
The southern border of this high region runs along the Persian Gulf, the Arabian Sea, and the Tigris, extending westward to the north-east angle of the Levant. To the north, the highland follows the southern shore of the Caspian and the Black Sea, reaching nearly 36 deg. N., and extends to the Sea of Marmora.

The eastern flank is defined by a line of hills near the Indus, between the Himalaya and the river's mouth, generally ranging from 4,000 to 5,000 feet, though some summits exceed 10,000 feet. The south-western face is formed by a mountain scarp that rises in many points to 10,000 feet, flanking the Mesopotamian plains and the Tigris, and extending through Armenia and Kurdistan to the 40th meridian before turning along the Taurus range. In the north-east, the Afghan table-land abuts on Tibet and the Himalaya. A mountain range stretches from the 36th parallel west toward the Caspian; its north-eastern portion separates the headwaters of the Indus and its Kabul tributary from the headwaters of the Oxus.

## Regional Provinces and Cities
The archive distinguishes several key areas within this geography:

**Upper Asia**
Beginning at the river Indus, this area includes provinces such as Gedrosia, Carmania, Drangiana, Arachosia, and Margiana. Other notable provinces include:
*   **Bactriana**: Capital was Bactra.
*   **Sogdiana and Hyrcania**: Hyrcania is located near the Caspian Sea (also known as the Hyrkanian lake).
*   **Parthia and Media**: The chief city of Media was Ecbatana.
*   **Persia**: Includes the cities of Elymais and Persepolis.
*   **Susiana**: The city of Susa.
*   **Assyria**: The city of Nineveh, situated on the Tigris.
*   **Mesopotamia**: Located between the Tigris and Euphrates.
*   **Babylonia**: The city of Babylon on the Euphrates.

**Asia Between the Caspian and Pontus Euxinus**
This region is divided into four provinces: Colchis (featuring mount Caucasus and the river Phasis), Iberia, Albania (the latter two now part of Georgia), and Greater Armenia. Greater Armenia is separated from Lesser Armenia by the Euphrates, from Assyria by mount Niphates, and from Mesopotamia by mount Taurus. Its cities include Tigranocerta (located in Gordyene) and Artaxata, with the river Araxes running through the province.

**Asia Minor**
This region is described as a bridge between Asia and Europe. It features a central plateau (2,500 to 4,500 feet) with a severe climate and no navigable rivers. The west coast is characterized by fertile valleys and rugged mountains.

## Historical Populations and Migrations
Early Asia Minor was occupied by non-Aryan races, including the "Syro-Cappadocians" or "Hittites" (known to Herodotus as "White Syrians"). Their power center was Boghaz Keui, east of the Halys. Later, an Aryan immigration from Europe occurred, dying out in the 10th and 11th centuries B.C. In Phrygia, Aryans established a kingdom that was eventually broken in the 8th or 9th century B.C. by the Cimmerii, who entered via Armenia. Subsequently, the kingdom of Lydia rose with its center at Sardis.

In the mountains overhanging Assyria, a Turanian tribe called the Gutu (meaning "warrior") held power. In Assyrian, this was rendered as Gardu or Kardu. After the fall of Nineveh, the Gutu coalesced with the Medes. Along with other nations of the high plateaus of Persia, Armenia, and Asia Minor, they became gradually Aryanized due to the immigration of overwhelming numbers of Aryan tribes.

## Political Transitions and Empires
The region has seen various successive powers:
*   **The Seljuks**: In 1067, Seljuk Turks ravaged Cilicia and Cappadocia, later capturing Nicaea in 1080. They founded the empire of Rum, with capitals at Nicaea and Iconium. This empire included nearly all of Asia Minor and parts of Syria and Armenia until the Mongols defeated the sultan of Rum in 1243.
*   **The Mongols**: The Mongols eventually destroyed the kingdom of the Seljukian Sultanate of Iconium. The last three sovereigns of that dynasty were Ghiath ed-din Kaïkhosru III, Ghiath ed-din Mas’ud II, and Ala ed-din Kaïkobad III.
*   **Lesser Armenia**: A small warlike state that grew with support from Latin princes in Cilicia; it fell in 1375 when Leo VI was captured by the Mameluke sultan of Egypt.
*   **The Osmanli Turks**: Following the decline of Mongol influence, the Osmanli Turks of Brusa asserted supremacy. By 1400, Sultan Bayezid I held Asia Minor west of the Euphrates, though he was later captured by Timur in 1402. Supremacy was re-established under Mahommed II, who annexed Trebizond and Karamania.
*   **The Khazars**: An ancient people (A.D. 190–1100) located in the Caucasus and along the Caspian. Their capital was Itil. They served as commercial carriers between the East and West, and Itil became a meeting place for the commerce of Armenia, Persia, Byzantium, and Russia.

## Natural History
The beech tree is widely distributed in the northern provinces of Persia and throughout Asia Minor. Additionally, horses of repute known as Turkman originate from the east of the Caspian. While Asia Minor was once the source of many high-repute breeds, it became poorer in horses than other Ottoman provinces.
