# The City of Lop and the Great Desert

The city of Lop is a large town situated between the east and north-east, located at the entrance of the Great Desert, also referred to as the Desert of Lop. The town belongs to the Great Kaan, and its inhabitants worship Mahommet.

## The City of Lop
Lop serves as a critical waypoint for travelers intending to cross the Great Desert. Because of the hardships of the journey ahead, those proposing to cross the desert typically take a week’s rest in the town to refresh themselves and their cattle. During this period, they prepare for the trek by securing a month’s supply of provisions for both man and beast.

The city is reached after a five-day ride through the sands from the province of Charchan. During this five-day journey, travelers find only bad and bitter water until they reach the area of Lop, where the water is sweet. In historical and geographical notes, Lop has been identified as the *Napopo* (or *Navapa*) mentioned by Hiuen Tsang, also known as the country of *Leulan*.

## The Great Desert of Lop
The Desert of Lop is characterized by its immense scale and harsh environment. It is composed entirely of hills and valleys of sand, and no food can be found within it. The length of the desert is so vast that it is said a rider would require a year or more to travel from one end to the other. At its narrowest point, the desert takes one month to cross.

Water sources within the desert are scarce and limited in quantity. After riding for a day and a night, travelers may find fresh water, though the amount is only sufficient for approximately 50 to 100 persons and their beasts. In total, there are some 28 places across the desert where good water can be found, along with four locations that provide brackish water.

## Regional Geography and Provinces
The city of Lop and its surrounding desert are situated among several provinces:

*   **Charchan:** A province of Great Turkey located between the north-east and east. Its people worship Mahommet and live in numerous towns and villages. The province is sandy and contains rivers that produce Jasper and Chalcedony, which are sold at high prices in Cathay. The inhabitants of Charchan are known to escape into the sandy waste with their families and cattle during army incursions, utilizing their knowledge of hidden water spots to survive while the wind erases their tracks.
*   **Camul:** A former kingdom located between two deserts: the Great Desert of Lop on one side and a smaller desert of three days' journey on the other. The people of Camul are Idolaters with a unique language. They are described as a people who enjoy singing, dancing, and playing. They possess a custom where a host considers it an honor to put his wife entirely at the disposal of a foreign guest, while the husband departs until the guest leaves.
*   **Pein:** A province located to the west of Charchan. The road from Pein to Charchan is sandy, and much of the water encountered is bitter and bad.

## Exploration and Research
Modern explorations have provided further context regarding the region. Nicolai Prjevalsky, a Russian explorer, visited Lop Nor in 1876 and later crossed the Altyn Tagh to Lop Nor during a journey between 1883 and 1885. Research by Sven Hedin revealed the periodic shifting of the Lop Nor system. Additionally, Hedin and Dr. M.A. Stein demonstrated that the sand-strewn wastes of the Turkestan desert once housed highly civilized centers of Buddhist art and industry.

Geographical analysis suggests that the region is part of a great central depression of the continent extending from the Pamir plateau through the Tarim desert to Lop Nor and the Gobi. Some sources note that the "hills and valleys of sand" described by travelers make the passage extremely difficult for cattle and carts.
