# Premysl Ottakar II

## Accession and Early Rule
Premysl Ottakar II was a prominent king of Bohemia and the son of Wenceslas I. During his father's reign, Ottakar served as the ruler of Moravia and for a period acted as the primary leader of the malcontents during a time of internal discord. However, a reconciliation between father and son occurred before Wenceslas I died in 1253, at which point Ottakar succeeded him as king of Bohemia.

## Territorial Expansion
Ottakar II is regarded as one of Bohemia's greatest kings, significantly expanding his dominions. During his father's lifetime, he had already acquired the archduchies of Austria. In 1251, the estates of Austria, weary of disorder and lacking help from central authority, chose Ottakar as their duke. This appointment was supported by the pope. To strengthen this position, Ottakar married Margaret, the sister of Duke Frederick II, and promised his assistance to William of Holland in exchange for his investiture.

His influence further extended to Styria. Although Bela IV, king of Hungary, initially conquered the land and signed a treaty with Ottakar in 1254 confirming his possession, the Styrians soon resented Hungarian rule. Ottakar utilized this resentment to interfere in the duchy's affairs, leading to war with Hungary. In March 1261, a treaty with Bela recognized Ottakar as the duke of Styria. Additionally, Ottakar inherited the duchy of Carinthia in 1269 following the death of Duke Ulrich III.

## Military Power and Wealth
The height of Ottakar's power followed his decisive victory over King Bela of Hungary at the great battle of Kressenbrunn in 1260. Following this triumph, he obtained possession of Istria and portions of northern Italy. At his peak, his territories stretched from the Adriatic to the Giant Mountains in Bohemia, encompassing nearly all areas west of the Leitha that would later form the Habsburg empire.

Due to his immense wealth, his contemporaries referred to him as "the man of gold," while his military strength earned him the moniker "the man of iron."

## Internal Policy and German Immigration
Ottakar sought to improve the conditions of the duchies under his control by restoring order and benefiting the inhabitants of the towns. He specifically introduced German colonists into the eastern districts. 

His decision to favor the immigration of Germans into his dominions was driven by political rather than racial causes. Ottakar hoped that the presence of German townsmen would serve as a counterpoise to the overwhelming power held by the Bohemian nobility.

## Aspirations for the German Throne
As his power grew, Ottakar began to aspire to the German throne. It is considered very probable that the German crown was offered to him, though he refused it. One of the causes for this was his Slavic nationality. In 1273, the position of king of the Romans was instead filled by the election of Rudolph, count of Habsburg.
