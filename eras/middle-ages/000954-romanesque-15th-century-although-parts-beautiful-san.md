# Romanesque Architecture of the 15th Century and Related Structures

The Romanesque style, while primarily associated with earlier medieval periods, appears in various forms across Europe during the 15th century, either as a continuing tradition, a transitional phase, or through the preservation of older elements within newer constructions.

## Romanesque in Spain
In the city of Avila, the capital of its province, several ecclesiastical buildings maintain Romanesque features. The churches of San Segundo, Santo Tomas, San Pedro, and San Vicente are characterized in their main features as Romanesque of the 15th century. However, the archive notes that portions of San Pedro and the "beautiful San Vicente" may date back as far as the 12th century. 

Avila's other major ecclesiastical structure, the Gothic cathedral, is traditionally said to date from 1107, though it is likely the work of the 13th or 14th century. This cathedral possesses the appearance of a fortress, featuring two solid towers and embattled walls.

## Romanesque in Dalmatia
In Dalmatia, the Romanesque style persisted for a significant duration. The belfry of S. Maria at Zara, erected in 1105, is noted as the first in a series of Romanesque buildings in the region. Other examples include a beautiful 12th-century campanile at Arbe and the cathedral of Trau, which is cited as the finest example of the style. 

The Romanesque style lingered in Dalmatia until the early years of the 15th century, at which point it was displaced by Venetian Gothic. Despite this shift, some structures continued to blend styles; for instance, in the republic of Ragusa, the Romanesque architecture of the Rectors' palace and the custom-house is combined with Venetian Gothic.

## Romanesque in France
Various regions of France exhibit Romanesque architecture across different eras. In the department of Dordogne, the church at Cadouin is described as a fine cruciform building in the Romanesque style, though its cloister is an example of Flamboyant architecture. Additionally, St Jean-de-Cole contains an interesting Romanesque church.

In the suburb of Déols, the parish church of St Stephen, which dates to the 15th and 16th centuries, features a Romanesque façade. The town also preserves a fine Romanesque tower belonging to the remains of a Benedictine abbey founded in 917.

## Romanesque in Other Regions
In Holland, the city of Maastricht contains the church of Our Lady, which is described as a late Romanesque building. It features two ancient crypts and a choir from the 13th century. The church of St Servatius also displays a varied character, incorporating late Romanesque and later Gothic architecture due to frequent restorations.

In Budapest, Romanesque architecture appears in more modern contexts. The Leopoldstadt Basilica, begun in 1851, is a Romanesque building with a dome reaching 315 feet in height. The Franzstadt church, erected in 1874, is also built in the Romanesque style.

In Iglesias, the churches of the Capuchins and Sta Chiara (the latter dating from 1285) demonstrate a transition between the Romanesque and Gothic styles.
