# BARTHOLOMEW FAIR

Bartholomew Fair was a historic fair held in West Smithfield, London. It took place on St Bartholomew's Day (the 24th of August, O.S.) and operated from 1133 until its final closure in 1855.

## Origins and Administration
The fair was established via a charter granted by Henry I to Rahere, a former minstrel of the king. Rahere had taken holy orders and founded the priory of St Bartholomew in close proximity to the fair's location. For many centuries, the event lasted a fortnight, though this duration was shortened to four days in 1691. By 1641, the fair had expanded to a size that involved four different parishes: St Sepulchre's, Christ Church, and both Great and Little St Bartholomew's.

The formal opening of the fair was the responsibility of the lord mayor of London, who performed the ceremony on St Bartholomew's Eve. As part of this tradition, the lord mayor would stop at Newgate on his way to the opening to receive a cup of sack from the governor. In 1753, the fair was proclaimed on the 3rd of September due to a change in the calendar.

## Commercial Significance
In its earlier history, Bartholomew Fair developed into a vast national market and served as the primary cloth sale in the kingdom. This commercial importance persisted for centuries; until 1854, it was customary for a representative of the Merchant Taylors' Gild to attend the cloth fair portion of the event. The representative's purpose was to use the company's silver yard to test the measures used for selling cloth.

## Entertainment and Culture
The fair was a center for various forms of public amusement and performance. Actors of the period, such as William Bullock (c. 1657–c. 1740), who was known for his comic vivacity, performed at booths at Bartholomew Fair during the summer.

The fair also featured puppet plays and "motions." A 1641 pamphlet describes the scene as one where "rogues" in antic shapes or "knaves in a fool's coat" used drums or trumpets to invite the public to view their puppets. Notable puppet plays associated with the fair included *The Humours of Bartholomew Fair*, while other period puppet shows included *Dick Whittington*, *The Vagaries of Merry Andrew*, and *Patient Grizill*—the latter of which was attended by Lady Castlemaine in 1667, as recorded by Pepys.

Musical entertainment was also prevalent. At the beginning of the 18th century, bands could be hired at the fair's booths. One such band consisted of a violin, pipes, a double drum, a tambourine, a Turkish jingle, and a "Dutch organ" (also known as a barrel-organ).
