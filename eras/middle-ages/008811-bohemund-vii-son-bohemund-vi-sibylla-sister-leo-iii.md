# Bohemund VII

Bohemund VII was the son of Bohemund VI and Sibylla, who was the sister of Leo III of Armenia. He succeeded his father as the count of Tripoli in 1275.

## Succession and Regency
Upon the death of Bohemund VI in 1275, Bohemund VII assumed the title of count of Tripoli. Because of the circumstances of his succession, his mother, Sibylla, served as regent.

## Reign and Conflicts
The reign of Bohemund VII is described as both short and troubled. During his time in power, he experienced conflict with the Templars, who had established themselves in Tripoli. His territorial holdings also suffered losses; in 1287, the same year as his death, the sultan of Egypt captured Laodicea.

## Death and Extinction of the County
Bohemund VII died in 1287 without issue. Following his death, Tripoli was captured within two years. Consequently, the county of Tripoli is considered to have become extinct with him.

## Lineage and Ancestry
Bohemund VII was part of a dynasty whose history is intertwined with the principality of Antioch and the county of Tripoli. His father, Bohemund VI (born 1237), was the son of Bohemund V and Luciana, a daughter of the count of Segni and nephew of Innocent III. Bohemund VI had succeeded his father in 1251 and had been knighted by St Louis in 1252.

The family's connections to other regional powers were maintained through strategic marriages. Bohemund VI's sister, Plaisance, married Henry I of Cyprus in 1250. This continued a Cypriot connection to Antioch that had been originally formed by the marriage of Bohemund V to Alice, the widow of Hugh I of Cyprus. Additionally, the marriage of Bohemund VI to Sibylla of Armenia helped bring peace to the hostility previously experienced between Armenia and the house of Bohemund V.
