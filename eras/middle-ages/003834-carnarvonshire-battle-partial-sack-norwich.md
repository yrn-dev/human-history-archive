# Carnarvonshire

Carnarvonshire is a region in North Wales that encompasses various geographical features, historical sites, and municipal centers. Its territory includes the city of Bangor and the island of Bardsey.

## Bangor
Bangor, formerly known as Bangor Fawr, serves as a seaport and market-town within the Carnarvon district of Carnarvonshire. Located near the northern entrance of the Menai Straits, it is situated approximately 240 miles northwest of London. As of 1901, the population of the city was 11,269. The city is divided into Upper and Lower Bangor, with the Lower section consisting practically of a single street.

The city is a municipal and contributory parliamentary borough. Its architectural and educational landmarks include the University College of North Wales (established 1883), a female students' hall, and several colleges, including the Normal, N. Wales Training, Baptist, and Independent colleges. Religious buildings in the city include a small Roman Catholic church, other churches, a deanery, and a disused bishop's palace.

The cruciform cathedral of Bangor, featuring a low pinnacled tower, stands on the site of a church founded around 525 by St Deiniol. This original church was destroyed by the English in 1071. The cathedral suffered further damage over the centuries: it was severely handled in 1102 and 1212, destroyed in 1211, and burned during the time of Owen Glendower. Subsequent restorations and additions were made by Bishop Dean during the reign of Henry VII, who rebuilt the choir, and Bishop Skevyngton in 1532, who added the nave and tower. Between 1866 and 1875, the cathedral was restored by Sir G. Scott.

Economically, the region is supported by Lord Penrhyn's slate-quarries at Bethesda, located 6 miles away, which provide the primary export via Port Penrhyn at the mouth of the stream Cegid.

## Bardsey Island
Bardsey, known in Welsh as *Ynys Enlli* ("isle of the current"), is an island located at the northern extremity of Cardigan Bay. While traditionally associated with South Wales, it is included in Carnarvonshire. The island measures approximately 2-1/2 miles in length by 3/4 of a mile in breadth, covering an area of some 370 acres, one-third of which is hilly. The island features high cliffs on its northwest side and a fairly deep harbour on its southeast side. The inhabitants, who are fishermen and farmers, grow oats and barley. The island is home to a locally celebrated 140-foot lighthouse with a fixed light visible for 17 miles.

Historically, Bardsey was a famous sanctuary established by St Dubricius, who died there in 612. It served as a burial place for monks whose friends could afford to transport their bodies there, with all the great abbeys of England sending their quota. A section of the island remains a large cemetery of 3 to 4 acres, characterized by close-set, rough graves with slabs. The ruins of the 13th-century tower of St Mary's abbey are located on the northeast side. There is no Anglican church on the island, as the inhabitants are Dissenters.

Access to the island is facilitated by roads running from the north, east, and south, which feature monks' wells at intervals of 7 to 9 miles. Following the massacre of Bangor-is-coed in 613 by Ethelfride of Northumbria, remaining priests fled to the island via the road of the Rivals (*Yn Eifl*) hill in South Carnarvonshire. On this hill, Pistyll farm continues to provide free food to travellers and pilgrims.

## Land Ownership and Transfers
The administration of Bardsey was historically linked to the farm "Cwrt" (Court), where the abbot held court. Following the battle and partial sack of Norwich by the Puritans during the Civil War, Bardsey was granted to John Wynn of Bodvel, Carnarvonshire. The property later passed to Mary Bodvel and her husband, the earl of Radnor. The earl subsequently sold the property to Dr Wilson of York, who then sold it to Sir John Wynn of Bodfean Hall and Glynllifon, Carnarvonshire.

Additionally, certain rectories in South Carnarvonshire—specifically those of Penmachno, Llangwnadl (Llangwynhoedl) in Lleyn, and Aberdaron (located on the mainland opposite Bardsey)—belong to St John's College, Cambridge.
