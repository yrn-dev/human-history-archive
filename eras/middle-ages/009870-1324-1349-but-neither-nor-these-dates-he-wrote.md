# Events and Figures of 1324 and 1349

The years 1324 and 1349 mark specific points of reference for various historical figures, legal records, and cultural developments across Europe and the Middle East.

## Literary and Clerical Activity in Bern
Ulrich Boner (also known as Bonerius), a German-Swiss writer of fables born in Bern, is documented in records specifically between 1324 and 1349, with no mentions appearing before or after this window. Descended from an old Bernese family, Boner took clerical orders and became a monk. However, because he later married, it is concluded that he received only the "tonsure," granting him the status of *clerici uxoriati*. This status allowed him to return to secular life after divesting himself of clerical garb.

Around 1349, Boner wrote a collection of one hundred fables titled *Der Edelstein* in Middle High German. This work was based primarily on the *Anonymus* (edited by I. Nevelet in 1610) and the 4th-century works of Avianus. Boner is noted for a clear, simple style and the use of humor to soften the didactic tone of the collection. He dedicated *Der Edelstein* to Johann von Rinkenberg, a poet and Bernese patrician who served as the *Vogt* (advocatus) of Brienz and died around 1350. The work was later printed in Bamberg in 1461 and is claimed by some to be the first book printed in the German language.

## Venetian Records and the Polo Family
In Venice, the legal year commenced on the 1st of March. Documents from the *Casa degli Esposti* (the Foundling Hospital) include records dated to 1324, which corresponded to the 7th of the Indiction. 

One such document, a Minute of a Resolution of the Great Council dated March 11, 1324, bears the signature of "Marcus Paullo." This signature has been scrutinized to determine if it belongs to the traveler Marco Polo. However, the date of 1324, combined with the fact that the name appears in similar entries until the middle of the century, has raised grave doubts regarding its authenticity as the traveler's autograph. It is presumed that this Marcus Paullo is the same individual listed as a voter in the elections of Giovanni Gradenigo and Marino Faliero, the Doges. While his exact relationship to the Polo family is unconfirmed, it is suspected he belonged to the branch of S. Geremia.

## Chivalry and the Order of the Garter
The middle of the 14th century saw the institution of the Order of the Garter during a period of magnificent court life and bright English chivalry. While the exact year of its founding is debated—with estimates ranging from 1344 to 1351—specific records provide a timeline of its constitution. 

Wardrobe accounts from September 1347 to January 1349 (during the 21st and 23rd years of Edward III) note the issue of motto-embroidered garters and habits for St George's Day. Furthermore, treasury accounts from the prince of Wales record a gift of twenty-four garters to the knights of the Society of the Garter in November 1348. Letters patent from August 1348 also relate to the preparation of the royal chapel of Windsor. Evidence suggests the order was fully constituted by the autumn of 1348, though it likely did not exist before the summer of 1346, as several original companions and the prince of Wales were not knighted until the middle of that year.

## Geography and Cartography
During the 14th century, Arabian geographers and travellers provided significant materials for map compilers. Among the foremost figures were Abul Feda (1331–1370) and Ibn Batuta (1325–1356). However, the maps that have survived from this era are described as being crude in the extreme. Specifically, maps produced by Ibn al Wardi in 1349 are characterized as being of a most rudimentary type.
