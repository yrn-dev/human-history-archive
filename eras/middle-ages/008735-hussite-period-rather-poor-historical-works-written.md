# Historical Writings of the Hussite Period

The Hussite period in Bohemia was characterized by incessant theological strife following the death of Huss. Because the anti-Roman or Hussite movement was largely democratic, the national language was typically used for writings of the era rather than Latin. However, the archive's records indicate that the period is rather poor in historical works written in the language of the country.

## Loss of Literature
A significant portion of the controversial literature from this era has perished. This was the result of the systematic destruction of Bohemian writings carried out by the Jesuits after the battle of the White Hill in 1620. The impact of this destruction varied by faction: the writings of the Taborites, who represented the extreme Hussite party, were entirely destroyed. In contrast, some works from the more moderate Hussites, known as the Utraquists or Calixtines, have been preserved. These include the *Lives of the Priests of Tabor* and a book titled *Of the Great Torment of the Holy Church*, both of which were written with a sense of violent hostility toward the Taborite community.

## Notable Writers and Works
Despite the loss of many texts, several significant figures and works from the period remain known:

*   **Peter Chelcicky:** Born in the final years of the 14th century and dying in 1460, Chelcicky was an independent religious writer. His primary ideas centered on a horror of bloodshed and a determination to accept all decrees of worldly authorities unresistingly, even those that were unjust. While he was a strenuous enemy of the Church of Rome, he did not join any of the Hussite parties. His masterpiece is the *Sit viry* (the net of faith). He also produced polemical writings in the form of letters to Bishop Nicolas of Pelhrimov and Archbishop John of Rokycan, as well as a work titled *Postilla*.
*   **Archbishop John of Rokycan:** A Bohemian work by the Archbishop has been preserved titled *Postilla*, which is similar to, though considered inferior to, the work of the same name written by Huss.
*   **Contemporary Chroniclers:** Some chroniclers who were contemporaries and occasionally eye-witnesses to the Hussite wars produced writings that were later collected and published by Frantisek Palacky under the title *Stare ceske letopisy*.

## Later Historical Documentation
The history of the Hussite period and broader Bohemian history has been addressed by various later scholars and collections. The *Fontes Rerum Bohemicarum*, published in Prague during the latter part of the 19th century, is a valuable collection of historical documents that superseded earlier works by De Florgy, Pelzl, and Marquard Freher (Freherus). 

Frantisek Palacky's *Geschichte von Bohmen* (published 1844-1867) superseded earlier historical works and covers the history of the region up to the year 1526. Other scholars who have dealt with Bohemian history include Professor Adolf Bachmann, whose *Geschichte Bohmens* (published in 1899 and 1905) is noted for having a strongly marked German tendency. Additionally, Professor Ernest Denis provided a continuous account of Bohemian history starting from the beginning of the 15th century in works such as *Jean Hus, et la guerre des Hussites* (1878) and *Fin de l'independance boheme* (1890).
