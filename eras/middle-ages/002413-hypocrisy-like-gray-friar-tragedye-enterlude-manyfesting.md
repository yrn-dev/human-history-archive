# Hypocrisy like a gray friar

The phrase "Hypocrisy like a gray friar" appears as a specific direction for the dressing of parts in the morality play *The Thre Laws of Nature, Moses and Christ, corrupted by the Sodomytes, Pharisees and Papystes most wicked*. This play was printed in 1538 and again in 1562.

## Dramatic Context and Authorship
The play was written by a figure described as "foul-mouthed Bale," who is noted for his scurrility and vehemence in attacking his enemies. His literary contributions are marked by a strong bias against Roman Catholic writers, which affects the critical value of his work. In addition to the morality play mentioned above, Bale wrote several other works in 1538, including *A Tragedye; or enterlude manyfesting the chief promyses of God unto Man...*, *The Temptacyon of our Lorde*, and *A brefe Comedy or Enterlude of Johan Baptystes preachynge in the Wyldernesse, &c.*

Bale is considered a figure of dramatic importance primarily for his work *Kynge Johan* (c. 1548). This play serves as a polemic against the Roman Catholic Church, depicting King John as a champion of English rites against the Roman see. *Kynge Johan* is viewed as a transitional work between the old morality play and English historical drama, though it does not appear to have directly influenced the creators of the chronicle histories, such as the authors of the 1591 *Troublesome Raigne of King John*.

## Allegorical Costuming
In *The Thre Laws of Nature*, the dressing of the characters is intended to reflect specific political and religious aims. The directions for the costumes are as follows:
*   **Hypocrisy**: To be dressed like a gray friar.
*   **Idolatry**: To be decked like an old witch.
*   **Sodomy**: To be dressed like a monk of all sects.
*   **Ambition**: To be dressed like a bishop.
*   **Covetousness**: To be dressed like a Pharisee or spiritual lawyer.
*   **False Doctrine**: To be dressed like a popish doctor.

## Historical Context of Friars
The "friars" referenced in the dramatic costumes were part of various religious orders. The Dominicans, for example, were known as the Friars Preachers (and as Black Friars in England due to their black mantles worn over white habits). Founded by St. Dominic in 1215 in Toulouse, the order adopted the Rule of St. Augustine. By 1221, friaries had been established across Europe, including in France, Italy, Spain, Germany, Poland, and England.

Other figures associated with the friars in the archive include Miles Coverdale, an Augustinian friar who prepared a complete English Bible by October 1535 and later served as the bishop of Exeter from 1551 to 1553. Additionally, historical accounts mention Friar Salimbene, who traveled from Lyons to Auxerre to drink a famous wine, and Friar Odoric, who wrote about Chinese rice-wine.
