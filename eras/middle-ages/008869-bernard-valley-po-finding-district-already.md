# The Boii

The Boii were a Celtic people whose original homeland was Gallia Transalpina. They were known to the Romans as early as the time of Plautus, who made a contemptuous reference to them in the *Captivi*. In their early history, the Boii split into two primary groups: one migrated into Germany, while the other entered Italy. Some members of the people remained in their original territory; for example, during the Second Punic War, a Boian prince named Magalus offered to guide Hannibal into Italy after the Carthaginian leader had crossed the Pyrenees.

## Migration and Settlement in Italy
The group of Boii that immigrated to Italy is said to have crossed the Pennine Alps via the Great St Bernard pass and entered the valley of the Po. Upon discovering that the district was already occupied, they crossed the river and drove out the Umbrians and Etruscans. They established their settlements as far as the Apennines in the region of modern Romagna. According to Cato, as cited in Pliny's *Nat. Hist.*, the Boii consisted of as many as 112 different tribes. Evidence from tombs at La Tene, Hallstatt, and other sites suggests they were fairly civilized.

## Conflict with Rome
The Boii engaged in several wars with the Romans. They suffered a defeat at the Vadimonian lake in 283, where they were defeated alongside the Etruscans. Later, in 224, they were forced to submit following the battle of Telamon in Etruria. Despite this submission, the Boii maintained a hatred for Rome. This animosity was further irritated by the Roman foundation of the colonies of Placentia and Cremona. Consequently, during the Second Punic War in 218, the Boii provided valuable assistance to Hannibal.

The struggle against Rome continued until 191, when P. Cornelius Scipio Nasica finally subdued them. As a result of this defeat, the Boii were deprived of nearly half of their territory.

## Later Migrations and Distribution
Following their defeat, Strabo records that the Boii were driven back across the Alps to settle on the Danube on the land of the Taurisci, their kinsmen, in an area adjoining Raetia and Vindelicia. However, most authorities believe a settlement of the Boii had existed on the Danube from a very early period in part of modern Bohemia (historically known as *Boiohemum*, meaning "land of the Boii").

Further migrations occurred around 60 B.C., when some Boii moved to Pannonia and Noricum. During this period, 32,000 Boii joined a Helvetian expedition into Gaul, where they shared in a defeat near Bibracte.
