# Bavaria and Related Historical Conflicts

The history of Bavaria is characterized by its involvement in imperial elections, territorial disputes, and various European military campaigns spanning several centuries.

## Early Duchy and Territorial Disputes
In the 10th century, the East Mark was a district approximately 60 miles in breadth on the eastern frontier of Bavaria. In 976, Leopold, a count in the Donnegau, was described as the margrave of this mark, likely as a reward for his fidelity to Emperor Otto II during the Bavarian rising of that same year. This region eventually grew into the duchy of Austria.

By the early 14th century, the region saw internal conflict between the sons of Louis II, duke of Upper Bavaria and count palatine of the Rhine. Louis IV (c. 1287–1347), surnamed the Bavarian, inherited Upper Bavaria and the Palatinate jointly with his elder brother Rudolph. Following a dispute over their joint possessions, the lands were divided in 1310, with Louis receiving the north-western part of the duchy. In 1310, Louis also undertook the guardianship of the two young sons of Stephen I, duke of Lower Bavaria, which sparked a war lasting until June 1313. During this period, nobles in Lower Bavaria sought aid from Frederick I (the Fair), duke of Austria; however, Louis defeated him at Gammelsdorf on November 9, 1313.

## The Bohemian Conflicts and the Duke of Bavaria
Bavaria played a significant role in the reconquest of Bohemia in the early 17th century. In 1619, the Bohemian estates deposed Ferdinand and elected Frederick, elector palatine, as their king. In 1620, the powerful duke Maximilian of Bavaria joined forces with Ferdinand, who had become emperor. A large imperialist army under the command of the duke of Bavaria, along with Bouquoi and Tilly, entered Bohemia in September 1620. Following several defeats of the Bohemians, the imperial forces attacked the Bohemian army on November 8, 1620, at the plateau of "Bila Hora" (White Hill). The Bohemians were defeated in a few hours, leading King Frederick to flee Prague the following morning.

## The War of the Austrian Succession
In the mid-18th century, the elector of Bavaria was involved in a campaign against the Austrians. French forces joined the elector's forces on the Danube to advance on Vienna, though the objective later shifted toward Prague. The elector marched on Budweis while the French stormed Prague on November 26, 1741. On December 19, 1741, the elector of Bavaria, styling himself archduke of Austria, was crowned king of Bohemia, and on January 24, 1742, he was elected imperial throne as Charles VII.

Despite these titles, the Austrians launched an offensive winter campaign. Field Marshal Khevenhueller advanced on December 27, driving back the allies and pressing into Bavaria. Munich surrendered to the Austrians on the same day Charles VII was crowned. Following the death of Charles VII, the imperial title was subject to a new election. In 1745, the Bavarian army was driven from its winter quarters, and the young elector was forced to abandon Munich again. The peace of Fuessen, signed on April 22, 1745, allowed the elector to secure his hereditary states on the condition that he support the candidature of the grand-duke Francis, consort of Maria Theresa.

## Later Developments and Military History
In the 19th century, the region saw urban development under the Bavarian monarchy. In 1843, King Louis I of Bavaria founded the town of Ludwigshafen in the Bavarian Palatinate on the left bank of the Rhine.

In more recent military history, during the Great War, the German army's strategic plan involved the Crown Prince of Bavaria. While the German right was led by Kluck, the detached German left wing, under the Crown Prince of Bavaria, threatened Nancy. The German plan intended for Prince Rupprecht of Bavaria to turn the right of the French line.
