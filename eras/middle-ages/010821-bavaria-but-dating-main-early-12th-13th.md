# Bavaria in the 12th and 13th Centuries

During the 12th and 13th centuries, the kingdom of Bavaria saw the development of several key urban centers, the expansion of ecclesiastical architecture, and shifts in ducal administration.

## Urban Development and Administration
The town of Donauworth grew during the 11th and 12th centuries, developing under the protection provided by the castle of Mangoldstein. By the 13th century, the town had become a seat for the duke of Upper Bavaria. However, the duke eventually withdrew from Donauworth to Munich. This relocation was prompted by a desire to escape the *manes* of his wife, Maria of Brabant, whom he had beheaded following an unfounded suspicion of infidelity.

## Ecclesiastical Architecture and Monuments
Several major religious structures in the region underwent significant construction or rebuilding during this period:

*   **Aschaffenburg:** The *Stiftskirche*, or cathedral, was originally founded in 980 by Otto of Bavaria. While its origins are earlier, the building dates in the main from the 13th and early 12th centuries.
*   **Bamberg:** The cathedral in Bamberg, a late Romanesque building founded in 1004 by Emperor Henry II and finished in 1012, was partially burnt and subsequently rebuilt in the 13th century. Additionally, the Michaelskirche, located on the Michaelsberg, is a 12th-century Romanesque structure that previously served as the church for a Benedictine monastery.
