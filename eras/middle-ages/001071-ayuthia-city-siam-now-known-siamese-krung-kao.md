# AYUTHIA

Ayuthia is a city in Siam, referred to by the Siamese as *Krung Kao*, meaning "the Old Capital." It is located at 100deg 32' E., 14deg 21' N., and has a population of approximately 10,000. The city is situated on a large island surrounded by the river Me Nam, which is broken into a network of creeks.

## Geography and Urban Layout
The inhabitants of Ayuthia primarily reside in floating houses, a style characteristic of lower Siam. These houses are moored to the edges of the creeks, which serve as the city's thoroughfares. Ayuthia is positioned 42 miles from Bangkok and is located on the northern main line of the state railways.

## History and Architecture
Founded in the 14th century, Ayuthia served as the capital of Siam for more than four centuries. It is regarded as the most historically interesting spot in Siam. The city contains innumerable ruins, including fortifications, churches, pagodas, and palaces. These remains are noted for their archaeological interest and are deeply marked by Hindu influence. The former glories of the city were recorded by early European travellers, who established the foundations for current trade and foreign intercourse.

The city's history was marked by conflict with the Burmese, who destroyed the town twice—first in 1555 and again in 1767. Following the second destruction, Ayuthia ceased to be the capital of the country.

## Administration and Economy
Ayuthia serves as the headquarters of a high commissioner and is the chief town of one of the richest agricultural provincial divisions of Siam. Its government is that of an ordinary provincial division, known as a *Monton*. The government offices are housed in spacious buildings that previously served as a royal summer retreat.

The economy of the region is centered on agriculture. Great quantities of paddi are transported annually to Bangkok via rail and river. In exchange, the agriculturist peasantry imports cloth and other goods to meet their needs; there is no other trade in the area.

## Local Landmarks and Culture
The city maintains a local museum that houses a large collection of relics. Outside the town, there is an ancient masonry enclosure used periodically for the capture of elephants.
