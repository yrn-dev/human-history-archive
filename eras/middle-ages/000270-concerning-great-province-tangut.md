# CONCERNING THE GREAT PROVINCE OF TANGUT

The Province of Tangut was a significant territory belonging to the Great Kaan, comprising several kingdoms and provinces. It was known to the Chinese as *Si Hia* (Western Hia) or *Ho si* (West of the Yellow River), and the natives were referred to by the Chinese as *T’ang-hiang* and by the Mongols as *T’angu* or *T’ang-wu*.

## History and Governance
The kingdom of Tangut was declared independent in 982 by Li Chi Ch’ien, who held the dynastic title of Tai Tsu. The rulers of Tangut were scions of the Toba race, who had previously reigned over North China as the Wei Dynasty from A.D. 386 to 557. Claiming descent from the ancient Chinese Hsia Dynasty of the second millennium B.C., they adopted the title of *Ta Hsia* ("Great Hsia").

The real founder of the dynasty is identified as Li Yuan-hao, who declared independence in 1032 after conquering the cities of Suhchau and Kanchau from the Uighúr Turks in 1031. In 1036, Li Yuan-hao adopted a special script. The capital of the dynasty was Hia chau (now Ning hia), located on the Yellow River.

The province faced multiple invasions by Chinghiz. Some sources list five invasions (1205, 1207, 1209/1210–1211, 1218, and 1226–1227), while others specify three (1206, 1217, and 1225). During the final struggle in 1226, the cities of Kanchau, Liangchau, and Suhchau fell to the Mongols. Following the death of Chinghiz in 1227, the last ruler of Tangut, Li H’ien, surrendered to Okkodaï and was killed.

## Geography and Territories
In the middle of the 11th century, the dominions of Tangut were bounded by the Tartars (Tata) to the north, the Liao (Khitan) to the north-east, the Sung Empire to the south and east, the Tibetans to the south-west, and the Uighúr Turks (Hui-hu) to the west. The Alashan Mountains formed the northern frontier.

The province included several key regions and cities:
*   **Campichu:** A great and noble city that served as the capital and place of government for the entire province of Tangut.
*   **Erguiul:** A kingdom belonging to the Great Kaan.
*   **Egrigaia:** A province containing numerous cities and villages, with its capital at Calachan.
*   **Sukchur:** A province containing numerous towns and villages, the chief of which was Sukchu.
*   **Yetsina:** Identified as one of the seven *Lu* or Circuits of the Province of Kansuh (or Tangut).

Additionally, Tangut nomads dwell around the shores of Koko-nor (known to the Tanguts as *Tso-ngombo*), a salt lake in Central Asia situated in the extreme north-east of Tibet.

## Population and Culture
The population of Tangut was religiously diverse. In the kingdom of Erguiul, the people consisted of Idolaters, Nestorian Christians, and worshippers of Mahommet. Similarly, the people of Campichu were Saracens, Christians, and Idolaters. In Campichu, Christians maintained three fine churches, while Idolaters possessed many abbeys and minsters. These Idolaters worshipped numerous polished idols made of stone, clay, or wood, some reaching ten paces in stature and covered in gold.

In the province of Egrigaia, the population was chiefly Idolater, though Nestorian Christians also maintained fine churches. The people of Sukchur were divided between Christians and Idolaters.

## Economy and Trade
The city of Calachan in Egrigaia was renowned for the production of camlets made from camel's wool, which were considered the finest in the world. These camlets were produced in various colors, including white, derived from white camels. These textiles were purchased by merchants and distributed for sale throughout the world.
