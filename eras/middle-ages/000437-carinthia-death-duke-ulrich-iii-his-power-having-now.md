# Carinthia and the Succession of Duke Ulrich III

## Transition to Ottakar of Bohemia
In 1269, the duchy of Carinthia was inherited by Ottakar, the king of Bohemia, following the death of Duke Ulrich III. This acquisition significantly expanded Ottakar's power, leading him to aspire to the German throne. During his tenure, Ottakar worked to improve the conditions of his duchies by restoring order, benefiting the inhabitants of towns, and introducing German colonists into the eastern districts.

## Conflict with Rudolph of Habsburg
The growing power of Ottakar eventually menaced the German king, Rudolph, count of Habsburg, who ascended to the throne in 1273. King Rudolph questioned Ottakar's title to three duchies, including Carinthia, and sought to recover imperial lands previously held by Emperor Frederick II. After being summoned twice before the diet and facing a declaration from the imperial court against him, Ottakar was placed under the ban in July 1275.

The resulting war led to Ottakar's submission in November 1276, at which time he renounced his claims to the duchies of Carinthia, Styria, and Austria.

## Habsburg Administration and Meinhard IV
Following Ottakar's renunciation, King Rudolph administered the three duchies in his capacity as head of the Empire. Rudolph intended to transform these lands into hereditary possessions for his family and move the Habsburg headquarters from the Rhine to the Danube. On December 27, 1282, Rudolph invested his sons, Albert and Rudolph, with the duchies of Styria and Austria.

Rudolph retained the duchy of Carinthia personally until 1286. In that year, he bestowed Carinthia upon Meinhard IV, count of Tirol, as a reward for valuable services.

## Later Successions and the House of Austria
The succession of Carinthia continued through various familial shifts in the following century. Henry, the duke of Carinthia and count of Tirol, was the father of Margaret Maultasch. Upon Henry's death in 1335, the duchy of Carinthia passed to Albert II, duke of Austria, while Margaret and her husband, John Henry, inherited Tirol.

By the late 14th century, the administration of Carinthia was linked with Styria and Tirol. In 1379, Leopold III and Albert III divided their lands; Albert retained Carniola and Austria proper, while Leopold received Tirol, Carinthia, and Styria. Following Leopold's death at the battle of Sempach in 1386, Albert served as guardian for his four nephews.

In the early 15th century, the joint rulers of these lands—William, Leopold, Ernest, and Frederick (the sons of Leopold III)—saw the early deaths of William and Leopold. In 1406, the remaining brothers divided the territories, with Ernest becoming the duke of Carinthia and Styria, while Frederick became the count of Tirol. Ernest was succeeded in 1424 by his sons, Frederick and Albert.
