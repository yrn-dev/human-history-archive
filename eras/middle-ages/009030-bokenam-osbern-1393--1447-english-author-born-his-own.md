# BOKENAM, OSBERN

Osbern Bokenam (1393?–1447?) was an English author who, according to his own account, was born on October 6, 1393. There are differing accounts regarding his origins; Dr. Horstmann suggests he may have been a native of Bokeham (currently Bookham) in Surrey, deriving his name from that location. However, a concluding note in his *Lives of the Saints* describes him as "a Suffolke man, frere Austyn of Stoke Clare." Bokenam wrote his works plainly, utilizing the Suffolk speech, and justified his lack of literary decoration by stating that the finest flowers had already been plucked by Lydgate, Gower, and Chaucer.

## Literary Works and Influences
Bokenam was a follower of Lydgate and Chaucer, and he likely had Chaucer's *Legend of Good Women* in mind while composing his work. He wrote a series of thirteen legends focusing on women and holy maidens. These poems were primarily composed in stanzas of seven and eight lines, with nine of the legends featuring prologues.

His primary source for these legends was the *Legenda Aurea* written by Jacobus de Voragine, the archbishop of Genoa, whom Bokenam refers to as "Januence." While the *Legenda Aurea* was his chief source, it was not his only one.

## Dedications and Manuscripts
Bokenam dedicated several of his works to pious ladies who wished to read the history of their name-saints. The first of his legends, *Vita Scae Margaretae, virginis et martiris*, was written for Thomas Burgh, a monk from Cambridge and a friend of the author.

A unique copy of Bokenam's work is preserved in the British Museum as Arundel MS. 327. According to the concluding note in this manuscript, the work was finished in 1447. The scribe, Thomas Burgh, presented the volume to an unnamed convent so that the nuns might remember him and his sister, Dame Betrice Burgh.

## Additional Writings and Editions
In addition to the legends, Bokenam's work includes a dialogue written in both English and Latin. This dialogue, which appears in Dugdale's *Monasticon Anglicanum*, features a "Frere answerynge" and a "Secular asking" at the grave of Dame Johan of Acres. The text details the lineal descent of the lords of the honour of Clare from 1248 to 1356.

Bokenam's poems have been edited in two notable editions:
* *Lyvys of Seyntys* ..., edited in 1835 for the Roxburghe Club.
* *Osbern Bokenams Legenden*, edited by Dr. Carl Horstmann in 1883 (published in E. Kolbing's *Altengl. Bibliothek*, vol. i).

## Personal Life and Travels
Bokenam traveled to Italy on at least two separate occasions. Additionally, in 1445, he traveled as a pilgrim to Santiago de Compostela.
