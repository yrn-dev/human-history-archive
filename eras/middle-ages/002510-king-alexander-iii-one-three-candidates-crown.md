## Alexander III of Scotland
King Alexander III of Scotland reigned during the 13th century. In 1284, the estates of Scotland determined that should Alexander die without children, the crown would pass to his granddaughter. Alexander was killed in March 1286.

Following his death, his granddaughter, Margaret—known as the "maid of Norway"—became queen. Margaret was the daughter of Alexander III and his daughter, also named Margaret. The elder Margaret died shortly after the birth of the titular queen. Margaret, the "maid of Norway," died in the Orkneys around the end of September 1290, leaving the Scottish throne vacant.

## The Succession Crisis of 1290
The death of Margaret led to a vacancy of the Scottish throne, resulting in a dispute among candidates for the crown. John de Baliol was one of three candidates whose pretensions to the throne were seriously considered. 

Baliol based his claim on his maternal grandmother, Margaret, who was the eldest daughter of David, earl of Huntingdon (a grandson of King David I). His primary rival was Robert Bruce, earl of Annandale. The conflict was characterized as a dispute between the nearest of kin (Bruce) and the eldest by descent (Baliol).

The dispute was settled by King Edward I of England, who was invited to intervene. After the Scottish nobles and the competitors, including Baliol, acknowledged Edward's position as overlord of Scotland, a court consisting of twenty-four Englishmen and eighty Scotsmen was appointed. Based on the principles of feudal law for an indivisible inheritance, the court decided in favor of Baliol. He was crowned King of Scotland at Scone on November 30, 1292.
