# Basil

## Reign and Power
Basil's reign is characterized as the highest point of power for the Eastern empire since the time of Justinian I. While some credit for this status is attributed to his predecessors, Tzimisces and Nicephorus, the greater part of the credit is assigned to Basil himself. He was a ruler who dedicated himself unsparingly to the laborious duties of governing.

## Character and Public Image
Basil is described as having been hard and cruel, lacking refinement or an interest in culture. Throughout his rule, he had to contend with the ill-will of a section of his subjects who were both powerful and rich. A contemporary portrait of Basil, found in a psalter preserved in the library of St Mark at Venice, depicts him with a grey beard, wearing a crown and imperial robes.

## Historical Sources
The life and reign of Basil are documented through several primary and secondary sources. These include:
*   **Biographical and Chronological Works:** The *Vita Basilii*, written by his grandson Constantine VII (found in bk. v. of the *Continuation of Theophanes*), and the works of Genesius and Leo Diaconus.
*   **Chronicles and Histories:** The *Chronicle* of George Cedrenus (transcribed from John Scylitzes), the writings of Psellus, and the works of Zonaras.
*   **Contemporary and Regional Accounts:** Yahyā of Antioch, who wrote a contemporary Asiatic chronicle, and Al Mekin (Elmacinus) in the *Historia Saracenica*.
*   **Other Records:** The *Strategikon* of Cecaumenus and the "Laws (*Novellae*) of Basil."
*   **Arabic Sources:** Tabari is noted as the most important among the Arabic sources.
*   **Modern Analysis:** The history of his reign has been analyzed in works by Gibbon (*Decline and Fall*), Finlay (*History of Greece*), and G. Schlumberger (*L'Epopee byzantine*).

## Other Figures Named Basil
The name Basil (translated as Vasily in Russian) was also held by four tsars of Muscovy and grand-dukes of Moscow.
