# The Duties of the Duke of Austria

## Establishment of the Duchy
The foundation of Austria is attributed to Otto the Great, who was elected German king in 936. Following a significant victory over the Magyars on the Lechfeld in August 955, Otto freed Bavaria from their presence and refounded the East Mark to defend his kingdom. In 976, the emperor Otto II entrusted the government of this mark to Leopold of the Babenberg family. Under the administration of Leopold and his descendants, the mark was managed with success and vigour, eventually becoming an important German state.

The Babenberg rulers focused on expanding the eastern frontier down the Danube valley through the colonization of lands on both sides of the river. This expansion was reflected in the movement of the capital, which shifted from Poechlarn to Melk, then to Tulln, and finally to Vienna around 1140. Further territorial additions included the incorporation of the country as far as the Leitha and, in 1156, the addition of the district between the Inn and the Enns.

## Rank and Privileges
The year 1156 serves as a pivotal date in Austrian history. In an effort to restore peace to Germany, King Frederick I raised Austria to the rank of a duchy and granted it exceptional privileges. These privileges extended to the investiture of Duke Henry and his second wife, Theodora. The terms of the duchy's succession were specifically outlined: in the event that there were no male heirs, the duchy would descend to females. Furthermore, if a duke had no children, he was permitted to nominate his own successor.

## Jurisdiction and Imperial Duties
The duke held comprehensive authority within his territory, controlling all the jurisdiction of the land. Because of this extensive local control, the duke's obligations toward the Empire were limited to two specific duties:
* He was required to appear at any diet that was held in Bavaria.
* He was required to send a contingent to the imperial army for any military campaigns taking place in the countries that bordered upon Austria.

## Territorial Expansion under the Babenbergs
Following the establishment of the duchy, the Babenberg dukes continued to expand their holdings. In 1186, Duke Leopold I entered into a treaty with Ottakar IV, the duke of Styria. This arrangement resulted in the acquisition of upper Austria and Styria by the Babenbergs in 1192. Later, in 1229, Duke Leopold II purchased lands from the bishop of Freising, which allowed him to take the title of lord of Carniola. The house of Babenberg continued to rule until the family became extinct in 1246.
