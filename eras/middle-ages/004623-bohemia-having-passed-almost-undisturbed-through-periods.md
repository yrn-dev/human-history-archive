# Bohemia

Bohemia is a region characterized by a distinct geography, a complex history of dynastic rule, and a significant cultural revival of its language and literature.

## Geography and Topography
The interior of Bohemia is described as an undulating plateau, generally over 1,000 feet high, consisting of a series of terraces that slope from south to north. The region is divided into highland (south of a line passing through Hohenmauth, Prague, and Komotau) and lowland (north of that line). The lowest point in Bohemia is located where the Elbe escapes into Saxony, at an altitude of 367 feet.

The region is bordered by several mountain ranges:
*   **North-west:** The Erzgebirge (Czech *Rudo Hori*), with an average altitude of 2,600 feet and a highest point at Keilberg (4,080 feet). The village of Gottesgab, at 3,345 feet, is the highest place in both Bohemia and central Germany.
*   **North-east:** The Sudetes, which include the Lausitzergebirge, the Isergebirge (peaking at Tafelfichte, 3,683 feet), the Jeschkengebirge, and the Riesengebirge (Czech *Kroknose*). The Schneekoppe in the Riesengebirge reaches 5,264 feet, making it among the highest mountains in central Europe after the Alps.
*   **South/South-east:** The Bohemian-Moravian Hills, a plateau of primitive rocks reaching 2,500 feet.
*   **Interior:** Notable ranges include the Brdywald, Tepler Gebirge, Karsbader Gebirge, Kaiserwald, and the volcanic massif of the Mittelgebirge.

## Dynastic Rule and Early History
The Premyslide dynasty's rule over Bohemia ended in 1306 with the murder of Wenceslas III. Prior to this, Wenceslas II had governed during a period of great happiness for the country after a time of anarchy following the unpopular rule of Otto of Brandenburg. Wenceslas II had also seen Poland accept his rule and was offered the Hungarian crown before his sudden death in 1305.

In 1346, Charles was elected sovereign by the German electors and succeeded as king of Bohemia. He is remembered as one of the region's greatest rulers. Charles significantly expanded Prague, his favorite residence, by founding the *nove mesto* (new town), which tripled the city's size and population. He also established the university of Prague and attempted to codify the country's contradictory laws, though this was blocked by the nobility. During his reign, the first symptoms of a church reform movement appeared; while accused of subservience to Rome, Charles protected several priests who favored these reforms.

## Territorial Shifts and Conflict
Bohemia's borders and possessions shifted frequently. Upper Lusatia was connected to Bohemia at various times, granted to King Ladislas around 1160, and restored in 1319 and 1329. It remained under Bohemian authority until the Treaty of Prague in 1635, which transferred it to Saxony.

The city of Breslau was purchased by John, king of Bohemia, in 1335 and remained under Bohemian successors until approximately 1460. It later passed to the Habsburgs in 1526 when Ferdinand was chosen king of Bohemia.

The Thirty Years' War began in Prague, and Bohemia served as a theater of hostilities for much of the conflict. The region was occupied by Saxons in 1631 and pillaged by Swedish troops, culminating in the Swedish general Konigsmark taking part of Prague in 1648. Earlier, in 1620, the battle of Weisser Berg near Prague resulted in the failure of the elector palatine's hopes.

## Habsburg Rule and Political Changes
In 1720, the estates of Bohemia recognized the Pragmatic Sanction, sanctioning female succession to the throne to ensure the indivisibility of the Habsburg realm. However, upon the death of Emperor Charles VI, Charles, elector of Bavaria, invaded Bohemia and occupied Prague in 1741. Maria Theresa eventually recovered the territory.

Under Maria Theresa and her son Joseph II, the autonomy of Bohemia was further limited. A 1749 decree abolished separate Bohemian law-courts, and an Austro-Bohemian chancellor was later appointed to control the administration of Bohemia and the German domains of the house of Habsburg.

## Language and Literary Revival
The revival of Bohemian language and literature is attributed primarily to four figures:
*   **Joseph Jungmann (1773-1847):** He produced Bohemian translations of English and German writers and authored a monumental German and Bohemian dictionary and a history of Bohemian literature.
*   **John Kolar (1793-1852):** The greatest poet of the revival, known for *Slavy dcera* (daughter of Slavia), a personification of the Slavic race.
*   **Paul Joseph Safarik (1795-1861):** Author of *Starozitnosti Slovanske*, which recorded the history and literature of the early Slavs.
*   **Francis Palacky (1798-1876):** Regarded as the greatest Bohemian historian, specifically for his history of Bohemia from the earliest period to 1526.
