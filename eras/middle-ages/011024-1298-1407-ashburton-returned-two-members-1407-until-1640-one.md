# Ashburton

Ashburton, also known as Essebretona, Asperton, or Ashperton, is an ancient stannary town and a borough by prescription. Historically, the town was governed by a bailiff and a portreeve, both of whom were elected on an annual basis at the court leet held by the lord of the manor.

## Land Ownership and Governance
According to Domesday records, Ashburton was held in chief by Osbern, the bishop of Exeter, and rendered geld for six hides. In 1552, the bishop sold the two manors of Ashburton Foreign and Ashburton Borough, at which point the land became crown property. Eventually, the manors were acquired in moieties by the Clinton family. The joint lords of the manor are currently Sir Robert Jardine and the present Lord Clinton.

## Parliamentary Representation
The town's representation in parliament shifted several times over the centuries. In 1298 and 1407, Ashburton returned two members. From 1407 until 1640, the town returned only one member. Following 1640, it again returned two members. This representation continued until the Reform Act of 1832, which deprived the town of one member, and the Reform Act of 1885, which deprived it of the second.

## Trade and Markets
The commercial development of Ashburton was marked by the granting of various markets and fairs:
* **Reign of Edward II:** Bishop Stapledon obtained a Saturday market and two annual fairs. These fairs lasted three days each and were held on the feasts of St Martin in winter (November 11) and St Laurence (August 10).
* **1672:** John Ford was granted a Tuesday market specifically for the sale of woollen goods and wool made from English yarn.
* **1705:** Andrew Quicke obtained two annual fairs for the sale of merchandise, corn, and cattle, held on the first Thursdays of June and March.

## Notable Figures
John Dunning, 1st Baron Ashburton (1731–1783), was born in Ashburton on October 18, 1731. The son of an attorney, John Dunning of Ashburton, he was educated at the local free grammar school. Dunning became a prominent English lawyer and politician, serving as solicitor-general from December 1767 until May 1770. He gained significant reputation for his legal work, including a defense of the British East India Company in 1762 and his counsel for Wilkes in 1763. In parliament, he represented the borough of Calne from 1768 until his promotion to the peerage. He was known for opposing pensions and sinecure officers, though he himself was appointed chancellor of the duchy of Lancaster—a rich sinecure—in 1782 under the prime ministership of the marquis of Rockingham.
