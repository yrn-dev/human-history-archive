# Boniface VIII

## Early Life and Career
Boniface VIII, born Benedetto Gaetano, was born into a noble family in Anagni. He pursued studies in civil and canon law, with education taking place in Italy and potentially in Paris. His early ecclesiastical career included appointments to canonicates in France and at Todi in June 1260. He subsequently served as a notary and an advocate at the papal court.

In April 1267, while serving alongside Cardinal Ottoboni to assist King Henry III of England against the bishops of the baronial party, he was besieged in the Tower of London by the rebellious earl of Gloucester. He was rescued from this siege on April 27, 1267, by the future Edward I. His rise within the church continued with his creation as a cardinal deacon in 1281 and as a cardinal priest (SS. Sylvestri et Martini) in 1291. During this time, he became highly influential within the Sacred College and was entrusted with numerous diplomatic missions.

## Election and Papacy
Boniface VIII was chosen as pope at Naples on December 24, 1294, following his assistance in the abdication of the ineffective Celestine V. Unlike typical elections, his was not unanimous, a result likely caused by the hostility of certain French cardinals. He was crowned in Rome in January 1295 with great pomp.

His vision for the papacy differed sharply from that of his predecessor; while Celestine V had ruled with humility and extreme monastic poverty, Boniface VIII ardently asserted the lordship of the papacy over all the world's kingdoms. Although he initially planned to pacify the West and recover the Holy Land from the infidel, his nine-year reign was characterized by controversies with leading European powers rather than peacemaking.

## Political Conflicts and Foreign Relations
Boniface VIII's reign was marked by arrogance, lofty claims, and avarice, which earned him many enemies. His political efforts included:

*   **The House of Anjou:** He supported the interests of the house of Anjou in Sicily, a policy that ended in grand failure. He also declared that the crown of Hungary should fall to the house of Anjou, assuming he was the overlord of that nation.
*   **German and Danish Relations:** He humbled Eric VI of Denmark. However, until 1303, he refused to recognize Albert of Austria as the rightful king of Germany.
*   **Conflict with England:** He attempted to try Edward I, the conqueror of Scotland, on charges of interfering with a papal fief. This effort was unsuccessful, as the parliament declared in 1301 that Scotland had never been a fief of Rome.
*   **France:** His most noted conflict was with Philip IV of France.
*   **Internal Roman Conflict:** He attempted to build great estates for his own family, which made most of the Colonna his enemies.
