# Ashby Castle

## Overview and Location
Ashby Castle is located to the south of Ashby-de-la-Zouch, a market town in the Bosworth parliamentary division of Leicestershire, England. The site is characterized by extensive remains of the former fortification.

## History and Construction
The land associated with Ashby-de-la-Zouch was part of the estates of Hugh de Grentmaisnel during the Domesday survey. It was subsequently held by Robert Beaumeis and later passed through female descent to the la Zouch family, from whom the town derived the adjunct to its name; prior to this, the location was known as Essebi or Ashby.

In the 15th century, the manor was held by James Butler, the earl of Ormond. Following Butler's attainder, the manor was granted to Lord Hastings in 1461. In 1474, Lord Hastings obtained a royal licence to fortify and build a castle, as well as to empark 3,000 acres of land.

## Notable Occupants and Events
The castle served as a place of detention in 1569 for Mary, queen of Scots, who was kept there under the custody of the earls of Shrewsbury and Huntingdon. 

During the English Civil War, the castle was held and fortified for the king by Colonel Henry Hastings. King Charles visited the site in 1645. Following the conclusion of the war in 1648, the castle was dismantled by order of parliament. In addition to its historical role, the castle is featured prominently in the work *Ivanhoe* by Sir Walter Scott.

## Surrounding District
The town of Ashby-de-la-Zouch is situated 118 miles north-west by north from London and is accessible via the Leicester-Burton branch of the Midland railway. The urban district had a population of 4,726 in 1901. 

The neighboring district is characterized by extensive coal-mines, including those at Moira. From Moira, the Ashby-de-la-Zouch canal runs south to connect with the Coventry canal. Other local features include the Ivanhoe baths (erected in 1826), which utilize saline waters containing bromine for the treatment of rheumatic and scrofulous complaints, and a Queen Eleanor cross created by Sir Gilbert Scott to commemorate the countess of Loudoun.
