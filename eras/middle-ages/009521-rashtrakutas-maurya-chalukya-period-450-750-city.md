# Rashtrakutas

The Rashtrakutas were a dynasty that played a significant role in the political landscape of western and southern India, emerging as a dominant power following a prolonged struggle for mastery in the southern Deccan.

## Rise to Power and Conflict with the Chalukyas
In the southern Deccan, the Rashtrakutas and the Chalukyas engaged in a struggle for regional control. During the 7th century, the Chalukyas had successfully overcome the Rashtrakutas and other rivals, absorbing smaller kingdoms into their empire. However, the political tide shifted after the Chalukyas struggled with the Pallavas—a conflict highlighted by the capture of the Pallava capital by Vikramaditya II around 740.

Following these events, the Chalukyas succumbed to their ancient rivals, the Rashtrakutas. The Rashtrakutas succeeded to the bulk of the Chalukyan dominions, which included the region of Gujarat. To manage this territory, the Rashtrakutas established a branch line in Gujarat.

## Territorial Influence and Administration
The influence of the Rashtrakutas extended across various regions of western India. They were one of the successive dynasties dominant in western India, alongside the Satavahanas, Mauryas, and Chalukyas, whose dominions included the islands that would later be known as Bombay.

In the district of Belgaum, the Rashtrakutas succeeded the Chalukyas, who had held the country from the middle of the 6th century A.D. until approximately 760.

## Balance of Power and Decline
From approximately 750 to 950, a balance of power existed between the Rashtrakutas and the Gurjaras. During these two centuries, neither kingdom possessed sufficient strength to encroach significantly upon the other. The Rashtrakutas' ability to pursue large-scale schemes of conquest was further hindered by internal dissensions with their branch line.

Following the eventual break-up of Rashtrakuta power, a portion of their legacy survived through the Rattas (875–1250). The Rattas eventually established Venugrama (the ancient name for Belgaum) as their capital from 1210 onward, before they themselves succumbed to the Yadavas of Devagiri in 1250.
