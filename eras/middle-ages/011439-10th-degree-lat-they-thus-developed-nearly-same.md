# Cyclones of the China Sea and Indian Ocean

## Characteristics of Hot Sea Storms
The hotter seas of Asia are subject to remarkable phenomena in the form of revolving storms or cyclones. These occur frequently during the hot months of the year. In the China Sea, these revolving storms are specifically known as typhoons. In both the China Sea and the Indian Ocean, these storms appear to advance toward areas of greatest heat, and the wind invariably circulates from north by west, through south, to east.

## Cyclones of the Bay of Bengal
In the Indian Ocean, cyclones in the Bay of Bengal typically originate over the Nicobar and Andaman islands. These storms are commonly propagated in a north-westward direction, striking the Indian peninsula's east coast at various points. From there, they often advance with an easterly tendency over land and pass across the delta of the Ganges with extreme violence. These events occur throughout the hot months from June to October, and more rarely in November. They appear to be originated by the meeting of south-west monsoon currents with adverse currents from the north.

## Cyclones of the China Sea
The cyclones of the China Sea also occur during the hot months of the year. Unlike those in the Bay of Bengal, these storms advance from north-east to south-west, although they occasionally move from east to west. They originate near the island of Formosa and extend to approximately the 10th degree of N. lat. Consequently, they develop in nearly the same months and latitudes as the storms of the Indian Sea, despite their different direction of progress.
