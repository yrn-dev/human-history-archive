# The Black Death: How Plague Reshaped Europe

## Overview
The Black Death (1347–1351) was one of the most devastating pandemics in human history, caused by the bacterium *Yersinia pestis*. Originating in Central Asia and traveling via the Silk Road and maritime trade routes, the plague entered Europe through Sicilian ports in 1347. In less than five years, it is estimated to have killed between 30% and 60% of the European population.

While the immediate effect was catastrophic loss of life, the long-term consequences triggered a fundamental restructuring of European society. The sudden scarcity of labor shattered the foundations of feudalism. Peasants, once tied to the land in a state of serfdom, found their labor in high demand. This shifted the economic balance of power, allowing survivors to demand higher wages and better living conditions, eventually leading to the decline of the manorial system.

## Key Facts
*   **The Biological Vector:** The plague was primarily spread by fleas living on black rats (*Rattus rattus*). However, historians and epidemiologists continue to debate whether the speed of transmission was solely due to rats or if human-to-human transmission (via respiratory droplets in the pneumonic form) played a larger role.
*   **Social Upheaval:** The economic desperation of the ruling class led to laws like England's *Statute of Labourers (1351)*, which attempted to freeze wages at pre-plague levels. These oppressive measures contributed to violent uprisings, most notably the Peasants' Revolt of 1381.
*   **Religious Crisis:** The inability of the Church to stop the plague led to a complex spiritual reaction. Some turned toward extreme piety, such as the Flagellant movement, while others questioned the institutional authority of the clergy, sowing early seeds for the Reformation.
*   **Medical Evolution:** The failure of Galenic medicine (based on the "four humors") forced a slow shift toward empirical observation and public health measures, including the invention of the *quarantena* (40-day isolation) in Venice.

## Did You Know?
1. **The "Plague Doctor" Mask:** The iconic bird-like mask was not used during the Black Death of the 1340s; it was actually invented in the 17th century by Charles de Lorme.
2. **Genetic Legacy:** Modern genomic studies of plague pits have confirmed that *Yersinia pestis* was indeed the culprit, debunking older theories that the pandemic was a viral hemorrhagic fever.
3. **Agricultural Shift:** Because there were fewer people to farm grain, much of Europe's marginal farmland was converted to pasture for sheep, fueling the rise of the wool trade.
