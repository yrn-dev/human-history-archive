# Budweis

Budweis (known in Czech as *Budějovice*) is a town located in Bohemia, Austria, situated 80 miles south-southwest of Prague by rail. The town is positioned at the junction of the Moldau and Maltsch rivers, a point where the Moldau becomes navigable.

## History and Governance
The old town of Budweis was founded during the 13th century by Budivoj Vitkovec, who was the father of Závis of Falkenstein. In 1265, Ottokar II founded the new town, which was shortly thereafter established as a royal city. The town was granted numerous privileges by Wenceslaus and his father, Charles IV.

While the population was mainly Catholic, Budweis declared its support for King George Poděbrad. This led to the town being taken in 1468 by crusaders under the leadership of Zdenko of Stenberg. Following this event, the town remained faithful to the royal cause. In 1547, Emperor Ferdinand granted Budweis the privilege of ranking at the diet alongside Pilsen and Prague. These privileges were further confirmed after the outbreak of the Thirty Years' War.

## Urban Features and Architecture
Budweis features a beautiful square characterized by fine arcaded buildings. The most prominent of these is the town-hall, which was constructed in 1730 in the Renaissance style. Other significant architectural landmarks include:
* **The Cathedral:** Dating from 1500, it features a detached tower.
* **The Marien-Kirche:** Notable for its fine cloisters.

Outside the town, to the north in the Moldau valley, is the castle of Frauenberg. Belonging to Prince Schwarzenberg, this castle was built in the middle of the 19th century based on the model of Windsor Castle; it stands on the site of a previous castle from the 13th century.

## Economy and Infrastructure
Budweis serves as the principal commercial center of South Bohemia, functioning as both a river port and an important railway junction. The town engages in extensive trade involving salt, timber, lignite, corn, industrial products, and beer, with the beer being mostly exported to America.

The town possesses a growing and varied industrial sector, which includes the manufacture of:
* Machinery, bricks, and tiles.
* Chemicals, paper, and matches.
* Black-lead pencils.
* Boat-building and bell-founding.
* Saw mills and corn mills.

In terms of transportation, the railway from Budweis to Linz was the first line constructed in Austria, having been laid in 1827 for horse-cars.

## Demographics and Religion
Budweis has been the see of a bishop since 1783. The town is the center of a German enclave within Czech Bohemia, though the Czech element has been steadily increasing. As of 1908, the population of the town was 60% Czech. The total population of the town was recorded as 39,630 in 1900.
