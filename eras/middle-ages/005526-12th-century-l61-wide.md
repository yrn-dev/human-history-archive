# The 12th Century

The 12th century was a period characterized by significant developments in art, architecture, linguistics, and legal administration across Europe and the Mediterranean.

## Architecture and Urban Development
In France, several towns maintain structures dating to the 12th century. In Auxerre, the church of St Eusebe dates in part to this period, and the ancient episcopal palace preserves a Romanesque gallery from the 12th century. Additionally, the crypt beneath the choir of the former cathedral of St Etienne contains mural paintings from this era. In Avallon, the chief building, the church of St Lazare, dates from the 12th century and features two western portals adorned with ornate Romanesque sculpture. Similarly, the town of Brioude contains the church of St Julian, a 12th and 13th-century building in the Romanesque style of Auvergne, noted for its choir, radiating chapels, and exterior mosaic ornamentation.

In Corsica, the ruins of the cathedral of the vanished town of Nebbio date to the 12th century. The church at Murato, featuring Pisan architecture, dates to either the 12th or 13th century. In other regions, the town of Eperjes was founded around the middle of the 12th century by a German colony.

## Arts and Craftsmanship
The 12th century saw high levels of technical skill in textile and book production. Siculo-Saracenic brocade from this period included richly decorative shuttle-woven fabrics. One such example, 16½ inches wide, demonstrates the use of colored silks and gold thread. Another example from the 11th or 12th century features a cream-colored ground with Kufic inscriptions of "Arrahm[=a]n" (The Merciful), as well as patterns of geese and seated animals separated by conventional trees.

In the field of bookbinding, the 12th and 13th centuries were a period of perfection for "blind" stamping—the use of intaglio metal stamps to create cameo-form impressions on leather without gold leaf. This art was particularly excelled by English binders in locations such as London, Cambridge, Oxford, and Durham. A notable surviving example is the 12th-century binding of the Winchester Domesday Book.

Byzantine art of the 10th to 12th centuries is characterized by fine, delicate drawing and technical finish in applied colors. While some miniatures of the era suffered from formal convention—such as emaciated limbs and sombre colors contrasted with bright vermilion and gold—some works remained free from this conventionalism. An example is the 12th-century Gospel lectionary (Harleian MS. 1810) in the British Museum, which contains scenes from the life of Christ with figures of noble design.

## Language and Literacy
The 12th century marked a transitional phase in the Bulgarian language. Between the 12th and 15th centuries, writers attempted to follow archaic models of "Old Bulgarian" (archaic Slavonic), though the vernacular speech had already diverged significantly from the language of St Cyril and St Methodius. The written language of this period is referred to as "Middle Bulgarian," serving as a midpoint between modern speech and old ecclesiastical Slavonic.

## Legal and Administrative Practices
The 12th century saw a shift in how legal documents were executed. The use of "marks or signs manual" (_signum manuale_) fell out of general use after the 12th century. During this time, the common method for executing deeds transitioned to the affixing or appending of seals. It was not until the 14th century that the practice of signing a name-signature alongside a seal grew in usage as education and writing became more widely diffused.
