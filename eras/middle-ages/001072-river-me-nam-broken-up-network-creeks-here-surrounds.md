# Ayuthia

## Geography and Layout
Ayuthia is a city in Siam, referred to by the Siamese as *Krung Kao*, meaning "the Old Capital." It is located at 14deg 21' N. and 100deg 32' E. The city is situated on a large island created by the river Me Nam, which is broken up into a network of creeks that surround the land.

The population of the city is approximately 10,000. A characteristic feature of the settlement in lower Siam is the use of floating houses, in which the bulk of the inhabitants reside. These houses are moored to the edges of the creeks, which serve as the primary thoroughfares for the residents.

## Historical and Archaeological Significance
The island of Ayuthia contains the ruins of a famous city that served as the capital of Siam for more than four centuries. These ruins are considered to be of great archaeological interest. Additionally, the city possesses a collection of relics which are housed in a local museum.

Outside the town, there is an ancient masonry enclosure used for the capture of elephants; this structure continues to be used periodically.

## Administration and Economy
Ayuthia serves as the headquarters of a high commissioner and is the chief town of one of the richest agricultural provincial divisions of Siam. The government of the area is organized as an ordinary provincial division, known as a *Monton*. The government offices are located in spacious buildings that previously served as a royal summer retreat.

The economy of the region is primarily agricultural. The local peasantry relies on the import of cloth and other goods to meet their needs. In exchange, great quantities of paddi are sent annually to Bangkok via rail and river. Aside from this exchange, there is no other trade.

## Infrastructure and Transport
The city is connected to Bangkok, located 42 m. away, via the northern main line of the state railways. In addition to the railway, the river and its network of creeks provide essential transport for the movement of goods and the daily navigation of the inhabitants.
