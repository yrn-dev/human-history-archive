# Black Death in Buckinghamshire

## Impact on the Region
The county of Buckinghamshire was severely visited by the Black Death. The plague had a devastating effect on the local population, leading to the near-total depopulation of several areas. Specifically, the district of Winslow was one of many locations within the county that were almost entirely depopulated as a result of the outbreak.

## Historical Context
The history of Buckinghamshire has been shaped by its proximity to London, which involved the county in major national events. Following the Norman invasion, the county was likely part of the earldom of Leofwine, son of Godwin. Because Buckinghamshire supported Leofwine at the battle of Hastings, the region suffered sweeping confiscations after the Conquest.

Later conflicts also left their mark on the county. During the war between King John and his barons, Falkes de Bréauté captured and demolished Hanslape Castle in 1216, which had been held against the king by William Mauduit. During the civil war, Buckinghamshire was among the first counties to join a mutual defence association supporting the parliament, maintaining garrisons at Brill and Aylesbury. Royalist troops briefly garrisoned Newport Pagnell, and the king established his headquarters at Buckingham in 1644.

## Legal and Educational Institutions
Administratively, the county is divided into thirteen petty sessional divisions and has one court of quarter sessions. Assizes are held at Aylesbury, and the county falls within the midland circuit. The boroughs of Wycombe and Buckingham maintain separate commissions of the peace. 

The administrative county comprises 230 civil parishes. Ecclesiastically, 215 parishes are located wholly or partly within the diocese of Oxford, which covers almost the entire county. Politically, the county is split into three parliamentary divisions—Southern (Wycombe), Mid (Aylesbury), and Northern (Buckingham)—each returning one member. A small portion of the parliamentary borough of Windsor is also contained within the county. A notable institution in the region is Eton College, a famous public school founded by Henry VI.
