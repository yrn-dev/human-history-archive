# Barfurush

## Geography and Location
Barfurush is a town located in the province of Mazandaran in Persia, situated at 36deg 32' N. and 52deg 42' E. The town is positioned on the left bank of the river Bawul [Babul], where a bridge consisting of eight arches crosses the water. It is located approximately 15 miles from the southern shore of the Caspian Sea. The small town of Meshed i Sar serves as the port for Barfurush. In terms of regional distance, it is 26 miles from Sari and 90 miles from Teheran.

## History and Development
The town was formerly known as Mamatir. Its current name is derived from a settlement called Barfurush-deh, which was added to the old city in A.D. 1012. 

## Commerce and Economy
Barfurush serves as the commercial capital of Mazandaran and has a population of approximately 50,000. The town's size and importance are entirely attributed to its commercial prosperity, as it is peopled entirely by tradesmen and merchants. This success is notable because the town is built in a swampy, low country and is accessed via roads that are deep and nearly impassable, conditions that would not otherwise seem favorable for a seat of extensive inland trade.

The primary articles of trade in the region are cotton and rice. Additionally, hemp (_Kanab_), flax (_Kat[=u]n_), and some sugar cane (_nai shakar_) are grown. Shipping trade is conducted briskly between the port of Meshed i Sar and other ports on the Caspian Sea, with steamers from the Mercury and Caucasus Company calling weekly.

## Urban Structure and Architecture
The town possesses a peculiar aspect and structure, as it is situated within a forest of tall trees. These trees conceal the buildings and separate them to such an extent that the town does not appear populous, except when one is in the bazars. The houses are described as comfortable and in good repair, featuring substantial enclosing walls and roofs made of tiles. Unlike other Persian towns, there are no ruins to be seen. While the streets are generally unpaved, they are neat, broad, and kept in good order.

The town lacks public buildings of significant importance. The primary points of interest are the bazars, which extend for a full mile. These consist of ranges of shops that are well stored with commodities and covered with roofs of wood and tiles. The town also contains approximately ten commodious caravanserais.

## Education and Landmarks
In addition to its commercial reputation, Barfurush is celebrated for learning, evidenced by the presence of a number of colleges known as _medresseh_. To the east of the town, on an island within a small lake, lies a garden called Bagh i Shah (the garden of the Shah), which contains baths and ruined palaces.
