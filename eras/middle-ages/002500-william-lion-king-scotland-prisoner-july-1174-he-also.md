# William the Lion

William the Lion was a king of Scotland who died in 1214. His reign was marked by significant administrative developments, including the establishment of royal burghs and the creation of earldoms.

## Captivity and the Treaty of Falaise
In July 1174, William the Lion was taken prisoner by the northern barons who had raised the siege of Alnwick; among those involved in his capture was the younger Bernard de Baliol. The king's eventual liberation occurred in 1175 under the terms of the Treaty of Falaise. To ensure the performance of this treaty, hostages were provided, one of whom was Waltheof, the first member of his family to be styled "Comes de Dunbar" (around 1174).

## Royal Burghs and Administration
During his reign, William the Lion expanded the network of royal burghs in Scotland. He designated Kintore, located on the Don in Aberdeenshire, as a royal burgh. Toward the close of the 12th century, he also established Ayr as a royal residence and subsequently as a royal burgh. In Ayr, the king provided the corporation with an extensive grant of lands through his liberality. Additionally, in 1174, William the Lion created the earldom of Lennox.

## Family and Descendants
William the Lion had children who married into prominent noble families. His daughter Isabella married Roger, the 3rd earl of Norfolk, though the couple had no sons. He also had a natural daughter, Ada, who married Patrick Dunbar, the 5th earl of Dunbar and keeper of Berwick Castle. Their descendant, Patrick Dunbar (the 8th earl of Dunbar and 1st earl of March), later claimed the crown of Scotland in 1291 based on his descent from Ada.
