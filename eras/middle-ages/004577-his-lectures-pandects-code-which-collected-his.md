# Lectures on the Pandects and the Code

The *Lectures on the Pandects* and the *Code* were a series of legal teachings delivered by Joannes Bassianus, a 12th-century Italian jurist. While the lectures themselves have unfortunately perished, they were originally collected by his pupil, Nicolaus Furiosus.

## Joannes Bassianus
Joannes Bassianus was a professor at the law school of Bologna. Although little is known regarding his origins, Corolus de Tocco states that he was a native of Cremona. In his academic lineage, Joannes was a pupil of Bulgarus and served as the master of Azo. 

Bassianus was noted for his talent in creating ingenious forms to explain his ideas with precision. His most celebrated work is the *Arbor Arborum*, or "Law-Tree," which has been the subject of numerous commentaries. This work utilizes the imagery of a tree to arrange various kinds of actions as fruit on its branches. He divided these into two primary groups:
* **Civil actions** (*actiones stricti juris*), numbering forty-eight, arranged on one side.
* **Equitable or praetorian actions**, numbering one hundred and twenty-one, arranged on the other side.

Furthermore, Bassianus established a scientific division of actions under twelve heads. He employed an ingenious system of notation that allowed students to immediately classify any civil or praetorian action under its proper scientific head. He included a few glosses alongside the tree to justify and explain this classification. Another of his preserved works, the *Summary on the Authentica*, was regarded by Savigny as one of the most precious works produced by the school of the Gloss-writers.

## The Pandects and the Code
The subjects of Bassianus's lost lectures, the Pandects and the Code, are foundational legal texts associated with the legal labors of Justinian. 

### The Pandects
Also referred to as the Digest, the Pandects contain the sentiments of celebrated civilians. The texts cover a wide array of legal subjects, including:
* **Torture:** The Pandects (l. xlviii. tit. xviii.) strictly confine the use of torture to slaves. The civilian Ulpian acknowledged that the "res" (thing/matter) is fragile, dangerous, and may fail to provide the truth.
* **Contracts:** The subject of contracts by consent is spread across four books (xvii.—xx.) of the Pandects. Additionally, the Pandects (l. xix.) define the covenants of rent.
* **Family and Social Status:** The Pandects (l. i. tit. v. vi. l. xxxviii. tit. i.—iv., and the entire xlth book) address the state of slaves and freedmen. They also contain information on the filial peculium (l. xv. tit. i. l. xli. tit. i.).
* **Succession:** The law of succession is detailed in the Pandects (l. xxxviii. tit. vi.—xvii.).

### The Code
The Code, along with the Pandects and the Institutes, formed part of Justinian's legal project. The Code contains:
* **Penal Laws:** The Pompeian and Cornelian laws *de sicariis* and *parricidis* are abridged in the Code (l. ix. tit. xvi. xvii.), including supplements from Valentinian, Constantine, and Alexander Severus.
* **Administrative Law:** In the code of Justinian (l. i. tit. xxxix. leg. 3), the prefect is declared to command and precede all city magistrates.
* **Other Regulations:** The Code addresses the filial peculium (l. iv. tit. xxvi. xxvii.), the state of slaves and freedmen (l. vi. tit. iv. v. l. vii. tit. i.—xxiii.), covenants of rent (l. iv. tit. lxv.), and the law of succession (l. vi. tit. lv.—lx.).

## Historical Context and Translations
The Code and Pandects were translated during the time of Justinian, with the Pandects translated by Thalelaeus. Other related works include the *Institutes*, for which Theophilus provided an elegant paraphrase, and the *Novels* (Novellae). While Justinian did not collect the Novels himself, the legal standard consists of ninety-eight Novels in nine collations, though this number was later increased by Contius, Haloander, and Julian. Julian, the antecessor of Constantinople in A.D. 570, provided an elegant Latin translation of 120 Greek Novels for use in Africa and Italy.
