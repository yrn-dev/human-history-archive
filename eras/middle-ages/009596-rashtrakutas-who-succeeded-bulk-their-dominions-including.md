# Rashtrakutas

The Rashtrakutas were a dynasty that played a significant role in the political landscape of western and southern India, particularly in the Deccan region. They emerged as a major power through a prolonged struggle for mastery in the southern Deccan against the Chalukyas.

## Rise to Power and Territorial Expansion
During the 7th century, the Chalukyas had established dominance in the southern Deccan, overcoming the Rashtrakutas and other rivals to absorb smaller kingdoms into their empire. However, the political tide shifted as the Rashtrakutas eventually succeeded their ancient rivals. After the Chalukyas succumbed to the Rashtrakutas, the latter succeeded to the bulk of the Chalukya dominions. 

This expansion included the acquisition of Gujarat, where the Rashtrakutas established a branch line of their dynasty. Their influence extended to the islands and city of Bombay, which served as an outlying portion of the dominions of the successive dynasties dominant in western India, including the Rashtrakutas.

## Regional Relations and Balance of Power
For approximately two centuries, from c. 750 to 950, a balance of power existed between the Rashtrakutas and the Gurjaras. During this period, neither kingdom possessed sufficient strength to significantly encroach upon the other. Despite their power, the Rashtrakutas were prevented from pursuing large-scale schemes of conquest due to internal dissensions involving their branch line.

## Succession and Decline
The Rashtrakuta power eventually broke up. In certain regions, such as the area around the town of Belgaum (anciently known as Venugrama), the Rashtrakutas had succeeded the Chalukyas, who had held the country from the middle of the 6th century A.D. until approximately 760. Following the collapse of the Rashtrakuta power, a portion of their legacy survived in the form of the Rattas (875–1250). The Rattas eventually made Venugrama their capital from 1210 onward before they were ultimately overthrown by the Yadavas of Devagiri in 1250.
