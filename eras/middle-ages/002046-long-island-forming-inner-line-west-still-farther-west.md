# Long Island and the Inner Line of the Bahamas

The geography of the Bahamas, an archipelago of the British West Indies, is characterized by a series of islands, cays, and rocks extending approximately 630 miles. This chain runs from Florida in the northwest to Haiti in the southeast, situated between Cuba and the open Atlantic Ocean. The archipelago is estimated to consist of 2,387 rocks, 661 cays, and 29 islands.

## Geographic Arrangement and the Inner Line
The islands of the Bahamas are organized into distinct lines based on their position relative to the open ocean. The outer line, which faces the open ocean, includes Rum Cay, Watling (also known as Guanahani), Cat, and Eleuthera—a name likely derived from the Spanish *Isla de Tierra*. This outer line is continued by the Caicos and Turks islands, although these are geographically part of the archipelago but not politically.

Positioned to the west of this outer line is an inner line of islands. This inner line is formed by New Providence, the Exuma chain, and Long Island.

## Westernmost Islands and Channels
Located still farther west than the inner line is Andros. Named after Sir Edmund Andros, who served as the governor of Massachusetts at the end of the 17th century, Andros is often referred to as a single island, though it is actually divided into several islands by narrow straits. The Andros islands cover an area of 1,600 square miles and reach a length of 95 miles.

The archipelago is surrounded by mostly shallow seas, though it is intersected by three well-defined channels:
*   **The Florida or New Bahama channel:** Located between Florida and the north-western islands, this channel is followed by the Gulf Stream.
*   **The Providence channels:** These consist of a north-east and north-west channel. From these, a depression called the Tongue of Ocean extends southward along the eastern side of Andros.
*   **The Old Bahama channel:** This channel separates Cuba from the archipelago.

## Major Islands and Population
The total land area of the Bahamas is estimated at 5,450 square miles, with the main islands occupying 4,424 square miles. Key islands include:
*   **New Providence:** The most important island because it contains the capital, Nassau. It has an area of 85 square miles and a length of 19-3/8 miles. In 1901, it supported a population of 12,534.
*   **Great Abaco:** 70 miles long with an area of 680 square miles.
*   **Grand Bahama:** 66 miles long with an area of 430 square miles.
*   **Great Inagua:** 34 miles long with an area of 530 square miles.

Other islands in the archipelago include Little Abaco, the Crooked Islands, and Mayaguana.

The total population of the Bahamas was recorded as 43,521 in 1881 and grew to 53,735 by 1901. Of the 1901 population, approximately 12,000 were whites, while the remainder were coloured.
