# Die Reichsgesetze 900-1400 and Wittelsbachische Regesten

## Overview
*Die Reichsgesetze 900-1400* (published in Frankfort, 1832) and *Wittelsbachische Regesten von der Erwerbung des Herzogtums Bayern bis zu 1340* (published in Stuttgart, 1854) are historical collections edited by the German historian Johann Friedrich Bohmer. These works are part of a broader effort by Bohmer to collect and tabulate imperial documents and charters from the middle ages.

## Authorship and Editorial Context
Johann Friedrich Bohmer (1795–1863) was a historian born in Frankfort-on-Main. His academic background included education at the universities of Gottingen and Heidelberg. Throughout his career, Bohmer served as the librarian and archivist for the city of Frankfort, as well as the secretary of the *Gesellschaft fur altere deutsche Geschichtskunde*. 

Bohmer's professional interests were centered on the study of history, specifically the collection of original authorities and imperial documents. His research is noted as being of great value to students, although it is observed that his critical sense may have been somewhat warped by his personal preferences; he held a strong affection for the Roman Catholic Church and Austria, while maintaining a great dislike for the Protestant faith and Prussia.

## Related Historical Collections
The publication of *Die Reichsgesetze 900-1400* and the *Wittelsbachische Regesten* occurred alongside several other significant scholarly projects led by Bohmer:

*   **Regesta chronologico-diplomatica**: Bohmer produced several abstracts and series of *Regesta*, including those for the Roman emperors from 911–1313 (Frankfort, 1831) and the Carolingians (*Regesta chronologico-diplomatica Karolorum*, Frankfort, 1833). He further expanded these with volumes covering the periods 1314–1347 (1839) and 1246–1313 (1844), as well as the period 1198–1254 (Stuttgart, 1849).
*   **Fontes rerum Germanicarum**: Published in Stuttgart between 1843 and 1868, this collection serves as a source of original authorities for German history during the 14th and 13th centuries. The final volume of this work was edited by A. Huber following Bohmer's death.
*   **Codex diplomaticus Moeno-Francofurtanus**: This work, the *Urkundenbuch der Reichsstadt Frankfurt*, was published in Frankfort in 1836, with a subsequent new edition released by F. Law in 1901.
