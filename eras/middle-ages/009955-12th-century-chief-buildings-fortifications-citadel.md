# 12th Century Buildings, Fortifications, and Citadels

The 12th century was a period of significant architectural development across various regions, characterized by the construction of major religious edifices and the establishment of strategic military and civic structures.

## Religious Architecture of the 12th Century
Throughout Europe, the 12th century saw the creation of several prominent churches and cathedrals. In France, the church of St Lazare in Avallon dates from this period, featuring two western portals decorated with sculpture in the ornate Romanesque style. In Auxerre, the church of St Eusebe belongs in part to the 12th century, as do the mural paintings found within the 11th-century crypt of the church of St Etienne. Additionally, the ancient episcopal palace in Auxerre preserves a Romanesque gallery from the 12th century.

Other notable 12th-century religious structures include:
*   **Bonifacio:** The cathedral of Santa Maria Maggiore belongs mainly to the 12th century.
*   **Corunna:** The church of Santiago dates from the 12th century.
*   **Douai:** The church of Notre-Dame dates from the 12th and 14th centuries.
*   **Aschaffenburg:** The *Stiftskirche* (cathedral), while founded in 980, dates in the main from the early 12th and 13th centuries.

## Citadels and Fortifications
Citadels and fortifications served as critical defensive hubs, though their construction dates vary widely across different cities.

### The Citadel of Cairo
In Cairo, the citadel known as El-Kala was built by Saladin around 1166. While it has undergone frequent alterations over time, it remains a primary edifice in the Arab quarters. The citadel currently houses a mosque of Oriental alabaster and a palace erected by Mehemet Ali. Within its walls, there are four other mosques, including that of Ibn Kalaun (built 1317).

### European Fortifications and Citadels
In other regions, citadels and fortifications were often developed or modified in later centuries:
*   **Bonifacio:** The citadel and fortifications date from the 16th and 17th centuries, though the town also possesses a massive medieval tower used as a powder-magazine.
*   **Corunna:** The upper town is defended by a citadel and remains surrounded by bastions and walls.
*   **Lublin:** The town possesses an old citadel, though of its former fortifications, only four gates remain, one of which dates to 1342.
*   **Ayr:** A fortification termed the citadel was built by Cromwell to command the country; it enclosed ten or twelve acres and included the church of St John, which was used as a storehouse.
*   **Avallon:** Remains of ancient fortifications are still visible, including seven flanking towers.
*   **Auxerre:** Ancient fortifications have been replaced by wide tree-lined boulevards, although a cylindrical tower and high wall remain from a once-fortified abbey.
*   **Douai:** The old fortifications have been demolished for gardens and boulevards, with the 15th-century Porte de Valenciennes serving as the chief survival.

## Other 12th Century Developments
Beyond individual buildings, the late 12th century saw the rise of specific social and civic organizations. In the south of France, the Bridgebuilding Brotherhood (*Fratres Pontifices*) emerged during the latter part of the 12th century. This confraternity built bridges, looked after ferries, and maintained hospices at the chief fords of principal rivers. The brotherhood received recognition from Pope Clement III in 1189.

Additionally, the history of Ayr begins to be known at the close of the 12th century, when William the Lion established it as a royal burgh and a royal residence.
