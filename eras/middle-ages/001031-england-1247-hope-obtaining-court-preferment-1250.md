# England in the Mid-13th Century

## The Foundation of Bethlehem Hospital
In 1247, Simon FitzMary, who served as the sheriff of London, founded Bethlehem Hospital. This institution was the first lunatic asylum in England and the first in Europe, with the exception of one similar asylum located in Granada, Spain. Originally, it was established as a priory for the brethren and sisters of the order of the Star of Bethlehem. One of its primary purposes was to provide housing and entertainment for the canons and bishop of the mother-church, St Mary of Bethlehem, during their visits to England. The hospital's initial site was located in Bishopsgate Street. While it is not certain when the facility began receiving lunatics, records mention it as a hospital in 1330, and some patients were present there by 1403.

## Aymer of Valence and the See of Winchester
During this period, the children of Hugo of Lusignan (the count of La Marche) and Isabelle of Angouleme arrived in England in 1247. Isabelle was the second wife of King John and the mother of Aymer of Valence, who was a half-brother to King Henry III. The children came to the country seeking court preferment.

In 1250, King Henry III exerted strong pressure on the electors to secure the see of Winchester for Aymer. This appointment was considered unsuitable because Aymer was illiterate, had no knowledge of the English language, and lived a wholly secular life. Consequently, Aymer became the focus of popular indignation directed toward foreign favourites.

Aymer's tenure was marked by conflict with the barons. At the parliament of Oxford in 1258, he and his brothers repudiated a new constitution created by the barons. This led to Aymer being pursued to Winchester and besieged in Wolvesey castle, eventually forcing him to surrender and leave the kingdom. Because he had never been consecrated, the chapter of Winchester held a new election in 1259. Although Aymer gained the support of the pope and attempted to return to England, he died of a fatal illness in Paris.

## Linguistic Evolution
The mid-13th century represents a transitional period in the development of the English language. According to chronological nomenclature, the period from 1150 to 1250 is classified as Early Middle English. Following 1250, the language entered the stage of typical Middle English, which lasted until 1400. This era is characterized by the leveling of inflections, distinguishing it from the full inflections of Old English.
