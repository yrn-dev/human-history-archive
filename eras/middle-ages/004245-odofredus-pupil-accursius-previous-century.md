# Odofredus

Odofredus was an Italian jurist who played a significant role in the development of legal scholarship and the transmission of Roman law during the medieval period. He is primarily recognized for his contributions to the methodical exposition of law and his early application of specific teaching methodologies.

## Academic Lineage and Education
Odofredus was a pupil of two prominent legal scholars. He studied under Jacobus Balduinus, a Bolognese jurist of the 13th century who was himself a pupil of Azo. Balduinus, who was elected *podesta* of Genoa and authored some of the earliest treatises on procedure, served as the master to Odofredus, as well as to Hostiensis and Jacobus de Ravanis. Additionally, Odofredus is identified as a pupil of Accursius.

## Contributions to Legal Scholarship
Odofredus contributed to the preservation and organization of the works of Azo (c. 1150-1230). Azo, a professor of civil law at the university of Bologna and a pupil of Joannes Bassianus, produced "Readings on the Code" that provided a methodical exposition of Roman law. These readings were of such significant weight before tribunals that the phrase "Chi non ha Azzo, non vada a palazzo" was used. Odofredus, along with Hugolinus, provided additions that helped complete the collection of these readings, which had been initially gathered by Alessandro de Santo Aegidio.

Furthermore, Odofredus is cited as the authority for the belief that Burgundio, a 12th-century professor at the university of Paris and Greek scholar, translated various Greek fragments found in the Pandects into Latin. These translations, performed shortly after the Pandects arrived in Bologna, were integrated into the text of the Pandects as part of "The Vulgate," distinguishing them from the Florentine text.

## The Dialectical Method
Odofredus is noted for his use of the dialectical method of teaching law. While later historians often attributed the introduction of this method to the famous 14th-century jurist Bartolus de Saxoferrato, the archive records that Odofredus had already employed the dialectical method in the previous century. 

Following Odofredus, his successors continued to use this approach; however, they are noted to have abused the method to such an extent that their writings often became unprofitable to read, as the actual subject matter was obscured by dialectical forms. This contrasted with the later work of Bartolus, who utilized the dialectical method with advantage while simultaneously reviving the exegetical system of teaching law that had been neglected since the time of Accursius.
