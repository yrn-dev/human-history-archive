# Ashford

Ashford (historically referred to as Esselesford, Asshatisforde, or Essheford) is a market town located in the Southern or Ashford parliamentary division of Kent, England. It is situated on a gentle eminence near the confluence of the upper branches of the river Stour, approximately 56 miles southeast of London. As of 1901, the population of the urban district was 12,808.

## History and Administration
At the time of the Domesday survey, Ashford was held by Hugh de Montfort, who had arrived in England with William the Conqueror. The town has never been incorporated. 

The town's commercial privileges were expanded through various royal grants. In 1243, Henry III granted the lord of the manor an annual fair and a Saturday market. Subsequent annual fairs were granted by Edward III in 1349 and Edward IV in 1466. In 1607, following a petition from the inhabitants, James I granted Sir John Smith, Kt., the right to hold a court of record in the town every third Tuesday. Later, in 1672, Charles II granted a market to be held every second Tuesday, which included a court of pie-powder.

During the latter half of the 18th century, the cattle trade increased due to the fertility of the pasture-land in Romney Marsh to the east and south of the town. This growth led to the establishment of a stock market in 1784.

## Infrastructure and Economy
Ashford serves as a hub for the South-Eastern & Chatham railway, which maintains large carriage and locomotive works in the town. Other local industries include breweries and agricultural implement works. 

The town's educational facilities include a grammar school founded during the reign of Charles I by Sir Norman Knatchbull. Architecturally, the town features a fine Perpendicular church dedicated to St Mary, characterized by many interesting monuments and a well-proportioned, lofty tower. On the south-eastern outskirts of the town lies the populous village of Willesborough, which had a population of 3,602.

## Regional Geography and Antiquities
Ashford is located in a region of Kent noted for its beauty and fertility. Nearby Charing, situated to the north-west, contains the ruins of a palace mainly from the Decorated period, where the archbishops of Canterbury maintained a residence from pre-Conquest times. To the east and south, the area is influenced by the Romney Marsh.

The region has a history of monastic foundations. Bilsington priory, established in 1253 and belonging to the Augustinian canons, is located near Ashford, though only slight remains exist.

In the vicinity of Ashford, specifically at Bethersden (located between Ashford and Tenterden), marble quarries were once worked extensively. These quarries provided material for local churches as well as the cathedrals of Rochester and Canterbury.

## Other Locations Named Ashford
The archive contains records of another Ashford located in Derbyshire. This location is noted for the production of high-quality Carboniferous black marbles, which are used for ornamental objects such as vases and chimney-pieces. Additionally, a rare and beautiful dense brown laminated limestone known as "rosewood marble" is derived from Ashford in Derbyshire; it is used primarily for inlaid work and occurs in limited quantities.
