# The Departure of the Polo Family from the Court of the Great Kaan

The departure of Messer Nicolo, Messer Maffeo, and Messer Marco Polo from the court of the Great Kaan marked the conclusion of a lengthy period of service and exploration. Before their final leave, Messer Marco had spent approximately seventeen years in the employment of the Kaan. During this time, he traveled extensively on missions entrusted to him by the Lord, as well as on private affairs conducted with the Kaan's authority. Marco gained the Emperor's great love and favor by diligently gathering knowledge of matters that would interest the sovereign and relating them in regular order upon his return to court. This discretion and success led the Emperor to employ him on the most weighty and distant missions, keeping him so close to his person that some Barons became envious.

## Authorization and Provisions for Departure

When the Prince observed that the two brothers and Messer Marco were ready to set forth, he summoned the three to his presence. To ensure their safety and support, the Great Kaan provided them with two golden Tablets of Authority. These tablets were designed to secure liberty of passage throughout all his dominions and guaranteed that all necessary provisions would be supplied for the Polos and their entire company wherever they traveled.

In addition to these tablets, the Great Kaan charged the party with delivering messages to the kings of Christendom, specifically naming the King of England, the King of France, and the King of Spain.

## The Naval Expedition

To facilitate their return to their own country, the Great Kaan caused thirteen ships to be equipped. These vessels were characterized by having four masts each and frequently spreading twelve sails. Some of these ships were notably large, carrying crews of 250 or 260 men.

The party that took leave of the Great Kaan included the two brothers, Messer Marco, three Barons, and a Lady. They embarked with a large company of people and were provided by the Emperor with all necessary supplies for a duration of two years.

## The Journey Home

The voyage began with a three-month sail to the south, arriving at the island of Java. Following their time there, the party continued to navigate the Sea of India for an additional eighteen months before reaching their destination. 

The journey was marked by extreme mortality. Although the party numbered some 600 persons upon embarkation (excluding the mariners), nearly all died during the voyage, leaving only eight survivors. Upon their arrival, they discovered that Argon had died, and the Lady in their company was delivered to his son, Casan.

## Historical Context of the Polos

The family lineage identifies Messer Marco as the son of Messer Nicolo and the grandson of Messer Andrea Polo of San Felice. While some texts suggest Marco was born after his father left Venice, other sources, such as the old French texts, indicate he was fifteen years old when his father found his mother had died. 

Following their return and subsequent events, Messer Marco was later imprisoned in Genoa. In the year 1298, while an inmate of the prison, he dictated his experiences—spanning twenty-six years of travel—to Messer Rusticiano of Pisa, who reduced the accounts to writing.
