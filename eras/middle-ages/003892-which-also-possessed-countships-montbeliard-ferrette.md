# The Countships of Montbeliard and Ferrette

The countships of Montbeliard and Ferrette were historical territories and titles that passed through several noble houses and sovereign entities, including the house of Mousson, the archdukes of Austria, and the dukes of Burgundy and Lorraine.

## The House of Mousson and the Counts of Bar
In the 11th century, the lords of the territory of Bar (Barrois) held the title of counts. These lords belonged to the house of Mousson, which possessed the countships of Montbeliard and Ferrette in addition to Bar. During this period, the lords of Mousson typically fought within the French ranks, contrasting with their neighbors, the dukes of Lorraine, who adhered to the German side. The countship of Bar had been granted in 951 by Otto the Great to Count Frederick of Ardenne, eventually passing to the lords of Montbéliard in 1093.

## Montbeliard and Belfort
The town of Belfort is first mentioned in historical records during the early part of the 13th century, at which time it was in the possession of the counts of Montbeliard. Through marriage, Belfort passed from the counts of Montbeliard to the counts of Ferrette, and subsequently to the archdukes of Austria. 

In the modern era, Montbeliard serves as one of the arrondissements of a department that also includes Besancon, Baume-les-Dames, and Pontarlier. The area near Montbeliard, specifically Mandeure, contains numerous antiquities and is situated on the site of the Roman town of *Epomanduodurum*.

## The Countship of Ferrette and the Burgundian Ambitions
The countship of Ferrette became a focal point of the territorial ambitions of Charles the Bold, the count of Charolois and son of Philip. In 1469, Charles purchased the countship of Ferrette, along with the landgraviate of Alsace, from Archduke Sigismund of Austria. 

The administration of Ferrette under the Burgundians was marked by conflict. Peter von Hagenbach, the lieutenant of the duke, was established at Ferrette as the *Landvogt* (or *grand bailli*) of Upper Alsace. His tyranny sparked an insurrection supported by the Swiss and the inhabitants of the free towns of Alsace. This unrest contributed to a broader conflict involving Duke René II. of Lorraine, who declared war against Charles. While Charles succeeded in conquering Lorraine in 1475, he was eventually defeated and killed before Nancy on January 6, 1477.

## Transition to French and Austrian Control
The town of Belfort, previously linked to the countships of Ferrette and Montbeliard, underwent several changes in sovereignty. Following the Treaty of Westphalia in 1648, the town was ceded to Louis XIV, who then granted it to Cardinal Mazarin. Prior to this, during the Thirty Years' War, Belfort was besieged twice (1633 and 1634) and was the site of a 1635 battle between the duke of Lorraine and allied French and Swedish forces under Marshal de la Force.
