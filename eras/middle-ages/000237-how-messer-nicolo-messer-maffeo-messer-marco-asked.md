# How Messer Nicolo, Messer Maffeo, and Messer Marco Asked Leave of the Great Kaan

The request for leave to depart the court of the Great Kaan was the culmination of a lengthy period of service and residence in the Orient by Messer Nicolo, Messer Maffeo, and Messer Marco Polo. Their departure followed years of diplomatic and administrative employment under the sovereign.

## Service of Messer Marco Polo
Messer Marco Polo remained in the employment of the Great Kaan for approximately seventeen years. During this time, he traveled extensively, moving "hither and thither" on missions entrusted to him by the Lord. In some instances, he conducted his own private affairs with the authority and permission of the Great Kaan.

Marco Polo distinguished himself by his diligence in gathering knowledge regarding matters that would interest the sovereign. Upon returning to court from his travels, he would relate his findings in a regular order. This habit earned him the great love and favor of the Emperor, who consequently entrusted him with the most distant and weighty missions. Marco carried out these assignments with success and discretion. Because the Emperor kept him so close to his person and treated him with high distinction, some of the Barons became envious of him. As a result of his constant desire to spy out and enquire into everything to provide reports for the Lord, Marco Polo visited or gained knowledge of more different countries of the world than any other man.

## The Request for Departure
The process of leaving the court involved a request for favor from the Great Kaan. The circumstances leading to this request involved the arrival of three Barons at the court. These Barons delivered a message and were received with hospitality and honor. In compliance with a demand made by these Barons, the Kaan presented them with a beautiful and charming maiden of seventeen years named Cocachin, who belonged to the family of the deceased Queen Bolgana.

While this was occurring, Messer Marco returned from a mission to India, where he had served as the Lord's ambassador. He reported on the various seas he had voyaged and the things he had seen. The three Barons, observing that Nicolo, Maffeo, and Marco were not only Latins but men of "marvellous good sense," decided they wanted the three Polos to accompany them. The Barons intended to return to their own country by sea to avoid the great fatigue of a long land journey for the Lady Cocachin.

The ambassadors were particularly eager for their company because the Polos possessed extensive experience and knowledge of the countries they would encounter and the Indian Sea, with Messer Marco being especially knowledgeable in this regard. Consequently, the Barons approached the Great Kaan and begged as a favor that he allow the three Latins to travel with them.

## Return to Their Own Country
Following the request for leave, Messer Nicolo, Messer Maffeo, and Messer Marco took their leave of the Great Kaan and began the journey back to their own country. This return marked the end of a sojourn in the Orient that lasted twenty-six years.
