# Arundel

Arundel is a municipal borough and market town located in the Chichester parliamentary division of Sussex, England. It is situated on the slope of a hill overlooking the river Arun, which remains navigable for small vessels up to Littlehampton at its mouth, located 6 miles to the south. As of 1901, the population of the town was 2,739. The town is located 58 miles south-south-west of London and is accessible via the London, Brighton & South Coast railway.

## Arundel Castle
The summit of the hill in Arundel is the site of Arundel Castle, a structure that originally served to guard the passage through the hills along the river. A castle existed at this location during the time of King Alfred. Following the Conquest, the castle was rebuilt by Roger de Montgomerie, though it was later seized from his son due to a rebellion against King Henry I.

In 1397, the castle was the site of a conspiracy to murder the lords of the council and dethrone Richard II; this plot was organized by the duke of Gloucester, the archbishop of Canterbury, and the earl of Arundel, but it was discovered before it could be executed. During the civil wars of the 17th century, the stronghold suffered great damage due to frequent assaults by opposing parties. It was later restored by Charles, 11th duke of Norfolk (who died in 1815), transforming it into one of England's most splendid baronial mansions. Toward the end of the 19th century, the castle underwent extensive reconstruction in the style of the 13th century.

## Town and Governance
The town's name is derived from *hirondelle* (a swallow), according to a whimsical etymology found on the corporation seal. The town hall is a castellated building that was presented to the corporation by the duke of Norfolk.

## Religious Institutions
Arundel contains several notable religious buildings:

**The Church of St Nicholas**
Founded around 1375, this Perpendicular style church features a low tower rising from its center. The church is recognized for its iron work and its reredos. Within the north aisle of the chancel, there are several ancient monuments dedicated to the earls of Arundel. The chancel is the property of the duke of Norfolk and is screened from the rest of the building. This exercise of ownership rights by the duke was the subject of an appeal and a legal action in 1880.

**The Roman Catholic Church of St Philip Neri**
This church was built in 1873 by the duke of Norfolk.
