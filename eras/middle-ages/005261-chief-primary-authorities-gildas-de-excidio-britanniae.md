# Primary Authorities of Early British History

The study of early British history relies upon a specific set of primary authorities and foundational texts. Among the most significant of these are Gildas, author of *De Excidio Britanniae*, and Nennius, who wrote *Historia Britonum*.

## Chief Primary Sources
The primary authorities for the period include several key historical and legal records. In addition to the works of Gildas and Nennius, essential sources include:
*   **Bede**: *Hist. Eccl.* (also referred to as *Hist. Ecclesiast. Gentis Angloruml.*).
*   **The Saxon Chronicle**: Also known as *Chron. Saxonicum*.
*   **Legal Texts**: The *Anglo-Saxon Laws* and the *Leges Wallicae*.
*   **Charters**: Specifically the *Cartularium Saxonicum* compiled by W. de G. Birch.
*   **Other Records**: The works of Th. Mommsen in *Mon. Germ. Hist., Auct. Antiquiss.*

## Historical Perspectives on the Britons
Records indicate that the Britons were not ignorant of the use or manufacture of arms. While they initially experienced amazement during the successive and disorderly attacks of the Saxons, the events of the war provided them with experience and discipline, which added to their native valor.

## Modern Scholarly Contributions
Modern historians have expanded upon these primary sources to provide a more comprehensive understanding of the era. Notable contributors include:
*   **Early Modern and 19th Century Writers**: Mr. Carte and Mr. Whitaker are cited as influential modern writers. Whitaker's work on the history of Manchester is noted for covering a subject nearly as extensive as the general history of England and for exposing absurdities that had been overlooked by general historians. Other significant historians include Sh. Turner (*History of the Anglo-Saxons*) and Sir F. Palgrave (*Rise and Progress of the English Commonwealth* and *Sketch of the “Early History of England”*).
*   **Specialized Academic Studies**: Further analysis has been provided by J.M. Kemble (*The Saxons in England*), J.R. Green (*The Making of England*), and T. Hodgkin (*History of England from the Earliest Times to the Norman Conquest*). 
*   **Legal and Social Analysis**: The evolution of law and society has been examined by Sir F. Pollock and F.W. Maitland (*History of English Law*), F. Seebohm (*The English Village Community* and *Tribal Custom in Anglo-Saxon Law*), and P. Vinogradoff (*The Growth of the Manor*).
*   **Institutional and Material History**: H.M. Chadwick has contributed *Studies on Anglo-Saxon Institutions* and *The Origin of the English Nation*. Material culture is documented in the works of M. Heyne, R. Henning, and G. Baldwin Brown.

## Complementary Archaeological and Numismatic Evidence
The historical narrative is supported by physical evidence and catalogues, such as the *Catalogue of Anglo-Saxon Coins in the British Museum* by C.F. Keary and the *Collectanea Antiqua* by C. Roach Smith. Additionally, R.C. Neville has provided research on *Saxon Obsequies*.
