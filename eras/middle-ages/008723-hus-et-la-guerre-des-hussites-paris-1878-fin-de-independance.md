# Jean Hus and the Hussite Wars

The history of Bohemia from the beginning of the 15th century is documented through various historical accounts, including the works of Professor Ernest Denis. In his 1878 publication, *Jean Hus, et la guerre des Hussites*, Denis provides a continuous account of this period, which he further extends in his two-volume work, *Fin de l'independance boheme* (1890), and *La Boheme depuis la Montagne Blanche* (1903).

## The Hussite Period and Religious Thought

The era of the Hussite wars was characterized by significant religious upheaval. One notable figure from this period was Peter Chelcicky, a religious writer born in the final years of the 14th century who died in 1460. Described as a quite independent writer, Chelcicky is sometimes compared to the Tolstoy of the 15th century.

Chelcicky's philosophy was defined by a horror of bloodshed and a firm determination to accept all decrees of worldly authorities without resistance, even those that were unjust. While he was a strenuous enemy of the Church of Rome, he did not align himself with any of the Hussite parties. His primary masterpiece is titled *Sit viry* (the net of faith), and his other significant contributions include his *Postilla* and various polemical letters addressed to Bishop Nicolas of Pelhrimov and Archbishop John of Rokycan.

## Historical Documentation of Bohemia

The recording of Bohemian history has evolved through several stages of scholarship. Early historical works produced by De Florgy, Pelzl, and Pubitschka were eventually superseded by the work of Frantisek Palacky, specifically his *Geschichte von Bohmen* (published between 1844 and 1867), though Palacky's account concludes in the year 1526. Other historians, such as Gindely and Rezek, have addressed the history of Bohemia for the period following 1526.

Additionally, Professor Adolf Bachmann published a *Geschichte Bohmens* in two volumes (1899 and 1905) covering the period up to 1526, a work noted for having a strongly marked German tendency.

Regarding primary sources, the collection of historical documents known as *Fontes Rerum Bohemicarum*, published in Prague during the latter part of the 19th century, superseded earlier collections such as those by Marquard Freher (Freherus), titled *Rerum Bohemicarum Antiqui Scriptores*.

## Contemporary Accounts

The Hussite period is noted for being relatively poor in historical works written in the native language of the country. However, there were chroniclers who were contemporaries and, in some instances, eye-witnesses to the events of the Hussite wars. These contemporary writings were collected and published by Frantisek Palacky under the title *Stare ceske letopisy*.
