# THE KAAN, HIS COURT AND CAPITAL

## The Great Kaan and His Power
Cublai Kaan (also referred to as Kublai) is the reigning Great Kaan, characterized by his great puissance. His administration oversaw the expansion of his empire, including the war against the Sung Empire. Although the conflict was retarded for nearly five years by the strenuous defense of Fan-cheng and Siang-yang—two cities on opposite sides of the river Han that commanded approach lines to the Yangtsze-kiang basin—the Sung capital eventually surrendered in 1276. This victory was achieved under the command of Bayan, the Great Kaan's best lieutenant, who is described as a man of noble character and high military genius. Following the fall of the capital, the young emperor and his mother were sent as prisoners to Kaan-baligh. While some adherents of the native throne attempted to maintain their cause in Kwang-tung and Fu-kien, these efforts were extinguished by 1279.

## The Capital and Palaces
The official name of the new city is T'ai-tu, meaning "great court," though it is known to western people and Mongols as Kaan-baligh ("city of the khan"). The city was finished in 1267. The archive also references the city of Chandu in relation to the court of the Great Kaan, as well as Kemenfu, which served as the summer residence for Kúblái.

## Court Administration and Government
The government of the Great Kaan involved a distribution of the empire into twelve governments. There were twelve Barons who formed a Council of State at the capital, though these Barons also held seats in provincial governments, such as in the city of Yangchau. 

The court was a place of international interaction. The Great Kaan received visitors such as Messer Nicolo, Messer Maffeo, and Marco Polo. The Kaan showed interest in the manners of Christians and specifically inquired about the Pope of Rome. In turn, the Kaan utilized envoys and provided them with tablets of gold bearing his orders. Marco Polo himself was employed by the Kaan and learned four characters during his time there.

## Court Life and Customs
The court of the Great Kaan was marked by elaborate festivals and strict protocols of dress. The Tartars celebrate high festivals yearly on their birthdays; the Great Kaan's birthday falls on the 28th day of the September moon, marking the greatest feast of the year, second only to the New Year's Day festival. 

During these great festivals, twelve thousand Barons receive robes of cloth of gold from the Emperor, with thirteen changes of clothing each. Other accounts of Mongol court festivals, such as those of Mangku Kaan and Kuyuk Kaan, describe similar customs where nobles appeared in different colored raiment each day—ranging from white and ruby to blue—with every item from the boots to the turban matching the day's color.

The court also featured various entertainments. Following dinner, it was customary to introduce artistes into the dining hall. Additionally, the court included the presence of exotic animals, such as tame lions.

## Sports and Hunting
The Great Kaan maintained a significant interest in the chase and hunting expeditions. He enjoined his people to supply him with game and kept a variety of animals for the hunt, including wolves, leopards, lynxes, and the Búrgút eagle. The tiger was termed a "lion" by Polo, and the hunting leopard was also known as the cheeta. 

The Kaan's hunting expeditions were highly organized, involving:
* Two brothers who held charge of the Kaan's hounds.
* Specialized hawking establishments.
* The use of a litter for transport.
* The use of great tents made of sable and ermine.

Upon returning from these hunting expeditions, the Great Kaan would hold a great entertainment and court.

## The Imperial Family
The court included the Lady Jamui, the favorite Queen of Kublai. Following the fall of the Sung capital in 1276, the Princesses of the Imperial Sung family were sent to Peking, where they were treated graciously by Lady Jamui. It is noted that the Queen herself was likely one of the captive princesses raised at the Court of Khánbálik.
