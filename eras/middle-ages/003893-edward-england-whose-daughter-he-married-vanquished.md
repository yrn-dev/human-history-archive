# Edward I of England

## Political Alliances and Conflicts
Edward I of England was involved in various diplomatic and military engagements during his reign, particularly concerning the territories of France and Scotland. In 1301, an alliance was formed between Edward I and Henry III, the count of Bar. This alliance was further solidified by marriage, as Henry III had married the daughter of Edward I. However, this partnership ended in defeat when Henry III was vanquished by Philip the Fair. As a result of this victory, Philip the Fair compelled Henry III to perform homage for a specific portion of Barrois located west of the Meuse, a region known as *Barrois mouvant*.

## Relations with Scotland
Edward I's interactions with Scotland involved complex negotiations with the Scottish nobility. Alexander de Baliol, who served as the chamberlain of Scotland and was first mentioned in that role in 1287, was a key figure in these dealings. Alexander participated in the negotiations between the Scottish nobles and Edward I, which resulted in two significant agreements: the treaty of Salisbury in 1289 and the treaty of Brigham in 1290. 

By approximately 1296, Alexander de Baliol was likely deprived of his office as chamberlain and may have been imprisoned alongside his kinsman, King John de Baliol. Following these events, Alexander fought in Scotland on behalf of Edward I and was subsequently summoned to several English parliaments.

## Lineage and Descendants
The descendants of Edward I continued to influence the nobility of England and Ireland through marriage and hereditary titles. One daughter of Edward I had a daughter who married Humfrey, the earl of Hereford and Essex and high constable of England. Through this lineage, James, the son and heir of Edmund the Butler (also known as Edmund Walter), married Eleanor de Bohun in 1327. Eleanor was the granddaughter of the daughter of Edward I. On November 2, 1328, James was created an Irish earl under the title of Ormonde.
