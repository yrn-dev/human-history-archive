# Golden Legend

The *Golden Legend* is a work of Christian legends. Within its narratives, it includes stories regarding the wood destined to form the Cross. These accounts are developed from earlier Rabbinical stories, such as the legend of the staff of Adam—created in the twilight of the approaching Sabbath and passed from Adam to Enoch and the line of Patriarchs—or the legend of Seth obtaining a branch of the Tree of Life. In these traditions, the wood eventually becomes the rod of power obtained by Moses.

## Printing and Distribution in the 15th Century

During the 15th century, the production of books varied significantly in size and cost. Large and heavy volumes, such as Bibles, legal commentaries, and great choir-books, were often bound with stamped leather over wooden boards, sometimes leaving half of the boards uncovered. These works utilized extraordinarily thick and strong paper. In contrast, popular books often used a small folio size, while small thin quartos became fashionable toward the end of the century. The smallest books in use were typically prayer-books, books of hours, and Psalters.

The cost of books during this period is difficult to determine due to changes in coinage and the value of money. However, it is noted that a rubricated and illuminated vellum copy of the first printed Bible from Mainz (c. 1455), consisting of two large folio volumes, was said to be worth 100 florins. By 1467, the bishop of Aleria reported to Pope Paul II that the introduction of printing had reduced book prices to one-fifth of their previous costs.

## The Caxton Bequest

A specific historical record mentions fifteen "Legends" bequeathed by Caxton to St Margaret's, Westminster. These volumes were sold at prices ranging from 5s. to 6s. 8d. While such prices would be considered cheap for a large work like the *Golden Legend*, it is more probable that the bequest consisted of copies of the Lectionary or the Sarum *Legenda*, which were much smaller books.
