# Jerusalem

Jerusalem has been a focal point of various military campaigns, political transitions, and religious conflicts throughout history, involving figures ranging from ancient kings of Judah to medieval emperors and counts.

## Ancient Conflicts and Rulers
In the ancient period, Jerusalem was the center of the kingdom of Judah. During the reign of Josiah, king of Judah, the city served as the place where Josiah was carried after being wounded and subsequently dying following a defeat by Nechao at the valley of Megiddo. Nechao, who had marched to the Euphrates to check the progress of the Babylonians and Medes, later arrived at Jerusalem. There, he placed Eliakim (whom Nechao called Jehoiakim), a son of Josiah, upon the throne in place of Jehoahaz, who had been taken prisoner to Egypt. Nechao imposed an annual tribute on the land consisting of one talent of gold and one hundred talents of silver.

In the fifth year of the reign of Rehoboam, Jerusalem was marched against by Sesach. This campaign was prompted by the transgression of the Israelites against the Lord. Sesach arrived with sixty thousand horse and twelve hundred chariots of war, accompanied by multitudes of Ethiopians, Troglodytes, and Libyans. After Sesach mastered the strongest cities of Judah and advanced to Jerusalem, the princes of Israel and the king humbled themselves and sought protection from the God of Israel. Through the prophet Shemaiah, God informed them that because of their humility, they would not be utterly destroyed, though they would become servants of Sesach to learn the difference between his service and that of other kingdoms. Sesach eventually retired from Jerusalem, but not before plundering the king's house and the treasures of the house of the Lord, including three hundred gold shields created by Solomon.

## Medieval Acquisitions and Losses
During the 13th century, Jerusalem was the subject of several shifts in power involving European crusaders and Egyptian forces. In 1228, Emperor Frederick II embarked upon the Sixth Crusade and acquired Jerusalem. However, this control was temporary; by 1244, the Egyptian Sultan recaptured the city, an event that led to the Seventh Crusade.

## The Role of Bohemund VI
In the mid-13th century, Bohemund VI, the son of Bohemund V and Luciana, played a role in the political landscape of the region. Having established himself in Antioch in 1252, Bohemund VI procured the recognition of his nephew, Hugh II (the son of Henry I of Cyprus and Plaisance), as the king of Jerusalem in 1257. To counter the advance of the Egyptian sultan, Bohemund VI formed an alliance with the Mongols. Despite this alliance, Bohemund VI lost Antioch to Bibars in 1268 and held only the title of count of Tripoli at the time of his death in 1275.
