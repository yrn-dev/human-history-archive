# The Derivation from the Saxon Aesc and Tun

The derivation of the name from the Saxon words *aesc* (meaning ash) and *tun* (meaning an enclosed place) provides the basis for the earliest orthography of the location recorded as Estun.

## Geographic and Political Context
The location was historically identified by the addition *subtus lineam* in ancient deeds. This designation was due to its position below the line or boundary of Cheshire. This boundary once served as the frontier between the kingdoms of Mercia and Northumbria.

## Ownership and Manorial History
The manor was originally granted to Roger de Poictou by William I. However, before the reign of William I ended, the manor became part of the barony of Manchester and passed to the Greslets. From 1335 until 1515, the manor was held by the Asshetons. In 1515, it passed via marriage to the Booths of Dunham Massey. The representative of that family, the earl of Stamford, currently holds the manor.

The lord of the manor continues to hold the court-baron and court-leet, which are ancient courts held half-yearly in November and May. These courts take cognizance of breaches of agreement among tenants, with a particular focus on the cultivation of lands and the repair of roads.

## Administrative and Economic Development
Although the place enjoyed the name of borough for a long period, it did not receive a charter of incorporation until 1847. Under the Reform Act of 1832, the area returns one member. One of the markets in the location dates back to 1436.

The economic history of the area began with the ancient industry of woollen goods. Following the invention of the spinning frame, the cotton trade was introduced. By 1769, the weaving of calicoes, nankeens, and ginghams was being carried out. Eventually, the weaving of cotton yarn by machinery became the staple industry of the area.
