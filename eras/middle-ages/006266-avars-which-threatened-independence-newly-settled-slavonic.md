# Avars

The Avars were a nomadic people of a Turanian race, akin to the Huns, Tatars, Petchenegs, and Finns. They were active in Eastern and Central Europe during the early Middle Ages, where they established an extensive kingdom and conducted wide-ranging raids.

## Territorial Expansion and Invasions
The Avars were involved in a series of incursions across various regions of Europe. In the late 4th century, they devastated Macedonia, though these specific incursions were noted as having no lasting significance. Later, after 565, they devastated Dalmatia. In the region of Budapest, the Avars invaded the site of the former Roman colony of Aquincum after the Huns and Ostrogoths.

During the seventh and eighth centuries, the Avars utilized the Hungarian plain as a base to launch raids toward the south and west. Their influence extended into the southern parts of what is now Austria; the Slovenes (or Corutanes), a Slavonic people who had settled the district around 590 and were part of the kingdom of Samo, were eventually included in the extensive kingdom of the Avars.

## Conflict with Slavonic Populations
The Avars frequently clashed with the newly-settled Slavonic inhabitants of Europe. In the first half of the 7th century, the Avars launched invading hordes against the Slavonic people in Bohemia. These forces were repulsed by Samo at the pass of Neumark (also known as the pass of Neugedein), a location that served as the principal approach to Bohemia from Germany.

While the Avars threatened the independence of some Slavonic groups, they also exerted political control over others. In the lands north of the Danube, the Avars possessed the land inhabited by the Slovenes, rendering any claims of authority by the Franks over that people insignificant while Avar rule persisted.

## Religion and Culture
The Avars are characterized as pagans. Their religious beliefs were similar to the primitive religion of the Greeks and the paganism of the Germans and Slavs. This was a "manly religion" where priests, temples, and sacrifices played a minimal role. Instead, their gods were viewed as a kind of "school prefects" of more powerful beings who interfered in human affairs in an impulsive and irregular manner.

## Decline and Assimilation
The Avar kingdom began to collapse under the pressure of the Frankish empire. In 791, Charlemagne moved against the Avars after establishing authority over the Bavarians and crossing the river Enns. Following this initial attack and subsequent campaigns by his lieutenants, the Avars were finally subdued in 805. Their lands were then incorporated into the Frankish empire, leading Charlemagne to erect the East Mark to defend the empire's eastern border.

In the Hungarian plain, the Avars were eventually massacred or assimilated by the Magyars.
