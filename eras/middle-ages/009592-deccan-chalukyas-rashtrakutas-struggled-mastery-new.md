# The Struggle for Mastery in the Southern Deccan

The history of the southern Deccan is characterized by a prolonged struggle for dominance between the Chalukyas and the Rashtrakutas, two dynasties that competed for mastery over the region and its surrounding territories.

## The Rise and Dominance of the Chalukyas
During the 7th century, the Chalukyas emerged as a primary power in the southern Deccan. After overcoming the Rashtrakutas and various other rivals, the Chalukyas absorbed smaller kingdoms into their expanding empire. Their influence extended to the Bombay harbour, where the city of Puri on Elephanta Island served as a principal location during the Chalukya period (which lasted from 450 to 750).

The Chalukyas also engaged in successful conflicts with the Pallavas. This struggle culminated around 740 when Vikramaditya II captured the Pallava capital. In other regions, such as the area around Belgaum, the Chalukyas held the country from the middle of the 6th century until approximately 760.

## The Ascendancy of the Rashtrakutas
Despite the earlier dominance of the Chalukyas, they eventually succumbed to their ancient rivals, the Rashtrakutas. The Rashtrakutas succeeded to the bulk of the Chalukya dominions, which included Gujarat, where the Rashtrakutas established a branch line. Following the decline of the Chalukyas, the Rashtrakutas took control of the country around Belgaum.

For approximately two centuries, between 750 and 950, a balance of power existed between the Rashtrakutas and the Gurjaras. During this era, neither kingdom possessed sufficient strength to significantly encroach upon the other. However, the Rashtrakutas' ability to pursue large-scale conquest was hindered by internal dissensions with their branch line.

## Regional Context and Neighboring Powers
The struggle for the Deccan occurred amidst a complex landscape of other regional dynasties:
* **The North and West:** In Kathiawar, a dynasty founded by a chief named Bhatarka at Valabhi lasted from approximately 500 until 770, when it was overthrown by Arab invaders from Sind. The Gurjaras, who likely entered India with the White Huns, established power over Gujarat and north-eastern Kathiawar around 600. For a period, Malwa, Gujarat, and Kathiawar were subject to the empire of Harsha (who died in 647 or 648), though his southern boundary ended at the Nerbudda.
* **The Coasts:** The northern Konkan was held by the Mauryas of Puri, while the southerly coast was controlled by the Kadambas of Vanavasi.
* **The South:** In the Kistna region, the Chalukyas were eventually replaced by the Cholas, who were later ousted by the Reddi kings during the 14th century.

## External Pressures and Later Transitions
The political landscape of the Deccan was further impacted by external invasions. In 710-711, Arabs invaded India and established themselves in Sind by 712. While they conducted raids, they did not launch serious attacks on the Chalukya or Gurjara empires at that time, though they did destroy the city of Valabhi in 770.

Following the eventual break-up of the Rashtrakuta power, portions of their influence survived through the Rattas (875-1250). The Rattas faced a long struggle with the Kadambas of Goa, eventually overthrowing them by 1208 before the Rattas themselves succumbed to the Yadavas of Devagiri in 1250. The Yadava dynasty had been established by Bhillama I around 1187, a prince who had renounced his allegiance to the Chalukyas.
