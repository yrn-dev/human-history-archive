# Basil I. Dmitrevich and the Invasion of Timur

## Reign of Basil I. Dmitrevich
Basil I. Dmitrevich (1371-1425) was the son of Dmitri (Demetrius) Donskoi, whom he succeeded in 1389. During his tenure, the grand-duchy of Muscovy asserted its supremacy over all surrounding principalities and became practically hereditary. Basil married Sophia, the daughter of Vitovt, the grand-duke of Lithuania.

Despite the increasing power of Muscovy, Basil was required to pay tribute to the grand khan, Tokhtamuish, and received his investiture, or *yarluik*, from the Golden Horde. 

## Territorial Expansion and Treaties
Basil I. Dmitrevich expanded the territories of Muscovy through various means:
* **Annexations:** He annexed the principality of Suzdal, along with Murom, Kozelsk, Peremyshl, and other locations.
* **Vassalage:** He reduced the grand-duchy of Rostov to a state of vassalage.
* **Treaties:** Basil acquired territory from the republic of Great Novgorod by treaty.

## The Invasion of Timur
In 1395, the reign of Basil I. Dmitrevich was marked by the invasion of Timur. While Timur ruined the Volgan regions, he did not penetrate as far as Moscow. This raid proved beneficial to the Russian prince, as it nearly wiped out the Golden Horde. Following this event, the Golden Horde entered a state of anarchy that lasted for twelve years. During this period of instability, no tribute was paid to the khan; instead, vast sums of money were collected within the Moscow treasury for military purposes.

Timur, also known as Tamerlane, is categorized as one of the great conquerors of central Asian tribes. In his own memoirs, Timur mentions that the Jets used incantations to produce heavy rains to hinder his cavalry, but the storms ceased after a *Yadachi* was captured and beheaded. Beyond Russia, Timur's campaigns included the pillaging of a city in Syria in 1400 and the conquest of regions in 1369, where he established his capital at Samarkand. In the Persian history of Timur, it is noted that assemblies were frequently held to countenance the resolutions of their master, though Timur himself contented himself with the title of Sultan or Emir while one of his subjects, a descendant of Zingis, retained the title of Khan.

## Later Conflicts and Events
Following the period of anarchy in the Golden Horde, Mirza Edigei ravaged Muscovite territory in 1408, though he failed to take Moscow. By 1412, Basil found it necessary to make a visit of submission to the Horde to pay long-deferred obligations.

The reign of Basil I. Dmitrevich was also marked by a terrible visitation of the "Black Death" which decimated the population. In 1425, a significant ecclesiastical event occurred when Vitovt, grand-duke of Lithuania, elevated the Bulgarian Gregory Tsamblak to the metropolitan see of Kiev. This action had the immediate political consequence of weakening Muscovy's hold on the south-western Russian states.
