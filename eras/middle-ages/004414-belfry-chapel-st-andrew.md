# Belfry and the Chapel of St Andrew

## Belfries and Bell Towers
Belfries and bell towers serve various architectural and functional roles across different regions and eras. In Dunkirk, France, a belfry standing 290 feet high serves as a signaling tower and contains a fine peal of bells. This structure was originally the western tower of the church of St Eloi, though it is now separated from the church by a street. In Scotland, the "Old Steeple" is a noble square tower 156 feet high that once served as the belfry for a church erected in 1193 by David, earl of Huntingdon, as a thank-offering for escaping a shipwreck on the shoals at the mouth of the Tay. While the church itself perished, the bell-tower remained and underwent restoration between 1871 and 1873 by Sir Gilbert Scott.

In Moreuil, the former abbey church of a Benedictine Monastery featured a square belfry tower and a nave gable situated above two large porches. The northern front of the belfry's base contained a pointed window with rich 16th-century ornamentation. However, this tower was destroyed during the battles of 1918.

In England, the social status of belfries shifted over time. During the latter half of the 18th and first half of the 19th century, belfries were frequently associated with the lower phases of parochial life and were often an adjunct to the pothouse. This changed in the last half of the 19th century with a revival of change-ringing, which led to belfry reform and improvements.

## The Chapel of St Andrew and Related Structures
The name St Andrew is associated with various chapels and churches across Europe. In the basilica of Parenzo in Istria (c. A.D. 542), the ground plan identifies a specific area designated as the Chapel of St Andrew (marked as *i* on the archive's ground-plan). This basilica is noted for its small dimensions (121 ft. by 32 ft.), its Byzantine capitals, and its gorgeous mosaics.

In Rome, a small chapel dedicated to St Andrew is located where the Monasterium Gregorianum once stood on the side of the Caelian hill. This site, which fronts the Palatine, is currently occupied by the Camaldoli.

In Scotland, several structures are dedicated to St Andrew:
* **Dundee:** The Roman Catholic pro-cathedral of St Andrew's is built in the Early English style.
* **Dumfries:** St Andrew's is a Roman Catholic church built between 1811 and 1813 in the Romanesque style, serving as the pro-cathedral for the diocese of Galloway.
* **St Andrews:** James Kennedy, bishop of St Andrews (c. 1406-1465), built a chapel for St Salvator's college. This chapel is noted for its oaken doors bearing the bishop's arms and is frequented by students in scarlet gowns.
* **Edinburgh:** A chapel dedicated to the Order of the Thistle (which was dedicated to St Andrew) was begun in 1909 within St Giles's.

Additionally, in Northumberland, England, the church of St Andrew at Bothal contains architectural details ranging from the Early English to the Perpendicular date.
