# The Byzantine Empire: The Eternal Flame of Rome

## Overview
For centuries, popular history viewed the "Byzantine Empire" as a distinct Greek entity that emerged after the fall of the West. However, from a professional historical perspective, the Byzantine Empire was not a successor state—it *was* the Roman Empire. While the city of Rome fell to Germanic tribes in 476 CE, the imperial administration, law, and identity continued uninterrupted in the East, centered in Constantinople.

The transition from "Roman" to "Byzantine" (a term coined by historians in the 16th century) was gradual. The citizens of Constantinople called themselves *Rhomaioi* (Romans) until the very day the city fell to the Ottomans in 1453. This "Eastern Roman Empire" preserved the classical heritage of antiquity, blending Roman governance, Greek culture, and Orthodox Christianity to create a bulwark of civilization during the Early Middle Ages. While the West entered a period of fragmentation, the East maintained a centralized bureaucracy, a professional army, and a sophisticated legal system that would eventually form the basis of modern European law.

## Key Facts

*   **The Corpus Juris Civilis:** Emperor Justinian I ordered the reorganization of centuries of Roman law. This "Code of Justinian" preserved Roman legal principles and remains one of the most influential documents in the history of jurisprudence.
*   **Cultural Synthesis:** The empire was a bridge between worlds. It maintained the Roman political structure (Emperor, Senate) but adopted Greek as the official language of administration by the 7th century.
*   **The Theodosian Walls:** For over a millennium, Constantinople was the most fortified city in the world. Its triple-layered walls protected the empire from Huns, Persians, and Arabs, ensuring the survival of Roman statehood.
*   **Preservation of Antiquity:** While much of the classical Greek and Roman literature was lost in the West, Byzantine scribes painstakingly copied and preserved the works of Plato, Aristotle, and Homer.

## Did You Know?

1. **Greek Fire:** The Byzantines possessed a terrifying secret weapon—a liquid incendiary that could burn on water. The formula was a state secret so closely guarded that it was lost to history after the empire fell.
2. **The Imperial Court:** Byzantine court ritual was incredibly complex, designed to reflect the divine order of heaven. Visitors were often required to prostrate themselves (*proskynesis*) before the Emperor.
3. **The Longest Life:** The Roman Empire didn't "fall" in 476 CE; it actually lasted for nearly 1,100 years after that date.
