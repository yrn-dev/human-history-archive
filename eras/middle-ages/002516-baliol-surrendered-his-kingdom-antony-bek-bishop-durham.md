# Antony Bek and the Surrender of John de Baliol

## Antony Bek: Life and Career
Antony Bek (d. 1311) was a member of a Lincolnshire family who entered the church and received several benefices. He attracted the attention of King Edward I, who secured his election as the bishop of Durham in 1283. Bek was described as a man of great energy and courage, as well as being generous and chaste, though he was also noted for his ostentation and haughtiness. He possessed significant wealth both as a private individual and as a bishop, maintaining one of the most magnificent households and retinues in the land.

Bek's interests leaned more toward the roles of a soldier and hunter than those of a bishop, and he constructed castles at Eltham and other locations. His career was marked by significant diplomatic and military service to Edward I. Following the death of King Alexander III in 1285, Bek was employed by Edward I to handle affairs in Scotland. In 1294, he was sent on a diplomatic mission to Adolph of Nassau, the German king.

## Military Actions and the Surrender of Baliol
Antony Bek participated in the campaigns of Edward I in Scotland. In 1296, at Brechin, the bishop received the surrender of John de Baliol. Later, in 1298, Bek led one division of the English army during the battle of Falkirk.

Following the forfeiture of John de Baliol's English estates in 1296, the bishop attempted to seize Barnard Castle. Bek claimed the castle and town on the basis that they were located within his palatinate of Durham. However, Edward I denied the bishop's rights to the property and instead granted the town and castle to Guy Beauchamp, earl of Warwick.

## Conflicts and Later Years
Upon returning to England, Bek became embroiled in a quarrel with Richard de Hoton, the prior of Durham. Bek deposed and excommunicated the prior, but Richard secured the support of the king. Bek's refusal to yield in the face of other complaints led to the lasting enmity of Edward I. 

In 1302, Bek visited Rome in obedience to a command from Pope Boniface VIII. During this absence, the king seized and administered Bek's lands. The bishop recovered these lands after returning and submitting to Edward, though he continued his unrelenting hostility toward Richard de Hoton and was subsequently harassed by the king.

Bek was eventually restored to royal favor under Edward II, who appointed him lord of the Isle of Man. He died at Eltham on March 3, 1311.

## The Baliol Family and John de Baliol
The Baliol family was founded in England by a Norman baron, Guy (or Guido) de Baliol, who held fiefs in Normandy. His descendant, Bernard (or Barnard) de Baliol, built Castle Bernard (Barnard Castle) in Durham. The family played a significant role in Scottish history. John de Baliol (1249–1315) became the head of the family in 1278, inheriting extensive lands in Scotland, France, and England.

John de Baliol rose to prominence in 1290 when the Scottish throne became vacant after the death of Margaret, the "maid of Norway." He was one of three candidates for the crown. Claiming descent through his maternal grandmother, Margaret (daughter of David, earl of Huntingdon), Baliol's primary rival was Robert Bruce, earl of Annandale. 

Edward I was invited to settle the dispute. After the Scottish nobles tacitly admitted Edward's position as overlord, a court of twenty-four Englishmen and eighty Scotsmen was appointed. Based on the principles of feudal law for an indivisible inheritance, Edward decided in favor of Baliol. After swearing fealty to the English king, Baliol was crowned king of Scotland at Scone on November 30, 1292. He performed homage to Edward at Newcastle and, in January 1293, released the English king from obligations made while Scotland was in his hands. However, these relations were eventually disturbed, leading to Baliol being summoned to the English court.

## Related Figures
Antony Bek had a brother, Thomas Bek (d. 1293), who served as the treasurer of England in 1279 and became the bishop of St David's in 1280. Other family members include a Thomas Bek (1282–1347), who served as bishop of Lincoln from 1341, and another Antony Bek (1279–1343), who was the chancellor and dean of Lincoln cathedral and later the bishop of Norwich.
