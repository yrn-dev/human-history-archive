# Bridgwater

Bridgwater (historically referred to as *Briges*, *Briggewalteri*, and *Brigewauter*) is a settlement in Somerset that likely grew up during Saxon times. The town's origin as a trade centre is attributed to its strategic position at the mouth of the chief river in Somerset.

## Legal Status and Governance
The town became a mesne borough through a charter granted by John in 1201. This charter established Bridgwater as a free borough, ensuring that its burgesses were free and exempt from all tolls. Under this same charter, William de Briwere was appointed as the overlord. 

Further legal developments occurred under Henry III, who granted charters in 1227—which were subsequently confirmed in 1318, 1370, and 1380—granting the town a gild merchant. Bridgwater was later incorporated by a charter issued by Edward IV in 1468. This incorporation was confirmed in several subsequent years: 1554, 1586, 1629, and 1684. The town held parliamentary representation from 1295 until the Reform Act of 1870.

## Markets and Fairs
The charter of 1201 provided for a Saturday market and an annual fair held on the 24th of June. Over the following centuries, additional trading opportunities were added:
* **1468:** A fair at the beginning of Lent was established.
* **1554:** A second market on Thursday was added, along with fairs at Midsummer and on the 21st of September.
* **Later additions:** Charles II granted an additional fair to be held on the 29th of December.

During the medieval period, these markets and fairs were of significant importance for the sale of wine, wool, and later, cloth. However, the importance of these specific trades has since declined.

## Trade and Shipping
For centuries, Bridgwater has imported timber and corn. While the medieval trade in cloth, wool, and wine faded, the port's shipping trade experienced a revival following the construction of a new dock in 1841.
