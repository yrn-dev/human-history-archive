# Boleslaus II of Poland

Boleslaus II, known as "The Bold," served as the king of Poland from 1039 to 1081. The eldest son of Casimir I, Boleslaus II succeeded his father in 1058. While Casimir I had worked painstakingly to restore tranquillity and domestic order within the kingdom, Poland had experienced a territorial shrinkage since the era of Boleslaus I. Consequently, the primary objective of Boleslaus II's reign was to restore the importance and dignity of the Polish state.

## Foreign Relations and Military Campaigns

Boleslaus II faced immediate challenges from Bohemia, to whom Poland had recently been forced to pay tribute for Silesia, one of Poland's oldest possessions. An initial war against Bohemia proved unsuccessful and was concluded through the marriage of Boleslaus's sister, Swatawa, to the Czech king Wratyslaus II.

In other regions, Boleslaus II found more success. He provided assistance to the fugitive Magyar prince Bela, who succeeded in winning the crown of Hungary. Boleslaus II achieved significant success in the East; in 1069, he placed Izaslaus on the throne of Kiev. This action confirmed Poland's overlordship over Russia and provided Boleslaus with Russian auxiliaries, which he used to chastise his enemies, including Bohemia.

## Conflicts with the Empire and the Papacy

The conflict with Bohemia continued as Wratyslaus of Bohemia appealed to the emperor for assistance. A full-scale war between the Empire and Poland was avoided only due to the sudden rupture between Henry IV and the Holy See, and the subsequent events that led to the emperor's surrender at Canossa. While there is no evidence that Boleslaus II participated in that specific struggle, he maintained a positive relationship with Gregory VII. During this period, there were discussions regarding the dispatch of papal legates to restore order within the Polish Church.

## Coronation and Downfall

On December 26, 1076, Boleslaus II crowned himself with the royal diadem. This act is viewed as evidence that Polish kings at the time did not yet consider their titles to be entirely secure. Boleslaus's final recorded exploit was a second successful expedition to Kiev to reinstate his protege, Izaslaus.

Shortly thereafter, in 1079, Boleslaus II became an exile in Hungary, where he died around 1081. His sudden fall from power was caused by the cruel vengeance he took upon the *milites*, or the noble order. This noble class had been attempting to curb royal power, following the example of their counterparts in Bohemia. The churchmen involved in these events were led by Stanislaus Szczepanowski.
