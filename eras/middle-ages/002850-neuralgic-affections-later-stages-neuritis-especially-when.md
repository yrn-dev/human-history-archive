# Neuralgic Affections and Neuritis

Neuralgic affections and the later stages of neuritis are medical conditions that have been addressed through various historical and therapeutic approaches, ranging from the classification systems of feudal China to the balneotherapeutic practices of the spa.

## Balneotherapeutic Treatment
In the context of balneotherapy, diseases of the nervous system are generally treated with limited success. However, neuralgic affections and the later stages of neuritis are often cured or relieved by these means, particularly when these conditions are dependent on rheumatism or gout.

When advising on such treatment, it is necessary to consider the physical, psychical, and pecuniary conditions and habits of the patient. This is based on the principle that the spa must be fitted to the patient rather than the patient to the spa. Additionally, the idiosyncrasy of the individual must be taken into account, as the same morbid condition may require very different treatments in different people.

## Feudal Chinese Classification
In the feudal age, physicians in China utilized a classification system that categorized diseases according to the four seasons of the year. Under this system, neuralgic affections and headaches were classified under the season of spring.

These complaints were treated using suitable doses of ingredients selected from five classes of drugs. These drug classes were derived from:
* Grains
* Minerals
* Living creatures
* Trees
* Herbs

Each of these five classes contained medicines of five different flavors, which were believed to possess specific nourishing properties. Specifically, sour flavors were used for nourishing the bones, acid for the muscles, salt for the blood-vessels, bitter for general vitality, and sweet for the flesh. In these treatments, the pulse was highly significant; doctors were expected to be familiar with at least twenty-four varieties of pulse, though some eminent physicians claimed to distinguish up to seventy-two.
