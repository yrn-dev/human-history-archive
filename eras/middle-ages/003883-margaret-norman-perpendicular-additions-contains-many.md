# St Margaret's Church, Barking

## Architectural Overview
The church of St Margaret, located in the market-town of Barking in Essex, England, is a structure of Norman origin. Over time, the building has been modified with perpendicular additions. Within the church, there are many monuments of interest.

## Location and Town Context
Barking is situated on the river Roding, near the point where it joins the Thames. The town is positioned 8 miles east of Liverpool Street station and Fenchurch Street station in London. It is accessible via the Great Eastern and London, Tilbury & Southend railways. As of 1891, the urban district of Barking town had a population of 14,301, which grew to 21,547 by 1901.

## Local History and Landmarks
The town was historically celebrated for its nunnery, which was among the richest and oldest in England. Founded around 670 by Erkenwald, the bishop of London, the nunnery was destroyed by the Danes and subsequently restored in 970 by King Edgar. The abbess of this institution held the rank of baroness *ex officio*. At the time of the dissolution of the monasteries, the revenue of the nunnery was L1084. A turreted gateway in the perpendicular style remains from this period.

Other notable landmarks in Barking include:
* An ancient market-house that serves as a town-hall.
* Public recreation grounds and Victoria Gardens, which serve as a public pleasure-ground.

## Industry and Administration
Barking is a suffragan bishopric within the diocese of St Albans. The area is characterized by significant industrial works, including:
* Extensive rubber works.
* The Gaslight and Coke Company's works located at Beckton.
* Great sewage works at Barking Creek (the mouth of the Roding), which receive the Northern Outfall sewer from London.
* Chemical works.

The town also maintains a shipping trade, which consists principally of fish and timber.
