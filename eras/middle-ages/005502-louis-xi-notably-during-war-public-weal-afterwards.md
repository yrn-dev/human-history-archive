# Louis XI

Louis XI was a French monarch whose early reign was characterized by high-handed governance and significant conflict with the nobility, most notably during the War of the Public Weal.

## Early Reign and Conflict with the Nobility
Louis XI began his rule by applying the same assertive treatment of the nobility that had defined his previous administration in Dauphiné. His policies included forbidding nobles from hunting without his explicit permission and forcing the clergy to pay feudal dues that had been long neglected. Additionally, the king engaged in intrigues in Italy against the great houses of Orleans and Anjou.

These actions created a climate of hostility. Discharged officers of the previous king, Charles VII—including John II, duke of Bourbon, and Jean Dunois—stirred up opposition against the "new men" of the king. Simultaneously, Francis II, the duke of Brittany, became embroiled in a conflict with Louis XI due to the king's attempts to assert royal control over the practically independent duchy of Brittany.

## The War of the Public Weal
By 1465, the dissatisfied nobility formed a "league of public welfare" (also referred to as the League of the Public Weal) and declared war on the king. The league's primary ally was Charles the Bold, who later became the duke of Burgundy. The nominal leader of the rebellion was the king's eighteen-year-old brother, Charles, duke of Berry; however, he was described as a weak character who served as a tool for the rebels.

The league included nearly every great noble in France, with the exception of the counts of Eu and Vendôme, and Gaston de Foix, who maintained control of southern France for the king. While the country appeared to be on the verge of anarchy, Louis XI was saved by two factors: the refusal of the lesser gentry to join the uprising and an alliance with the citizen class, who were not deceived by the league's claims of acting for the "public weal."

The conflict involved a successful campaign by Louis in the Bourbonnais and an indecisive battle on July 16, 1465, at Montlhéry, after the Burgundians had marched on Paris. Following a short siege in Paris, a truce was reached with Charles the Bold on September 28. The war officially ended in October through the treaties of Saint Maur-les-Fossés and Conflans.

## Terms of Peace and Aftermath
The treaties resulted in the king yielding at all points. Louis XI surrendered the "Somme towns" in Picardy to Philip the Good, despite having previously paid 200,000 gold crowns for them. This concession brought the Burgundians into close proximity to Normandy and Paris. Furthermore, the king's brother, Charles, was granted Normandy as an apanage, which linked the territories of the rebellious duke of Brittany with those of Charles the Bold. Following these events, the "public weal" ceased to be a topic of discussion while the kingdom suffered plundering.

The instability of this period affected various regions and individuals:
* **Louviers:** The town experienced various troubles during the period of the League of the Public Weal under Louis XI.
* **Thomas Basin:** The bishop of Lisieux and historian, who had previously written a memorandum for Louis XI regarding the misery of the people and suggested alleviating measures, joined the league in 1464. Consequently, he fell into the king's disfavour, and Louis XI seized the temporalities of his see.
* **Antoine de Chabannes:** The count of Dammartin took part in the war of the public weal against Louis XI, though he later fought for the king against the Burgundians.

## Subsequent Relations with Burgundy and Brittany
Following the War of the Public Weal, Louis XI continued to struggle with Charles the Bold. A significant encounter occurred at Péronne in 1468, where the king was forced to confirm the duke's possession of the towns of the Somme. Charles the Bold later made a fruitless attempt on Beauvais in 1472.

Louis XI's conflicts also extended to the ducal power in Brittany. Francis II, the duke of Brittany, fought against Louis XI during the War of the Public Weal and continued his opposition afterward.
