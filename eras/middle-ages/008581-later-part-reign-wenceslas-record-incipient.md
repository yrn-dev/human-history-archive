# The Later Reign of Wenceslas IV and Incipient Religious Conflict

The later part of the reign of Wenceslas IV is characterized by the emergence of religious conflict in Bohemia. This period marked the decline of the House of Luxemburg's rule over the region. Wenceslas is described as a weak, incapable, and irresolute sovereign. During the early years of his reign, he was engaged in a continuous struggle with the powerful Bohemian nobility, who imprisoned him on two separate occasions. He also entered into a dispute with the archbishop, an event that led to the death of John of Nepomuk.

## Roots of Religious Tension
The influence of the Church of Rome in Bohemia had been weakened prior to Wenceslas's later reign during the time of King Charles. This weakening was driven by attacks on the immorality of the clergy led by pious priests, including Waldhauser and Milic. These tensions were further exacerbated by the church schism, in which rival pontiffs engaged in medieval theological strife, using wild threats and objurgations. This conflict served to further alienate the Bohemian people from the Roman church.

## The Rise of Huss
As a result of these conditions, almost the entire Bohemian nation supported the cause of Huss. While Wenceslas displayed his characteristic weakness and irresolution regarding these disputes, Queen Sophia openly supported Huss, serving as his patron and employing him as her confessor for a period of time. Some older historians have leveled exaggerated accusations against Wenceslas and Sophia, which are attributed to the fact that they furthered the cause of church reform, thereby incurring the displeasure of Romanist writers.

## The Council of Constance and Execution
Huss traveled to the council of Constance carrying a letter of safe conduct provided by Sigismund, the king of the Romans and brother of Wenceslas. Despite this, the council declared Huss a heretic, and he was burnt on July 6, 1415. Because Huss was greatly revered by the majority of the population in Bohemia, his execution led to an immediate and inevitable outbreak of civil war.

## The Protestatio Bohemorum
On September 2, 1415, the nobles of Moravia and Bohemia convened in Prague. They dispatched the *Protestatio Bohemorum* to the council, a formal protest against the execution of Huss. In this document, they described Huss as a "good, just and catholic man" who had been known for many years for his fame, conduct, and life, and asserted that he had been convicted of no offence.

The nobles further declared that anyone claiming heresy existed in Moravia and Bohemia were "sons of the devil," "vile traitors," "liars," and "calumniators of Bohemia and Moravia," labeling them the worst of all heretics. The protest concluded with a statement that the nobles would defend the law of the Lord Jesus Christ and its steadfast, humble, and pious preachers at the cost of their own blood, regardless of human decrees or fear.

## Outbreak of the Hussite Wars
The *Protestatio Bohemorum* functioned as a declaration of war against the Roman church and signaled the beginning of the Hussite wars. Although the council summoned the nobles to appear before its tribunal, they refused to do so.
