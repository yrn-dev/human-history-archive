# 1415: The Execution of John Huss and the Protestatio Bohemorum

The year 1415 was a pivotal moment in the religious and political history of Bohemia, marked by the execution of the reformer John Huss and the subsequent formal protest of the Bohemian nobility. These events occurred against a backdrop of ecclesiastical instability known as the Great Schism, during which rival pontiffs engaged in theological strife and mutual threats, further alienating the Bohemian people from the Church of Rome.

## The Rise of John Huss
John Huss was a learned Czech who, around 1396 or 1398, delivered a series of lectures at the University of Prague based on the teachings of the Englishman Wycliffe. Huss eventually became the rector of the university, and his teachings spread rapidly beyond the educated classes, generating significant popular enthusiasm. This movement was supported by a climate in which the hold of the Roman Church had already been weakened by pious priests, such as Waldhauser and Milic, who attacked the immorality of the clergy.

By 1412, the church responded to Huss's teachings by excommunicating him. Despite this, he remained highly revered by a large majority of the Bohemian population. Within the royal court, Queen Sophia openly favored Huss, serving as his confessor for a period of time, while King Wenceslas was characterized by irresolution and weakness regarding these religious disputes.

## The Council of Constance and Execution
Between 1414 and 1418, the Council of Constance was convened to settle the Great Schism and reunite Christendom. John Huss was invited to the council under a promise of safe conduct provided by the emperor (and specifically by Wenceslas's brother, Sigismund, king of the Romans). 

Upon his arrival, Huss was seized and put on trial for heresy. The council ordered him to recant certain opinions; however, Huss refused, stating that he could not recant until he was convinced of his error. When told it was his duty to recant regardless of his personal conviction if his superiors required it, he maintained his refusal. Consequently, he was declared a heretic and burnt alive on July 6, 1415.

## The Protestatio Bohemorum
The execution of Huss triggered an immediate reaction in Bohemia. On September 2, 1415, the nobles of Moravia and Bohemia convened in Prague. They drafted and sent a document to the council known as the *Protestatio Bohemorum*. In this text, the nobles strongly protested the execution, describing Huss as a "good, just and catholic man" who had been known for many years through his fame, conduct, and life, and asserting that he had been convicted of no offense.

The *Protestatio Bohemorum* further declared that anyone claiming heresy existed in Bohemia was a "vile traitor," a "liar," and a "calumniator of Bohemia and Moravia," labeling such individuals as "sons of the devil" and the "worst of all heretics." The nobles concluded by stating they would defend the "pious, humble and steadfast preachers" of the law of the Lord Jesus Christ at the cost of their own blood, regardless of human decrees or fear.

## Aftermath and the Hussite Wars
The *Protestatio Bohemorum* served as a declaration of war against the Roman church and marked the beginning of the Hussite wars. Although the council summoned the Bohemian nobles to appear before its tribunal, they refused to do so. The execution of Huss led to an insurrection of the Hussites, initiating a series of religious wars that contributed to the break-up of Latin Christendom. In response to this insurrection, Pope Martin V—who had been specially elected at Constance to lead a reunited Christendom—preached a Crusade against the Hussites.

## Other Events of 1415
Beyond the events in Bohemia, 1415 saw other significant developments in Europe:
* **The Papacy:** On July 4, 1415, Pope Gregory XII (who had reigned since 1406) presented his resignation to the Council of Constance. This act is described as the noblest of his pontificate.
* **England:** Reginald Grey, 2nd Baron Grey de Ruthyn, served as a member of the council governing England while Henry V was absent in France. Additionally, Edward Grey (1415–1457), a younger son of Reginald, was born in this year.
* **Territorial Changes:** In Brighton, a manor that had previously belonged to the earls of Arundel passed to the duke of Norfolk in 1415.
