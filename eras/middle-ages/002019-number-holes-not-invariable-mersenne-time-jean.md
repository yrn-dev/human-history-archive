# The Musette and Related Bag-pipes

The musette is a complex reed instrument that evolved during the 15th century and was fully developed by the beginning of the 17th century. It is characterized by a "rustic Watteau-like grace" and is inflated by a small bellows worked by the arm, which prevents the performer's face from being distorted by the act of inflating the bag.

## Construction and Design
The musette's design replaced long, cumbersome drones with a compact short barrel droner. This barrel contains the tubing for four or five drones, which are pierced longitudinally through the wood in parallel channels. These channels communicate with each other in groups of twos or threes to provide the necessary length for each drone. 

The instrument utilizes double "hautbois" reeds set within a wooden box or stock inside the bag. To achieve a simple harmonic bass—primarily consisting of the common chord—the length of the drone pipes is adjusted using slides or regulators known as *layettes*, which move up and down in longitudinal grooves around the barrel's circumference. The chaunter features a narrow cylindrical bore and a double reed. In the version described by Mersenne, the chaunter had eleven holes, four of which were equipped with keys, providing a compass of twelve notes from F to C (F4 to C6).

## Evolution and Improvements
The configuration of the musette was not static. Following the time of Mersenne, Jean Hotteterre, a court musician of the *Musique de la Grande Ecurie* who played the *dessus de hautbois*, introduced improvements to the musette's drones. His son, Martin Hotteterre, further modified the instrument by adding a second chaunter. This second pipe, known as the *petit chalumeau*, was shorter than the first and was attached to it rather than being inserted into the stock. While the *grand chalumeau* possessed seven keys, eight finger-holes, and a vent-hole in the bell, the *petit chalumeau* had six keys. All of these keys were operated by the little finger.

## Related Instruments and Variations
The musette is related to several other bag-pipe varieties:

*   **The Surdelina (or Sampogna):** Described by Mersenne as the *musette de Naples*, this instrument had a very complicated construction. It featured two drones, each with ten keys, and two chaunters with narrow cylindrical bores containing both keys and finger-holes. All four pipes were fixed in a single stock and used double reeds, with the bag inflated by bellows. Mersenne attributed its invention to Dom Julio, Vincenze, and Jean Baptiste Riva, who lived in Paris in 1620, though Mersenne himself made alterations to the original.
*   **The Cornemuse and Chalemie:** These were the bag-pipes used in the Netherlands, Italy, and France before the musette. One variety used by shepherds as a solo instrument featured a leather bag inflated by a valved blow-pipe, a large drone (*gros bourdon*) of 2-1/2 ft, and a small drone (*petit bourdon*) of 1 ft. Its chaunter had a conical bore and eight holes. A second variety was played in concert with the *Hautbois de Poitou* and featured a single drone with a double reed rather than a beating-reed.
*   **Northumbrian and Lowland Bag-pipes:** The Lowland bag-pipe is distinguished from the Highland variety by being blown by bellows. The Northumbrian (or Border) bag-pipe is also blown by bellows and features a chaunter stopped at the lower end, rendering the pipe silent when all holes are closed. It has seven finger-holes (including one for the thumb) and a varying number of keys. Its four drones are fixed in one stock and tuned via stoppers.

## General Bag-pipe Mechanics
Broadly, the bag-pipe serves as a link between the syrinx and the primitive organ by using a reservoir for wind-supply. It consists of an air-tight leather bag with three to five apertures containing stocks that hold the pipes. These pipes are categorized into three types: the blow-pipe for inflation, the chaunter (melody-pipe) which uses a double-reed and possesses lateral holes, and the drones, which are cylindrical pipes producing a single fixed note.
