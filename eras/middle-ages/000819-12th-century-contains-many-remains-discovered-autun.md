# Autun

Autun (historically known as *Augustodunum*) is a town located in east-central France. It serves as the capital of an arrondissement in the department of Saone-et-Loire and is situated on the slope of a hill at the foot of the Arroux, approximately 62 miles southwest of Dijon.

## Origins and Roman History
Autun succeeded the ancient Gaulish town of Bibracte as the capital of the Aedui during the reorganization of Gaul by Augustus. Augustus relocated the inhabitants from Bibracte—a hilltop settlement 2,500 feet above sea-level featuring a temple of Bibractis and various workshops—to the new town of Augustodunum in an effort to destroy free native traditions.

Under Roman rule, Autun was a flourishing center renowned for its schools of rhetoric and covered double its current extent. The town's former greatness is evidenced by numerous Roman remains, including:
* Two well-preserved stone gateways, the Porte St Andre and the Porte d'Arroux, both featuring four archways and surmounting arcades.
* Remains of aqueducts and old ramparts.
* An amphitheatre and a theatre.
* A square tower known as the Temple of Janus.
* A pyramid in the nearby village of Couhard, which likely served as a sepulchral monument.

During the reign of Constantine, the emperor visited Autun and remitted arrears of tribute, reducing the assessment from twenty-five thousand to eighteen thousand heads. Despite this, the territory suffered from oppressive taxation, leading to uncultivated land and many provincials living as outlaws.

## Medieval Period and the Duchy of Burgundy
In the centuries following the Roman era, Autun faced attacks from the Normans, the Saracens, and barbarians. In 880, the counts of Autun became dukes of Burgundy, and the town served as the residence for these dukes until 1276.

The duchy was originally created by Carolingian kings to resist Boso and was granted to Boso's brother, Richard the Justiciary, who was the count of Autun. Initially, this duchy included the countships of Sens, Nevers, Langres, Chalon-sur-Saône, Mâcon, and Autun. After a thirteen-year war ending in 1015, King Robert II reunited the duchy with the kingdom, eventually bestowing it in 1032 upon his brother Robert. This established the first ducal house of Burgundy, which flourished until 1361. Notable later dukes include Hugh III (1162–1193), Odo III (1193–1218), and Hugh IV (1218–1272).

## Architecture and Art
The town contains several significant religious and civic structures. The cathedral of St Lazare, located in the highest part of the town, was originally a chapel for the residence of the dukes of Burgundy. While it belongs mainly to the 12th century, the chapels and Gothic central tower were added in the 15th century by Nicolas Rolin, the chancellor of Burgundy. Key artistic features of the cathedral include a painting by Ingres of the martyrdom of St Symphorien (which occurred in Autun in 179) and a sculptured group of the Last Judgment on the tympanum above the west door. In the cathedral square is the Renaissance-style fountain of St Lazare.

Other notable structures include:
* The chapel of St Nicolas, dating to the 12th century, which houses many remains discovered at Autun.
* The hotel Rolin, a 15th-century house that holds the collections of the "Aeduan literary and scientific society."
* Modern buildings including the theatre, the law-court, and the hotel de ville, the latter of which contains a museum of paintings.

## Administration and Economy
Autun is the seat of a bishopric, as well as tribunals of commerce and first instance. Its educational and training facilities include a communal college, an ecclesiastical seminary, and a cavalry school. 

The town serves as a commercial center for much of the Morvan, engaging in significant trade in cattle and timber. Local industries include:
* Metal-founding and marble-working.
* The manufacture of furniture and machinery.
* Leather manufacture.
* The extraction of oil from bituminous schist found in the surrounding area.
