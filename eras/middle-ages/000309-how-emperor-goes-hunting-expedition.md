# How the Emperor Goes on a Hunting Expedition

The hunting expeditions of the Great Kaan are characterized by immense scale, strict legal protections for game, and a highly organized military-style structure. These expeditions typically occur over a period of three months, with the Emperor tarrying in a specific location from March to the middle of May. Following this period, he returns to his capital city of Cambaluc, the capital of Cathay, while continuing to engage in hawking and hunting throughout the journey.

## Regulations and Conservation of Game
To ensure an abundance of animals for the imperial sport, the Emperor enforces strict laws across his territories. From the month of March to the month of October, it is forbidden for anyone to hunt four specific animals: the buck, the stag, the hare, and the roe. The population is so obedient to these commands that an individual would not touch one of these animals even if it were found asleep by the roadside. This restriction allows the game to multiply rapidly, ensuring the Emperor has as much as he desires. Outside of this March-to-October window, others may take these animals as they wish.

Furthermore, in the area surrounding the Emperor's hunting spot, a radius of 20 days' journey is established where no one, regardless of their status, is permitted to keep hounds or hawks.

## The Masters of the Hounds
The management of the hunt is overseen by two brothers, Baian and Mingan, who hold the title of *Chinuchi* (or *Cunichi*), meaning "The Keepers of the Mastiff Dogs." These two Barons are responsible for supplying the Kaan’s Court with 1,000 head of game daily—consisting of birds or beasts, excluding quails—from October until the end of March. They are also tasked with providing fish, with three fish counted as equivalent to one head of game.

Each brother commands a body of 10,000 men. To ensure they are easily recognized during the chase, one group is dressed in red livery and the other in blue. Within these forces, 2,000 men from each group are specifically charged with the care of one or more great mastiffs.

## Execution of the Chase
When the Emperor goes hunting, the two Barons deploy their forces in a massive pincer movement. One Baron leads his 10,000 men and approximately 5,000 dogs to the right, while the other leads his party to the left. The hunters move abreast of one another, creating a line that extends over a full day's journey, ensuring that no animal can escape.

As the Lord rides across the plains for fowling, the mastiffs drive the game toward him. Packs of hounds are seen pursuing various beasts, such as stags or bears, running them down on either side of the Emperor.

## Other Imperial Hunting Traditions
While the Great Kaan's expeditions are defined by their organization, other historical records indicate different uses of the hunting expedition. In Japan, hunting trips were occasionally used as a cover for political assassination; for example, Prince Oshiwa was lured on an expedition and killed by Yuryaku on the moor of Kaya, and another son of Richu was invited to a hunt and treacherously shot by Prince Ohatsuse.

In the case of Alexander, hunting expeditions were viewed as a means of enduring hardship. Alexander exposed himself to fatigues and hardships during his hunts to set an example for his friends, who had fallen into luxury. One notable event involved Alexander slaying a great lion, a scene later immortalized in bronze figures at Delphi by the sculptors Lysippus and Leochares, as well as by Kraterus.
