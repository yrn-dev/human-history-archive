# BOKENAM, OSBERN AND BONNEVAL, CLAUDE ALEXANDRE

## Osbern Bokenam
Osbern Bokenam (1393?–1447?) was an English author who, according to his own records, was born on October 6, 1393. There is scholarly suggestion from Dr. Horstmann that Bokenam may have been a native of Bokeham (currently known as Bookham) in Surrey, deriving his name from that location. However, a concluding note in his work *Lives of the Saints* describes him as "frere Austyn of Stoke Clare," a "Suffolke man."

Bokenam's literary output includes a series of thirteen legends focusing on women and holy maidens. These works were primarily composed in stanzas of seven or eight lines, with nine of the legends featuring prologues. In his writing, Bokenam utilized the Suffolk speech and adopted a plain style; he justified this lack of decoration by stating that authors such as Lydgate, Gower, and Chaucer had already "plucked" the finest flowers. Bokenam was a follower of Lydgate and Chaucer, and his work was likely influenced by Chaucer's *Legend of Good Women*. His primary source was the *Legenda Aurea* by Jacobus de Voragine, the archbishop of Genoa, whom Bokenam refers to as "Januence."

Among his works, the *Vita Scae Margaretae, virginis et martiris* was written for Thomas Burgh, a monk at Cambridge and a friend of the author. Other legends were dedicated to pious women who wished to read the history of their name-saints. A unique copy of his work is held in the British Museum as Arundel MS. 327. This copy was finished in 1447 and presented by the scribe, Thomas Burgh, to an unnamed convent for the purpose of remembering him and his sister, Dame Betrice Burgh.

Bokenam's poems were edited in 1835 for the Roxburghe Club as *Lyvys of Seyntys* and later by Dr. Carl Horstmann in 1883 as *Osbern Bokenams Legenden*. Both editions contain a dialogue in English and Latin sourced from Dugdale's *Monasticon Anglicanum*. This dialogue, occurring at the grave of Dame Johan of Acres between a Frere and a Secular, details the lineal descent of the lords of the honour of Clare from 1248 to 1356. Bokenam also traveled as a pilgrim to Santiago de Compostela in 1445 and visited Italy on at least two occasions.

## Claude Alexandre de Bonneval
Claude Alexandre, Comte de Bonneval (1675–1747), was a French adventurer and descendant of an old Limousin family, born on July 14, 1675. He began his military career at age thirteen by joining the Royal Marine Corps, moving into the army three years later, where he eventually commanded a regiment. He demonstrated military ability and courage while serving under Vendome, Villeroi, and Catinat in Italian campaigns, as well as under Luxemburg in the Netherlands.

Bonneval's career was marked by a volatile temperament. In 1704, his insolence toward the minister of war led to a court-martial and a death sentence, which he avoided by fleeing to Germany. With the assistance of Prince Eugene, he secured a general's command in the Austrian army. He fought with distinction against Turkey and France, participating in the battle of Malplaquet and sustaining severe wounds at Peterwardein. Following these events, the French proceedings against him were dropped, allowing him to visit Paris and marry the daughter of Marshal de Biron.

Despite his success at Belgrade, Bonneval's relationship with Prince Eugene soured, resulting in his appointment as master of the ordnance in the Low Countries. A subsequent quarrel with the marquis de Prie, Eugene's deputy governor, led to Bonneval's confinement and another court-martial. He was again sentenced to death, though the emperor commuted this to banishment and one year of imprisonment.

Upon his release, Bonneval entered the service of the Turkish government, where he took the name Ahmed and professed the Mahommedan faith. As a pasha, he was tasked with commanding and organizing the artillery. He provided valuable service to the sultan during wars with Nadir Shah and Russia. Although he was rewarded with the governorship of Chios, he eventually fell under the suspicion of the Porte and was banished to the shores of the Black Sea. He died while contemplating a return to Christianity and Europe. It is noted that the *Memoirs* published under his name are spurious.
