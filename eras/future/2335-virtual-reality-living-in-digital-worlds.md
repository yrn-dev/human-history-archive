# The Great Migration: The Era of Digital Existence

## Overview
From the perspective of the 24th century, the transition from physical-centric living to "Digital Existence" is viewed as the most significant sociological shift since the Neolithic Revolution. What began in the early 21st century as a tool for gaming and niche industrial training evolved into a comprehensive societal structure. By the mid-22nd century, the "Great Migration"—the process by which a significant portion of the human population spent more than 90% of their waking hours in simulated environments—became a reality.

Historians categorize this era by the blurring of the line between "authentic" and "simulated" experiences. The shift was driven by the convergence of high-bandwidth neural interfaces and the collapse of physical urban centers due to environmental pressures. While early adopters sought escapism, later generations viewed digital worlds as the primary site for governance, commerce, and kinship.

## Key Facts
*   **The Neural Shift:** The pivotal moment in this history was the move from *optical* VR (goggles) to *neural* VR. This allowed for "Full-Dive" experiences, where sensory input was intercepted at the brainstem, rendering the physical body dormant.
*   **The Socio-Economic Divide:** A major point of historical debate among scholars is the "Physicality Gap." Some historians argue that the migration to digital worlds was a democratic liberation from physical scarcity. Others contend it was a tool of class stratification, where the wealthy maintained physical estates while the working class were relegated to "server-slums."
*   **Digital Sovereignty:** By the 22nd century, the concept of a "nation-state" shifted from geographic borders to server protocols. This led to the first "Protocol Wars," where conflicts were fought over data ownership and bandwidth rather than land.

## Did You Know?
1.  **The Sensorama:** The roots of digital living trace back to Morton Heilig’s 1962 "Sensorama," a mechanical booth that stimulated multiple senses to simulate a bike ride through New York City.
2.  **The Latency Crisis:** Early digital societies nearly collapsed during the "Latency Crisis" of the late 21st century, where desynchronization between neural inputs caused widespread "simulation sickness" and psychological dissociation.
3.  **The Legacy Archives:** To prevent the total loss of human history, the "Physicality Preservation Act" was passed, ensuring that a small percentage of the population remained "unplugged" to maintain physical libraries and seed banks.
