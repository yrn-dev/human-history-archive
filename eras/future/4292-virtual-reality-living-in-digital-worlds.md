# The Great Migration: The Era of Digital Habitation

## Overview
From the perspective of the late 22nd century, the transition from "using" virtual reality (VR) to "living" within digital worlds represents one of the most profound sociological shifts in human history. What began in the early 21st century as a niche tool for gaming and industrial training evolved into a comprehensive socio-economic ecosystem. This era, often termed the "Great Migration," saw the decoupling of human consciousness and social identity from physical geography.

The shift was driven by the convergence of high-bandwidth neural interfaces and the collapse of traditional urban centers during the mid-century climate crises. As physical travel became costly and environmentally prohibitive, the "Digital World" ceased to be a simulation and became a primary residence. Historians continue to debate whether this transition constituted a liberation of the human spirit or a surrender to corporate-owned simulated realities.

## Key Facts
*   **The Sovereignty Debate:** A central conflict among historians is the "Ownership Paradox." While early digital worlds were owned by private conglomerates (the successors to companies like Meta and Epic Games), the late 21st century saw the rise of Decentralized Autonomous Worlds (DAWs), where users held collective governance.
*   **Economic Transition:** The global economy shifted from a resource-based model to an experience-based model. "Digital Architects" became the most influential professional class, designing the physics and aesthetics of the worlds where billions spent their waking hours.
*   **Biological Maintenance:** The era was characterized by the "Soma-Support" industry—massive physical facilities designed to maintain the biological bodies of "residents" while their minds remained immersed in digital strata.

## Did You Know?
*   **The First "Digital Native":** Historians point to the generation born around 2020 as the first to treat virtual spaces as equally "real" as physical ones, a phenomenon documented in early 21st-century sociological studies on the "Metaverse."
*   **Sensory Lag:** Early VR users suffered from "vestibular mismatch" (motion sickness), a biological hurdle that wasn't fully solved until the development of direct neural stimulation.
*   **The Digital Divide:** The transition was not universal; a significant "Physicalist" movement emerged, rejecting digital habitation in favor of traditional agrarian lifestyles.
