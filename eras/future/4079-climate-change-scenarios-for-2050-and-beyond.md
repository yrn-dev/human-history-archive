# The Great Transition: Climate Projections for 2050 and Beyond

## Overview
From the perspective of historical climatology, the mid-21st century represents a critical "inflection point" in human civilization. Based on the Shared Socioeconomic Pathways (SSPs) developed by the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC), the trajectory of the planet beyond 2050 depends entirely on the decarbonization efforts undertaken in the early 2020s. 

Historians and climate scientists categorize future scenarios into two primary schools of thought: the "Mitigation Path," where global warming is capped near 1.5°C or 2°C, and the "High-Emission Path," where temperatures rise by 3°C or more. The debate among experts centers not on *if* the climate will change, but on the *velocity* of the feedback loops—such as the thawing of Arctic permafrost—and whether these "tipping points" are inevitable regardless of human intervention.

## Key Facts
*   **Sea Level Rise:** According to the IPCC Sixth Assessment Report, sea levels are projected to continue rising throughout the 21st century. Depending on the scenario, coastal cities may face increases ranging from 0.3 to 1.0 meters by 2100, threatening megacities in Asia and North America.
*   **The 1.5°C Threshold:** The Paris Agreement (2015) established 1.5°C as a critical limit to avoid the most catastrophic effects. Current data suggests this threshold may be breached in the early 2030s, leading to a debate over whether "overshoot" (temporarily exceeding the limit) is a manageable risk.
*   **Agricultural Displacement:** Future scenarios predict a significant shift in "breadbasket" regions. While northern latitudes (Siberia, Canada) may see increased arable land, equatorial regions face systemic crop failures due to extreme heat and erratic precipitation.

## Did You Know?
1. **Tipping Points:** Scientists warn of "cascading tipping points," where the collapse of the West Antarctic Ice Sheet could trigger a chain reaction affecting global ocean currents (AMOC).
2. **Carbon Capture:** Many 2050 "Net Zero" models rely on Negative Emission Technologies (NETs), which are currently not yet scalable to the required industrial level.
3. **The Methane Bomb:** The thawing of permafrost in the Arctic could release massive amounts of methane—a greenhouse gas far more potent than CO2—potentially accelerating warming beyond human control.
