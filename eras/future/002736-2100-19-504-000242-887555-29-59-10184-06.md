## Astronomical Equations and the Calendar
In the study of solar and lunar equations counting from the Reformation, the year 2100 is identified as a significant point of convergence. The solar equation occurs in specific years, including 1700, 1800, 1900, 2100, 2200, 2300, and 2500. Separately, the lunar equation occurs in years such as 1800, 2100, 2400, 2700, 3000, 3300, 3600, and 3900.

These equations affect the epacts: the solar equation diminishes epacts by unity, while the lunar equation augments them by unity. In the year 2100, both the solar and lunar equations occur together. Because they occur simultaneously, they compensate for one another, resulting in no change to the epacts.

## Ancient Chronologies and Dynasties

*   **Winckler (1905):** Proposed that the first dynasty ended and the second dynasty began approximately in 2100 B.C.

*   **Delitzsch (1907):** Estimated the transition between the first and second dynasties occurred around 2120 B.C.
*   **Maspero (1897):** Dated the end of the first dynasty to 2082 B.C.
*   **Lehmann-Haupt (1903):** Placed the end of the first dynasty in 2009/8 B.C.

*   **Rost (1897, 1900):** Suggested the first dynasty ended between 1928 and 1941 B.C.
*   **Hommel (1886, 1895, 1901):** Provided various dates, including a transition in 1731 B.C. or a range of 2050–1752 B.C.

## Babylonian Land Surveys
Archaeological evidence from Babylonia indicates that cadastral surveys for taxation were utilized as early as 3800 B.C., during the age of Sargon of Akkad. The British Museum holds a series of circular clay tablets containing land surveys that date back to either 2300 or 2100 B.C. One such tablet provides a rough depiction of lower Babylonia, showing the region encircled by the "salt water river" known as Oceanus.
