# The Great Expansion: A History of Humanity’s Multi-Planetary Era

## Overview
From the perspective of the 25th century, the transition of *Homo sapiens* from a single-planet species to a galactic civilization is viewed as the most pivotal epoch in human history. What began as a series of competitive nationalistic "Space Races" in the 20th century evolved into a coordinated effort of planetary survival and curiosity. 

The colonization process occurred in three distinct waves: the Lunar Industrialization, the Martian Terraforming, and the Outer Rim Expansion. The Moon served as the essential "proving ground," where humanity learned to harvest regolith and manage low-gravity physiology. Mars, however, became the spiritual and political heart of the expansion, evolving from a series of claustrophobic lava-tube colonies into a world of breathable air and flowing water. As we pushed beyond the asteroid belt, the discovery of subsurface oceans on Europa and Enceladus shifted the goal from mere colonization to the study of exogenous biology.

## Key Facts

*   **The Lunar Gateway:** The Moon is no longer a destination but a transit hub. The "Lunar Elevator," completed in the 22nd century, drastically reduced the cost of lifting materials from Earth's gravity well, making deep-space travel economically viable.
*   **Martian Sovereignty:** The "Mars Accords" of 2112 marked the first time a planetary colony declared independence from Earth. This led to a distinct Martian culture characterized by a communalist society and a heavy reliance on bio-engineering.
*   **Terraforming Ethics:** The colonization of Mars sparked the "Great Bio-Debate." Historians note the tension between those who wished to preserve the planet in its pristine state and the "Greeners" who sought to transform it into a second Earth.
*   **The O'Neill Cylinders:** Beyond planetary surfaces, millions of humans now reside in rotating space habitats. These artificial worlds provide simulated gravity and diverse ecosystems, serving as the primary residential zones for the Asteroid Belt's workforce.

## Did You Know?

1. **Biological Divergence:** Due to lower gravity on Mars and the Moon, "Space-Born" humans are typically taller and have lower bone density than their Earth-born ancestors, leading to the emergence of a distinct human subspecies.
2. **The Water Gold Rush:** In the early era of colonization, water ice found in the lunar south pole and Martian craters was more valuable than gold, as it provided both life support and hydrogen fuel.
3. **Time Dilatation:** Because of the vast distances and the use of near-light-speed propulsion for Outer Rim travel, "Time Lag" became a social issue, where explorers would return home to find their children had aged more than they had.
