# The Great Migration: The Era of Digital Residency

## Overview
From the perspective of the late 22nd century, the transition from "using" virtual reality to "living" within digital worlds represents one of the most profound sociological shifts in human history. What began as a tool for gaming and industrial simulation in the early 21st century evolved into a systemic restructuring of human existence. This era, often termed the "Great Migration," saw the decoupling of economic productivity and social identity from physical geography.

The shift was driven by the convergence of high-bandwidth neural interfaces and the collapse of traditional urban centers during the mid-century climate crises. As the physical environment became increasingly volatile, the "Digital Commons" offered a stable, customizable alternative for governance, education, and commerce. However, this transition was not seamless. Historians continue to debate the "Cognitive Divergence" period—a era where a socioeconomic divide emerged between the "Physicals" (those who maintained traditional biological lifestyles) and the "Synthetics" (those who spent over 90% of their waking hours in simulated environments).

## Key Facts
*   **The Economy of Experience:** The global economy shifted from a commodity-based system to an "Experience Economy." Value was no longer derived from physical goods but from the architecture of unique sensory simulations.
*   **The Sovereignty Debate:** A major point of historical contention remains the "Server Wars" of the 2070s. Historians argue whether these conflicts were genuine political revolutions or merely algorithmic glitches orchestrated by the platform providers to drive engagement.
*   **Biological Atrophy:** The medical community of the 21st century struggled with "Sensory Drift," a condition where the brain began to reject physical stimuli in favor of the hyper-saturated colors and sounds of digital worlds.

## Did You Know?
1.  **Early Roots:** The concept of virtual reality dates back much further than the digital era; the "Sensorama," created by Morton Heilig in the 1950s, was one of the first attempts to stimulate multiple senses.
2.  **The Metaverse Precursors:** Early 21st-century attempts at "Metaverses" were limited by "latency" (lag), a concept that seems archaic to modern neural-link users.
3.  **Legal Precedents:** The first legal battles over virtual property rights occurred in the early 2000s within early virtual worlds like *Second Life*.
