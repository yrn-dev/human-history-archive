# Shorawak

Shorawak is a geographical point of convergence for two major hydrographic systems in the region of Southern Baluchistan. It serves as the meeting point for the Lora system, which drains the Pishin valley to the north, and the southern Lora affluents.

## Regional Geography and Hydrography
Shorawak is situated in an area where the drainage generally trends toward the west and south, moving either toward the central swamps of Mashkel and Lora or toward the Arabian Sea. The region is characterized by a complex network of internal communication formed by considerable streams. These include the Dasht and the Hingol (ancient *Tomerus*), which drain Makran, as well as the Purali (ancient *Arabus*), which drains the low-lying flats of Las Bela, and the Hab river, which marks the boundary to the west of Karachi.

An exception to the general south-west drainage trend is the Mulla river. The Mulla carries floods from the Kalat highlands into the Gandava basin, serving as one of the most significant ancient highways connecting Kandahar to the Indus plains.

## The Kalat Hub
The fortress of Kalat acts as a central "hub" for the area's radiating water systems, including the Mulla, the Bolan, and the southern Lora affluents. Kalat is positioned approximately midway between the sources of the Mulla and the Bolan, located near a small tributary of the Lora. It sits on the western edge of a cultivated plain surrounded by hills, at an elevation of about 6,800 feet above sea level.

The landscape surrounding Kalat is defined by distinct geological formations:
* **To the North:** The Sarawan ridges consist of long sweeping lines that enclose narrow, fertile valleys and extend south-west toward the edge of the Kharan desert.
* **To the East and South:** The rugged bands of Jalawan are present; the Mulla rises within these bands and reaches the Gandava plain through a series of magnificent defiles.

Routes converging on Kalat from the south typically follow the strike of denuded hill ridges, traveling through narrow wooded valleys with easy grades.

## Termination of the Lora System
After the northern Pishin valley and the southern Lora affluents unite at Shorawak, the combined waters flow westward. From this point, the systems lose themselves in the swamps and desert located to the west of Nushki, on the road leading to Seistan.

Further south, approximately sixty miles from Kalat and beyond the sources of the Mulla, another hydrographic system begins, encompassing all of south-western and southern Baluchistan. This area is bordered to the west by the Kharan desert, characterized by intermittent river channels that are frequently lost.
