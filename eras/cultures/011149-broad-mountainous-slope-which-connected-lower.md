# The Broad Mountainous Slope of Tibet

The geography of Tibet is characterized by a mean elevation of 15,000 feet above sea level. A defining physical feature of the region is the broad mountainous slope that connects the high plateau of Tibet with the lower levels of Hindostan. This specific slope contains the mountain ranges known as the Himalaya.

## Regional Slopes and Designations
While the southern slope connects to Hindostan, the northern slope of Tibet descends toward the central plains of the Gobi. This northern slope is generally referred to by the name Kuen-lun. According to archive records, Kuen-lun is apparently a Chinese designation and is not the name used locally for these mountains. Historically, a distinct civilization developed and spread from the slopes of the Kuen-lun mountains and the then-fertile (now desolate) valley of the Tarim, moving into the valley of the Yang-tse-kiang and down the course of the Hwang-ho.

## Climate and Vegetation
The climate of Tibet is marked by extreme rigour, combining great drought with great cold. These conditions render the country essentially very poor, with the majority of the region being little more than desert. 

Vegetation across the region is most scanty. Trees are rarely found, except in sheltered spots where they have been artificially planted. This stark environment influences the distribution and lifestyle of the population:
* **Lower and warmer valleys:** Inhabitants live in houses and engage in agriculture.
* **Higher regions:** The population consists of nomadic shepherds who are thinly scattered over a large area.
