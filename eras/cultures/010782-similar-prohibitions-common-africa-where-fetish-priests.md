# Fetish and Primitive Religious Practice

## Definition and Nature of Fetish
Fetish is described as a form of "savage science," representing the earliest human attempts to understand the system of cause and effect. In this framework, primitive man often connected effects with incorrect causes; for example, believing that eating the heart of a valiant enemy would grant the consumer strength. This system differs from modern science in that it is entirely uncritical and unsystematic, which leads to frequent errors in association.

## The Role of the Fetish Priest
The first priests emerged from the human tribe as individuals with steadier and more forceful minds who shared the imaginations and fears of their peers. These experts in fetish, known as Medicine Men, asserted themselves to command, prescribe, and advise. Their role involved interpreting dreams, warning others, and performing "complicated hocus pocus" intended to avert calamity or bring luck. In these early societies, religion was characterized less by spiritual belief and more by practice and observance, with the priest dictating an arbitrary practical science.

## Fetish Practices in Africa
In various African contexts, fetishism has manifested through specific social and dietary structures:

*   **Dietary Restrictions:** In some parts of Africa, fetish priests are frequently reduced to a diet consisting of roots and herbs.
*   **The Krumen:** Among the Krumen—a robust negro people of the West Coast of Africa living along the coast of Liberia—the head fetish-man serves as the president of the government. He is responsible for guarding national symbols, and his house serves as a sanctuary for offenders until their guilt is proven.
*   **The Ba-Luba and Bena Riamba:** The Ba-Luba are a cultivated Bantu negroid race in Central Africa. In 1870, a politico-religious revolution led to the establishment of a secret society called the *Bena Riamba*. Members of this society abandoned their old fetish worship in favor of a cult of hemp-smoking and a form of communism based on the blood-brotherhood of all members.

## Transition to Missionary Religions
Historically, religion based on fetish, mystical sacrifice, and initiated priests is characterized as a system that ruled the common man through fear, treating him like a slave. This is contrasted with the development of missionary-teaching religions, which shifted the focus toward the individual soul and personal conscience. 

Figures such as the Hebrew prophets, Jesus and his disciples, Mani, and Muhammad are identified as exhorters who brought the personal conscience face to face with God. Specifically, the Hebrew prophets during the period around the Babylonian captivity introduced a power of individual moral appeal that challenged the "slavish loyalties" and fetish sacrifices that had previously bridled the human race.
