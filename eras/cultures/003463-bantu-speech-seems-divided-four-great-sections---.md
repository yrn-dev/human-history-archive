# Bantu Languages

The Bantu languages constitute a family of speech characterized by a striking homogeneity. This consistency is noted as being particularly distinct when compared to the linguistic confusion found in Africa north of the Bantu borderland. The family is defined by a pronominal-prefix structure, a linguistic type that is considered to have been created well before the exodus from the Bantu mother-land.

## Geographical Classification
Broadly speaking, the domain of Bantu speech is divided into four great sections:
*   **The Great Lakes and East Coast:** This section extends down to and includes the Zambezi basin.
*   **The South-Central Group:** This group includes Bechuana-Zulu.
*   **The South-West:** This region spans from the southern part of the Belgian Congo to the Angola-Congo coast and Damaraland.
*   **The Western Group:** This section includes Cameroon languages and all Northern and Central Congo languages, likely including group No. 2 of the Semliki river and Albert Nyanza.

While these broad categories exist, there are small patches of languages spoken by a few villages that are emphatically Bantu but possess isolated characters, making them difficult to include in these primary groups.

## Archaic Dialects and Evolution
The most archaic dialects—those possessing typical structure and archaic form—are widely spread. Notable examples include the speech of Uganda, Unyoro, Ruwenzori, Mount Elgon, the Bemba country (south-west of Tanganyika), and the north coast of Tanganyika. Other archaic types include the Subiya speech of the western Zambezi, the Ci-bodzo of the Zambezi delta, and the Nkonde and Kese dialects of the north and north-east coasts of Lake Nyasa. 

In Damaraland, Oci-herero contains typical features and many original Bantu roots, despite some strange divergencies. On the east coast, Kimakonde is considered a primitive tongue; Ki-swahili of Zanzibar is primitive in its roots, though not in its prefixes. The Zulu-Kaffir language was once regarded as the most archaic type before the languages of the Great Lakes were known; it now is seen as unusually divergent from the typical Bantu in some features, exhibiting recent deviations in phonetics and vocabulary.

## Linguistic Structure and Prefixes
Bantu languages are characterized by a system of prefixes and concord. In the most archaic speech, such as that of Mount Elgon, the second prefix is *Baba-* in its definite form (appearing as *Ngaba* in Zulu-Kaffir), with a concord of *-ba-*. This plural prefix may degenerate into *A-*, *Ma-*, *Wa-*, or *Va-*.

Other prefix examples include:
*   **4th Prefix:** *Mi-*, with concords such as *-mi-*, *-ji-*, *-i-*, or *-gi-*.
*   **5th Prefix:** The older form is *Di-*, though the common modern form is *Li-*. It may become *Ji-*, *I-*, *Ni-*, or *Ri-*.
*   **7th Prefix:** *Ki-*, which may transition through *Ci-* into *Si-* (or *Shi*).
*   **8th Prefix:** Originally *Mbibi-* or *Ibi-*, serving as the plural of the 7th. It varies across dialects into forms such as *Zi-*, *Ri-*, *Psi-*, *Zwi-*, *By-*, *I-*, *[vS]i-*, *P[vs]i-*, *Fy-*, *Fi-*, *Pi-*, or *Vi-*.
*   **10th Prefix:** Originally *Zin-*, *Zi-*, *Tin-*, or *Ti-*. It can become *[theta]on-*, *[theta]in-*, *Lin-*, *Din-*, *Rin-*, or *Jin-*. The persistence of the concord for this prefix is found in almost every known Bantu language, even when the prefix itself has fallen out of use as a definite article, as seen in the Swahili of Zanzibar.
*   **11th Prefix:** *Lu-*, likely descended from *Ndu-*. In archaic dialects, it is usually pronounced *Ru-* or *Du-*.

## Regional Groups and Subdivisions
The Bantu domain can be further subdivided into numbered groups. In the north-east, languages are closest to the hypothetical parent speech. 

*   **Lihuku-Kuamba (Group 2):** These are peculiar dialects south and south-west of Albert Nyanza. Lihuku is a very isolated type with no special affinity to the nearby Uganda-Unyoro groups. Kuamba is spoken on the north and west slopes of Ruwenzori.
*   **Kavirondo-Masaba:** This distinct division includes *Igizii*, *Lukonde*, *Luwanga*, and *Lu-nyara*. It is related to the Luganda section and is considered the most archaic in its prefixes.
*   **Tanganyika Languages (Group 12):** Including *Kabwari*, *Ki-rega*, and *Kiguha*, these are spoken from the Lukuga outlet of Tanganyika to the vicinity of Lake Albert Edward.
*   **Manyema (Group 13):** This group comprises "corrupt" Bantu dialects between the main stream of the Luapula-Congo and the western watershed of Tanganyika, including *Lokele*, *Soko*, *Yalulema*, *Kusu*, and *Tu-rumbu*.
*   **Congo and West African Groups:** These include the commercial languages of the Upper Congo (*Bangala-Bobangi-Liboko*), the *Bateke* group west of the Sanga river, and the *Duala* group of the Cameroon river estuary, which is of a much purer Bantu type. In contrast, the *Fan* or *Pangwe* forms of speech in the interior of French Gabun and German Cameroon are so corrupt they are only just recognizable as Bantu.

## Origins and Affinities
The exact birthplace of the Bantu language remains a puzzle. There are distinct affinities in a few verbal roots and numerals (2, 3, 4, and sometimes 5) between Bantu and the languages of the Gold Coast, Calabar, lower Niger, Benue river, and N. Togoland. Fula shows some resemblance in grammatical construction (though it uses suffixes instead of prefixes) and possesses concord and affinities in numerals 3 and 4. While the Timne languages of Sierra Leone use pronominal prefixes and a system of concord similar to Bantu, there is absolutely no resemblance in their word-roots.
