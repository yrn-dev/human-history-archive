# Balsams Destitute of Cinnamic and Benzoic Constituents

In strict chemical terms, the label "balsam" is properly limited to resins or oleo-resins that contain benzoic acid, cinnamic acid, or both. These substances, such as the balsam of Tolu, balsam of Peru, liquidambar, and liquid storax, form a distinct class based on their composition and properties. However, there are several substances found in commerce referred to as balsams that are entirely destitute of these cinnamic and benzoic constituents.

## Mecca Balsam and the Balm of Gilead
Mecca balsam, also known as the Balm of Gilead, is derived from the tree *Commiphora opobalsamum*, which grows in Abyssinia and Arabia. In its fresh state, it is a viscid fluid characterized by a penetrating odour, though it solidifies as it ages. This substance was held in the highest esteem by nations of antiquity and continues to be highly prized by the people of the East. It is believed to be the *balsamon* mentioned by Theophrastus and the balm referenced in Scripture.

Historical accounts from Josephus indicate that the best palm trees and the only balsam gardens during his time were located near Kugaddi and Jericho, in the northern region of the Dead Sea. This is where Alexander the Great observed the balsam drop. While some have argued that this precious balsam was peculiar to Judea, other sources suggest it was also found in Arabia, Egypt, and particularly Sabea. It is suggested that the queen of Sheba may have first brought the balsam tree into Judea from Arabia or Ethiopia.

It should be noted that some references to "balm" or "balsam" in other contexts—such as the presents sent by Jacob to the governor of Egypt or the "balm of Gilead" in Jeremiah 8:22—may be mistaken translations. In those instances, the term may instead denote the juice of the turpentine tree, known today as the turpentine of Cyprus or Chio.

## Wood Oil and Gurjun Balsam
Under the name of Gurjun balsam or "wood oil," an oleo-resin is obtained from several species of *Dipterocarpus* (chiefly *D. turbinatus*) in the Eastern Archipelago and India. This substance possesses properties and an odour similar to copaiba and is used for the same purposes. Additionally, wood oil serves as a varnish in India and provides an effective means of protection against attacks from white ants.

## Canada Balsam
Canada balsam, also referred to as Canada turpentine, is an oleo-resin produced by the tree *Abies balsamea*, which is native to the northern United States and Canada. It is a transparent substance with a mild terebinthous odour and a delicate yellow colour. While somewhat fluid when first run, it thickens significantly with age. 

The chemical composition of Canada balsam consists of:
* 24% essential oil
* 60% resin soluble in alcohol
* 16% resin soluble only in ether

Its primary applications are as a cement for glass in optical work and for mounting preparations for the microscope.

## Other Related Terms
The term "balsam" is also applied to plants that do not produce these resins, such as the garden balsam (*Impatiens balsamina*), which is an annual plant, and the balsam apple, which is the fruit of *Momordica balsamina* of the order Cucurbitaceae.
