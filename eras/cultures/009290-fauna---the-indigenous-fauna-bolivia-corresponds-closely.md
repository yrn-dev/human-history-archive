# Fauna of Bolivia

The indigenous fauna of Bolivia is characterized by a close correspondence to the fauna found in the neighboring districts of Peru, Brazil, and Argentina. The distribution of species varies significantly across the country's diverse geographical regions, from the tropical forests to the high Andean plateaus.

## Tropical Forests and Lowlands
The tropical regions of Bolivia are inhabited by numerous species of monkeys. Other mammals found in these forested areas include the jaguar, puma, wildcat, coati, sloth, ant-bear, tapir (also known as the *anta*), paca (*Coelogenys paca*), and capybara. The peccary is also present, ranging from the densely wooded northern districts to the Chaco, where it prefers partially open country.

The lower tropical districts are well represented by amphibia. In the lagoons and tributaries of the Paraguay, alligators are found. Turtles and lizards are numerous in these regions, and several species of batrachians are present. The region is also home to numerous snakes, including the great boa-constrictors of the Amazon region and rattlesnakes.

## Andean Foothills and Plateau Regions
In the wooded foothills of the Andes, a rare species of bear known as the spectacled bear (*Ursus ornatus*) is found. The colder plateau regions are inhabited by the chinchilla (*C. laniger*), a species also found in Chile and northern Argentina that is prized for its fur. Other animals found in the plateau regions include the viscacha (*Lagidium cuvieri*) and the South American otter (*Lutra paranensis*), both of which are hunted for their skins.

## General Distribution of Mammals
Certain mammals are widely distributed across the region, including the skunk, ferret, opossum, and armadillo. Deer are present but not numerous; the most common species is the large marsh deer of the Chaco, and there are two or three species of deer in total.
