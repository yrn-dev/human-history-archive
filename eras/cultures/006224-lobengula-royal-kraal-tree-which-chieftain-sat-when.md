# Lobengula's Royal Kraal

## Overview and Location
The royal kraal of Lobengula was located in Bulawayo, which serves as the capital of Matabeleland, a western province of southern Rhodesia in South Africa. The site is situated on a tableland positioned between the Zambezi and Limpopo rivers, at an elevation of 4,469 feet above the sea. In the present-day town, the site of the royal kraal is the location where Government House was built. An avenue nearly 1½ miles long and 130 feet broad, lined with trees, connects the town to this site.

## History and Foundation
The settlement of Bulawayo, a Zulu word interpreted as the "Place of Slaughter," was founded around 1838 by Mosilikatze, the father of Lobengula. The original foundation was located some distance south of where the current town stands. It served as the royal residence until November 1893, when it was occupied by the forces of the British South Africa Company. Following this occupation, a new town was established. By 1897, a railway connecting the area to Cape Town had been completed.

## The Royal Tree and Governance
Within the grounds of the royal kraal, a specific tree was preserved. This was the tree under which the chieftain Lobengula sat when he was giving judgment.

## Definition of a Kraal
In the context of South and Central Africa, a kraal (also spelled *craal* or *kraul*) is defined as a native village. These settlements are typically roughly circular in form and are surrounded by a mud wall, a palisade, or other types of fencing. The term is used to describe both the physical enclosure and the community that lives within it. Additionally, the term kraal is applied to enclosures created specifically for defensive purposes or as folds for animals.
