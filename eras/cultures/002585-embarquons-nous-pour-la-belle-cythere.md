# Embarquons-nous pour la belle Cythere

"Embarquons-nous pour la belle Cythere" is a poetic invitation to depart for a fictional land, presented as a sanctuary for contemplative spirits seeking escape from a deleterious world. The text depicts a journey toward a divine landscape, though it acknowledges the inherent perils and the tragic state of the destination.

## The State of Cythere
The destination, Cythere, is described as being in a state of mourning. Its gardens, once vibrant, have been battered by storms and are now characterized as a "sombre ecueil" (dark reef) agonizing under a wild sun. The shores of the island are inhabited by solitude, and grey serpents are said to crawl upon the threshold where Cypris—the "dear image" with golden braids and a sweet welcome—once smiled.

## The Nature of the Journey
Despite the desolate state of the island, the speaker urges a departure toward "pays fictifs" (fictitious countries) and a clear landscape. The journey is framed as a flight for those whose hearts are "ramiers plaintifs" (plaintive doves) and who act as fugitives from the world. 

The voyage is fraught with danger; the text explicitly states that the "great sea" will serve as a coffin and the travelers will become prey to a "black shipwreck." This fate is presented as a punishment for pride by the "fire of heaven," while an "aiguillon" (goad) maintains its outrage. Nevertheless, the speaker insists that these risks are acceptable in the pursuit of a "sacred mystery" where idle desires may be quenched.

## Imagery and Symbols
The poetic vision includes several distinct images:
* **The Amours:** These figures are positioned on the highest rigging, singing the "adored hymn of the voyage."
* **The Travelers:** Described as "hidden heroes" within "sickly bodies," they depart in "light skiffs."
* **The Divine Grove:** The destination includes a "divin bocage" (divine grove) where a panther weeps for love beneath "lascif" (lascivious) rosebushes.
* **The Envoi:** The concluding section calls for "pensive eyes" to be satiated with azure and encourages singing birds to fly toward "primitive gods," warning them not to soil their wings upon the earth.
