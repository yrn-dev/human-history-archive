# Balearic Islands

The Balearic Islands (Baleares) are an archipelago located in the Mediterranean Sea off the east coast of Spain. The archipelago forms a province of Spain and covers an area of 1,935 square miles. As of 1900, the total population was 311,649.

## Geography and Composition
The archipelago is situated between 1° and 5° E. and between 38° 40' and 40° 5' N. It is divided into two distinct groups:

*   **The Eastern Group:** This larger group corresponds to the ancient *Insulae Baleares*. It consists of the two principal islands, Majorca (Spanish: Mallorca) and Minorca (Spanish: Menorca), along with seven islets: El Rey, Dragonera, Cabrera, Pinto, Botafoch, Aucanada, and Aire.
*   **The Western Group:** This group corresponds to the ancient Pityusae, also known as the Pine Islands. It includes two relatively large islands, Formentera and Iviza (Spanish: Ibiza or formerly Ivica), as well as the islets of Espalmador, Pou, Conejera, and Ahorcados.

While all eleven islets were inhabited as of 1900, their combined population was only 171. Most are considered unimportant, with the exception of Cabrera. Cabrera is characterized by its numerous caverns and was previously utilized as a place of banishment. In 1808, Spanish captors landed a large group of Frenchmen on Cabrera, where they were left to nearly perish from starvation.

## Etymology
The origin of the name "Baleares" is considered a matter of mere conjecture. However, the names of the primary islands have clearer origins. Majorca and Minorca are derived from the Latin words *Major* and *Minor* via the Byzantine Greek forms *Maiorika* and *Minorika*. Iviza is the modern form of the older name *Ebusus*, which is likely Carthaginian in origin. Formentera was known to the Greeks as Ophiusa and to the Romans as Colubraria.

## Administration
The administration of the Balearic Islands is identical to that of other Spanish provinces located on the mainland. The province is divided into five judicial districts, known as *partidos judiciales*. These districts are named after their primary towns: Palma, Manacor, Inca, Port Mahon, and Iviza.
