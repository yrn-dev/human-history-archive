# Fauna and Flora of Asia

The distribution of life across the Asian continent is heavily dependent on climatic conditions, with distinct variations between the northern, southern, and central regions.

## Northern Asia
The fauna of northern Asia is closely related to that of northern Europe, though it is enriched by numerous additional species. The region is characterized by forms restricted to temperate and cold climates.

### Mammals
The order Carnivora is numerous, particularly animals such as foxes, wolves, weasels, and bears. Among the Insectivora, hedgehogs, shrews, and moles prevail. Rodents are well represented by hares, mice, and squirrels, with pikas (tailless hares, *Lagomys*) and marmots (*Arctomys*) serving as characteristic forms. The order Ungulata includes several peculiar forms of antelope, such as the yellow sheep (*Gazella gutturosa*) and the saiga (*Saiga tatarica*), as well as various sheep; ten to twelve wild species of *Ovis* are found in the mountain chains. Deer of the typical genus *Cervus* are present, and the musk deer (*Moschus*) is a peculiar type restricted to this region.

Other mammals include the European badger (*Meles taxus* or *M. meles*), which is found across northern Asia. In eastern Asia, this is replaced by the Chinese badger (*M. chinensis*) and the white-tailed badger (*M. leucurus*), while *M. anacuma* is found in Japan. Monkeys (Quadrumana) are nearly unrepresented, with only one species in Japan and one or two others in Tibet and northern China. Bats are numerous, though only a single frugivorous species exists in Japan.

### Flora
The flora of northern Asia is essentially the same as that of northern Europe, differing primarily in species rather than genera. Notable absences east of the Ural include heaths and the oak. Principal trees include birch, larch, and pine on mountains, while poplars, alders, and willow grow on lower ground. In Siberia, the northern limit of the pine is approximately 70 deg. N.

## Southern and Central Asia
A flora essentially European in character extends from the Mediterranean to the Himalaya. While many European species reach the central Himalaya, Japanese and Chinese forms are more numerous in the eastern parts of the chain. In central Europe and Asia, an order of plants containing 85 genera and about 1200 species is widely spread, though it becomes less frequent as it moves north.

### Mammals
The bandicoot-rat (*Nesocia bandicota*), an Anglo-Indian name for a large rat, inhabits Ceylon and India. It measures 12 to 15 inches to the root of the tail, with a tail measuring 11 to 13 inches. Other species of this genus are found in central Asia and from Palestine to Formosa. The typical species is especially common in the south of the Indian peninsula, frequenting towns, villages, and cultivated grounds.

In the mountains and plains of Central Asia, numerous species of marmots exist, including the red marmot (*A. caudata*), which is larger with a longer tail than the European varieties. The striped hyena (*H. striata*) is found in southern Asia.

The typical Indian buffalo (*Bos (Bubalus) bubalis*) is restricted to Ceylon and India, though local races may exist in the Islands and Malay Peninsula. These animals frequent grass-jungles near swamps and are known to wallow in mud. In Assam, two local races existed: *B. bubalis fulvus*, characterized by tawny hair and hide, and *B. bubalis macrocercus*, which possessed immensely sized horns directed outwards.

## Aquatic Life of Asia
The fresh waters of southern Asia are primarily inhabited by spined eels, siluroids, carp, and climbing or walking fishes.

### Fish Species
The *Cyprinidae* (carp) are largely represented and reach sizes unknown in Europe; for example, a *Barbus* in the Tigris has weighed 300 lb. The *Siluridae* are most developed in tropical regions, and one species of *Silurus* extends across Africa, Europe, and southern Asia. The *Clupeidae* (herrings), including the hilsa, are numerous and can ascend rivers for many hundred miles.

Sturgeons are found in Asiatic Russia, with an allied form extending to southern China. The *Salmonidae* are entirely absent from southern Asian waters, though they exist in rivers flowing into the northern Pacific (extending perhaps to Formosa) and the Arctic Ocean. Trout are found in streams flowing into the Caspian, though they are unknown in Indian rivers.

Walking or climbing fishes, peculiar to Africa and south-eastern Asia, are organized to breathe outside of water, allowing them to survive in regions of rain and periodical drought.
