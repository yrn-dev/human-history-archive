# BAHAMAS (Lucayos)

The Bahamas, also known as the Lucayos, is an archipelago located in the British West Indies. Geographically, the archipelago extends along a line approximately 630 miles in length, stretching from Florida in the northwest to Haiti in the southeast, positioned between Cuba and the open Atlantic Ocean. Its coordinates range from 80° 50' to 72° 50' W. and 22° 25' to 26° 40' N.

## Geography and Composition
The archipelago is estimated to consist of 2,387 rocks, 661 cays, and 29 islands. The total land area is estimated at 5,450 square miles, with the main islands occupying 4,424 square miles. The islands and groups, listed from the northwest, include:
*   Great Bahama, Little Abaco, and Great Abaco.
*   Eleuthera (a name likely corrupted from the Spanish *Isla de Tierra*).
*   An outer line toward the open ocean consisting of Rum Cay, Watling (or Guanahani), and Cat Cay.
*   An inner line to the west consisting of Long Island, the Exuma chain, and New Providence.
*   Andros, located further west and named after Sir Edmund Andros, the governor of Massachusetts at the close of the 17th century. While often spoken of as one island, it is divided into several by narrow straits.
*   Inagua, Mayaguana, and the Crooked Islands.

The Turks and Caicos islands continue the outer line and are geographically part of the archipelago, though they are not politically so.

Among the larger islands, Andros has a length of 95 miles and an area of 1,600 square miles; Great Abaco is 70 miles long with an area of 680 square miles; Great Inagua is 34 miles long with an area of 530 square miles; and Grand Bahama is 66 miles long with an area of 430 square miles. New Providence, while smaller at 19-3/8 miles in length and 85 square miles in area, is the most important island as it contains the capital, Nassau.

The surrounding seas are generally shallow, though three distinct channels exist: the Old Bahama channel (between the archipelago and Cuba), the Florida or New Bahama channel (between Florida and the northwestern islands, followed by the Gulf Stream), and the Providence channels (north-east and north-west). A depression called the Tongue of Ocean extends southward along the east side of Andros from the Providence channels.

## Population and Society
In 1881, the population of the Bahamas was 43,521, increasing to 53,735 by 1901. Of the 1901 population, approximately 12,000 were whites, with the remainder being coloured. New Providence alone supported a population of 12,534 in 1901.

Beyond the archipelago, immigrants from the Bahama Islands are a significant element of the population in Key West, where they are known as "Conchs" and are employed in sponge fishing.

## Government and Infrastructure
The colony is administered by a British governor. He is assisted by an executive council of nine members (partly official and partly unofficial) and a legislative council of nine members nominated by the crown. Additionally, there is a legislative assembly consisting of 29 members representing 15 electoral districts. 

The franchise is open to white and coloured men at least 21 years of age who have resided in the colony for at least twelve months. To vote, they must either possess land valued at £5 or more, or be householders for six months with a rental of at least £2:18s. in New Providence, or £1:4s. in other islands. Members of the assembly must possess real or personal estate valued at £200.

Infrastructure in the colony is limited; there are no railways, though New Providence has good roads and a few exist elsewhere. Nassau is connected to West Jupiter in Florida via cable. Education is provided through government-supported elementary free schools controlled by a nominated board of education with local supervision by partly elected committees. Higher schools and a Queen's College are located in Nassau, which has also been the seat of a Church of England bishopric since 1861.

## Economy and Trade
The average annual revenue and expenditure of the colony are approximately £75,000, with expenditure slightly exceeding revenue. The public debt is about £105,000. The colony's average annual imports are valued at slightly over £300,000, while exports are valued at £200,000. The average annual tonnage of shipping entering and clearing the ports exceeds 1,000,000.

## Military and Naval History
The Bahama Channel has been a site of naval conflict. On October 1, 1748, a British squadron under Sir Charles Knowles met a Spanish naval force under Admiral Regio in the Bahama Channel. The British sought to intercept Spanish convoys carrying bullion from American mines. While the action was indecisive compared to later British successes, the advantage lay with Sir Charles Knowles. The pursuit was halted by news that peace had been established in Europe.

In the broader context of the British Empire, the Bahamas were among the "minor slave-labour possessions" in the West Indies as of 1815.
