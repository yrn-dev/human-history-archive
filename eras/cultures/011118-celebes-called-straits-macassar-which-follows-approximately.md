# Celebes and the Straits of Macassar

Celebes is an island in the Dutch East Indies, characterized by a rough and mountainous surface with some volcanic peaks reaching heights of 8,000 feet. It shares similar natural characteristics and conditions with the neighboring island of Borneo.

## The Straits of Macassar and Wallace's Line
The Straits of Macassar form a channel of physical separation between the islands of Borneo and Celebes. This waterway follows approximately 120 deg. E. and serves as a significant biological boundary known as Wallace's line, named after the naturalist A.R. Wallace. 

To the west of this line, the fauna and flora are essentially Asiatic in type. To the south and east, the Australian element becomes distinctly marked and eventually predominant. This separation is evidenced by the presence of mammals of the Marsupial order in Celebes and the Molucca Islands, while such animals are absent from Borneo and Java. Conversely, mammals such as Ruminants, Carnivora, Insectivora, and Quadrumana abound in the western Archipelago but are absent from Celebes. The island is home to a singular ruminant, the Anoa, and a peculiar form of baboon, *Cynopithecus*, both of which appear allied to African animals rather than Asiatic ones. Similar peculiarities are found among the birds of the region.

## Macassar
Macassar (also spelled Makassar or Mangkasar) is the chief town of the Dutch government of Celebes and the capital of a district of the same name. Located on the west coast of the island's southern peninsula, it sits near the southern extremity of the Macassar Strait. The town is divided into a Malay town located inland and a Dutch town and port known as Vlaardingen.

The population of Macassar is 17,925, which includes 2,618 Chinese, 940 Europeans, and 168 Arabs. The city is a significant commercial center with an annual trade amounting to approximately £1,250,000. Primary trade goods include spices, coffee, copra, gums, valuable timber, and trepang.

## Boni and the Bugis People
Boni (or Bone) is a vassal state of the government of Celebes located on the Gulf of Boni in the south-west peninsula. It covers an area of 2,600 sq. m. and produces sugar-cane, cotton, coffee, tobacco, and rice, though these are not important as exports. The state is noted for highly esteemed breeds of horses and buffaloes. The chief town of Boni is situated 80 miles northeast of Macassar.

The native race of Boni is the Bugis (or Bughis), a people of Malayan stock. Originally occupying only the kingdom of Boni, they spread across Celebes and founded settlements throughout the Malay Archipelago. The Bugis are described as robust, enterprising, brave, and haughty, with a complexion slightly lighter than the average Malay. They are industrious and ingenious, practicing agriculture more than neighboring tribes and manufacturing cotton-cloth for both local use and export. They also trade in pearls, gold-dust, camphor, nut-megs, and tortoise-shell, maintaining an almost total monopoly of trade around Celebes due to their love of the sea.

The Bugis speak a language allied to that of the Macassars and use similar characters for writing. Their society includes their own schools. Politically, the confederation of Boni consists of eight petty states. The king is elected for life from among their own number by the chiefs of these states and cannot decide on public measures without their consent. While some chiefships are hereditary, others are open to any member of the privileged classes, and women have governed some states.

## History and Religion of Boni
Boni was once the most powerful state on Celebes, with other princes regarded as vassals to its ruler. In 1666, Rajah Palakkah allied with the Dutch against the tyrant of Sumatra, Hassan, whose family had murdered Palakkah's father and grandfather. This established undisputed Dutch influence until the early 19th century. In 1814, the British held Boni for two years before it reverted to the Dutch following the downfall of Napoleon.

Dutch authority was resisted several times by the natives. An expedition in 1825 under General van Geen was not fully successful, and two further expeditions were required in 1858 and 1859 to oppose a princess regent's attempt to gain independence. A new prince who owed allegiance to the Dutch was installed in 1860. In Boni and other native states in Celebes, succession to the throne gives precedence to the female line over the male line.

Regarding religion, the Bugis have been Mahommedans since the 17th century. Prior to this, they practiced nature-worship influenced by Hinduism, and they continue to retain rites associated with the worship of Siva.
