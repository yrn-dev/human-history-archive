# Europe

## Geography and Borders
The boundary between Europe and Asia is marked by a narrow channel of sea. In the region of the Propontis, those traveling westward can observe the high lands of Thrace and Bithynia, as well as the snow-covered summit of Mount Olympus. In this area, the sea separating the two continents contracts into a narrow channel at Gallipoli. Various accounts of the distance of the Propontis exist, with one source contracting the measure to 125 small Greek miles, while another describes it vaguely as a sail of one day and one night.

## Cultural Heritage and Folk Traditions
Europe possesses a shared immemorial inheritance in the form of nursery tales, known to the Germans as *Maerchen*. This inheritance is shared by all European peoples, including Celts, Germans, and Slavonic peoples. 

A similar pattern of shared heritage is found in ballad literature. While some discussions once suggested that Scottish ballads were the production of professional minstrels—such as court-bards or wandering *jongleurs* (strollers)—this theory fails to explain the universal sameness of legend, incident, tone, and primitive poetical formulae found across Europe. These commonalities are shared among the ballads of Italy, Portugal, Provence, France, Greece, and Denmark. Although the language of these ballads has altered automatically through centuries of oral recitation, the stock ideas and situations of many romantic ballads are considered to be of world-wide diffusion and dateless age.

## Political and Social Development
In modern Europe, the development of the "Power" idea reflects certain facts about human nature. While Aristotle described man as a political animal, modern world-politics suggests that humans maintain instincts of the family tribe and a disposition to attach themselves to larger entities such as a state, nation, city, or tribe. This disposition is often vague and uncritical; individuals tend to accept their government or city as a given part of their nature, often fearing the isolation that might result if such systems were discredited. These loyalties are viewed as educational results derived from the environment, such as those who grow up identifying with "Holy Russia" or "Merry England."

## Military and Naval Activities
The United States has maintained naval activities in Europe that grew by accretion rather than by a formal system. These activities have been cooperative in character and varied in scope, falling under the supervision of the commander of United States Naval Forces in Europe and the Allied Naval War Council. Specific cooperating activities have included:
* Naval Pipe-Line Units
* United States Naval Railway Batteries
* Naval Radio Stations (such as the one at Croix d'Hins)
* Naval liaison officers at Army General Headquarters
* United States Naval Aviation

## Historical Conflicts and Diplomacy
European history has been marked by periods of tension where the threat of war appeared as vague and menacing clouds that would occasionally disperse. In some instances, there have been hopes that conflicts could be confined to the East of Europe, allowing nations such as Germany and France to remain aside while Austria and Russia settled their quarrels.

In France, political instability was evidenced by the actions of the king, who relied on National Guards of uncertain loyalty and a Swiss guard of nearly a thousand men. During a period of conflict involving the Commune and the Assembly, the Swiss guard fought until they were ordered to desist by the king, after which they were largely massacred by an angry crowd.
