# The Duala Group

The Duala group is a classification of Bantu languages spoken within the Western group of the Bantu domain. Geographically, these languages are located on the delta and estuary of the Cameroon river.

## Linguistic Characteristics
Within the broader context of Bantu speech, the Duala group is distinguished by being of a "much purer Bantu type." This contrasts with other nearby forms of speech, such as the Fan or Pangwe languages found in the immediate hinterland of the Duala Cameroon, which are described as being so corrupt that they are only just recognizable as Bantu.

The defining feature of the Bantu language family, which includes the Duala group, is the use of prefixes to govern unchangeable roots. In the purer forms of these languages, the syllable *-ntu* (originally meaning "person" or "object") is combined with different prefixes to create various meanings. For example, *muntu* signifies "a man," *bantu* means "men," *kintu* refers to "a thing," *bintu* to "things," and *kantu* to "a little thing."

## Regional Context and Related Groups
The Duala group is situated among several other Bantu and semi-Bantu linguistic divisions in the Cameroon and Gabon regions:

*   **The Fan or Pangwe group:** Located in the interior of French Gabun and German Cameroon, extending to the Mbam, Sanaga, and Sanga rivers.
*   **The Isubu-Bakwiri group:** Found on the west slopes of the Cameroon Mountains and the coast-lands north of the Cameroon delta at Ambas Bay.
*   **The Barondo-Bakundu group:** This group begins at the Rio del Rey near the Cross river estuary and includes various "much-debased" dialects spoken on the Cameroon river (Magombe) and the eastern slopes of the Cameroon mountains.
*   **Semi-Bantu languages:** To the north-east of the Barondo-Bakundu group, languages such as *Bafut*, *Bayon*, *Mbe*, *Bati*, *Nki*, and *Mbudikum* are characterized as "semi-Bantu," showing affinities with the Fan group and the basin of the Central Benue river.

## The Bantu Domain
The Duala group belongs to the Western group of Bantu speech, which encompasses all Cameroon and Central and Northern Congo languages. The overall Bantu domain is a vast linguistic family stretching from a short distance north of the equator to the southern extremity of Africa. 

The homogeneity of the Bantu family is noted as being particularly striking compared to the linguistic confusion found in Africa north of the Bantu borderland. While the exact birthplace of the Bantu mother-speech remains a puzzle, the family is broadly divided into four sections: the languages of the East Coast and Great Lakes, the South-Central group (including Zulu and Bechuana), the South-West group (extending from the Angola-Congo coast to Damaraland), and the Western group, to which the Duala belong.
