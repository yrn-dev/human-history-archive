# The Eighth Bantu Prefix

The eighth prefix in the Bantu language group is characterized by its function as a plural marker and its significant variability across different dialects. In its oldest and most archaic form, this prefix is identified as *Ibi-* or *Mbibi-*.

## Function and Correspondence
Within the system of Bantu substantives, nouns are divided into classes or genders indicated by pronominal particles prefixed to the root. These prefixes function in either a singular or plural sense. Except for the "abstract" prefix *Bu* (No. 14), singular prefixes cannot be used as plurals, nor can plural prefixes be used as singulars.

There is a specific correspondence between certain singular and plural prefixes. The eighth prefix (*Ibi-* or *Mbibi-*) serves invariably as the plural counterpart to the seventh prefix (*Iki-*).

## Linguistic Evolution and Variation
While the oldest form is *Ibi-* or *Mbibi-*, the prefix has evolved into numerous different forms across various Bantu speeches. These variations include:
* *Vi-*, *Pi-*, *Fi-*, and *Fy-*
* *P[vs]i-*, *[vS]i-*, and *I-*
* *By-*, *Bzi-*, *Psi-*, *Zwi-*, *Zi-*, and *Ri-*

The concord associated with this prefix—the pronominal particle attached to connected adjectives, pronouns, prepositions, and verbs—follows a similar pattern of variation.

## The System of Concord
The Bantu linguistic structure utilizes a system of concord (as described by Dr. Bleek) where the pronominal particle of the governing noun is attached as a prefix to the roots of other parts of the sentence. Although these particles may differ in form from the substantive's prefix over time, they share a common origin. These particles must always precede the nominal, pronominal, adjectival, and verbal roots, though they may follow the roots of some prepositions and auxiliary prefix-participles used in verb conjugation.
