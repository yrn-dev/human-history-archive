# Developments and Artifacts of the 17th Century

The 17th century was a period marked by significant advancements in scientific observation, linguistic evolution, the production of luxury textiles, and the development of musical instruments.

## Musical Instruments: The Cornemuse
During the 16th and 17th centuries, two distinct types of *cornemuse* (bag-pipes) were utilized in France. While similar in general form, they differed in one important structural detail that affected the timbre of the instrument. 

The first variety was used as a solo instrument and by shepherds, bearing similarities to the Highland bag-pipe. This instrument featured a leather bag inflated via a valved blow-pipe and two drones tuned to C: a large drone (*gros bourdon*) measuring 2-1/2 feet with a 2-1/2 inch beating-reed, and a small drone (*petit bourdon*) measuring 1 foot with a 2 inch beating-reed. Its chaunter possessed a conical bore and a double reed hidden in the stock. When removed from the stock and played separately, the chaunter's compass (C to C') could be increased to E by overblowing the D and E an octave.

The second variety of *cornemuse* was played exclusively in concert with a family of instruments called *Hautbois de Poitou*. Unlike the first variety, this instrument had only one drone. The primary structural difference was that the drone's reed was a double reed, similar to that of the chaunter, rather than a beating-reed. Its chaunter featured eight holes, with the lowest hole covered by a key enclosed in a perforated box.

## Science and Entomology
The foundations of modern entomology were established through memoirs on development and anatomy published during the 17th and 18th centuries. In 1651, Harvey and Aristotle viewed the pupa as a second egg and the insect larva as an embryo that had hatched prematurely. However, J. Swammerdam later demonstrated that pupal structures existed beneath the larval cuticle. 

Other significant 17th-century contributions include the work of the Italian naturalist F. Redi, who used experiments to disprove the spontaneous generation of maggots from putrid flesh, proving instead that they develop from fly eggs. Additionally, M. Malpighi published a famous treatise on the silkworm in 1669.

## Textiles and Art
The early 17th century saw the production of "Polish carpets," which are high-quality pile carpets. These were typically constructed with silk pile for the ornamentation and a ground of gold and silver thread textile, following the style of Brusa fabrics from the early 17th century. The designs frequently featured long curved serrated leaves and arabesques, which were more typical of 17th-century Turkish ornament than the 16th-century Rhodian pottery decoration they resembled. While some believe these were made by Persian workmen at the Mazarski silk factory in Poland during the 18th century, other evidence suggests they were likely produced in Damascus or Constantinople.

In the realm of visual arts, the 17th century in Bologna was characterized by the works of the Bolognese eclectics, including Guercino, Domenichino, Guido Reni, and the Caracci.

## Language and Literature
The 17th century influenced several linguistic and literary developments across Europe:
*   **Slavonic Languages:** A great number of ecclesiastical books were introduced from Russia into the Balkan Peninsula during the 17th and 18th centuries. This led to the "Russian-Slavonic" liturgical language becoming the general usage among Orthodox Slavs in the region.
*   **Biblical Translation:** In the early 17th century, the translators of 1611 utilized the Massoretic text. Later revisers of the Old Testament were able to provide a more faithful translation of the received text due to advances in the study of Hebrew that occurred since the beginning of the century.
*   **Regional Records:** In the middle of the 17th century, the Rev. Mr Coker wrote a "Survey of Dorsetshire," which was later published in 1732.

## Regional History and Architecture
In Ireland, the diocese of Killala was joined with Achonry early in the 17th century, and the town's cathedral church of St Patrick is a structure dating from that century. 

In Eperjes, the town played a significant role during the religious wars of the 17th century. Its German educational establishments maintained a wide reputation during the 16th and 17th centuries. However, the period also saw the "butchery of Eperjes" in 1687, a tribunal led by the Austrian general Caraffa that confiscated property and condemned numerous citizens to death on accusations of Protestantism.
