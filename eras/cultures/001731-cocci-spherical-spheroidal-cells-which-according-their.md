# Cocci

Cocci are a category of cells characterized by their spherical or spheroidal shape. Within the Schizomycetes, these cells may be classified into different types—specifically *Micrococci*, *Macrococci*, and potentially *Monas* forms—based on their relative sizes, although these size distinctions are not very well defined.

## Classification and Taxonomy
Cocci belong to the order Haplobacterinae, which consists of unicellular vegetative bodies that may be spheroidal, cylindrical, or spirally twisted. The family Coccaceae is specifically defined by vegetative cells that are spheroidal. This family is further divided into two sub-families based on the nature of their division and motility:

*   **Allococcaceae**: In this group, division occurs in any or all planes. The resulting colonies are indefinite in shape and size, appearing as isolated cells, pairs, irregular clumps, or short chains. Examples include the non-motile *Micrococcus* and the motile *Planococcus*.
*   **Homococcaceae**: This group is characterized by regular and definite division planes. *Sarcina* (non-motile) and *Planosarcina* (motile) divide in three successive planes at right angles, creating packet-like groups. *Pediococcus* divides in two successive planes at right angles, forming tablets of four or more cells. *Streptococcus* divides in only one plane, resulting in the formation of chains.

## Morphology and Structure
The individual cell of a coccus can range from a minute sphere to larger spheroidal forms. While some unicellular forms of Schizomycetes are less than 1 micron (0.001 mm) in diameter, others may reach thicknesses of 4, 5, 7, or 8 microns. 

Every bacterial cell is enclosed by a definite cell-membrane. This membrane is generally a modification of the general protoplasm consisting of a proteid substance; it typically does not react as cellulose or contain chitin, though exceptions exist. In certain instances, the cell-walls may swell into a gelatinous mass, creating a clear, transparent space around the cell. When this swollen wall is regular and dense, it is referred to as a "capsule."

## Growth and the Zoogloea Stage
The growth of these cells involves elongation and division into two equal halves across the long axis via the formation of a septum. While most cells divide in one direction, iso-diametric cells may divide in two or three directions, producing irregular, spheroidal, or flat colonies of practically unlimited size.

A significant phenomenon in the life history of Schizomycetes is the formation of the zoogloea stage, which serves as a resting condition. This occurs when vegetative cells—including cocci and rodlets—become fixed in a matrix formed by their own enormously swollen and contiguous cell-walls. This matrix is created when progeny cells secrete cell-wall substance that absorbs many times its volume of water. Depending on the species, this matrix may be composed chiefly of a proteid substance or cellulose, and its consistency can range from a thick gum-like solution to a horny, resistant material.

Zoogloeae can manifest as membranes on the surface of a medium, irregular submerged clumps, or branched masses. They may assume various forms, including ovoid, spherical, reticular, lamellar, or fruiticose. For example, *Bacterium merismopedioides* contains cocci arranged in tablets within the zoogloea, and *Beggiatoa roseo-persicina* features large crowded cocci with fused gelatinous walls. Under favorable conditions, the cells can become active again, leave the matrix, and resume growing and multiplying in the surrounding medium.

## Other Occurrences of Cocci
The term "cocci" is also used in botany to describe specific fruit structures. In the Geraniaceae family, cocci usually contain a single seed and separate from the carpophore, curving upwards due to adherent styles before opening via the ventral suture. Similarly, in the Euphorbiaceae family, such as the sandbox tree, cocci containing a single seed burst open with force.
