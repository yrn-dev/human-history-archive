# Azerbaijani Carpet Weaving: Art and Heritage

## Overview
Azerbaijani carpet weaving is not merely a craft but a profound expression of national identity, blending nomadic traditions with settled urban artistry. For centuries, the looms of Azerbaijan have served as visual archives, recording the flora, fauna, and spiritual beliefs of the region. The art is characterized by a sophisticated use of geometric and floral patterns, as well as a distinct palette derived from natural dyes.

Historically, Azerbaijani carpets are categorized into several regional schools, most notably the Guba, Shirvan, Baku, and Karabakh schools. While the Guba and Shirvan styles are often recognized for their intricate, smaller-scale geometric precision, the Karabakh carpets are celebrated for their larger compositions and lush floral motifs. Historians often debate the precise origins of certain patterns, noting that the Silk Road facilitated a cross-pollination of motifs between Persian, Turkic, and Caucasian influences. However, the specific technical execution and symbolic vocabulary remain uniquely Azerbaijani.

## Key Facts
*   **The Symbolic Language:** Every motif carries meaning. The *buta* (almond-shaped leaf), for instance, is a symbol of fire and eternity, reflecting the region's Zoroastrian roots.
*   **Natural Pigments:** Traditional carpets utilize dyes extracted from pomegranate skins, madder root (for reds), indigo (for blues), and walnut shells (for browns).
*   **The Loom Structure:** The vertical loom is the standard tool, allowing weavers to create massive pieces with high knot density, ensuring the longevity of the fabric.
*   **Regional Diversity:** The Baku school is particularly noted for its "city carpets," which often featured more opulent materials and designs tailored for the aristocracy.

## Did You Know?
*   **UNESCO Recognition:** In 2010, the art of Azerbaijani carpet weaving was officially recognized by UNESCO as an Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
*   **The "Buta" Influence:** The *buta* motif is so central to Azerbaijani art that it appears not only in carpets but also in embroidery and jewelry.
*   **Material Purity:** High-quality traditional Azerbaijani carpets are made from 100% natural wool, which provides a natural luster and resistance to wear.
