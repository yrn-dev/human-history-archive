# Buddhist Birth Stories

The *Jataka* book is a collection of verses believed to have been spoken by the Buddha regarding his previous births. This collection consists of 550 folk-tales that were current in India during the period when the canon was being formed. In these stories, the Buddha is identified as the hero of each tale in a previous incarnation.

## Nature and Preservation of the Jatakas
The *Jataka* stories are characterized as a highly authentic, complete, and old collection of folklore. While the verses themselves are part of the core material, the prose elements of these stories have been preserved only within the commentary. Due to their nature as folk-tales, many of these fables and stories migrated to Europe, where they appear in story-books, poems, and medieval homilies.

## Relation to Other Buddhist Texts
The *Jatakas* exist alongside other canonical and Sanskrit works that detail the life and philosophy of the Buddha. While the *Jatakas* focus on previous births, other texts focus on his final life:

*   **Canonical Pali Texts:** These reached their current form before the 4th century B.C. and include episodes such as the birth of the Buddha, his youth at home, his "going forth," and his attainment of Buddhahood and Nirvana.
*   **Sanskrit Texts:** Works such as the *Mahavastu* (likely 2nd century B.C.) and the *Lalita Vistara* (likely 1st century B.C.) also address the Buddha's life. The *Mahavastu*, specifically associated with the Maha-sanghikas school, begins its narrative with the Buddha's previous births, during which he accumulated "Buddha qualities." This text describes how the Buddha eventually won victory over ignorance under the Bo Tree to attain Sambodhi, or the "higher Wisdom" of Nirvana.

## The Birth of Gotama
According to the *Sutta Nipata* (specifically the "Song of Nalaka"), the Buddha was born in the Sakiya land at a grove or garden called Lumbini. Angels in this ballad describe the "Wisdom-child" as a precious, unmatched jewel born for the joy and weal of the world of men.

Commentaries on the *Jatakas* and the *Majjhima* provide further detail on the circumstances of this birth. The mother of the future Buddha was traveling from Kapilavastu, the capital of the Sakiyas, to her own mother's home in Devadaha, the capital of the Koliyas, to give birth. However, her labor began during the journey, and she turned into the grove at Lumbini, located near Devadaha, where she gave birth to her son. This account is repeated in all later Buddhist Sanskrit and Pali records.

## Academic Translations and Scholarship
The *Jataka* book has been translated into English under the editorship of Professor Cowell and published by Cambridge University Press. Additionally, T.W. Rhys Davids published a translation of the first volume of the *Jataka* in 1880 under the title *Buddhist Birth Stories*. Further volumes (i-vi) were later produced by Rouse, Francis, Neil, and Chalmers between 1895 and 1897.
