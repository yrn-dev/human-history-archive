# HOMOCOCCACEAE

The Homococcaceae is a sub-family of the family Coccaseae, which belongs to the order Haplobacterinae. The vegetative body of organisms within the order Haplobacterinae is unicellular and may be cylindrical, spheroidal, or spirally twisted; these cells may exist in isolation or be connected in other growth series or filamentous forms. Within the family Coccaseae, the vegetative cells are specifically spheroidal.

## Characteristics and Classification
The sub-family Homococcaceae is distinguished from the sub-family Alococcaceae by the nature of its cellular division. While the Alococcaceae exhibit division in any or all planes—resulting in irregular clumps, isolated cells, pairs, or short chains—the Homococcaceae are characterized by division planes that are both definite and regular.

## Member Genera
The Homococcaceae sub-family includes several genera, categorized by their motility and the specific geometry of their division planes:

*   ***Sarcina* (Goods.)**: The cells of this genus are non-motile. Division occurs in three successive planes at right angles, which results in the formation of groups that are packet-like.
*   ***Planosarcina* (Migula)**: This genus follows the same growth and division patterns as *Sarcina* (division in three successive planes at right angles), but the cells are motile.
*   ***Pediococcus* (Lindner)**: In this genus, division occurs at right angles in two successive planes. This process results in cells arranged in tablets consisting of four or more cells.
*   ***Streptococcus* (Billr.)**: This genus is characterized by divisions that occur in one plane only, which results in the formation of chains of cells.
