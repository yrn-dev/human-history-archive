# Lulua and Ki-tabwa

The Lulua and Ki-tabwa are groups associated with the Rua-Luba-Lunda-Marungu linguistic or ethnic classification. They are situated within the south central basin of the Congo.

## Geographic Distribution
The region occupied by the Rua-Luba-Lunda-Marungu group, which includes the Lulua and Ki-tabwa as well as the Kanyoka, is defined by several geographic boundaries. To the east, the area is bounded by the south-west coast-line of Tanganyika. To the west, it is limited by the main streams of the Kwango and the Kasai. The northern boundary is marked by the Bakuba country, while the southern limit is the Zambezi watershed.

## Linguistic Context
The Lulua and Ki-tabwa are part of a broader distribution of Bantu languages across Africa. Research into these languages suggests that approximately 3,000 years ago, a powerful tribe of negroes speaking the Bantu mother-language migrated from the heart of Africa—specifically the region between the western Nile, Congo, and Shari watersheds—into the southern half of the continent. 

During this expansion, the Bantu speakers swept across vast areas, assimilating or absorbing much of the existing populations. These preceding populations included Forest Pygmies and negroes of a low type in the center of the Congo basin, as well as West Coast negroes on the Atlantic seaboard. In other regions, such as the East African coast and the areas south-west of Lakes Nyasa and Tanganyika, the populations consisted of Hottentot, Bushman, and Nilotic-negroes.

## Physical Characteristics of Bantu Speakers
While the Lulua and Ki-tabwa fall under the Bantu linguistic umbrella, the archive notes that speakers of Bantu languages do not belong to a single, definite physical type. There is significant diversity in physical characteristics, head shape, and outward appearance among the negroes of Bantu Africa. For example, some Bantu-speaking tribes are dwarfish and belong to the Forest Pygmies, while others show relationships to Hottentots or resemble the exaggerated types of black West African negroes. Some, particularly in the north, are of Nilotic or Gala (Galla) origin.
