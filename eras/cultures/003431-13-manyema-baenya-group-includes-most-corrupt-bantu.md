# The Manyema (Baenya) Group

The Manyema, also referred to as the Baenya, constitute a specific linguistic group within the broader Bantu family of languages. This group is characterized by the presence of most of the corrupt Bantu dialects located between the main stream of the Luapula-Congo and the western watershed of Tanganyika.

## Geographical Distribution and Extent
The Manyema group extends northward from the Luapula-Congo and Tanganyika regions. Its reach includes the languages of the Aruwimi basin. The geographical boundaries of the group are defined by neighboring linguistic classifications: to the west, the Manyema are bounded by the languages of the Lomami valley, which are categorized under groups No. 15 and 16. To the east, the Manyema genus merges into the Bantu dialects of groups No. 1 and 12, which are described as being much purer.

## Included Languages and Dialects
The Manyema group comprises several languages, including:
*   Soko
*   Lokele
*   Kusu
*   Yalulema
*   Tu-rumbu

Additionally, the Lihuku-Kuamba section (No. 2) is shown to be connected with the Manyema group. The Kibira dialects, found in the Ituri district of the north-eastern Congo Forest, may also be placed within this section.

## Linguistic Relationships
The Manyema group is part of a wider classification of Bantu speech. In a broad division of the Bantu domain, the Manyema are included in the Western group. This larger section encompasses all Cameroon and Central and Northern Congo languages, as well as group No. 2 of the Semliki river and Albert Nyanza.

The relationship between the Manyema and other nearby groups is further illustrated by the Lihuku-Kuamba dialects. These dialects, found south and south-west of Albert Nyanza in the Mboga country, show a marked relationship with the languages of the Aruwimi and northern Congo, specifically in vocabulary rather than grammar. Lihuku (or Libvanuma) is noted as a very isolated type of Bantu that lacks special affinity with the Uganda-Unyoro groups, despite being in close juxtaposition with them.

## Context within the Bantu Family
The Bantu languages are a vast family stretching from a short distance north of the equator to the southern extremity of Africa. They are distinguished by a striking homogeneity and a pronominal-prefix type of language. While the Manyema group contains "corrupt" dialects, other Bantu groups vary in purity; for example, the Duala group is described as being of a much purer Bantu type, whereas the Fan or Pangwe forms of speech are so corrupt they are only just recognizable as Bantu.
