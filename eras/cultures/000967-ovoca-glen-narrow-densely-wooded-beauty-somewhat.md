# Ovoca

The Vale of Ovoca, also referred to as the Vale of Avoca, is a mountain glen located in the south-eastern portion of County Wicklow, Ireland. The vale is characterized as being narrow and densely wooded.

## Geography and Hydrology
The glen is formed by the junction of two small rivers, the Avonbeg and the Avonmore. These rivers rise in the central highlands of County Wicklow. Their united waters create the Ovoca river, which flows in a south and south-easterly direction until it reaches the Irish Sea at Arklow.

Within the vale, there are two distinct "meetings of the waters." The upper meeting occurs where the Avonmore and Avonbeg join, and the lower meeting occurs where the Ovoca joins with the Aughrim. The upper meeting is located near the fine seat of Castle Howard.

## Cultural Significance and Landmarks
While the vale is one of many beautiful glens in the district, it has achieved lasting celebrity due to the poet Thomas Moore. In one of his *Irish Melodies*, Moore sang the praises of the glen. Because of this song, the name "Avoca" is the most familiar form of the name, although it is spelled "Ovoca" locally.

The area is also noted for the birthplace of Charles Stewart Parnell, who was born at Avondale. This location is situated by the Avonmore, above the upper meeting of the waters.

## Infrastructure and Industry
The natural beauty of the glen is somewhat marred by human activity and industry. The vale contains lead and copper mines. Additionally, the main line of the Dublin & South Eastern railway passes through the area. The Ovoca station is located midway in the vale, at a distance of 42-3/4 miles south of Dublin.
