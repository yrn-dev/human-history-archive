# Distribution of Flora and Fauna in Temperate and Tropical Regions

The distribution of biological orders, species, and climatic zones varies significantly across the globe, with many forms of life spanning both temperate and tropical regions. These distributions are often influenced by geography, altitude, and specific environmental conditions.

## Botanical Orders and Species
Certain botanical orders are widely distributed across both temperate and tropical regions. One such order contains approximately 1200 species across 85 genera. Its primary center is located in the Mediterranean region, extending into Asia and central Europe, though it becomes less frequent as it moves north. A smaller center exists on the Pacific side of North America, while the order is less developed in the south temperate zone. This order is of limited economic value, although roots from genera such as *Alkanna tinctoria* (alkanet) yield brown or purple dyes, and *Pulmonaria* and borage were previously used for medicinal purposes. Additionally, *Heliotropium peruvianum* (cherry-pie or heliotrope) is a recognized garden plant.

Other plant genera exhibit wide distribution in temperate regions. The genus *Fagus* includes the native beech and about 15 other species. *Fagus americana* is widely distributed in the forests of eastern North America and is distinguished from *F. sylvatica* by its lighter green, more sharply-toothed leaves and paler bark. In Japan, *F. sylvatica* var. *Sieboldi* is an abundant deciduous-leaved forest tree. *Fagus* species are also found in New Zealand, Australia, and the forests of Patagonia and southern Chile. In Tierra del Fuego and along the shore of the Straits of Magellan, the forests consist largely of *Fagus antarctica* (deciduous) and *Fagus betuloides* (evergreen).

In south-eastern Asia—including the Malayan peninsula, islands, and India—the flora is a heterogeneous mix of alpine, temperate, and tropical plants. While tropical plants are dominant, the region contains an estimated 12,000 to 15,000 indigenous species. The most numerically important orders include Leguminosae, Rubiaceae, Orchidaceae, Compositae, Gramineae, Euphorbiaceae, Acanthaceae, Cyperaceae, and Labiatae. In rainy regions, certain forms such as *Osbeckia*, *Begonia*, various Scitamineae, Cyrtandraceae, and some epiphytical orchids extend beyond the tropics into temperate mountain zones.

## Ichthyology and Marine Life
The *Scombridae* family, which includes the bonito, tunny, albacore, and other tropical genera, consists of pelagic fishes known as mackerel. While they have fewer species than many other fish families, they are extremely abundant and widely spread throughout the temperate and tropical oceans in countless schools. Mackerel (genus *Scomber*) are characterized by iridescent colors and an elegantly shaped body covered in small, thin, deciduous scales. They possess two dorsal fins—the anterior containing 11-14 feeble spines—and a crescent-shaped caudal fin.

In Africa, the fish fauna south of the Sahara shows a gradual disappearance of specifically tropical forms toward the southern extremity, despite the more temperate climate. Silurids, Cyprinids, and *Anabas* reach the southern coast. The only new form in South Africa is a *Galaxias* at the Cape of Good Hope. In the north-east, the African fauna extends through the isthmus of Suez into Syria, with the Jordan system containing many African types.

## Regional Case Study: Bolivia
Bolivia provides a comprehensive example of how flora and fauna vary across climatic zones due to altitude.

### Climatic Zones and Flora
The natives identify several distinct zones:
*   **Yungas:** Lowlands and mountain valleys up to 5,000 ft. This tropical region is exceedingly humid with a mean temperature of 70 deg. F. It is characterized by luxuriant forests, though malaria and fevers are common.
*   **Valle:** Deep valleys from 5,000 to 9,500 ft. This sub-tropical region is described as a place of perpetual summer.
*   **Cabezera de valle:** The heads of deep valleys from 9,500 to 11,000 ft. This temperate region has regular seasons favorable for vegetables and cereals.
*   **Puna:** The central plateau between 11,000 and 12,500 ft. The air is cold and dry with a mean temperature of 54 deg. F., supporting only barley and potatoes.
*   **Puna brava:** From 12,500 ft. to the snow limit (approximately 17,500 ft.), consisting of bleak territory.
*   **Arctic zone:** The region of eternal snow above 17,500 ft.

Indigenous plants in the tropical and sub-tropical zones include pineapple, guava, vanilla, peanuts, cassava, sweet potato, and hardwoods such as mahogany, cedar, and rosewood. Coca is cultivated on the eastern Andean slopes at 5,000 to 6,000 ft., while calisaya (quina) is found between 3,000 and 9,000 ft.

### Fauna
Bolivian fauna closely mirrors that of Peru, Brazil, and Argentina. Tropical forests are inhabited by jaguars, pumas, monkeys, sloths, tapirs, and coatis. The spectacled bear (*Ursus ornatus*) is found in wooded Andean foothills. In the colder plateau regions, the chinchilla (*C. laniger*) is found and prized for its fur. Other distributed species include the armadillo, skunk, opossum, and ferret. The lower tropical districts have well-represented amphibia, including turtles, lizards, and alligators in the lagoons and tributaries of the Paraguay.
