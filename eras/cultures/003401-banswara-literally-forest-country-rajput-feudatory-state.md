# BANSWARA

Banswara, whose name literally means "the forest country," is a Rajput feudatory state located in Rajputana, India. The state covers an area of 1,946 square miles, measuring approximately 45 miles in length from north to south and 33 miles in breadth from east to west. As of 1901, the population of the state was 165,350.

## Geography and Boundaries
Banswara borders Gujarat and is bounded by several other states and dominions. To the north lie the native states of Udaipur (also known as Mewar) and Dungarpur. Its eastern and northeastern boundaries are shared with Partabgarh. To the south, it is bordered by the state of Jabua and the dominions of Holkar. To the west, it is bounded by the state of Rewa Kantha. 

The Mahi is the sole river within the state. Due to this limited water source, the region experiences a great scarcity of water during the dry season.

## History and Political Status
The chief of Banswara belongs to the family of Udaipur. Historically, the state was a dependency of the Delhi empire during its period of vigour. Following the decline of that empire, Banswara passed under the control of the Mahrattas.

By 1812, the chief of Banswara, exhausted by the oppressions of the Mahrattas, petitioned for English protection. This request was made on the condition that the state would become tributary once the Mahrattas were expelled. This arrangement was formalized through a treaty in 1818. Under the terms of this treaty, Britain guaranteed the prince protection against refractory chiefs and external enemies. In return, the prince pledged to be guided by the British representative in the administration of the state.

The state's origins are linked to the territories of Bagar. Following the death of Rawal Udai Singh of Bagar at the battle of Khanua in 1527, his territories were divided into the states of Banswara and Dungarpur. The name Bagar is still frequently used to refer to the area covered by these two states.

## Administration and Economy
The administration of the state is managed by the chief, who is assisted by a minister known as a *hamdar*. The estimated gross revenue of Banswara is £17,000, with a tribute of £2,500. The petty state of Kushalgarh serves as a feudatory to Banswara.

## Society and Culture
While the custom of suttee (widow-burning) has long been abolished in Banswara, the population retains superstitions regarding sorcery and witches. An example of this occurred as late as 1870 at Kushalgarh, where an eighty-year-old Bhil woman was swung to death after being accused of witchcraft. Although the perpetrators were sentenced to five years of rigorous imprisonment, they maintained the sympathy of the local people.

## The Chief Town
The chief town of Banswara is situated approximately 8 miles west of the Mahi river. The town is characterized by an old, disused rampart and various Hindu temples, with the battlements of the chief's palace overlooking the area. In 1901, the population of the town was 7,038.
