# The Ba-Kwiri

## Origins and Social Structure
The Ba-Kwiri are a Bantu nation located in West Africa within German Cameroon. According to their own traditions, they are migrants who arrived from the eastward. The name "Ba-Kwiri" translates to "Brushmen." Socially, the people are organized into approximately sixty separate clans.

## Characteristics and Culture
The Ba-Kwiri are described as an intelligent and lively people who are daring hunters and brave fighters. They possess a high degree of skill in elocution, music, and songs, which is noted as being superior to many negro races. Within their society, they are open-handed and generous toward one another. However, they adhere strictly to the law of blood for blood, which is fulfilled mercilessly even in instances where the homicide was accidental. Their domestic affections are also strongly developed.

## Physical Traits
A primary physical peculiarity of the Ba-Kwiri is the significant disparity between the sexes regarding complexion and size. Most women are far lighter in color and much shorter than the men.

## Religion and Beliefs
The religious practices of the Ba-Kwiri consist of ancestor-worship combined with magic and witchcraft. They believe in the existence of both evil and good spirits, and they hold a particular fear of the spirits found in the seas and forests.

## Communication Systems
The Ba-Kwiri utilize two distinct specialized languages for communication:
*   **Drum Language:** Shared with their neighbors, the Dualla, the Ba-Kwiri use drum-tapping to convey news between clans. This language is restricted; women and slaves are not permitted to master it. However, those who have been initiated are required to repeat the messages to ensure they are passed on.
*   **Horn Language:** The Ba-Kwiri also possess a horn language that is peculiar to their own nation.
