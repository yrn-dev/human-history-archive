# The Bakhtiari and the Region of Luristan

The Bakhtiari are one of the great nomad tribes of Persia. They occupy a hilly district known as the Bakhtiari province, which extends from Chaharmahal (located west of Isfahan) in the east to near Shushter in the west. The province is bounded to the north by the Dizful river (Ab i Diz), which separates it from Luristan, and to the south it touches Ram Hormuz and Behbahan.

## Tribal Structure and Demographics
The Bakhtiari are divided into two primary divisions: the Haft-lang and the Chahar-lang, along with various clans and branches. Until the 15th century, they were known as the "Great Lurs." This distinguishes them from the "Little Lurs," who are tribes settled in the district of Luristan, where Khorremabad serves as the capital.

The population of the Bakhtiari consists of approximately 38,000 to 40,000 families, totaling under 200,000 individuals. Their occupied district covers an area of about 25,000 square miles. While they were capable of fielding 20,000 well-equipped horsemen in the middle of the 19th century, long-lasting feuds between branches and government misrule—which the state often instigated or fostered—have impoverished the district. Consequently, it would currently be difficult to find 4,000 horsemen.

## Geography and Administration
The Bakhtiari province falls under the jurisdiction of the governor-general of Arabistan and pays an annual tribute of approximately £5,000. In 1897, with the permission of the shah to improve the road between Isfahan and either Ahvaz or Shushter, several bridges were constructed: a stone bridge over the Karun at Do-pu-lan, a 70-foot span iron suspension bridge over the Bazuft river at Pul i Amarat, and a 120-foot span iron suspension bridge over the Karun river at Gudar i Bulutek.

Additionally, the nomad Bakhtiari are listed as one of the administrative divisions of the province of Burujird. Burujird is a fertile region bounded by Isfahan to the south, Irak to the east, Nehavend and Malayir to the north, and Luristan to the west. The capital, the town of Burujird, is located on the river Tah[=i]j (a tributary of the Dizful river) in the Silakhor plain. The town manufactures felts, specifically caps and hats for the Bakhtiaris and Lurs, as well as various cotton stuffs.

## The Lurs and Luristan
In a broader sense, Luristan is the "Land of the Lurs," a mountainous part of western Persia extending roughly 400 miles from Kermanshah to Fars. The region is characterized by numerous ranges running northwest to southeast, with some summits reaching 13,000 feet. It is the source of several major Persian rivers, including the Karun, Zayendeh-rud, Abi, Diz, Kerkheh, and Jarahi.

The Lurs speak a Persian dialect known as the Lur language, which does not differ materially from Kurdish. They are thought to be aboriginal Persians with a mixture of Semitic blood. While outwardly Shiah Muslims, many show little veneration for the Koran or Prophet; some practice a mixture of Ali-Illahism, involving mysterious ancient heathen rites and a belief in successive incarnations.

## Historical Divisions and Governance
Historically, the region was divided into two principalities:
*   **Great Lur (Lur i Buzurg):** This area included the Bakhtiari division of the Arabistan province and the districts of Kuhgilus and Mamasennis. It was ruled by the Faslúyah Atabegs from approximately 1155 to 1424. Their stronghold was the hill fort of Mungasht, and they maintained a residence at Aidhej (or Mal-Amir) south of Shushan.
*   **Little Lur (Lurikuchik):** Located west of the Dizful river, this area was inhabited by the Feili Lurs, who are further divided into the Pushtkuh (ultra-montane) Lurs in the west and Pishkuh (cis-montane) Lurs in the east. From 1155 until the early 17th century, this region was governed by independent princes of the Khurshidi dynasty, known as atabegs. In the early 17th century, Shah Abbas I removed the last atabeg, Shah Verdi Khan, and granted government to Husain Khan, the chief of a rival tribe, who held the title of vali.
