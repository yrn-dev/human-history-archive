# Aryo-Indians and the Population of the Indian Peninsula

The population of the Indian peninsula is composed of various stocks, including the Dravidians and the Aryo-Indians. The term "Aryo-Indian" is specifically employed to distinguish the Indian people of the Aryan stock from the Dravidian and other stocks that also inhabit parts of the peninsula.

## Origins and Migration of the Aryans
The word *arya* carries dignified associations and was used by the peoples of the Eastern section of the Indo-Europeans to describe themselves. In scholarly terms, "Indo-Aryan" refers to the part of this Eastern branch that settled in India, distinguishing them from the Iranians who remained in Persia and Bactria.

While the Hindus possess no tradition regarding their invasion of India, they are not an indigenous people. Evidence suggests they arrived from the other side of the Hindu Kush. By the period of their earliest literature, roughly 1000 B.C., they were settled in the valley of the Indus. This settlement followed a movement where the Eastern portion of the stock pushed through the Kabul valley into the open country of the Indus.

## Early Settlement and Literature
The *Rig Veda* is believed to have been composed in the district around Umballa. Analysis of the text suggests the people were located to the west of the great rivers at that time, as evidenced by descriptions of the dawn gleaming over the waters before the wind arises. The vocabulary of this period remains largely similar to that of the Iranians; phonetic changes allow for the transliteration of whole sentences between the two languages without changing a single word.

## The Dravidians and Geographic Isolation
The Dravidians represent a distinct stock from the Aryans. Historically, they were divided from the Mongolians by several geographic barriers, including the Himalayas, the vaster massif of central Asia, and the northward extension of the Bay of Bengal into the Ganges valley. The primary link between the Dravidians and Southern Mongols was the canoe.

The practical separation of Dravidian India and China from the west continued almost into historical times. This stands in contrast to the Semites, Hamites, and Aryans, who were in close contact and vigorous reaction at the dawn of history.

## Intermixing in the Borderlands
The influence of different stocks is evident in the composition of the Baluch people. The Baluch are not a homogeneous race but are a mixture of people of Persian and Dravidian stock, as well as those of Arab extraction. 

Certain groups, such as the Bugti and Marri tribes located on the southern buttresses of the Suliman Mountains, are Rind Baluchis. These groups are almost certainly of Arab extraction, having arrived in Sind either with or after the Arab conquerors, where they mixed with the original Hindu inhabitants.
