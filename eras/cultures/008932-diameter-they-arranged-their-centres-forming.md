# Yarrow Boilers and Express Boiler Design

The Yarrow boiler is a type of water-tube boiler utilized extensively in the British navy as well as several other navies. Its primary structure consists of two lower water-chambers and a large cylindrical steam chest, which are connected by a series of numerous straight tubes.

## Tube Specifications and Arrangement
The diameter of the tubes used in Yarrow boilers varies depending on the vessel's requirements. In boilers designed for large vessels, all tubes possess an external diameter of 1-3/4 in. However, in large express boilers, the tubes are differentiated by their proximity to the fire: the two rows nearest the fire on each side have a diameter of 1-1/4 in., while the remaining tubes are 1 in. diameter.

These tubes are arranged so that their centres form equilateral triangles. This specific spacing is designed to facilitate external cleaning, allowing the tubes to be cleaned from the front of the boiler and cross-ways in two directions.

## Design Variations and Dimensions
To improve circulation, some boiler models feature large pipes located outside the casings that connect the water-chambers to the lower part of the steam-chest. 

The largest single-ended large tube boiler in use is characterized by the following specifications:
*   **Steam drum diameter:** 4 ft. 2 in.
*   **Grate area:** 73.5 sq. ft.
*   **Heating surface:** 3750 sq. ft.

Double-ended boilers, which are fired from both ends, have been constructed in sizes larger than the single-ended variety.

## Maintenance and Access
Access to the interior of most boilers is provided via manholes located in the water-chamber ends and the steam-chest. In smaller versions fitted for torpedo boats, the water-chambers are too small to accommodate manholes. In these instances, each water-chamber is arranged in two parts connected by a bolted joint, ensuring that all tube ends remain accessible.

## Express Boilers
Express boilers constitute a class of water-tube boilers commonly used in cruisers and torpedo-boat destroyers. These are employed in scenarios where the maximum power is required relative to the total weight of the installation. 

Compared to standard boilers, express boilers utilize tubes of a smaller diameter. They are designed to support a high rate of fuel combustion, which is achieved through a high degree of "forced draught." The Yarrow express boiler follows a construction similar to the large tube Yarrow boiler, though its tubes are more closely arranged and smaller in diameter.
