# Specific Gravity of a Lump of Solid Lead

In the field of artillery, specific terminology is used to distinguish between different states of material density. A primary distinction is made between specific gravity (S.G.) and gravimetric density (G.D.).

## Gravimetric Density and Specific Gravity
Gravimetric density is a term specific to artillerists. It is utilized to differentiate the specific gravity of a substance when it is filling a given volume in a state of gas from the specific gravity of the separate solid grain or cord of powder.

The relationship between these two measurements can be demonstrated using a lump of solid lead. When a lump of solid lead with a given S.G. is formed into a charge of lead shot—consisting of equal spherules that are closely packed—the resulting G.D. of that charge is determined by the following ratio:

G.D. of charge of lead shot / S.G. of lump of solid lead = 1/6 [pi] [sqrt]2 = 0.7403.

A similar principle applies to cordite; a bundle of cylindrical sticks of cordite has a G.D. relative to the S.G. of a single stick of cordite calculated as 1/6 [pi] [sqrt]3 = 0.9067.

## Water Volume and Weight Standards
For the purposes of these measurements, standard temperatures are applied. At a standard temperature of 62deg F, the volume of 10 lb of water is 277.3 cub. in. Alternatively, 1 cub. ft. (or 1728 cub. in.) of water at this temperature weighs 62.35 lb. Consequently, 1 lb of water occupies a volume of 27.73 cub. in. (1728 / 62.35).
