# Amblystoma

## Classification and History
The genus *Amblystoma* was established in 1838 by J. J. Tschudi. This classification was created for various salamanders originating from North America. Prior to the establishment of this genus, these animals had been described as *Salamandra* or *Lacerta*.

## Physical Characteristics
In terms of general appearance, *Amblystoma* species differ little from European salamanders. They possess a smooth and shiny body featuring vertical grooves on the sides. Their eyes are moderately large and are equipped with movable lids, while the upper lip is nearly straight. The tail of the animal is only feebly compressed.

Despite the general outward similarity to other salamanders, the dentition of the palate is very different. The jaws contain small teeth in a single row; these teeth also form a long series behind the inner nares (choanae), which may be either continuous or interrupted and is transverse in orientation.

## Life Cycle and Species
The *Amblystoma* undergoes a metamorphosis, after which it leaves the water. The final stage of this metamorphosis is characterized by the loss of the gills.

The genus consists of approximately twenty species. One of the most widely distributed and largest species is *Amblystoma tigrinum*. This species is found in the west and east of the United States, as well as in considerable portions of the cooler regions of Mexico. *Amblystoma tigrinum* rarely exceeds nine inches in length and varies in color, though it is usually described as blackish or brown with yellow spots that are sometimes arranged in transverse bands. It has been ascertained that the axolotl transforms into this specific species of *Amblystoma*.

## The Axolotl in Europe
Between 1870 and 1880, axolotls were particularly abundant in Europe. It is generally admitted that these specimens were descendants of a stock bred in Paris and distributed primarily by dealers, believed to have been originally handled by the late P. Carbonnier. In more recent times, specimens have become more difficult to procure and have commanded much higher prices, particularly in France and England. This decrease in numbers is likely caused by close in-breeding without the infusion of new blood.
