# Mysore

## Geography and Physical Features
Mysore is associated with a diverse physical landscape, including the Mysore table-land. A significant portion of this region is occupied by the District of Kolar, which borders the Eastern Ghats. The geography of this district is characterized by numerous hill ranges and a principal watershed located in the north-west around the hill of Nandidrug, which reaches an elevation of 4,810 ft. From this point, rivers radiate in all directions.

The primary rivers serving the region include the Papagani, the North Pinakini, the South Pinakini (also known as the Pennar), and the Palar. These waterways are utilized for irrigation through the use of tanks and anicuts. The geological composition of the area consists mostly of granite or syenite, with small amounts of feldspar and mica. The soil varies by elevation, with fertile loam found in the valleys and gravel and sand present in the higher levels. The hills are typically covered by brushwood, jungle, and scrub.

## Mining and Natural Resources
The region is noted for its mineral deposits. Corundum occurs in Mysore, as well as in Rewa and the Salem district of Madras. 

The District of Kolar is particularly significant for its gold industry, which began with prospecting in 1876. The industry is established on a secure basis, featuring mines operated by the Nandidrug, Ooregum, Champion Reef, and Mysore companies. By the end of 1904, these operations had produced gold with a total value of 21 million sterling. This production resulted in 9 million sterling paid in dividends and 1 million sterling paid in royalty to the Mysore state. 

The mining operations in Kolar utilize electricity for motive power, which is sourced from the falls of the Cauvery, located 93 m. away.

## Industry and Population
Beyond mining, the principal industries in the Kolar district include silk and cotton weaving, as well as the manufacture of sugar. 

As of 1901, the population of the District of Kolar was 723,600, representing a 22% increase over the previous decade. Within this district, the municipality of the Kolar Gold Fields had a population of 38,204 in 1901, though this community suffered severely from plague.

## Historical Sites and Military History
The hill fort of Nandidrug is a site of modern historical interest; it was stormed by the British in 1791 following a bombardment that lasted 21 days.

Additionally, the history of the Mysore princes is linked to the fort of Bellary in the Madras presidency. The upper fort of Bellary, a quadrangular building situated on a granite rock summit, was considered impregnable by the Mysore princes. The fortifications at Bellary were traditionally erected by Hyder Ali with the help of a French engineer.

## Regional Context
In a broader geographical context, the Bombay Presidency extends along the Indian Ocean from the north of Mysore to the south of the Punjab. The coast in this general region is described as rock-bound and difficult to access. While it possesses several bays that serve as fairweather ports for coasting trade, only a few harbours—specifically Karwar, Marmagoa, Bombay, and Karachi-in-Sind—are sufficiently land-locked to protect shipping during the south-west monsoon.
