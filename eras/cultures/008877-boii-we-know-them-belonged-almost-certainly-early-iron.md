# The Boii

The Boii were a Celtic people whose original homeland was Gallia Transalpina. Their name may potentially mean "the terrible." They are identified as belonging almost certainly to the Early Iron Age. Based on the size of the handles of the long iron swords they likely used for cutting blows, it is inferred that they were a race of large men.

## Migrations and Settlements
At an early date, the Boii split into two primary groups. One group migrated into Germany, while the other entered Italy by crossing the Pennine Alps via the Great St Bernard into the valley of the Po. Upon finding this district already occupied, they crossed the river, drove out the Umbrians and Etruscans, and established themselves as far as the Apennines in the modern Romagna. According to Cato, the Boii comprised as many as 112 different tribes.

Some Boii remained in their original homeland; during the Second Punic War, a Boian prince named Magalus offered to guide Hannibal into Italy after the crossing of the Pyrenees.

Other settlements occurred on the Danube. While some sources suggest the Boii were driven back across the Alps to settle on the land of the Taurisci (their kinsmen) on the Danube, adjoining Raetia and Vindelicia, most authorities assume a Boii settlement existed on the Danube from very early times. This area is part of modern Bohemia, anciently known as *Boiohemum*, meaning "land of the Boii." Later, around 60 B.C., some migrated to Pannonia and Noricum.

## Conflict with Rome
The Boii were known to the Romans at least by the time of Plautus, who referred to them contemptuously in the *Captivi*. Their history with Rome was marked by several wars:
* **283 B.C.:** The Boii, along with the Etruscans, were defeated at the Vadimonian lake.
* **224 B.C.:** Following the battle of Telamon in Etruria, the Boii were forced to submit.

Despite their submission, they maintained a hatred for Rome. In 218 B.C., during the Second Punic War, they were irritated by the Roman foundation of the colonies of Placentia and Cremona, leading them to provide valuable assistance to Hannibal. They continued their struggle against Rome until 191 B.C., when they were finally subdued by P. Cornelius Scipio Nasica and lost nearly half of their territory.

## Later History and Legacy
In the first century, the Boii, who had penetrated the Black Forest, were subdued by the Marcomanni. Those who settled in Noricum later mingled with the Lombards and became known as the Boiovarii or Boio Arii (Bavaria), with *var* meaning descendants or remains in some German dialects.

Additionally, in 60 B.C., 32,000 Boii joined a Helvetian expedition into Gaul, where they shared in a defeat near Bibracte. Evidence from tombs at La Tene, Hallstatt, and other sites indicates that the Boii were fairly civilized.
