# Investigations into Aryan Languages and Ancient Texts

## Aryan Language Research
The study of the separation of Aryan languages from other Indo-European languages has historically lacked clarity, with limited success in identifying unknown languages within cuneiform inscriptions. However, the investigation entered a more favourable stage following discoveries by German excavators at Boghaz Keui, a site identified as Herodotus' Pteria in Cappadocia. In this location, treaties dating to the beginning of the 14th century B.C. between the king of the Hittites and the king of Mitanni were found. These documents appear almost certainly to contain the names of Indra, Varuna, and Mitra, which are deities belonging to early Aryan mythology.

Further insights are expected from the analysis of collections from a German expedition to Turfan in Turkestan. A preliminary account provided up to 1909 described Tocharish, a previously unknown Indo-European language. Reports indicate that Tocharish is, in some respects, more akin to Western groups than to Aryan, though further investigation is required.

## Textual Analysis of the New Testament
Recent investigations into the Neutral and Alexandrian texts have refined the understanding of their relationship. While it was previously maintained that the Alexandrian text was a literary development of the Neutral text, and that the Neutral text was used chiefly in Alexandria, more recent research has cast doubt on the widespread use and age of the Neutral text. 

Evidence suggests that the archetype of *Codex Vaticanus* featured a peculiar order of Pauline epistles found only in Egypt. Furthermore, no non-Alexandrian writer has been discovered who used the Neutral text. Researches by Barnard into the Biblical text of Clement of Alexandria indicate that the Neutral text may not have been used in Alexandria during the earliest times; the New Testament text quoted by Clement shares more commonalities with the "geographically Western" or Old Latin text than with the Neutral text.

Consequently, the history of the text in Alexandria can be divided into three stages:
1. The earliest stage, represented by quotations in Clement and used at the end of the 2nd and beginning of the 3rd century.
2. The second stage, found in the quotations of Origen and fairly well represented in $\aleph$ B.
3. The third stage, identified as WH's Alexandrian, found in the quotations of Cyril of Alexandria and specific manuscripts (such as CL [Xi] [Delta] [Psi]). This stage was a literary revision of the second stage, influenced by a tendency to revive readings from the first stage that had been rejected in the second.

## Federal Criminal Investigation Procedures
In the United States, the Department of Justice updated its Attorney General Guidelines for Victim and Witness Assistance in May 2005 to include the Crime Victims' Rights Act (CVRA). These guidelines mandate that agencies involved in the prosecution, investigation, or detection of crime make their best efforts to ensure victims are notified of their CVRA rights. 

Criminal cases are divided into three distinct stages: the investigation stage, the prosecution stage, and the corrections stage. During the investigation stage, if the FBI is the investigating agency, the Special Agent in Charge is responsible for identifying victims at the earliest opportunity after a crime is detected and notifying them of their rights and available services under the VRRA and other federal statutes. The FBI is also mandated to comply with the Victims' Rights and Restitution Act (42 U.S.C. § 10607), which requires officials to protect victims and arrange for reasonable protection from suspected offenders.

During the investigation, the FBI complied with notification obligations by using a Victim Specialist to send computer-generated letters and hand-deliver pamphlets to victims following interviews. The FBI's notification process is independent of the U.S. Attorney's Office (USAO). The FBI stores victim contact information in a case management system, which is then exported to the VNS database. The VNS allows the FBI to generate seven types of notification letters:
1. Initial notification
2. Case is under investigation
3. Arrest of the defendant
4. Declination of prosecution
5. Other
6. Advice of victim rights
7. Investigation closed

Once a charging document is filed and the case enters the "prosecution stage," responsibility for notification shifts to the USAO's Victim Witness Specialist.
