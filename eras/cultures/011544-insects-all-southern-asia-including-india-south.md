# Insects of Southern Asia

## Regional Classification
The insects inhabiting southern Asia form a single cohesive group. This region encompasses China, Siam, the Malayan Islands, and India south of the Himalaya. The biological unity of this group is evidenced by the fact that not only the genera but often the individual species are identical on opposite sides of the Bay of Bengal.

## Geographic Boundaries and Distribution
The distribution of insect types is largely defined by latitudinal boundaries. Indo-Malayan types are encountered south of approximately 30 degrees North. North of this line, European forms are found; for example, nearly one-third of the Coleopterous insects known to inhabit east Siberia are also found in western Europe. Japan is categorized as part of the Europeo-Asiatic group.

## Interregional Connections
There is a marked connection between the insect populations of India and Africa. This is demonstrated by the occurrence of many genera that are common to both Africa and India and are confined exclusively to those two regions. Additionally, similarities in form are not uncommon between these two areas, even in instances where the genera are not peculiar to them.

## Ecological Interactions
Insects within these regions serve as a food source for various avian species. For instance, the kestrel (_Falco tinnunculus_), whose range extends over a great part of Asia, consumes insects as part of its diet. A smaller species of kestrel, _T. tinnunculoides_, is also widely spread across Asia and Africa, with specimens found in China and India being distinguished as _T. pekinensis_.
