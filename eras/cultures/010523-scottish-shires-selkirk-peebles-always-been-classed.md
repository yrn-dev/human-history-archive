# The Scottish Border Shires

The term "The Borders" refers to the territory situated on both sides of the boundary line between Scotland and England. While the name is applied to both sides, it is most frequently used in a geographical, historical, and literary sense to describe the Scottish side of the frontier.

## Geography and Boundaries
The boundary line between the two nations begins on the coast of Berwickshire, approximately 3 miles North by West of Berwick. From there, it runs West and South to reach the Tweed near the village of Paxton, following the river to a point just beyond Carham. The line then strikes South-South-East toward the Cheviot Hills. The watershed of these hills serves as the boundary for 35 miles, after which the line is formed by a series of streams: the Liddel, the Kershope, Bells Burn, and the Esk. After following the Esk for 1 mile, the boundary cuts west to the Sark, following it to the river's mouth at the head of the Solway Firth.

The total length of this boundary is 108 miles, although the direct distance from the North Sea to the Solway is only 70 miles. Two specific areas are noted at the extremes of this line:
* **The Liberties of Berwick:** A small district of 8 square miles north of the Tweed that is not included in Scotland, forming the "bounds" of the town and borough of Berwick-on-Tweed.
* **The Debatable Land:** A strip of country between the Esk and the Sark (up to the Esk's junction with the Liddel). This area was a "No man's land" and a haunt for brigands and outlaws for generations due to constant contention between Scotland and England, until boundaries were adjusted in 1552.

## Classification of the Shires
The English Border counties are Cumberland and Northumberland. On the Scottish side, the Border counties are Dumfries, Roxburgh, and Berwick. However, by usage and historical classification, the Scottish shires of Peebles and Selkirk have always been classed as Border shires.

## Regional Characteristics
The region is defined by its river systems. The Scottish side is watered by the Sark, Esk, Liddel, Kershope, Jed, Kale, Leet, Whiteadder, and the Tweed. Physically, the region differs from the English side; the southern side consists largely of rugged glens and bleak, lofty moorland used for the subsistence of cattle and sheep.

### Dumfriesshire
Dumfriesshire is a border county bounded to the south by the Solway Firth, to the southeast by Cumberland, and to the east by Roxburghshire. Its northern boundaries are shared with the shires of Selkirk, Peebles, and Lanark, while it is bounded to the west by Kirkcudbrightshire and Ayrshire. The county covers an area of 1,072 square miles (686,302 acres) with a coastline of 21 miles.

The terrain of Dumfriesshire slopes gradually from mountainous northern districts down to the sea, featuring lofty hills and stretches of rich fertile holms or tableland. The northern boundaries contain the highest mountains, including White Coomb (2,695 ft.), Hart Fell (2,651 ft.), Saddle Yoke (2,412 ft.), Swatte Fell (2,389 ft.), Lowther Hills (2,377 ft.), Queensberry (2,285 ft.), and Ettrick Pen (2,269 ft.).

The county is cleft from north to south by three great dales formed by its longest rivers:
* **The Nith:** Entering from Ayrshire, it flows south-easterly for 65 miles before entering the Solway below Dumfries.
* **The Annan:** Rising near the Devil's Beef Tub, it flows south for about 40 miles to the Solway at Barnkirk headland.
* **The Esk:** Formed by the confluence of the Black Esk and White Esk, it flows south-easterly, crosses the Border, and falls into the Solway through northwestern Cumberland.

Additionally, the county contains reclaimed moss lands near the Solway, such as Longbridge Moor, Lochar Moss, Craigs Moss, and Nutberry Moss.

## Agricultural and Demographic Data
In terms of orchard production, the Scottish counties of Peebles and Selkirk are among those returning less than 10 acres each. Demographic records indicate that in the district of Peebles, there were 21 illegitimate births per 1,000 of the population, while Selkirk recorded 46.
