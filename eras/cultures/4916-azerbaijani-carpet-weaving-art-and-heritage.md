# Azerbaijani Carpet Weaving: Art and Heritage

## Overview
Azerbaijani carpet weaving is not merely a craft but a profound expression of national identity, blending nomadic traditions with settled urban artistry. For centuries, these textiles have served as visual archives, encoding the spiritual beliefs, social structures, and geographical landscapes of the South Caucasus. 

The art is characterized by a sophisticated use of geometric and floral motifs, often utilizing natural dyes derived from madder root, indigo, and pomegranate skins. Historically, Azerbaijani carpets are categorized into several distinct schools—most notably the Guba, Shirvan, Baku, and Karabakh schools—each defined by unique knotting techniques, color palettes, and symbolic vocabularies. While the Guba and Shirvan styles are often noted for their intricate, smaller-scale patterns, the Karabakh carpets are renowned for their larger compositions and vibrant floral arrangements.

## Timeline of Evolution

| Era | Development |
| :--- | :--- |

## Key Facts
*   **Symbolism:** Every motif carries meaning. The *buta* (a stylized flame or almond shape) is perhaps the most iconic, symbolizing fire, eternity, and the spiritual essence of the region.
*   **The Schools:** Historians generally divide the craft into regional schools. The **Baku** school is known for its "city carpets" with complex floral designs, while the **Guba** school is praised for its technical precision and geometric rigor.
*   **Materials:** Traditionally, high-quality wool from local sheep is used for both the warp and weft, though silk is frequently integrated into luxury pieces to create a shimmering effect.
*   **Historiographical Debate:** There is an ongoing academic discussion regarding the exact origins of certain motifs. Some scholars argue for a primary influence from Central Asian nomadic migrations, while others emphasize the synthesis of local Caucasian traditions with the artistic mandates of the Persian empires.

## Did You Know?
1. **UNESCO Recognition:** In 2010, the art of Azerbaijani carpet weaving was officially recognized by UNESCO as a masterpiece of intangible cultural heritage.
2. **The Buta Motif:** The *buta* is so central to Azerbaijani identity that it appears not only in carpets but also in jewelry, architecture, and traditional clothing.
3. **Natural Chemistry:** Traditional Azerbaijani weavers used "mordants" (like alum) to fix dyes, ensuring that the deep reds and blues of the carpets remained vibrant for centuries without fading.
