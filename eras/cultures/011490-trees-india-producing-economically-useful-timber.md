# Economically Useful Timber Trees of India

The variety of trees in India that produce economically useful timber is comparatively limited. This scarcity is attributed to the lack of durability in wood caused by the region's extremely moist and hot climate.

## Primary Timber Species
The finest timber is supplied by the teak (*Tectona grandis*). Teak is found in its greatest perfection in the Indian peninsula and the forests of the west coasts of Burma, particularly in areas with the heaviest rainfall. These trees grow to heights of 100 to 150 feet and are typically mixed with bamboos and other tree species. Due to an essential oil that preserves steel and iron—unlike the tannic acid found in oak—teak has supplanted oak in the construction of iron ships.

The sal (*Shorea robusta*) provides a very durable wood and is more gregarious in growth than the teak. It is most abundant in central India and along the skirts of the Himalaya, extending from the Punjab to Assam.

## Regional and Specialized Woods
Different regions of the country yield specific useful woods:

*   **The Plains:** Useful woods found in the plains include the sissoo (*Dalbergia*), toon (*Cedrela*), and babool (*Acacia*).
*   **The Himalaya:** The deodar (*Cedrus deodara*) is the only timber in ordinary use obtained from the Himalaya proper.
*   **Southern India:** This region is the source of sandalwood (*Santalum*).
*   **General Distribution:** Many varieties of bamboo are found throughout all parts of the country.

Additionally, the cinchona has been introduced with complete success, and the mahogany of America has shown promise for timber use after reaching a large size.

## Palms and Other Economic Trees
Various palm species provide valuable commercial products. The toddy palm (*Phoenix sylvestris*) is used to produce date sugar from its sap or toddy; when not boiled into sugar, the juice is fermented and distilled into arrack or drunk fresh. In the hill country of southern India, a small species known as *Phoenix farinifera* is used to obtain date palm meal from its stem.

The date palm (*Phoenix dactylifera*) ranges from Northern Africa and the Canary Islands through south-east Asia to India. It is a beautiful tree reaching heights of 60 to 80 feet, characterized by a stem marked with old leaf-scars and a crown of shining pinnate leaves.

## Aromatic and Incense Plants
India also utilizes plants for aromatic purposes. The root of *Acorus Calamus*, known as the bach of the Hindus, is widely used for incense. Furthermore, an aromatic earth found on the coast of Cutch is used as incense within the temples of western India.

## Forestry in Burma
The forests of Burma are considered the finest in British India and a primary asset of the country's wealth. The global supply of teak for shipbuilding is mainly drawn from this region. Beyond teak, other valuable woods include *padauk*, india rubber, *sha* (or cutch), and *pyingado* (ironwood), the latter of which is used for railway sleepers.

Because of the value of these resources, the government strictly preserves the forests of Burma through a forest department. This department manages the conservation and cutting of timber, prevents forest fires, and plants young trees. In reserved areas, commercial timber can only be cut under a license that provides revenue to the state, while natives may cut unreserved trees for home consumption. The diversity of these forests is immense; an enumeration by Sulpiz Kurz in 1877 identified some 1,500 species in Lower Burma alone.
