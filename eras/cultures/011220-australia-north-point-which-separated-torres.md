# Australia

## Geography and Regional Context
Australia is a landmass located in the southern hemisphere, situated north of Kerguelen Island (also known as Desolation Island). Its northern point is separated from New Guinea (or Papua) by the Torres Straits. In a broader zoogeographical sense, Australia is part of the "Austrogaea" region, which encompasses the Australian, Papuan, and New Zealand subregions, as well as Polynesia.

While the islands of the Indian or Malay Archipelago appear to form an unbroken series from the Strait of Malacca to the southern coast of New Guinea, a definite barrier exists between the Indian and Australian regions. This frontier is marked by a strait approximately 15 miles wide that separates the islands of Bali and Lombok; Bali belongs to the Indian Region, while Lombok belongs to the Australian Region.

## Colonial History and Administration
The Australian continent has been divided into several states and territories with varying dates of British settlement and administrative changes:
*   **Tasmania:** Settlement occurred in 1803.
*   **Queensland:** Established in 1824 and later separated from New South Wales in 1859.
*   **West Australia:** Settlement began in 1826.
*   **Victoria:** Established in 1834 and separated from New South Wales in 1851.
*   **South Australia:** Settlement began in 1836.

Additionally, a protectorate was declared over Papua in 1884.

## Cartography and Surveying
Cadastral surveys conducted by surveyors-general and trigonometrical surveys have been ongoing for many years across the Australian states, though Western Australia is an exception regarding the latter. The publication of county, township, and parish maps has generally kept pace with the settlement of the country.

Among the states, Victoria possesses the most accurate topographical maps, comparable to those published in Europe. Victoria began a geodetic survey in 1858, producing maps on a scale of 1:126,730 and a general map on a scale of 1:506,930. Other states utilize different scales for their maps:
*   **New South Wales, South Australia, and Tasmania:** 1:560,000.
*   **Western Australia:** 1:560,000.
*   **Queensland:** 1:253,460.

## Flora and Fauna
Australia is home to distinct zoological features. The continent possesses one large species of bustard. In the rivers of Queensland, the *Ceratodontidae* is found; only one species of this group is currently living, though its remains have been discovered in various rocks across North America, Europe, India, Africa, and Australia. Additionally, the *Dipneusti* (specifically *Neoceratodus*) linger in Australia.

## Indigenous Culture and Traditions
Among the "blackfellows" of Australia, the bullroarer—a small flat slip of wood with a string—holds the highest sanctity and mystic significance. These instruments, known as "tunduns," are used in initiation ceremonies (the Bora) and are regarded with great awe.

There are two types of tunduns:
1.  **The "grandfather" or "man tundun":** Distinguished by a deep tone.
2.  **The "woman tundun":** A smaller instrument that produces a weaker, shriller note.

Strict social taboos govern the instrument; boys before initiation, as well as women and girls, are never permitted to see the tundun. During the Bora ceremonies, the humming sound of the bullroarer is believed to be the voice of the "Great Spirit," a sound that causes women to hide in terror.
