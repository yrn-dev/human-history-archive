# BAOBAB

The baobab, scientifically known as *Adansonia digitata*, is a tree of the natural order *Bombaceae*. It is native to tropical Africa and is recognized as one of the largest trees known to exist.

## Physical Characteristics
The baobab is characterized by its immense stem, which can reach 30 feet in diameter, although the tree does not achieve great height. The wood of the baobab is noted for being very soft and light. Due to these properties, the trunks of living trees are frequently excavated to be used as houses.

## Fruit and Botanical Classification
The tree produces a large, woody, multilocular syncarpous fruit. This fruit contains seeds buried within a mucilaginous pulp that possesses a pleasant, cool taste. 

## Utility and Resources
The baobab provides several materials for human use. The bark of the tree yields a strong fibre, which is utilized for weaving into cloth and for the manufacture of ropes.

## Distribution and Habitat
The baobab is found across various regions of Africa:
* **West Africa:** In the French protectorate of Bondu, the lower country is described as being finely clothed with the baobab, alongside the tamarind and other fruit-trees. In other interior regions of the West Coast, baobabs are found within forests that also contain the oil palm and coco-nut palm.
* **Other Regions:** The tree is found in the neighbourhood of the khors, where it is listed as one of the chief trees alongside acacias.

## Cultural and Geographical Significance
In the Sudan, the *Adansonia digitata* is referred to as the *kuka* or monkey bread tree. The modern town of Kuka, founded around 1810 by Sheikh Mahommed al Amin al Kanemi, is believed to have derived its name from this tree, as extensive plantations of the *kuka* are located in the surrounding area.

## Nomenclature
The genus name *Adansonia* was established by Linnaeus to honor Michel Adanson, who was a celebrated traveller and French botanist.
