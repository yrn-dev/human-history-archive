# Fichtelgebirge

The Fichtelgebirge is a mountain range located in Central Europe. It serves as a geographical link between other major massifs, combining with the Erzgebirge (also known as the Ore Mountains or *Rudo Hori*) to the east via the Elstergebirge. To the west, the Fichtelgebirge is united with the Bohmerwald (the Bohemian Forest) through the plateau of Waldsassen.

## Regional Geography and Visibility
The Fichtelgebirge is part of a broader orographic system that borders Bohemia on several sides. To the north-west, the Erzgebirge forms a frontier with an average altitude of 2,600 ft. and a highest point at the Keilberg, reaching 4,080 ft. To the south-west lies the Bohmerwald, which runs in a general direction from south-east to north-west.

The relationship between these ranges can be observed from specific vantage points in the region. For example, from the basaltic rock of Podhorn, which stands at an altitude of 2,776 ft. and is situated approximately 3 miles east of Marienbad, an extensive view of the Fichtelgebirge, the Bohmerwald, and the Erzgebirge can be obtained.

## Geology and Mineralogy
The Fichtelgebirge is noted for specific mineral deposits. In the norite of the Kupferberg within the Fichtelgebirge, masses of bronzite are found. Bronzite is a mineral that is sometimes cut and polished into convex forms for small ornamental objects. It is characterized by a more or less distinct fibrous structure, which, when pronounced, gives the mineral a sheen resembling that of a cat's-eye.

## Surrounding Environment
The Fichtelgebirge exists within a region characterized by diverse mountainous terrain and varying climates. The neighboring Bohmerwald is divided by the pass of Neumark into two parts: the northern part (*Cesky Les*), which reaches 3,300 ft. at the massif of Czerkov, and the southern part (*Sumava*), which is the most picturesque and highest section. The southern Bohmerwald includes the Plockenstein (4,513 ft.) and the Osser (4,053 ft.), though its highest peak, the Arber (4,872 ft.), is located in Bavaria. This range is known for its blue lakes, crystalline torrents, and forests of pine and oak.

The climate of the broader Bohemian region is continental and generally healthy, though rainfall varies significantly by district. While the interior of Bohemia receives a mean annual rainfall of 18 inches, the Bohmerwald receives significantly more, ranging between 60 and 70 inches.
