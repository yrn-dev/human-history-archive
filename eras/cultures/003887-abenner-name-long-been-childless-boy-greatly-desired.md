# King Abenner and the Story of Josaphat

## The Reign of King Abenner
In the Greek story, the setting begins in India, where the people had been converted by St Thomas. Following the emergence of eremitic life in Egypt, many Indians adopted this lifestyle. During this period, a powerful pagan king named Abenner rose to power. King Abenner was characterized by his hatred and persecution of Christians, with a particular focus on the persecution of ascetics.

## The Birth of Josaphat
For a long period of time, King Abenner remained childless. Eventually, a boy was born to him who was greatly desired and described as matchless in beauty. The king gave the child the name of Josaphat. Overjoyed by the birth, Abenner summoned astrologers to predict the destiny of his son. These astrologers foretold that the boy would achieve glory and prosperity that would exceed those of all his predecessors.

## The Prophecy of the Sage
While the astrologers predicted general success, one sage, who was the most learned among them, provided a more specific intimation. He assented to the prediction of glory but clarified that the scene of this glory would not be the king's paternal kingdom. Instead, Josaphat's glory would be found in another kingdom that was infinitely more exalted. Furthermore, the sage predicted that the child would eventually adopt the very faith that his father, King Abenner, persecuted.

## The Seclusion of the Prince
As the boy grew, he began to exhibit a devout and thoughtful nature. This behavior, combined with the memory of the sage's prediction, troubled King Abenner. In an attempt to prevent the prophecy from coming true, the king selected a secluded city and commissioned the construction of a splendid palace for his son.

The king established strict rules for the boy's upbringing to ensure he remained isolated from influences that might lead him toward the faith of Christ or a consideration of the afterlife. The conditions of Josaphat's residence were as follows:
* **Company:** He was to be attended only by servants and tutors who were in the flower of health and youth.
* **Access:** No strangers were permitted to have access to him.
* **Knowledge:** The boy was to be kept ignorant of the sorrows of humanity, specifically death, old age, disease, and poverty.
* **Environment:** He was to be exposed only to pleasant things so that he would have no inducement to think about a future life.
* **Religious Restriction:** He was never to hear a single word regarding the religion of Christ or Christ himself.
