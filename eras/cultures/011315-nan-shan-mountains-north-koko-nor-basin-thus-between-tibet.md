# Nan Shan Mountains

The Nan Shan mountains, also referred to as the Southern Mountains, are a significant mountain system in Central Asia located north of the Koko Nor basin. They form part of a complex geographical transition between the elevated highland steppes of Tibet and the low-lying sands of the Gobi.

## Geographical Position and Structure
The Nan Shan highlands encompass a region approximately 380 miles long and 260 miles wide. The system is characterized by parallel mountain ranges that run from the northwest to the southeast. These ranges are separated by broad, flat longitudinal valleys. In the western portion of the highlands, the peaks can reach altitudes of 18,000 to 20,000 feet, while in the eastern portion, they are generally about 2,000 feet lower. The mountain chains themselves typically range from 8 to 17 miles in width, though they seldom exceed 35 miles. The intervening valleys reach widths of 20 to 27 miles.

The highlands are situated at an average altitude of 12,000 to 14,000 feet, although the south-western border is lower, ranging from 9,000 to 10,000 feet. The passes within this region generally maintain altitudes between 12,000 and 14,000 feet.

## Regional Boundaries and Connections
The Nan Shan system is connected to other major geographical features of Central Asia:
*   **North and East:** To the northeast, the highlands descend to the Mongolian plateau (which has an average altitude of 4,000 feet) in the Ala-shan. In the southeast, they abut the highlands of the Chinese province of Kan-suh. Near the great northward bend of the Hwang-ho, they meet the escarpments that continue the In-shan and Great Khingan ranges.
*   **Northwest:** The range is fringed by the Da-sue-shan border range, a continuation of the Astin-tagh. This border range rises to 12,200–13,000 feet in its passes and separates the Nan Shan from the Pe-shan range.
*   **South and Southwest:** To the southwest, the Nan Shan consist of short, irregular chains separated by broad plains and lakes with altitudes between 8,800 and 9,000 feet. This area overlooks Tsaidam, a system of elevated valleys 8,000 to 9,000 feet above sea level. To the south lies the Koko Nor basin.

## Constituent Ranges and Features
The Nan Shan system includes several specific ranges and landmarks:
*   **The Higher Terrace:** This area (12,000–13,500 feet) includes Ritter's range, which features extensive snow-fields and passes at 14,680 and 15,990 feet. It also includes the Humboldt (or Ama-surgu) range, which runs from the Astin-tagh to approximately 38° N. Other parallel ranges in this area include the Semenov, Suess, Alexander III., Mushketov, and Bain-sarlyk.
*   **Eastern Nan Shan:** This region is dominated by three ranges of nearly equal altitude with peaks rising above the snow-line. One of these is referred to interchangeably as the Nan Shan, the Momo-shan, or the Richthofen Range.
*   **Lung-shan:** A range approximately 10,000 feet high that borders the Lian-chow and Kan-chow valley to the northeast.
*   **Lakes:** The valleys between the ranges are dotted with lakes, including Koko-nor (at 9,970 feet) and Khara-nor (at 13,285 feet).

## Climate and Ecology
The environment of the Nan Shan varies significantly between its western and eastern sections:
*   **Western Portion:** This area is characterized by a greater development of glaciers. The valleys are dry, and the slopes are devoid of vegetation, furrowed by deep ravines. The soil consists of clay and dry gravel with sparse growth of *Salsolaceae*, *Nitraria*, and *Ephedra* bushes. Good pasture is limited to areas near streams.
*   **Northeastern Portion:** This region receives more copious and better-distributed rainfall. Every gorge contains a stream, and both the valley bottoms and mountain slopes are covered in vegetation. Common flora include deciduous trees (birch, mountain ash, and Przhevalsky's poplar), conifers (*Picea obovata*), and various bushes. In picturesque gorges, willows, *Lonicereae*, *Spriaeae*, *Potentilla fruticosa*, and rhododendrons are found.
*   **Southern Portion:** In the area entering Kan-suh province, there are extensive accumulations of loess, and trees are found only on the northern slopes of the hills.

## Relation to Koko Nor
The Nan Shan mountains form the northern boundary of the Koko Nor (or Kuku-nor) lake, also known as *Tsing-hai* in Chinese and *Tso-ngombo* in Tangut. The lake is situated at an altitude of 9,975 feet in the extreme northeast of Tibet. It is a salt-water lake measuring 66 by 40 miles, containing several islands, one of which houses a Lamaist (Buddhist) monastery. The lake is surrounded by Tangut nomads and is bordered to the south by the southern Koko-nor range.
