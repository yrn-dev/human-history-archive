# Enrolments

The term "enrolment" appears across various historical contexts, ranging from the registration of legal conveyances and the administration of citizenship and education to the organization of military forces and the recording of historical achievements.

## Legal and Administrative Enrolments

In English law, the "bargain and sale" was a contract used to transfer real or personal property from a bargainer to a bargainee for valuable consideration. This method was often used to convey lands when feudal owners faced disabilities that made "feoffment with livery of seisin" (the handing over of feudal possession) difficult. While equity initially viewed the bargainer as seised of the land for the bargainee's use, the Statute of Uses (1535) converted the bargainee's interest into a legal estate. Because this allowed for secret or private conveyances, the Statute of Enrolments was passed in the same year. This law required every conveyance of freehold lands by bargain and sale to be enrolled with the *custos rotulorum* of the county or in a court of record within six months. This requirement applied specifically to estates for life or inheritance; estates for years did not require enrolment, which subsequently led to the development of "lease and release" as a replacement for the deed of bargain and sale regarding freeholds.

In the context of public education in Illinois, enrolment figures reflect the impact of compulsory attendance laws for children aged 7 to 16. For the period of 1907-1908, out of a school-age population of 1,500,066, there were 988,078 children enrolled in public schools.

## Civic and Political Enrolment

In ancient Greece, specifically Athens and other towns, the *ephebi* were young men aged eighteen to twenty who formed a state-controlled college. Upon reaching civil majority at seventeen, a youth could be enrolled on the register of his deme (*lexiarchikon grammateion*), provided he passed a scrutiny (*dokimasia*) regarding his physical capability, civic descent, and age, and belonged to the first three property classes. In the era of Aristotle, the names of these enrolled ephebi were recorded on bronze pillars, having previously been kept on wooden tablets.

Enrolment was also a tool for shifting political power and citizenship in the Ionic cities of Sicily. The democratical government of Leontini once resolved to strengthen the city by enrolling many new citizens and redistributing state territorial property to provide them with land. However, the town's aristocracy forestalled this by conspiring with Syracuse and expelling the Demos. Subsequently, wealthy Leontines dismantled their city and moved to Syracuse, where they were enrolled as Syracusan citizens, allowing them to take possession of the properties of the exiled Demos.

Similarly, in the Lacedæmonian confederacy, the Olynthians were forced to dissolve their federation and enrol themselves as sworn members of the confederacy, accepting obligations of service to Sparta. Other component Grecian cities were also enrolled severally as allies of Sparta.

## Military Enrolment and Conscription

Military enrolment has taken various forms, from voluntary enlistment to compulsory service. In the British armed forces during the Great War, enrolment numbers grew rapidly. In August 1914, the forces consisted of 250,000 Regulars, 200,000 trained Reserves, and 250,000 partly-trained Territorials. Following a request from Lord Kitchener for 100,000 volunteers, that number was enrolled in less than a fortnight, with 30,000 enlisting in a single day. By May 1916, over 5,000,000 men had enrolled voluntarily in the navy and army, reaching 8,500,000 enrolled in the Crown's armed forces by August 1918.

In other regions, such as the republic described in 1905, the military included 2,890 regulars and an enrolled force of 80,000 men. This enrolled force was divided into a second reserve (40,000), a first reserve (30,000), and territorial guards (10,000), though they remained unarmed and unorganized. A 1894 conscription law mandated compulsory service between ages twenty-one and fifty, though the law was considered a "dead letter."

In ancient military history, commanders such as Demetrius utilized enrolment to recover from failures; after a setback in his first campaign, Demetrius focused on enrolling fresh soldiers and drilling new levies to rebuild his strength.

## Commemorative Enrolment

Beyond legal and military registers, enrolment has been used to immortalize human achievement. In certain ancient cultures, victors of competitions, such as the Olympic games, were granted the honor of having the year dated by their name. This ensured their names would be enrolled in the annals for all future ages and placed at the front of all laws enacted in the year of their victory.
