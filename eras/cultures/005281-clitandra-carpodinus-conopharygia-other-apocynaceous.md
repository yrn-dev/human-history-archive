# Clitandra, Carpodinus and Conopharygia

## Overview and Botanical Classification
*Clitandra*, *Carpodinus*, and *Conopharygia* are types of plants categorized as apocynaceous. These plants are identified as sources of rubber, alongside other apocynaceous plants, as well as species of *Ficus* and *Landolphia*.

## Geographic Distribution and Habitat
These plants are found in British Central Africa. The region's flora is characterized by a landscape that is generally well-watered and covered with vegetation, described as having a pleasing and attractive character. The territory primarily falls within the savannah or park-like division of the continent, rather than the forest region of West Africa.

While actual forests of lofty trees—specifically those of a West African type—are few in number, they are located in specific areas:
* Portions of the Shiré Highlands, Angoniland, and Nyika plateaus.
* Certain nooks in valleys situated near the south end of Tanganyika.
* The slopes of Mounts Chiradzulu and Mlanje, where patches of tropical luxuriance can be seen.

Most West African forest trees are represented within British Central Africa. A comprehensive list of the known flora for this region has been compiled by assistants at Kew and Sir W. Thiselton-Dyer, and is included in the first and second editions of the work on British Central Africa authored by Sir H. H. Johnston.

## Commercial Significance
Within the context of the country's principal vegetable products, *Clitandra*, *Carpodinus*, and *Conopharygia* are noted for their commercial interest due to the rubber derived from them. Other commercially significant vegetable products in the region include:
* **Drugs:** The *Strophanthus* pod.
* **Cereals:** African cultivated cereals such as *Eleusine*, maize, rice, *Pennisetum*, *Sorghum*, and wheat (the latter being cultivated primarily by Europeans).
* **Other Crops:** Cotton, ground-nuts (*Voandzeia* and *Arachis*), and tobacco, which consists of both introduced and native varieties.
* **Coffee:** While European settlers have introduced cultivated coffee from abroad, wild coffee is reported to grow in some mountainous districts.
* **Palms:** Six species of palms, including the oil palm in the north-west.
