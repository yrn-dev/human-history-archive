# Family and Domestic Life in Historical Contexts

The records of the Human History Archive provide various perspectives on the nature of domestic life, the upbringing of children, and the emotional bonds between parents and their offspring across different cultures and eras.

## Marriage and Early Parenthood
In certain ancient marriage customs, the union began with the husband carrying off his bride by force. These women were not immature girls but grown women ripe for marriage. Following the abduction, the bride was received by a bridesmaid who cut her hair close, dressed her in shoes and a man's cloak, and placed her alone in a dark chamber. 

The bridegroom would visit the room after dining at his mess, without feasting or revelry. For a significant period, the husband continued to spend his days with his companions, visiting his wife by stealth. This arrangement was characterized by a mutual effort to meet unobserved, as the husband felt ashamed and afraid of being heard by others in the house. This practice of restricted intercourse was believed to exercise self-restraint and keep passions unblunted. In some instances, this continued for so long that children were born to couples before the spouses had ever seen one another by daylight.

## Parental Affection and the Upbringing of Children
The archive contains various accounts of parental love and the roles parents played in the development of their children. In the case of King Olaf Eirikson of Sweden, his children—including Emund, Astrid, and Holmfrid—were noted for being clever from childhood and handsome in appearance. However, domestic harmony was not always present; the queen behaved poorly toward her step-children, leading King Olaf to send his son Emund to be fostered by relations in Vindland. His daughter, Astrid, was brought up in the house of a worthy man named Egil in West Gautland, where she grew into a lovely, modest, and generous girl who frequently visited her father's house.

In other contexts, the term "children" is used metaphorically to describe a creator's attachment to their work. For example, the battle-cruisers *Repulse*, *Renown*, *Courageous*, *Furious*, and *Glorious* were described as "an old man’s children." Despite the Navy's view that these ships lacked the structural strength and armor required for new conditions of war, their "parent" loved them dearly and defended their qualities with vehemence against any slurs.

## Domestic Responsibilities and Social Standing
The archive highlights a contrast between those who prioritized domestic duties and those who neglected them. Some individuals expressed a lack of delight in work or "household cares, that breed up children bright," preferring instead the pursuit of war, ships, and weapons. Such neglect of home duties was sometimes associated with gaining a living through piracy and injustice abroad.

The financial state of a household also impacted the future of its children. The family of Cato is cited as having supplied Rome with consuls and prætors for four generations, with grandchildren and their descendants rising to the highest state offices. In contrast, the poverty of Aristeides—despite his standing in Greece—meant he could not provide dowries for his daughters or funeral expenses for himself. This lack of resources forced some of his family into the disreputable profession of interpreting dreams or onto public charity, preventing them from emulating the actions of their ancestor.

## Family Dynamics and Loss
Family structures often involved complex care arrangements and the tragedy of early death. Octavia, upon returning from Athens, chose to remain in her husband's house despite orders from Cæsar to dwell in her own. She took an honorable and liberal approach to caring for the children, including both her own and those of Fulvia. 

In the royal house of King Olaf and Queen Thyre, the birth of a stout and promising boy named Harald brought great joy. The king and queen loved the infant exceedingly, hoping he would inherit his father's position. However, the child lived barely a year after birth, an event that both parents took deeply to heart.
