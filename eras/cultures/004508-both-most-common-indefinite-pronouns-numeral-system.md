# Basque Pronouns and Numeral System

The Basque language utilizes a distinct system of pronouns and a specific numerical structure. Its pronominal system includes personal pronouns, demonstratives, and indefinite pronouns, while its numbering follows a vicesimal pattern.

## Personal and Relative Pronouns
The personal pronouns in Basque include *ni* for "I," *hi* for "thou," *gu* for "we," and *zu* for "you." In modern usage, *zu* has transitioned into a polite form of "thou." To express a true plural "you" (referring to more than one person), the pluralizing sign *k--zuek* is suffixed. 

Additional forms associated with verbal inflexions include *d*, *it*, and *t*, which can mean "I" or "me." Examples of these in use include *d-akus-t* ("it see I," meaning "I see it") and *d-arrai-t* ("it follows me"). The concept of the reflective "self" is expressed using the word *buru*, meaning "head."

Basque lacks a relative pronoun. Instead, it employs a verbal participle ending in *n*; for example, *doa* means "he goes," while *doana* means "he who is going." However, modern Basque has adopted the interrogatives *zein* and *zoin* as relatives, imitating Spanish or French.

## Demonstratives and Interrogatives
Third-person pronouns in Basque are mere demonstratives. There are three primary forms: *hura* or *kura* ("that"), *hau* or *kau* ("this"), and *ori* or *kori* ("this" or "that"). These demonstratives also function as articles, as seen in the phrases *gazt-en-or* ("this younger one") and *andre-ori* ("this lady at some distance").

Interrogative pronouns include *nor* ("who"), *zer* ("what"), and *zembait* ("how much").

## Indefinite Pronouns
The most common indefinite pronouns in the language are:
* *Bat*: "one"
* *Batzu*: "several"
* *Bakotch*: "each"
* *Norbait*: "some one"
* *Hanitz* or *hainitz*: "much"
* *Elkar*: "both"

## The Numeral System
The Basque numeral system is vicesimal. This is demonstrated by the construction of numbers such as 34, which is expressed as *hogoi ta hamalaur* ("twenty and fourteen").

The numbers from one to ten are as follows:
1. *bat*
2. *bi*
3. *hiru*
4. *lau*
5. *bortz* or *bost*
6. *sei*
7. *zazpi*
8. *zortzi*
9. *bederatzi*
10. *hamar*

Higher values include *hogoi* or *hogei* for 20, *berrogoi* for 40 (meaning "twice twenty"), and *ehun* for 100. The language possesses no genuine word for a thousand.
