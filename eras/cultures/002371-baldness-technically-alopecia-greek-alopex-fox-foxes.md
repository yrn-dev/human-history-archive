# BALDNESS

## Definition and Etymology
Baldness, technically referred to as alopecia, is the result of the loss of hair, specifically on the human scalp. The term is derived from the Greek word *alopex*, meaning a fox, a reference to the fact that foxes often have bald patches on their coats.

## Types of Alopecia
Among forms of alopecia that can be remedied, two distinct types are identified:

*   **Alopecia Seborrhoica:** This form manifests as premature baldness, which is frequently observed in young men. The condition is characterized by a steady advancement of hair loss from the forehead backwards, continuing until only a fringe of hair remains on the head. This type of baldness is always caused by an underlying disease known as seborrhoea.
*   **Alopecia Areata:** This form of hair loss is currently regarded as an epidemic disease.

## Treatment of Alopecia Seborrhoica
While alopecia seborrhoica progresses steadily if it is neglected, it is considered very amenable to treatment. The most valuable drugs for treating this condition are salicylic acid and sulphur. A recommended application consists of eighteen grains of each drug added to one ounce of vaseline, which should be rubbed into the scalp daily over a prolonged period.

For those who object to the greasiness of vaseline, a salicylic lotion may be used as a substitute, although the vaseline application is likely of greater value. The lotion consists of:
*   Ac. salicyl. [dr.] i-iv
*   Ol. ricini [dr.] ii-iv
*   Ol. ros. geran. [min.] x
*   Spt. vini ad [oz.] vi

Additionally, the head must be cleansed frequently. In cases that are very mild, a daily washing with soap spirit may occasionally effect a cure on its own.
