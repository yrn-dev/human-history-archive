# The Parsees

## Overview and Social Influence
The Parsees are a community located in western India. In the city of Bombay, they constitute one of the smallest population groups among the major religious sections, which also include Hindus, Mahommedans, Jains, and Christians. Despite their small numbers—totaling some 46,000—the Parsees are among the most influential residents of the city. This influence is evidenced by the fact that Parsee millionaires own most of the great business houses and have founded most of the large charities.

## Religious Traditions and Rituals
The Parsees maintain traditions that trace back to ancient times. They preserve the ritual of incense, a practice that was common in ancient Persia, as seen in the coins of the Sassanians and the monuments of Persepolis. In ancient Persia, Zoroastrian priests burnt incense on sacred fire altars five times a day. The Avesta (specifically the *Vendidad*, Fargard xix. 24, 40) refers to this incense as *vohu gaono*. While some have identified this substance as benzoin, it was likely frankincense, which was brought annually as tribute to Darius by the Arabs in the amount of 1,000 talents.

## Funerary Customs
In Bombay, the Parsees utilize specific structures known as the Towers of Silence, located toward the northern end of Malabar hill. According to their customs, the dead are exposed at these towers until vultures devour the flesh. Following this, the bones are cast into a well where they eventually crumble into dust.

## Demographic Distribution
While the Parsees have a significant presence in western India, they are also found in other regions, though in much smaller numbers. According to a census of 1901 in the province of Burma, the Parsee population was recorded at 245 individuals. This is a small fraction compared to other groups in that province, such as Buddhists (9,184,121), spirit-worshippers (399,390), Mussulmans (339,446), Hindus (285,484), and Christians (147,525).
