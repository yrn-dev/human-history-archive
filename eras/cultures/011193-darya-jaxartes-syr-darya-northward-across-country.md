# Central Asian River Basins and Territories

The region extending from the northern border of the Persian highlands to the Caspian and Aral Seas is characterized as a nearly desert, low-lying plain. This area stretches to the north-western extremity of the Tibeto-Himalayan mountains and extends eastward through the valleys of the Jaxartes (Syr-Darya) and the Oxus (Amu Darya), reaching northward across the country of the Kirghiz to the south-western border of Siberia.

## Geography and Environment
The landscape is largely inhabited by nomadic tribes, though the area includes Turkestan proper, Bokhara, and Khiva, where Uzbeg Turks are dominant. Rainfall in this tract is insufficient for agriculture, making irrigation a necessity. Consequently, the fixed population—which includes a non-Turkish element—is relatively small and concentrated in towns and districts near the rivers.

The Kizyl-kum is a significant desert of Western Asia located southeast of the Aral Lake, situated between the Syr-Darya to the northeast and the Amu-Darya to the southwest. Measuring approximately 370 by 220 miles, it features dunes and drift-sand that advance slowly toward the southwest. The desert slopes from an altitude of 2,000 feet in the southeast, where it is braided with fertile loess near the Tian-shan foothills, down to about 160 feet at the Aral Lake.

Geological evidence suggests the Caspian Sea was previously larger and its level was similar to that of the Black Sea, though it is now 86 feet lower. Aralo-Caspian deposits, typically yellowish-grey clay or sandstone, cover vast regions between the lower Volga, the Dzungarian Ala-tau, and the Aral-Irtysh water-divide.

## Major River Systems
The region is defined by several key watercourses:

*   **Oxus (Amu Darya):** The chief river of Bokhara, it serves as the border with Afghanistan to the south and flows along Bokhara's south-west border. It is navigable by steamboat from the mouth of the Surkhan up to Karki.
*   **Jaxartes (Syr-Darya):** A major river flowing through Russian Turkestan. The town of Khojent is located on its left bank.
*   **Khotan-Darya:** Rising in the Kuen-lun Mountains from the Kara-kash and Yurun-kash headstreams, this 300-mile river flows north across the Takla-makan desert. It carries water only in early summer and empties into the Tarim.
*   **Other Rivers:** The Zarafshan (660 miles long) is used extensively for irrigation but disappears into the sands 20 miles before reaching the Oxus. The Kashka-darya supplies the Shahri-sabs oasis before being lost in the desert west of Karshi.

## Key Urban Centers
**Khojent (Khojend):** Located in the province of Syr-darya on the Jaxartes, this town sits on the road between Bokhara and Khokand. As of 1900, its population was 31,881, with a majority of Tajik inhabitants. The town features an old citadel on a 100-foot artificial mound. Its economy involves the manufacture of cotton and silk goods, the rearing of silkworms, and the production of coarse ware imitating Chinese porcelain.

**Khokand (Kokan):** Situated in the province of Ferghana, 20 miles south of the Syr-Darya, this town had a population of 86,704 in 1900. It is the center of a fertile irrigated oasis and a major trade hub in Turkestan, exporting raw cotton and silk. Notable for its handsome bazaar and a former palace of the khans, the town also produced coins known as *khokands*.

**Kungrad:** A trading town in the Syr-darya province located in the Amu-darya delta, 50 miles south of Lake Aral. It serves as a center for caravan routes leading to the Uralsk province and the Caspian Sea.

## Political History and Russian Expansion
The khanate of Khokand, founded in 1732 by Abd-ur-Rahim, became a powerful state in the 18th century. In the 19th century, it was marked by conflict between the settled Sarts and nomad Kipchaks. Omar, a ruler who died in 1822, built fortresses on the Syr-Darya to protect Ferghana from Kirghiz raids, which eventually led to conflict with Russia.

Russian expansion into the region progressed through several stages:
*   **Khiva:** Russia established relations in the 17th century. Peter the Great sent a military force in 1717 to seek auriferous sand in the Oxus and open trade with India, though the expedition ended in disaster. Another attempt by General Perovsky in 1839 also failed. In 1847, Russia founded a fort at the mouth of the Jaxartes. Finally, in 1873, an army of 10,000 men under General Kaufmann occupied Khiva. Russia annexed 35,700 square miles of territory on the right bank of the Oxus.
*   **Khokand and Tashkent:** Russian operations culminated in the capture of Tashkent in 1865. In 1866, the Russians stormed Khojent, which had been a point of contention between Bokhara and Khokand.
