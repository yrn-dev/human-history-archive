# CONCERNING THE ADMINISTERING OF JUSTICE AMONG THE TARTARS

The administration of justice among the Tartars is characterized by severe penalties for theft and a system of corporal punishment that applies across various social strata, including the nobility and military.

## Corporal Punishment and the Cudgel
A primary method of administering justice among the Tartars is the use of the cudgel. This form of punishment is not reserved solely for thieves; it is also employed for state and military offenses. The application of the cudgel is widespread enough that even princes are subject to it without suffering fatal disgrace. According to the accounts of Carpini, the Tartars are beaten "like donkeys" if they commit any offense or fail to obey even the slightest beck.

The administration of these blows follows specific patterns. According to Wassáf, the number of blows delivered is always an odd number, ranging from 3 or 5 up to 77. In cases of petty theft, such as the stealing of a sheep, the perpetrator is beaten cruelly provided they have not been repeatedly caught in the act. In such instances, if one hundred blows are administered, one hundred separate sticks must be used.

## Laws Against Theft
The laws governing theft among the Tartars are described as being so severe that the society has no need for herdsmen or others to watch over their cattle. The penalties for theft vary based on the nature of the crime:

*   **Grand Larceny:** This offense is punished by death.
*   **Possession of Stolen Property:** If a man is found in possession of a stolen horse, he is required to restore the animal to its rightful owner and provide nine additional horses of the same value.
*   **Failure to Pay Restitution:** If a man cannot provide the required compensation for a stolen horse, his children are seized as compensation. If he has no children, the man is slaughtered like a mutton.

## Administrative Governance in Conquered Territories
The Tartars also applied their administrative will to the territories they conquered. In the kingdom of Kerman, which is located in Persia, the Tartars altered the existing political structure. While Kerman previously had a hereditary prince, the Tartar conquest ended this tradition. Instead, the Tartar authority sends whichever lord they please to administer the kingdom.
