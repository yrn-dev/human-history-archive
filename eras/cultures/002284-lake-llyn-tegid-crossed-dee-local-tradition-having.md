# Llyn Tegid

Llyn Tegid, also known as Bala Lake, is located in Merionethshire, North Wales. It is the largest lake in Wales, measuring 4 miles in length and approximately 3/4 of a mile in width. The lake is characterized as being deep and clear, though it is subject to sudden and dangerous floods.

## Natural History and Ecology
The waters of Llyn Tegid are home to various fish species, including trout, perch, pike, and eel. It is specifically noted for the *gwyniad* (*Caregonus*), a fish peculiar to certain waters such as those of Bala Lake. This species was fully described by Thomas Pennant in his 1776 work, *Zoology*.

## Geography and Local Tradition
The lake is crossed by the river Dee. According to local tradition, the waters of the Dee and the waters of the lake never mix, a phenomenon compared to the relationship between the sea and Alpheus.

## The Town of Bala
At the north end of the lake lies Bala, a market-town and urban district of Merionethshire. Situated 17 miles northeast of Dolgelley (Dolgellau), the town consists of little more than one wide street. As of 1901, its population was 1,554.

### Industry and Education
In the 18th century, Bala was well known for its manufactures of hosiery, including gloves, stockings, and flannel. The town's primary features include:
* An endowed grammar school founded in 1712.
* The theological college of the Calvinistic Methodists.
* A statue of the Rev. Thomas Charles, a distinguished theological writer who contributed significantly to the foundation of the British and Foreign Bible Society.

### Landmarks
The Tower of Bala is a notable landmark in the area. This "moat-hill" or tumulus stands approximately 30 feet high with a diameter of 50 feet. It was previously believed to mark the location of a Roman camp.
