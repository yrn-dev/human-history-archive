# The Western Ghats

The Western Ghats, also referred to as the Sahyadri, are a great range of hills in India that run generally parallel to the coast. This mountain system stretches southwards for more than 500 meters, maintaining a breadth of between 10 and 20 meters.

## Geography and Topography
The range is characterized by an average elevation of approximately 1,800 feet, though individual peaks can rise to more than double that height. One of the most prominent elevations is Mahabaleshwar, a fine plateau reaching 4,500 feet. Located 37 meters from Poona, Mahabaleshwar is covered in rich vegetation and serves as a sanitarium and summer retreat for the Bombay government.

The topography of the Western Ghats varies along the coast. In some areas, the hills rise as splendid precipices and bluffs directly from the water's edge; in others, they retreat inland, leaving a flat, fertile strip of land between 5 and 50 meters wide. The western declivity of the range is abrupt, with the land at the base being only slightly raised above sea level. Due to the trap formation of the hills, they typically descend toward the plains in terraces with abrupt fronts. Conversely, the landward slope is often very gentle, and the crest of the range is sometimes only slightly higher than the level of the Deccan plateau.

## Regional Influence and Districts
The Western Ghats influence several districts and states:

*   **Kolhapur:** This native state of India, located within the Deccan division of Bombay, stretches from the heart of the Western Ghats eastward into the Deccan plain. The state's western hills contain ancient Mahratta strongholds, including Bavda, Panhala, Rungna, and Vishalgarh. The slopes and valleys along the spurs of the main chain were, until recently, covered in rich forests and primarily produce timber.
*   **Kolaba (Colaba):** Situated between the sea and the Western Ghats in the southern division of Bombay, this district features isolated peaks, lofty detached ridges, and spurs that run at right angles to the main range. Notable peaks in the Western Ghats within this district include Miradongar and Raigarh, the latter of which served as the capital built by Sivaji.
*   **Bellary:** This district consists largely of an extensive plateau situated between the Western Ghats and the Eastern Ghats, with heights ranging from 800 to 1,000 feet above the sea.
*   **The Konkan:** This rugged and difficult country lies between the sea and the Western Ghats and is characterized by detached ranges of hills, isolated peaks, and creeks.

## Hydrology and Climate
The Western Ghats play a significant role in the region's hydrology and weather patterns. The range acts as a watershed for the great rivers of the Deccan, with spurs and masses of rock running eastward. Streams that rise in the Sahyadri range and flow westward into the Arabian Sea are generally of little importance; while they become formidable torrents during the rains, they dwindle away during fair weather.

In terms of climate, the Western Ghats are a primary factor in the summer rains of south Asia. Because the range consists of mountains of some extent that directly face vapour-bearing winds, the heaviest falls of rain occur along these lines.

## Geological Context
The rugged and mountainous country south of the Tapti marks the northern extremity of the Sahyadri or Western Ghats. This range is distinct from other regional highlands, such as the Aravalli range (of which the hills of Kathiawar and Cutch form the western extremity) and the Hala mountains in the north of the presidency on the right bank of the Indus. In the center of the country, the Satpura hills serve to separate the valley of the Nerbudda from the valley of the Tapti.
