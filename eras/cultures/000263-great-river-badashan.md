# OF THE GREAT RIVER OF BADASHAN

## Geography and Transit
The Great River of Badashan is navigated when leaving Badashan by riding for twelve days in a direction between east and north-east. This route involves ascending the river through lands belonging to a brother of the Prince of Badashan. The region surrounding the river is characterized by the presence of scattered habitations, as well as a good number of villages and towns.

## The Province of Vokhan
At the conclusion of the twelve-day journey along the river, one arrives at Vokhan. This province is small in size, extending no more than three days' journey in any direction. The inhabitants of Vokhan worship Mahommet and possess a peculiar language. They are described as gallant soldiers who serve as liegemen to the Prince of Badashan. The chief of the people of Vokhan is referred to by the title None, which translates to "Count."

## The Province of Badashan
Badashan is a very great kingdom with a hereditary royalty. The people of this province worship Mahommet and speak a peculiar language. According to the archive's records, those of royal blood in Badashan are descended from the daughter of King Darius, Lord of the Persian Empire, and King Alexander. Out of regard for Alexander the Great, these kings use the Saracen term Zulcarniain, meaning "Alexander."

The province is reached after a three-day journey from the town of Casem, a period during which no habitations, food, or drink can be found, requiring travelers to carry all necessary supplies. Within the broader region, the town of Casem serves as the head of a province of the same name, where peasants who keep cattle live in spacious houses carved into the earth of the mountains.

## Regional Context and Neighbors
Badashan is situated in a complex network of neighboring provinces:
* **Pashai:** Located ten days' journey to the south of Badashan. The people of Pashai are brown-complexioned "Idolaters" who live on rice and flesh. They are described as crafty, pestilent, and adepts in diabolic arts and sorceries. The men of this hot country wear brooches and earrings made of gold and silver set with pearls and stones.
* **Keshimur:** Located seven days' journey south-east of Pashai.
* **Other Territories:** The region has been traversed by Nogodar, a king who led 10,000 horsemen through Badashan, Pashai-dir, and Ariora-keshemur before entering India at the province of Dalivar.

## Cultural and Historical Notes
The archive notes several specific characteristics associated with the region of Badashan. The ladies of the province were historically known for the amplitude of their garments, specifically silken trousers that required a liberal quantity of fabric. 

In terms of urban history, Faizabad is identified as the new capital of Badashan, constructed near the site of the old capital. Additionally, tradition mentions that Lady Zobeidah, the wife of Ar-Rashid and daughter of Al-Mansúr, built a palace at Jaúzgún, the ruins of which remain visible.
