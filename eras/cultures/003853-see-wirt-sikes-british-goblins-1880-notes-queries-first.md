# Folklore of the British Isles: Spectre-Hounds and Death-Portents

The folklore of the British Isles contains various traditions regarding supernatural beings and spectral entities, many of which are associated with the foretelling of death. These beliefs are distributed across England, Ireland, Wales, and Scotland, often manifesting as auditory warnings or nocturnal sightings.

## The Barghest and Spectre-Hounds
The barghest is described as a nocturnal spectre whose appearance is regarded as a portent of death. In the north country of England, belief in the spectre-hound persists in wild areas; for example, in Nidderdale, Yorkshire, the name is used by nurses to frighten children. 

The nature and origin of the barghest vary by region:
* **Norfolk:** On the Norfolk coast, the creature is believed to be amphibious, emerging from the sea at night to travel through lonely lanes.
* **Wales:** A Welsh variant is known as the *Cwn Annwn*, or "dogs of hell," which is confined to parishes along the sea-coast.

The etymology of the word "barghest" is a subject of dispute. Some suggest it derives from *burh-ghest* ("town-ghost"), noting that "ghost" is pronounced "guest" in northern England. Other explanations propose a German origin, suggesting *Berg-geist* ("mountain demon") or *Bar-geist* ("bear-demon"), the latter referring to the creature's alleged ability to appear as a bear. In Norman folklore, the barghest has a kinsman known as the *Rongeur d'Os*.

## The Banshee and Related Spirits
The banshee (from the Irish *bean sidhe* or Gaelic *ban sith*, meaning "woman of the fairies") is a supernatural being found in general Celtic and Irish folklore. The banshee is known for "keening," or mournful screaming at night, which is believed to foretell the death of a member of the household being visited. 

In Ireland, these legends are specifically linked to certain families whose records chronicle periodic visits from the spirit. It is held that the Irish banshee is a distinction reserved only for families of pure Milesian descent. Similar ghostly informers are found in the folklore of Brittany.

Other regions have corresponding figures:
* **Wales:** The banshee is known as the *gwrach y Rhibyn* (witch of Rhibyn). Additionally, the Welsh recognize the *cyhyraeth*, a groaning spirit that serves as a death-portent and is often confused with the banshee and the *gwrach y Rhibyn*.
* **Scotland:** Sir Walter Scott noted a belief in the banshee within the highlands of Scotland.

## Other Supernatural Elements
Beyond specific death-portents, other supernatural associations exist within the British landscape. In the case of the old British tower and castle known as *Castell caled fryn yn Rhôs* ("castle of the hard hill in Rhôs"), the site is noted for having a goblin well.
