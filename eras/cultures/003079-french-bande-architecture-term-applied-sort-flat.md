# French Bande

In the context of architecture, the term "bande" (derived from the French word for a flat strip of material used for fastening) refers to a specific type of architectural feature characterized as a flat frieze or fascia.

## Architectural Application and Form
A bande is a horizontal element that runs around other parts of a building, such as a tower. It is particularly associated with perpendicular work and is commonly utilized in conjunction with the long shafts that are characteristic of the 13th century. In these applications, the bande often serves as base tables.

The structure of a bande generally includes a bold moulding that projects both above and below the flat section.

## Decorative Elements
The surface of the bande is frequently carved. While it is sometimes decorated with foliages, it is more generally adorned with quatrefoils or cusped circles. These decorative motifs frequently contain shields of arms.

## Etymology
The architectural term is derived from the English word "band," which refers to a cord, rope, tie, or strap used to bind or fasten one thing to another (such as the straps used in book-binding to fasten sheets to the back). The word "band" is a variant of "bond" and originates from the Teutonic stem *bindan*, meaning to bind. From this same source comes the word "bend," which refers to fastening a string to a bow to constrain and curve it. The specific sense of "band" as a flat strip of material for fastening entered the English language directly from the French *bande*.
