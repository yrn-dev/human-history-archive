# The Archaic Makonde or Mabiha of the Lower Ruvuma and the Coast

The Makonde, also referred to as the Mabiha, are a group located in the lower Ruvuma region and along the coast between Ibo and Lindi. In the study of African linguistics, they are categorized as a separate group (designated as No. 10 in specific classifications) and are characterized by their use of a primitive Bantu tongue.

## Linguistic Characteristics
The language of the Makonde, known as Kimakonde, is identified as a primitive Bantu tongue. Within the broader context of Bantu languages, it is noted as one of the dialects that maintains an archaic form and typical structure. These archaic features are found in several widely spread areas of Africa, including the north coast of Tanganyika, the Bemba country, and the vicinity of Lake Bangweulu, among others.

## Geographical Distribution and Boundaries
The Makonde occupy a specific territory on the east coast of Africa. Their domain is bounded to the north by the range of the Yao language in the Ruvuma valley. To the east of the Yao domain and the separate group of Ki-makonde lies the well-marked Makua genus. The Makua languages occupy much of Portuguese East Africa, excluding the watershed of Lake Nyasa.

## Relations to Other Language Groups
Due to their location and linguistic traits, the archaic Makonde or Mabiha might conceivably be attached to the Swahili branch. Swahili itself is a somewhat archaic Bantu dialect indigenous to the East African coast south of the Ruvu (Pangani) river. While Swahili has become a commercial *lingua franca* through intermixture with Arabic, it is distinct from the original local dialects of Pemba and Zanzibar. 

The Makonde are situated in a region where various Bantu groups intersect. To their west and south-west, the extensive Yao genus of languages stretches from the Lindi settlements in German East Africa, across the Ruvuma river to the north-east shores of Lake Nyasa, and further to the region east of the Shire river.
