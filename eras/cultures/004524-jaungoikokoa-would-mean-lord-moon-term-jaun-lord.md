# The Concept of Lordship and the Moon in Historical and Sacred Texts

## Basque Linguistic and Religious Traditions
In the study of ancient Basque culture, limited information exists regarding religious practices prior to Christianity. Available notices suggest a belief in the immortality of the body if it remained unburied and unburnt, as well as the worship of the morning star, the sun, the moon, and the elements. While the Romans assimilated local Basque deities into their own pantheon—as seen in Latin inscriptions referencing *Herauscorrtsehe* and *Deo Baicorrixo*—the Basques used their own terms for the Deity.

The names *Jaincoa* or *Jaungoikoa* are used to refer to the Deity, which may translate to "master," "lord," or "Lord of the high." However, in the Roncal dialect, the word *Goikoa* means "the moon," which would render *Jaungoikokoa* as "Lord of the moon." The term *Jaun* (lord or master) is also used in a secular context; for example, *Etcheko Jauna* refers to the master or lord of the house, a term applied to any householder.

## The Moon and Celestial Bodies in the Qur'an
The Qur'an presents the sun and the moon as signs of Allah and as entities subject to His laws and command. Allah is described as the one who created the sun, the moon, and the stars, making them subservient and constant in their courses for an appointed term. These celestial bodies are presented as portents for those who consider them.

The text explicitly commands that prostration should not be directed toward the sun or the moon, but rather to Allah, who created them. This is illustrated through the story of a figure who, upon seeing a star, the moon rising in splendour, and finally the sun rising, questioned if these were his Lord. Upon seeing each set, he concluded that he did not love "the setting ones" and declared himself free from associating partners with Allah.

## Eschatology and the Day of Resurrection
The Qur'an describes specific celestial events that will occur on the Day of Resurrection. During this time, the sight of man will be dazed or confounded, and the moon will be eclipsed, become dark, or be "buried in darkness." Furthermore, the sun and the moon will be joined or brought together.

On that day, man will seek a refuge or a place to flee, but it is stated that there is no place of safety except before the Lord alone, where there will be a place of rest. Man will be informed of everything he put forward and left behind, serving as a witness against himself.

## Comparative Terms of Lordship
Other historical and linguistic analyses provide varying interpretations of titles involving "Lord." For instance, the name Beelzebul has several proposed meanings:
*   **Lord of Dung:** Derived from *zebel* (dung) in the Targums, used as a term of contempt or to refer to a "Chief of false gods."
*   **Lord of Heaven:** Derived from *zebul* meaning "height," "house," or "temple," potentially meaning "Lord of the High House."

## Divine Authority and Creation
The archive's sources describe Allah as the Guardian-Lord and the Lord of the worlds, who created the heavens and the earth in six days (or periods of time) and is firmly established on the throne of authority. He is credited with spreading out the earth, placing firm mountains and flowing rivers upon it, and creating fruits in pairs. He is also described as the one who draws the night as a veil over the day.

The texts emphasize that Allah regulates all affairs and provides for all that is asked of Him, though it is noted that man is often unjust, ungrateful, and prone to injustice. Believers are encouraged to call upon their Lord humbly and in private, as He does not love those who exceed limits or trespass beyond bounds.
