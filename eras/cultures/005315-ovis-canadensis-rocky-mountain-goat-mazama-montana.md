# Ovis canadensis and Mazama montana

The *Ovis canadensis*, known as the big-horn or mountain sheep, and the *Mazama montana*, known as the Rocky Mountain goat, are among the larger mammals found in the mainland regions of British Columbia.

## Distribution and Habitat
Both the big-horn sheep (*Ovis canadensis*) and the Rocky Mountain goat (*Mazama montana*) are found exclusively on the mainland. They inhabit a region characterized by the Cordillera or Cordilleran belt of North America, specifically within the province of British Columbia. This area is essentially mountainous, consisting of parallel mountain ranges and long narrow valleys. The primary systems in this belt include the Coast Range on the south-west or Pacific side and the Rocky Mountains proper on the north-east side, with an "Interior Plateau" of hilly country situated between them.

The Rocky Mountains in this region are composed primarily of palaeozoic sediments from the Carboniferous to the Cambrian periods, with some subordinate infolded areas of Cretaceous that contain coal. The range reaches an average height of 8,000 ft. along the United States boundary, with the highest peak in the Canadian Rockies being Mount Robson at 13,700 ft.

In the Rocky Mountains, the dusky grouse replaces the large blue grouse of the coast. Additionally, ptarmigans are abundant in many of the higher mountain regions.

While *Ovis canadensis* is native to the North American mainland, other species of wild sheep exist in different global regions. For example, *Ovis poli* (also called Argali by the Mongols) is a large wild sheep found in the steppes of Pamir, the Plateau of Aksai in the western Thian Shan, and the Gobi. *Ovis poli* is noted for its immense horns; one specimen recorded by Colonel Gordon had a horn measuring 65½ inches on the curves. These sheep are reported to travel in great herds and are preyed upon by wolves and leopards.
