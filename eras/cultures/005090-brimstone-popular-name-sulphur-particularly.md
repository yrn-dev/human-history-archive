# BRIMSTONE

## Definition and Etymology
Brimstone is the popular name for sulphur, specifically referring to the commercial variety known as "roll sulphur." The term is derived literally from the meaning "burning stone." This etymology is rooted in the Middle English stem *brennen*, meaning to burn. Historical variations of the word include *brynstone*, *bernstone*, and *brenstone*.

## Religious and Symbolic Associations
In certain traditions and interpretations of Scripture, brimstone is associated with divine judgment and catastrophe. Specifically, it is linked to the prophecy that the empire and city of Rome—characterized as a "mystic Babylon" due to the profession of idolatry by emperors prior to Constantine—would eventually be destroyed. According to these beliefs, the city of the seven hills, including its temples, palaces, and triumphal arches, was destined to be buried in a vast lake of fire and brimstone.

This vision of a general conflagration coincided with the philosophy of the Stoics, traditions of the East, and the analogy of Nature. The region associated with this event was considered physically adapted for such a catastrophe due to its numerous volcanoes (such as Vesuvius, Ætna, and Lipari), deep caverns, and beds of sulphur.

## Industrial and Agricultural Applications
Sulphur is utilized in various commercial and agricultural capacities:

*   **Agriculture:** Sulphur is used as a trustworthy remedy for mildew or mould caused by the fungus *Podosphaera castagnei*, which affects hop-planters and their cones. It is typically applied as "flowers of sulphur" at a rate of 40 to 60 lb. per acre. The powder is distributed via a horse-drawn machine that uses a fan and blast-pipe to create a dense cloud over the hop-bines. Additionally, alkaline sulphides, such as "liver of sulphur" (a variety of potassium sulphide), are employed.
*   **Mining and Smelting:** In the liquidation of sulphur-bearing rocks, ore is piled into heaps and ignited at the bottom; one-fourth to one-third of the sulphur is sacrificed to raise the temperature enough to melt the remainder. In "pyritic smelting," copper ores containing iron pyrites ($\text{FeS}_2$) are smelted in a hot blast; the oxidation of the iron and sulphur provides sufficient heat to liquefy the metal and slag.
*   **Petroleum Refining:** Sulphur is an impurity found in crude petroleum, notably in oil from Texas wells. Because it retards combustion and storage, it is removed through distillation.

## Chemical Properties and Compounds
Sulphur exists in various forms and combines with other elements to create diverse substances:

*   **Calcium Compounds:** Calcium monosulphide ($\text{CaS}$) is a white amorphous powder that exhibits phosphorescence when exposed to the sun's rays or heated, earning it the name "Canton's phosphorus." Other forms include the sulphydrate $\text{Ca(SH)}_2$, the disulphide $\text{CaS}_2$, and the pentasulphide $\text{CaS}_5$. Calcium sulphite ($\text{CaSO}_3$) is used in the chemical manufacture of wood-pulp for paper making.
*   **Lead Compounds:** Lead sulphate is a very insoluble substance used in the gravimetric method for determining the amount of lead in ores. Lead sulphate consists of 68.30% metallic lead.
*   **Other Forms:** Sulphur can be present as organic sulphur, as sulphates of metals (such as aluminium or calcium), or as sulphides like iron pyrites.

## Biological Role
Sulphur is an element that circulates in nature through the action of various bacteria. In environments such as estuaries, marshes, and sulphur springs, bacteria of the genera *Monas*, *Spirillum*, *Chromatium*, and *Thiothrix* play important roles. 

Some bacteria thrive by oxidizing hydrogen sulphide ($\text{SH}_2$) and storing the sulphur within their own protoplasm, using $\text{SH}_2$ as a respiratory substance similar to how higher plants use carbohydrates. If $\text{SH}_2$ becomes scarce, these bacteria may oxidize the stored sulphur into sulphuric acid. Conversely, anaerobic forms such as *Spirillum desulphuricans* attack and reduce sulphates, reversing the process. In higher plants, sulphur is taken in as sulphates and built into proteids.
