# Languages and Demographics of Bolivia and the Andes

## Linguistic Landscape of Bolivia
In Bolivia, Spanish serves as the language of the dominant minority. However, the majority of the population consists of natives who speak Quichua, Aymara, and Guarani. A significant portion of the Indian population does not understand Spanish and has resisted efforts to force the language upon them. This linguistic divide extends to the *cholos* (mestizos), who are more familiar with native idioms than with Spanish, a pattern also observed in certain regions of Paraguay and Argentina.

## The Aymara People
The Aymara, formerly known as the Colla, are a tribe of South American Indians who originally inhabited the valleys of the Andes and the region surrounding Lake Titicaca. Today, they constitute the primary ethnical element in Bolivia, numbering approximately half a million people, including half-breeds; a small number also reside in southern Peru. 

Physically, the pure Aymara are characterized by a broad face, rounded forehead, black eyes, and a reddish complexion. They are typically short and thick-set with significant chest development. Their disposition is described as apathetic and sullen, a trait they share with the Quichuas.

## Historical Relations and Conquest
The Aymaras possessed a considerable culture prior to their conquest by the Incas during the 13th and 14th centuries, as evidenced by the megalithic ruins of Tiahuanaco. The region around Lake Titicaca was regarded as a "holy land" by the Incas, whose national legends attributed the origins of all Quichua (Inca) civilization to that area.

By the time the Spaniards arrived, the Aymaras had been under Inca domination for a long period and were in a decadent state. Despite this, they maintained certain privileges, including the continued use of their own language. The treatment of the Aymaras by their conquerors suggested that the conquerors believed themselves to be of Aymara blood.

## Education and Literacy in Bolivia
Bolivia maintains a compulsory and free school system, though progress in education has been limited and only a small percentage of the population is literate. As of official estimates from 1901, the republic had 733 primary schools with 938 teachers and 41,587 pupils. The school enrolment rate was one in 43.7 of the population, which was significantly lower than the rate of one in 10 found in Argentina.

The maintenance of these schools is shared between municipalities, the national government, private means, and the Church. In 1901, the total appropriation for education was 756,943 bolivianos. Higher education is provided by universities in Oruro, Santa Cruz, Potosi, Tarija, Cochabamba, La Paz, and Sucre. All of these institutions offer instruction in law, while the first four also teach theology, and the first three (Sucre, La Paz, and Cochabamba) teach medicine. Only the universities at La Paz and Sucre—the latter of which dates from colonial times—are considered sufficiently well-equipped to merit the title of university.

Other specialized institutions include:
* A military academy in La Paz.
* An agricultural school at Umala (La Paz department).
* A mining and civil engineering school in Oruro.
* Commercial schools in Trinidad and Sucre.

While a cabinet minister directs public instruction in general and universities control secondary instruction, the Church practically supervises all educational matters.

## Regional Contexts
In other parts of the Americas, the impact of Spanish language and culture varied. In Mexico, although the population remains largely of native blood, Spanish replaced native languages, and the existing culture is Spanish and Catholic. In Peru, the Quichua language persists in historical records and place names; for example, the city of Ayacucho derives its name from the Quichua word for "corner of death." 

The Quichua tongue also preserved literary works, such as the heroic historic play *Apu Ollantay*. This work is said to have been written down from native dictation by Spanish priests shortly after the conquest of Peru.
