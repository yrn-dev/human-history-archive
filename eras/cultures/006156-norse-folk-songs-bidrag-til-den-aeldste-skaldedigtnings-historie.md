# Norse Folk-Songs and Skaldic Poetry

The study of Norse folk-songs and early skaldic poetry involves the collection of traditional oral works and the critical analysis of early Scandinavian literature. Much of the academic foundation for these studies was established by philologists who examined the relationship between indigenous Scandinavian traditions and external cultural influences.

## Sophus Bugge and Philological Contributions
Sophus Bugge (1833–1907) was a prominent Norwegian philologist born in Laurvik, Norway. Educated in Berlin, Copenhagen, and Christiania, Bugge served as a professor of Old Norse and comparative philology at Christiania University beginning in 1866. Bugge was a prolific author of books on folklore and philology, and he dedicated significant effort to the collection of Norwegian traditions and folk-songs.

One of Bugge's primary contributions to the field was the publication of *Gamle Norske Folkeviser* in 1858, which serves as a collection of Old Norse folk-songs. He also produced a critical edition of the elder Edda, titled *Norroen Fornkvoedi*, published in Christiania in 1867. In his analysis of the *Edda* and earlier sagas, Bugge maintained that these works were largely based on Latin and Christian traditions that had been imported into Scandinavian literature via England.

## Bidrag til den aeldste skaldedigtnings historie
In 1894, Sophus Bugge published *Bidrag til den aeldste skaldedigtnings historie* in Christiania. This work contributes to the historical understanding of the oldest skaldic poetry. Bugge's broader body of work on early poetry and sagas also includes *Helge-digtene i den Aeldre Edda* (published in Copenhagen in 1896 and translated into English as *The Home of the Eddic Poems* in 1899), as well as *Norsk Sagafortaelling op Sagaskrivning i Island* (1901).

## Examples of Early Norse Poetic Content
Historical records provide specific examples of the themes and styles found in early Norse songs. The *Heimskringla* chronicle, which details the kings of Norway, records a song by Bjorn Krephende regarding King Magnus. The song describes a winter and spring foray into Halland around A.D. 1095, during which King Magnus plundered the region and laid waste to districts such as Viskardal. 

The lyrics of Krephende's song emphasize the devastation of the campaign, utilizing repetitive imagery of fire and mourning:
"Through Halland wide around / The clang and shriek resound; / The houses burn, / The people mourn, / Through Halland wide around. / The Norse king strides in flame, / Through Viskardal he came; / The fire sweeps, / The widow weeps, / The Norse king strides in flame."
