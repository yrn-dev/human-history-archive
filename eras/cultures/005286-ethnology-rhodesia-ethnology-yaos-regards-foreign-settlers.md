# Ethnology of North Eastern Rhodesia and Surrounding Regions

## Indigenous Population and Physical Types
The indigenous population of the region is represented by a single native type: the Negro. The general population typically exhibits a black or chocolate-colored appearance. Within this group, the Bantu races are the primary inhabitants, though some areas show signs of intermixture with Bushman or Hottentot types. In certain mountain districts, particularly Mount Mlanje, legends persist regarding the former existence of unmixed Bushman tribes, and stone implements attributed to Bushmen have been discovered at the south end of Lake Tanganyika.

At the north end of Lake Nyasa, the physique of the native Awankonde is noted for recalling that of the Nilotic negro, though otherwise, no trace of Hamitic intermixture is found in the region.

## Linguistic Characteristics
Linguistically, the native population belongs entirely and exclusively to the Bantu family. The region contains several interesting forms of Bantu speech, specifically in the districts located between the river Luapula, the south end of Lake Tanganyika, and the north end of Lake Nyasa. 

In the plateau country within these limits, the Bantu dialects are of an archaic type. Among these, Kibemba (or Kiwemba) and the Subiya of southern Barotseland are identified as being close to the original form of the Bantu mother-language. These languages are connected to the Bantu languages of Uganda via dialects spoken to the north and west of Tanganyika. Additionally, there is a slight resemblance to Zulu-Kaffir. Evidence suggests the Zulu-Kaffir race migrated from countries north-east of Tanganyika, crossing the Zambezi to reach their home; however, several hundred years later, conflicts and intestine wars led to a return migration of Zulus in a north-eastward direction from Matabeleland.

## Foreign Settlers and External Influence
Foreign settlement in the region has occurred in several waves:

*   **Arabs:** Arabs from Zanzibar settled in the country, though records indicate this did not occur earlier than the beginning of the 19th century. Some Arabs established themselves specifically on the north end of Lake Nyasa, leading to conflicts with British interests.
*   **Europeans:** Early European presence was limited to vague Portuguese wanderings during the 16th and 17th centuries. Later, the Portuguese official Dr. F.J.M. de Lacerda e Almeida traveled from Tete on the Zambezi toward Lake Mweru. 
*   **British Influence:** The modern history of the region is closely tied to David Livingstone, who discovered Lake Nyasa in 1859 and explored Lake Mweru and Lake Bangweulu. Following his death in 1873, missionary societies entered the region to begin evangelization. These missionaries, along with a small number of Scottish settlers, opposed Portuguese attempts to extend their influence from the Zambezi and the provinces of Moçambique.

## Political and Commercial Conflict
The entry of British interests led to the formation of the African Lakes Trading Corporation, a trading company that grew out of the missionary societies. This organization entered into a struggle for possession of the country against the established Arabs on the north end of Lake Nyasa. This conflict lasted from approximately 1885 until 1896. 

The African Lakes Corporation utilized volunteers in its unofficial war, including Mr. Alfred Sharpe and Captain F.D. Lugard, both of whom were wounded during operations. In 1889, Mr. H.H. Johnston was dispatched to arrange a dispute between the Corporation and the Arabs and to ensure that friendly native chiefs were protected from Portuguese aggression. These efforts and subsequent treaties resulted in the establishment of a British sphere of influence and protectorate north of the Zambezi.

## Regional Ethnological Context
From an ethnological standpoint, Barotseland is categorized as belonging to Central Africa rather than South Africa. This distinction is mirrored by the Zambezi river, which serves as a definite boundary line for the distribution of flora and fauna, as well as for ethnological distributions.
