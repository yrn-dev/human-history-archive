# Flora of Asia Minor and Northern Persia

## General Characteristics and European Affinities
The flora of Asia Minor and northern Persia is characterized by a strong similarity to the vegetation of the southern parts of Europe. This region is part of a broader warm temperate zone extending from the Mediterranean to the Himalaya, which is essentially European in character. This belt, which also encompasses Afghanistan and the southern slopes of the Himalaya, is virtually continuous with southern Europe. Due to its elevation, the zone maintains a temperate climate where rainfall is sufficient to support vigorous vegetation, despite hot summers and severe winters.

## Forest Composition and Tree Species
In areas where rainfall is abundant, the mountains of Asia Minor and northern Persia are clothed in forests. These forests consist of various Coniferae and the following genera:
*   *Quercus*
*   *Fagus*
*   *Ulmus*
*   *Acer*
*   *Carpinus*
*   *Corylus*

Among these, *Fagus* (the beech) is the only genus listed that is not found on the Himalaya. The beech is widely distributed across the northern provinces of Persia and Asia Minor. Additionally, fruit trees belonging to the plum tribe are abundant in these regions. The cultivated plants found here are those of southern Europe.

## Regional Comparisons and Distribution
The botanical characteristics of this region contrast with other parts of Asia. While the flora of northern Asia generally mirrors that of northern Europe, the flora of Asia Minor and northern Persia specifically aligns with southern Europe. 

In nearby regions, such as Afghanistan, the flora of the northern areas approximates that of the contiguous western Himalaya. In the higher elevations of both Persia and Afghanistan, thistles and Boraginaceae are abundant, along with gigantic Umbelliferae—including *Prangos*, *Peucedanum*, *Bubon*, *Dorema*, *Galbanum*, and *Ferula*—some of which extend into Tibet.

Conversely, the rainless regions of south-western Asia, which include south Persia, the shores of the Persian Gulf, and the peninsula of Arabia, possess a flora that is continuous with the desert flora of northern and eastern Africa. This desert flora extends from the coast of Senegal to the meridian of 75 deg. E.

## Climatic Influence on Vegetation
The distribution of life in these regions is closely dependent on climatic conditions. In Asia Minor, Persia, Syria, and Afghanistan, the chief seasons of condensation occur during the winter and spring. This differs from other parts of Asia where the majority of rain falls between May and September.

## Related Botanical Zones
The vegetation of Asia Minor also shares connections with the Balkans. The richer vegetation in the sheltered districts south of the Balkans recalls the flora of the adjacent parts of Asia Minor and the neighborhood of Constantinople. Furthermore, the Rila and Rhodope mountain groups combine indigenous types with those found in the mountains of Asia Minor and the central European Alps.
