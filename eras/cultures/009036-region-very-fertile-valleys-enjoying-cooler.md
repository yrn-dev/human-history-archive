# Geography and Fertility of the Bokhara State

## Regional Overview and Boundaries
Bokhara (also pronounced Bukhara) is a state located in central Asia under Russian protection. Geographically, it is situated on the right bank of the middle Oxus, positioned between 37 and 41 degrees North latitude and 62 and 72 degrees East longitude. The state is bounded by the Russian governments of Ferghana, Samarkand, and Syr-darya to the north, Afghanistan to the south, and the Transcaspian territory and Khiva to the west. To the east lie the Pamirs, though the south-eastern frontier in this area remains undetermined except where it meets Russian dominions. Including the khanates of Darvaz and Karateghin, the total area of the state is approximately 85,000 square miles.

## Topography and Land Use
The landscape of Bokhara is varied, consisting of plains, deserts, and high plateaus. The western portion of the state is a plain watered by the Zarafshan and a vast network of irrigation canals. In the north, the state is affected by an intrusion of the Kara-kum sand desert, while the east contains the Karnap-chul steppe, which is covered with grass during the early summer. Because of the arid nature of the region, land suitable for cultivation is restricted to oases; however, these oases are very fertile when watered by irrigation canals.

## The Central Highlands
The middle portion of the state is characterized by high plateaus reaching altitudes of about 4,000 feet. These plateaus slope from the Tian-shan and are intersected by numerous rivers that flow toward the Oxus. This specific region is densely populated and is noted for being very fertile in the valleys, benefiting from a climate that is damper and cooler than that of the lower plains. Consequently, cattle-breeding and agriculture are carried out extensively in this area. Notable towns located within this region include Guzar (or Huzar), Chirakchi, Shaar, Kitab, and Karshi.

## The Hissar Range and Southern Valleys
The Hissar range serves as a westward continuation of the Alai Mountains, separating the tributaries of the Oxus—specifically the Vakhsh, Kafirnihan, and Surkhan—from the Zarafshan. The range extends approximately 200 miles in length. Its passes are extremely difficult to traverse, reaching altitudes between 12,000 and 14,000 feet, which are 1,000 to 3,000 feet below the surrounding peaks. The range is pierced by rivers flowing in wild gorges between its spurs.

The climate along the ridge is characterized by scarce rains and extreme dryness, resulting in a lack of Alpine meadows and forests. However, the southern foot-hills, which are covered with loess, form the fertile valleys of the Vakhsh and Hissar. During periods when the melting of mountain snows coincides with heavy rainfall, the watercourses in this area transform into furious torrents.
