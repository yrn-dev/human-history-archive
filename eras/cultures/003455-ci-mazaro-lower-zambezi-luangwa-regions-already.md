# Ci-mazaro of the Lower Zambezi

## Linguistic Classification
Ci-mazaro is a dialect of the Nyanja language, which is identified as perhaps the most extensive group of cognate languages within the Bantu field. The Nyanja genus is associated with a wide geographic range, including the east and west shores of the southern half of Lake Nyasa, the valley of the Shire (excluding portions of the Shire highlands), and the lands on both banks of the Zambezi river extending to its delta.

Within this linguistic family, Ci-mazaro is specifically located in the Lower Zambezi region. It exists alongside other principal Nyanja dialects, such as:
* **Cinyanja**: Spoken in Eastern Nyasaland.
* **Ci-peta and Ci-maravi**: Found in South-West Nyasaland, extending to the watershed of the Luangwa river.
* **Ci-mananja**: Spoken in the Shire highlands.
* **Ci-mobo and Ci-machinjiri**: Located in the Shire valley.
* **Ci-sena (or Ci-nyungwe)**: Spoken in Sena and Tete along the Zambezi.
* **Ci-podzo**: A very distinct language found in the Zambezi delta that still belongs to the Nyanja genus.

## Regional Geography and Allied Languages
The region of the Lower Zambezi and the broader Nyanja domain interact with several other linguistic groups. To the west of Lake Nyasa, the Nyanja domain extends to the Central Zambezi and the Luangwa river via the Senga language. The Luangwa regions are occupied by Senga, which is described as a distinct but closely-allied language to Nyanja.

South of the Central Zambezi, the linguistic landscape includes various dialects such as Ci-nanzwa (near Victoria Falls), Ci-nyai, Shi-kalana, Ci-shuna (also known as Ci-gomo), Ci-loze, and potentially Shi-lenge and Ci-shangwe (or Ci-hlangane). These dialects serve as links to the Beira coast. In the coast-lands of Inhambane and Beira, as well as in Manika, dialects such as Chopi, Shi-lenge, and Ci-shangane have experienced significant influence from Ronga and Tebele (Zulu dialects).

## Physical and Geological Context of the Basin
The Lower Zambezi is part of a vast drainage system. The Zambezi basin includes subordinate basins such as those of the Luengwe-Kafukwe and Luangwa rivers, as well as Lake Nyasa. The river Luangwa is an independent affluent of the Zambezi.

The geology of the broader region is primarily Archean and Primary, consisting of gneiss, schists, amphibolite, quartz, felspar, and granite. In the basin of the Luangwa in North Eastern Rhodesia, sandstones of Karroo age are present. The region is also characterized by various mineral deposits; iron ore and mica are found almost universally, while gold, silver, and lead are present in the hills of the Nyasa-Zambezi waterparting.

## Transport and Communication
Communication from the lower reaches of the Zambezi to the interior is facilitated by light-draught steamers traveling from the Chinde mouth of the Zambezi to Port Herald on the lower Shire. During the dry season, which occurs from April to November, steamers may be unable to reach Port Herald, necessitating the use of barges to complete the journey.
