# Luganda

Luganda is a Bantu language spoken in the region of Uganda and the Victoria Nyanza. It is part of a broader group of tongues characterized by the governing of unchangeable roots by prefixes.

## Linguistic Classification and Affinities
Luganda is situated within the Bantu language family. It is more closely related to the Kavirondo-Masaba section—which includes languages such as Lukonde, Luwanga, Lu-nyara, and Igizii—than to any other group of Bantu tongues. The Kavirondo-Masaba group is considered a distinct division, noted for having the most archaic prefixes.

In contrast, Luganda and the Uganda-Unyoro groups show no special affinity with Lihuku (also known as Libvanuma), a very isolated type of Bantu found south and south-west of Albert Nyanza in the Mboga country.

## Grammatical Features
A leading feature of Luganda and related Bantu languages is the use of prefixes to modify roots. For example, the syllable *-ntu* (originally meaning "person" or "object") changes meaning based on the prefix attached: *muntu* means "a man," while *bantu* means "men."

### Concord and Pronominal Particles
Luganda utilizes a system of concord involving the repetition of pronominal particles. To illustrate this, a sentence in reconstructed Old Bantu—*Babo mbaba-ntu ba bi ba-bo-ta tu-ba-oga* (They these-they person they bad they who kill we fear them)—is rendered in the modern dialect of Luganda as: *Bo aba-ntu ba-bi ba-bota tu-ba-tia* (They are bad people who kill; we fear them).

### Prefix System
While sixteen prefixes are common to nearly all members of the Bantu family, Luganda and the Masaba dialects (and potentially some branches of Unyoro) utilize specific augmentative prefixes:
*   **Ogu-**: Used in an augmentative sense as a singular prefix.
*   **Aga-**: The corresponding plural prefix for augmentative use.

These prefixes are considered regular because they are supplied with the concord *-gu-* and *-ga-*. Additionally, the prefix *Wa-* is utilized in Luganda, whereas in Lusoga (on the north coast of Victoria Nyanza), this becomes *Gha*.

## Vocabulary
The vocabulary of Luganda shares similarities with other languages in its geographical area. For instance, the word for "fowl" in Luganda, as well as in other languages of the Victoria Nyanza and Uganda, and in Runyoro on the Victoria Nile, is *enkoko*. This is related to *kuku* in Ki-Swahili, *inkuku* in Zulu, and various forms such as *nkogo* or *nsusu* on the Congo.
