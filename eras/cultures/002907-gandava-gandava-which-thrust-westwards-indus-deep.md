# Kach Gandava

Kach Gandava is a region of flat alluvial desert located on the frontier of Baluchistan. It extends 130 miles from the Indus River to Sibi, which sits at the foot of the hills. The area forms a sort of inlet or bay that strikes into the Baluchistan mountain system, created by the curved recession of the Suliman Ranges to the north-west.

## Geography and Climate
The region serves as a drainage point for two local basins, the Nari and the Bolan, both of which flow south-eastwards from the central Quetta-Pishin plateau. Because these water-courses are fed by tributaries from narrow valleys with flat-backed limestone ridges—where rainfall accumulates rapidly—the area is prone to terrific floods. Despite these periodic Indus floods, the alluvial bay provides a unique opportunity for railway approach to Baluchistan.

The climate of Kach Gandava is extreme and is described as a land of poisonous winds and dust-storms. During the summer, temperatures never drop below 100°F, while in winter, they fall below the freezing point. The landscape is characterized by a "deadly monotony of dust-coloured scenery" for most of the year, with maximum heat and minimum rainfall. Due to these conditions, the climate debars the region from European occupation.

## Hydrography and Connectivity
Kach Gandava is a focal point for several water systems. In addition to the Bolan and Nari, the Mulla river carries floods from the Kalat highlands into the Gandava basin. The Mulla is noted as one of the most important ancient highways connecting the Indus plains to Kandahar. 

The region is strategically positioned as it leads to the Quetta and Pishin plateau, which serves as the base of the Kandahar highway and the central dominant water-divide of Baluchistan.

## Historical Governance and Conflict
Historically, Kach Gandava was held by various petty chiefs under the authority of the nawabs of Sind. Approximately two and a half centuries ago, the Brahuis gained mastery over the region. Abdulla Khan, a descendant of the leader Kambar, sought the conquest of Kach Gandava and, following various successes, the Kambaranis eventually possessed sovereignty over a significant portion of the plain, including the chief town of Gandava.

The region has frequently been a site of political instability and military movement:
* **The 1839 British Advance:** During the British army's advance through the Bolan Pass toward Afghanistan, British convoys were plundered in the Bolan Pass and through Kach Gandava. While initially blamed on Mehrab Khan, the ruler of Baluchistan, subsequent inquiries revealed the treachery was actually the work of his vizier, Mahommed Hussein, and allied chiefs.
* **Internal Power Struggles:** In early 1864, a political struggle for the leadership of the Kalat state culminated in the murder of Sher dil Khan in the Gandava Pass. Sher dil Khan had previously overthrown Khodadad Khan in 1863, but his rule ended with his death in the pass, leading to the re-election of Khodadad.
