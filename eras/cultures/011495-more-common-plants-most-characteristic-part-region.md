# Plants of Southern Arabia

The vegetation in the most characteristic part of southern Arabia is defined by its adaptation to aridity and long-continued drought. The flora of this region is characterized by a very small number of families relative to the area, with the number of genera and species being equally restricted. In many instances, a single species serves as the only representative of an entire order.

## Common and Rare Plant Families
The most common plants found in this characteristic region are those of the Capparidaceae and Euphorbiaceae families. Additionally, a few Leguminosae, as well as *Reseda* and *Dipterygium*, are present. Conversely, certain types of vegetation are rare in this area, specifically ferns, palms, Polygonaceae, and other cryptogams.

## Physical Characteristics and Adaptations
The aspect of the vegetation in southern Arabia is described as very peculiar. This appearance is commonly determined by the predominance of four or five specific species, while the remaining plants are either sparingly scattered across the area or are local. 

Several distinct physical adaptations have evolved to survive the environmental conditions:
*   **Coloration:** The flora lacks ordinary bright green colors; instead, almost all plants possess whitened or glaucous stems.
*   **Moisture Storage:** To combat long-continued drought, foliage is reduced to a minimum. Plants store moisture within fleshy or massive stems.
*   **Defense Mechanisms:** Aridity has encouraged the production of asperities and sharp thorns, which serve as a defense against external attack.
*   **Chemical Properties:** Pungency and aromatic odors are almost universal qualities of plants in these desert regions. Many species produce resins and gums, which leave the stems encrusted with exudations.

## Regional Variations and Cultivation
While the desert tracts are characterized by the traits mentioned above, other parts of the region exhibit different floral patterns. In the south-western mountainous regions known as Arabia Felix, where summits reach 6,000 or 7,000 feet, the rainfall is sufficient to support more luxuriant vegetation. The valleys in these areas possess a flora similar to the less elevated parts of Baluchistan, Afghanistan, and southern Persia, sharing characteristics with the hotter Mediterranean region. In these specific countries, aromatic shrubs are abundant. Trees are rare in these highlands, almost exclusively limited to *Dodonaea*, *Celtis*, *Pistacia*, poplars, and the date palm. The dry hills are covered by prickly forms of *Astragalus* and *Statice*. During the spring, there is an abundance of herbaceous vegetation and bulbous plants, some of which are identical to genera found in the Syrian region and extending to the Himalaya.

Regarding cultivated plants, Arabia grows crops similar to those of northern India, including the common *Sorghum*, barley, wheat, indigo, cotton, and lemons. Dates are also cultivated, and coffee is grown, though it is restricted to the slopes of the western hills.
