# Acanthaceae, Cyperaceae, and Labiatae

The plant families Acanthaceae, Cyperaceae, and Labiatae are among the principal orders of plants found in the region of south-eastern Asia. This geographical area encompasses the Malayan peninsula and islands, as well as India, extending from approximately the 105th to the 65th meridian.

## Distribution in South-Eastern Asia
Sir J.D. Hooker estimated that the number of indigenous plant species in south-eastern Asia ranges between 12,000 and 15,000. Within this region, the flora is characterized as a heterogeneous assemblage of alpine, temperate, and tropical plants. While the tropical plants are dominant enough to define the character of the flora as a whole, there is significant variation between the vegetation of arid regions and more humid ones. Additionally, the characteristics of flora found on the plains differ entirely from those found on higher mountain ranges.

The Indian flora is noted for providing a more general and complete illustration of almost all the world's chief natural families than any other country. Among the principal orders arranged by numerical importance are the Leguminosae, Rubiaceae, Orchidaceae, Compositae, Gramineae, Euphorbiaceae, Acanthaceae, Cyperaceae, and Labiatae. However, the presence of these families varies by location; for instance, Cyperaceae and Gramineae are deficient in some places, while Labiatae and Leguminosae are deficient in others.

## The Cyperaceae Family
The Cyperaceae family includes plants typically found in wet places. In the more rainy regions, Cyperaceae is noted as part of the herbaceous vegetation, alongside Gramineae, Orchidaceae, Orontiaceae, Scitamineae, and various Cryptogams such as ferns.

Specific examples within the Cyperaceae family include:
*   **Scirpus lacustris**: Commonly referred to as the bulrush, this plant is found in wet areas and is characterized by tall, usually leafless, spongy stems that bear tufts of many-flowered spikelets. These stems are utilized for purposes such as matting.
*   **Papyrus**: This member of the Cyperaceae order was abundant in the Nile and is identified as the bulrush associated with the hiding of Moses in Scripture.

It should be noted that the name "bulrush" is also generally applied to *Typha latifolia* (the club-rush or reed-mace), though *Typha latifolia* belongs to a different family than the Cyperaceae.

## The Labiatae Family
The Labiatae, also known as the Deadnettle order, includes fragrant herbs such as Balm (*Melissa officinalis*). Balm is characterized by small flowers that are white or rose-spotted, and opposite, crenulated, ovate leaves that appear wrinkled on the upper side. 

Originally native to southern and central Europe, Balm has become naturalized in the south of England and is grown in gardens; it also grows as a garden escape in North America. The name is derived from the Greek word *melissa*, as the plant is visited by bees. An allied southern European species found in the south and south-west of England is Bastard Balm (*Melittis Melissophyllum*).
