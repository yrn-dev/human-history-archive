# The Mediterranean, Arctic Sea, and Caspian Sea

The geography of Asia is defined in part by its relationship to three major water bodies: the Mediterranean, the Arctic Sea, and the Caspian. These bodies of water serve as primary drainage points for the northern section of the continent, which is characterized by an average altitude of less than 5,000 feet above sea level.

## Hydrography and Continental Divisions
The northern division of Asia, which includes the drainage basins falling into the Mediterranean, the Arctic Sea, and the Aralo-Caspian depression, covers an area of approximately 6,394,500 square miles. The distribution of this drainage is as follows:
*   **Arctic river basins:** 4,367,000 sq. m.
*   **Aralo-Caspian basin:** 1,759,000 sq. m.
*   **Mediterranean:** 268,500 sq. m.

In contrast, the southern division of the continent is nearly equal in extent at 6,514,000 square miles, divided between the Indian Ocean (2,873,000 sq. m.) and Pacific drainage (3,641,000 sq. m.). Between these lies a central section of depression with an average level likely not exceeding 2,000 feet above sea level, where drainage is lost in *hamuns* or swamps.

## The Caspian Sea
The Caspian Sea (known historically as *Mare Caspium* or *Mare Hyrcanium*) is an inland sea situated between Europe and Asia. It extends from 36 deg. 40' to 47 deg. 20' N. latitude and 46 deg. 50' to 55 deg. 10' E. longitude. It measures 760 miles from north to south and ranges from 100 to 280 miles in breadth, covering a total area of 169,330 square miles, including 865 square miles of islands.

The Caspian fills the deepest part of the Aralo-Caspian depression. Its surface is currently located 86 feet (or 96.7 feet according to Aral-Caspian levelling) below ocean level, and 242.7 feet below the level of the Aral.

### Salinity and Composition
The salinity of the Caspian is generally three-eighths that of the ocean and lower than that of the Black Sea. Salinity varies by region:
*   **Northern Section:** Due to the Volga, Ural, and Terek rivers, surface salinity is as low as 0.0075%, making the water drinkable.
*   **Middle Section:** Surface salinity increases to 0.015%.
*   **Southern Section:** Surface water averages 1.5% salinity.
*   **Kara-boghaz Bay:** This isolated basin on the east side reaches a salinity of 16.3%. Its bottom contains an estimated 1,000,000,000 tons of Epsom salts (sulphate of magnesia) in a deposit 7 feet thick.

### Fauna
The sea's fauna is a mixture of marine and fresh-water forms. Fresh-water species include the lobster and species of *Perca*, *Cyprinus*, and *Silurus*. Marine forms include the herring (*Clupea*), the sponge *Amorphina*, and various molluscs such as *Cardium edule*. Some species are unique to the Caspian, including twenty-five molluscs and the mammal *Phoca caspia*. The presence of *Phoca caspia* and certain *Mysidae* suggests Arctic characteristics, though no real proof of a recent geological connection to the Arctic has been discovered.

## Geological History and Evolution
Russian geologists suggest the Caspian was once part of the Sarmatian Ocean during the early Tertiary age, stretching from the middle Danube through South Russia and the Caucasus to the Aralo-Caspian region. This ocean had open communication with the great ocean, a fact believed by ancient geographers such as Pliny, Strabo, and Eratosthenes.

This communication likely closed during the Miocene period. By the Pliocene, the Sarmatian Ocean divided into sections, including the Aralo-Caspian sea. During the Ice Age, the Caspian flowed over northern steppes and may have remained connected to the Black Sea. It sent a gulf up the Volga valley as far as 56 deg. N. lat. and penetrated the Uzboi depression. Following the thawing of the ice cap and a period of desiccation, the Caspian shrank and its connections to the Black Sea and Sea of Aral were severed.

## Comparative Dynamics of the Mediterranean
The Mediterranean is described as a "thirsty sea" or a sea of evaporation. Because the rivers flowing into it cannot compensate for the water lost to evaporation, it requires a constant current of water from the Atlantic and the Bosphorus and Black Sea. In contrast, the Black Sea is an "overflowing sea" that receives more water from rivers than it requires. When the Mediterranean was previously cut off from the Atlantic and Black Sea, it became a shrinking sea with water levels sinking below those of the outside ocean, a condition similar to that of the modern Caspian and Dead Seas.

## Continental Boundaries
The northern boundary of Asia is the Arctic Ocean, with its northernmost point at Cape Sivero-Vostochnyi (Chelyuskin) at 78 deg. N. The western boundary reaches the Mediterranean at Cape Baba (26 deg. E.) near the Dardanelles. From there, the boundary follows the Mediterranean and the Red Sea to the strait of Bab-el-Mandeb. Alternatively, it passes through the Black Sea and the Caucasus range to the Caspian, then turns north along the Ural Mountains to meet the Arctic Ocean.
