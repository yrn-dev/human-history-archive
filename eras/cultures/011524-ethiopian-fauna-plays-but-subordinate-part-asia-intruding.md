# The Ethiopian Fauna in Asia

The distribution of animal life across the Asian continent is divided into several zoological provinces that correspond closely with botanical divisions. Among these, the Ethiopian region—which extends over Africa south of the Atlas mountains—plays a subordinate role within Asia. Its presence is primarily limited to the south-western corner of the continent.

## Geographic Distribution
The Ethiopian zoological district in Asia comprises Arabia, Palestine, and southern Persia. This region extends further east to include Baluchistan and Sind, mirroring the extent of the hot desert botanical tract. While the Ethiopian fauna is largely concentrated in the south-western corner and the desert districts of Syria and Arabia, certain characteristic species reach as far as western India and Persia.

## Characteristic Mammals and Birds
The influence of the Ethiopian type in Asia is evidenced by specific animal groups. Among mammals, the lion and the hunting leopard are considered Ethiopian types that extend into these Asian territories. Additionally, various species of jerboa and other forms adapted to desert environments are present.

In terms of ornithology, the Ethiopian influence is marked by the prevalence of stone chats and larks. A defining characteristic of the Ethiopian presence in this area is the complete absence of the many peculiar genera that are otherwise found in the Indian region.

## Comparison with Other Asian Regions
The Ethiopian region exists alongside other distinct zoological provinces in Asia:

*   **The Palaearctic Region:** This includes the northern portion of Asia as far south as the Himalaya. It is not zoologically distinct from Europe and the strip of Africa north of the Atlas. The ornithology of northern Asia is closely allied to Europe, featuring a development of groups characteristic of temperate and cold regions, such as the genus *Phasianus* and various *Passeres* (including finches, grosbeaks, and linnets).
*   **The Indian Region:** This region comprises Asia south of the Yang-tse-Kiang and the Himalayas, including islands west of Lombok and Celebes. It possesses one of the world's richest and most varied assemblages of birds, surpassed only by tropical America. It is characterized by the family *Eurylaemidae* (entirely confined to this part of Asia) and peculiar genera such as *Buceros*, *Lophophorus*, *Pajo*, *Ceriornis*, *Harpactes*, and *Euplocamus*.
*   **The Australian Region:** This includes Papua, Celebes, and other islands east of Java beyond Wallace's line.

## Broader Zoological Classifications
In the broader classification system proposed by Dr. Sclater, these divisions—including the Ethiopian, Indian, Palaearctic, and Nearctic regions—are categorized. The term "Arctogaea," coined by Huxley, is used to encompass all these regions in opposition to "Notogaea." Within Arctogaea, the Nearctic and Palaearctic areas are often grouped together under the generally accepted term "Holarctic region."
