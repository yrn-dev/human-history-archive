# Sunnite Mahommedans

The Sunnite sect represents a major branch of the Mahommedan faith, characterized by specific doctrines regarding law, leadership, and the nature of the Imamate.

## Doctrines of Leadership and the Imamate
In the Sunnite tradition, the Imam is viewed as a leader or guide—a pattern whose example is followed. The Sunnites maintain that the appointment of the head of the Moslem community is a matter for men to decide. The basis for this appointment is not found in the Koran, analogy, or tradition from Mahomet, but is instead rooted in agreement and obedience to the general usage of the Moslem peoples since the earliest times.

A primary distinction between the Sunnite and Shi'ite perspectives is the nature of the Imam's fallibility. Sunnites regard the Imam as liable to err and believe he should be obeyed even if he personally sins, provided that he maintains the ordinances of Islam. For the Sunnite sect, the essential requirement for leadership is that it be effective. This differs from the Shi'ite belief that a divinely appointed Imam is preserved from sin and divinely illumined.

## Regional Observances and Variations
The adherence to Sunnite doctrines varies by region and population. In Badakshan proper, the population—consisting of Arabs, Turks, and Tajiks—professes the orthodox doctrines of the Mahommedan law as adopted by the Sunnite sect.

In contrast, the Kazaks are described as being nominally Sunnites, though they are "Shamanists at heart." Their spiritual life involves the worship of the Shaitan (a bad spirit) and the Kudai (a good divinity), with a strong belief in charlatans and soothers called *talchi*. Among the Kazaks, the traditional structures of the faith are largely absent; there are no regular prayers, mollahs, mosques, or practices of ablution and fasting. While they have been Mussulmans since the start of the 16th century, their pilgrims typically visit shrines of "saints" in eastern Turkestan rather than traveling to Mecca.

The Kazaks also hold an abstract doctrine that the "Kafir" may be lawfully oppressed. This category of "Kafir" includes not only Christians and Buddhists but also Mahommedans of the Shiah sect.

## Concepts of Destiny
Mahommedans use the term *kismet* to describe fate or destiny. This term is used to express the various incidents and details of a person's lot in life. The word *kismet* is the Turkish form of the Arabic *gismat*, derived from *gasama*, meaning "to divide."
