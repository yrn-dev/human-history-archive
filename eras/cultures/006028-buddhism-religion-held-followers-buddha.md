# BUDDHISM

Buddhism is a religion held by the followers of the Buddha, covering a large geographical area across India and central and east Asia. It has spread to include Tibet, Japan, China, Burmah, Ceylon, Manchuria, and Turkestan. In China, it is the dominant religion and serves as a refuge for people facing worldly trouble or sickness.

## The Buddha
According to Buddhist theory, a "Buddha" appears in the world from time to time to preach the true doctrine. Over time, this teaching is corrupted or lost, remaining absent until a new Buddha appears. In a historical context, the term refers to the last historical Buddha, whose family name was Gotama. He was the son of Suddhoda, a chief of the Sakiyas, one of the republican clans in India.

While some historical figures, such as Professor Wilson of Oxford in 1854, once argued that the Buddha was an imaginary being and his life a myth, it is now admitted that a basis of truth exists beneath the miraculous and legendary tales surrounding him. The Buddha consistently referred to himself by the title Arahat; he did not call himself the Buddha, nor did his followers address him by that name.

## Essential Doctrines and Teachings
The Buddha's system was first expounded to five men he sought to win over to his faith. These teachings are preserved in the sacred books, specifically within the *suttas* (containing doctrine) and the *Vinaya* (rules of the society or order he founded).

A central tenet of the Buddha's teaching was the rejection of the Indian caste system. He proclaimed that just as four streams lose their names when they mingle in the Ganges, all who believe in Buddha cease to be Sudras, Vaisyas, Kshatriyas, or Brahmins.

Buddhism rejects the idea of a priesthood possessing mystical powers. Salvation is viewed as entirely dependent on an individual's own exertions and the growth or modification of their own inner nature. While the life of a recluse is considered the most conducive to achieving a state of "sweet serenity," the life of a believing householder is also held in high honor. Laypeople may "enter the paths" through a life of kindness and rectitude to ensure rebirth under conditions more favorable for holiness.

In China, the religion evolved from a subtle doctrine into a more defined form where the Buddha is viewed as the savior of the people through righteousness, and Buddhist saints are believed to possess intercessory powers.

## History and Spread
The Buddha's early ministry included a period at the Deer-forest near Benares, where he gathered about threescore personal followers and a larger number of outside believers. Among his prominent followers was Yasa, a rich young man who brought many friends to the teacher; Yasa's mother and wife became the first female disciples, and his father became the first lay devotee.

Buddhism prevailed in India for several centuries, often prospering side by side with Brahminism. However, it was eventually defeated and driven out of Indian life by the persistence of Brahmin caste ideas and the vitality of the Brahmins. Despite this, remnants persisted; for example, in the first half of the 7th century, Hiuen Tsang recorded 100 convents and 5,000 monks in Kashmir. Later, in the end of the 11th century, King Harshadeva was noted as a protector of the faith.

## Sacred Texts and Literature
The oldest extant documents include the *suttas* and the *Vinaya*. Other significant works include the *Dhammapada*, *Sutta Nipata*, and the *Jataka* (Buddhist Birth Stories).

Later literature developed in periods between the time of the Buddha and Asoka, from Asoka to Kanishka, and from Kanishka to Buddhaghosa. Notable works from these periods include:
*   ***Milinda***: An early historical novel depicting a discussion where a Buddhist elder named Nagasena converts Menander (Milinda), the Greek king of Bactria.
*   ***Mahavastu* (Sublime Story)**: A Sanskrit work of the Maha-sanghikas school. It details the Buddha's previous births and his victory over ignorance under the Bo Tree to attain *Sambodhi* (the higher Wisdom of Nirvana). This text reflects views that developed into the Mahayana, or modern, form of Buddhism in India.
*   ***Lalita Vistara* and *Buddha Carita***: Later Sanskrit epic poems on the same subject.
