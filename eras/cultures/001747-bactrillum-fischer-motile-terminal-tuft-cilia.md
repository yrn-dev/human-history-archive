# Bactrillum (Fischer)

*Bactrillum* is a genus of bacteria classified under the system developed by Fischer. It is categorized within the order Haplobacterinae, the family Bacillaceae, and the sub-family Bacilleae.

## Morphological Characteristics
The vegetative cells of *Bactrillum* are characterized as sporogenous rodlets. These cells are straight and may be described as cylindric, ovoid, or ellipsoid in shape. In accordance with the general characteristics of the family Bacillaceae, the division planes of these cells are always perpendicular to the long axis.

## Motility and Cilia
*Bactrillum* is a motile organism. Its motility is facilitated by special motile organs known as cilia or flagella, which are fine protoplasmic outgrowths of the cell. In the specific case of *Bactrillum*, these cilia are arranged in a terminal tuft, a condition referred to as lophotrichous.

According to Fischer, these cilia are derived from the central protoplasm and pass through minute pores in the cell wall, although they appear to be attached to the wall as they remain unaffected by plasmolysis. These structures are invisible under ordinary illumination in unstained preparations or living cells; they require special staining and preparation methods, first utilized by Loeffler, to be clearly seen.

## Classification and Taxonomic Challenges
Within Fischer's classification of the sub-family Bacilleae, *Bactrillum* is distinguished from related genera by the arrangement of its cilia. While *Bactrinium* is monotrichous (possessing one polar flagellum) and *Bactridium* is peritrichous (having cilia over the entire surface), *Bactrillum* is defined by its lophotrichous terminal tuft.

However, this system of classification has faced several principal objections:
* **Observation Difficulties:** It is extraordinarily difficult to obtain satisfactory preparations that show the cilia. Furthermore, these organs may only develop during short periods of activity when the organism is young and vigorous, or may not form on all substrata.
* **Inconstancy of Characters:** The lophotrichous and monotrichous conditions are not always constant during the motile stage. For example, *Pseudomonas rosea* (Mig.) can possess one, two, or three cilia at either end. Consequently, such an organism could be distributed between *Bactrinium* and *Bactrillum* depending on which state is observed at the time.

## General Biological Context
As a member of the Haplobacterinae, *Bactrillum* possesses a unicellular vegetative body. The broader study of such organisms involves the investigation of the nucleus—a subject of significant discussion. While most forms do not demonstrate a nucleus typical of higher plants, some researchers have identified "chromatin-granules" as potential rudimentary nuclei, while others, such as Vejdovsky and Mencl, suggest that typical nuclei and modes of division exist in some of the largest bacteria.
