# AYMARA

The Aymara, known anciently as the Colla, are a tribe of South American Indians. Historically, they inhabited the region surrounding Lake Titicaca and the adjacent valleys of the Andes. In the present era, they constitute the primary ethnical element in Bolivia, though they are of very mixed blood. A small number of Aymaras are also located in southern Peru.

## History and Culture
Prior to their conquest by the Incas during the 13th and 14th centuries, the Aymaras appear to have possessed a very considerable culture. Evidence of this early civilization remains in the megalithic ruins of Tiahuanaco. The region around Lake Titicaca was regarded as a "holy land" by the Incas, whose own national legends attributed the origin of all Inca (Quichua) civilization to that specific area.

By the time of the arrival of the Spaniards, the Aymaras had been under Inca domination for a long period and were in a decadent state. Despite this, they maintained certain privileges under their conquerors, including the continued use of their own language. The nature of their treatment by the Incas suggests that the Incas believed themselves to be of Aymara blood.

## Physical and Behavioral Characteristics
Physically, a pure Aymara is characterized as being short and thick-set with a great development of the chest. They share several physical traits with the Quichuas, including a broad face, black eyes, a rounded forehead, and a reddish complexion. In terms of disposition, the Aymaras are described as apathetic and sullen, traits they share with the Quichuas.

## Population and Social Status
Including half-breeds, the Aymara population in Bolivia numbers approximately half a million. They are categorized as one of the "civilized tribes of the Andes" who were once part of the nationality ruled by the Incas. Those located south of the latitude of Lake Titicaca attained a considerable degree of civilization before the discovery of America and have had closer contact with Europeans than other Bolivian tribes, although it is doubtful if their condition improved under these influences.

The Aymara, along with other Andean and regional groups such as the Mojos and Chiquitos, are described as industrious and peaceable, serving as an important labouring element. However, they are noted for the excessive use of *chica*, a native beer produced from Indian corn. Their lack of ambition to improve their social condition is attributed, in part, to the state of peonage in which they are held and their profound ignorance.
