# Congo Basin and Gabon

The Congo basin is a vast region of Equatorial Africa, the majority of which is occupied by the Belgian Congo. Formerly known as the Independent State of the Congo, it was annexed by Belgium in 1908. The region is characterized by its extensive river systems and lakes, which serve as the primary natural links for communication with the seaboard.

## Geography and Hydrography
The basin is defined by the Congo river and its numerous headstreams. One of the most remote headstreams of the Congo is the Chambezi river, which flows into a swamp on the east side of Lake Bangweulu. Lake Bangweulu is a shallow lake located in British Central Africa between 10° 38' and 11° 31' S. and 30° E. It sits in a central basin on an extensive plateau approximately 3,700 feet above sea level. The lake is surrounded by a morass of reeds and papyrus, with the Luapula river leaving the lake at its southernmost point.

Other significant water bodies associated with the broader region include Lake Tanganyika, Lake Mweru, and Lake Bangweulu. In the southern territories, the river Chambezi, the Luapula (upper Congo), and the Kafue (or Kafukwe) are prominent. The region also contains the Great Mweru Swamp and various smaller lakes such as Moir's Lake, Lake Malombe, and the salt lake Chilwa.

## Bantu Migration and Population
The current population of the Congo basin is largely the result of a migration that occurred no more than 3,000 years ago. A powerful tribe of negroes speaking a Bantu mother-language—physically allied to negroes of the southern Lake Chad and south-western Nile basins, and impregnated with the Caucasian Hamite—pushed forcibly from the heart of Africa. This heartland was the region between the western Nile, the Congo, and the Shari watersheds.

Prior to this Bantu invasion, the Atlantic seaboard of the Congo basin and the south-western Nile watershed were occupied by West Coast negroes, while the center was inhabited by Forest Pygmies and negroes of a low type. As the Bantu swept across the continent, they assimilated or absorbed the vast majority of these preceding populations.

Physical characteristics among Bantu speakers in this region are diverse. While some resemblance exists among tribes such as the Babangi of the upper Congo, there is great variety in head shape and outward appearance. Some Bantu-speaking tribes are dwarfish and belong to the Forest Pygmies group, while others resemble West African negroes or are of Nilotic or Gala (Galla) origin in the north.

## Linguistic Groups of the Congo and Gabon
Bantu speech in the Western group includes the languages of Cameroon and the Central and Northern Congo. These are divided into several distinct assemblages:

*   **Bangala-Bobangi-Liboko Group:** This group includes commercial languages of the Upper Congo, such as Ngala, Bangi, Liboko, Poto, Ngombe, and Yanzi. These dialects are found along the main stream of the Congo from the junction of the Sanga in the west to the Lomami and Rubi rivers in the east, extending north to approximately 3° 30' N.
*   **Bateke (Batio) Group:** This group generally encompasses Bantu dialects east of the Atlantic coast-lands, south of the Ngoko and Upper Ogowe rivers, northwest of the Lower Congo, and west of the Sanga river.
*   **Fan or Pangwe Forms:** Found in French Gabon and the interior of German Cameroon, these forms of speech extend to the Mbam, Sanaga, and Sanga rivers, and down to the Ogowe. These are described as being so corrupt as to be barely recognizable as Bantu.
*   **Duala Group:** Located on the delta and estuary of the Cameroon river, these languages are considered to be of a much purer Bantu type.
*   **Bakuba Assemblage:** These Central Congo dialects (including Songe, Babuma, and Shilange) are located between the Upper Kwilu and Kwa-Kasai in the west and the Lomami river in the east, with the Sankuru river serving as the northern boundary.
*   **Rua-Luba-Lunda-Marungu Group:** This group, which includes Lulua, Kanyoka, and Ki-tabwa, occupies the south central Congo basin between the Zambezi watershed to the south and the Bakuba country to the north.
*   **Balolo Group:** These languages are found in the Northern Congo bend, bounded by the main stream of the Congo.
*   **Manyema (Baenya) Group:** This group consists of corrupt Bantu dialects between the main stream of the Luapula-Congo and the western watershed of Tanganyika. It includes languages of the Aruwimi basin, such as Lokele, Soko, Kusu, Tu-rumbu, and Yalulema. The Kibira dialects of the north-eastern Congo Forest (Ituri district) may also be placed in this section.

## Infrastructure and Communication
The development of tropical Africa has relied on opening communications via the Congo river and its lakes. During the war, two railways were constructed in the Congo territory to extend communications from the center to the south and from east to west. These developments created a transport chain allowing travel from the Indian Ocean at Dar-es-Salaam to the Atlantic Ocean via a series of railways and steamers passing through Ujiji, Lake Tanganyika, Albertville, Kabalo, Kongolo, and Kindu.
