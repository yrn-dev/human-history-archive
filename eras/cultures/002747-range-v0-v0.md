# Range

In geographical and physical terms, a range refers to a series of mountains or hills, often running parallel to one another and forming distinct belts or systems across various global regions.

## The Kuen-lun System
The Kuen-lun system is a rugged mountainous region characterized by several distinct sections.

### Western Kuen-lun
The western extremity of this system is defined by a double border-ridge consisting of the Sarik-kol and the Muztagh (or Kashgar) ranges. This ridge separates the Pamir highlands from the East Turkestan lowlands. The western foot of this ridge sits on the Pamirs at 10,500 to 13,000 ft., while the eastern foot descends into the Tarim basin at 4,000-4,500 ft. The Muztagh-ata peak reaches 25,780 ft.

East of the meridian of Yarkand (77° E.), this double border-ridge continues as twin ranges running from the W.N.W. to the E.S.E. The northerly range includes the Kilian (or Kiliang), Khotan, and Keriya Mountains, while the southerly range comprises the Raskem (or Raskan), Sughet, and Ullugh-tagh Mountains. These ranges generally decrease in altitude from west to east, though they still reach 19,000 ft., with some peaks ascending an additional 2,000-2,500 ft. High-altitude passes include the Sughet-davan (17,825 ft.), Kyzyl-davan (16,900 ft.), and Sanju-davan (16,325 ft.). The Raskem (or Yarkand-darya) gorge separates the southerly range from the Karakorum Mountains, while the Khotan-darya glen separates the Sughet and Kilian Mountains.

### Middle Kuen-lun
The middle Kuen-lun begins around 86° E., east of the Cherchen-darya, where ranges radiate outward like fingers. These include the Astin-tagh, Chimen-tagh, Kalta-alaghan, and Arka-tagh. This region consists of nearly parallel ranges running W.S.W. E.N.E. to W.N.W. E.S.E., separated by high intermont valleys containing shallow lacustrine basins. The crests often appear as rounded domes due to aerial denudation.

The Chimen-tagh range, identical in its western parts to the Piazlik-tagh and equated in the east with the Tsaidam chain, acts as a "breakwater" for clouds, resulting in more abundant water and animal life than the Akato-tagh or Astin-tagh. It features passes at 13,970, 13,230, and 13,760 ft.

The Kalta-alaghan (also known as the Columbus or Chimen-tagh Range) is the culminating range of this section. Unlike most parallel ranges in Northern Tibet, it increases in elevation toward the east. It features the Avraz-davan pass at 15,700 ft. and is linked to the Tokuz-davan via the Muzluk-tagh.

## Nan-shan Highlands
The Nan-shan Highlands overlook Tsaidam on the N.E., covering an area 380 m. long and 260 m. wide. The region is occupied by parallel ranges running N.W. to S.E., with longitudinal valleys at altitudes of 12,000 to 14,000 ft. (dropping to 9,000-10,000 ft. at the south-western border). These valleys contain lakes such as Khara-nor (13,285 ft.) and Koko-nor (9,970 ft.).

On the N.W., the highlands are fringed by the Da-sue-shan border range (a continuation of the Astin-tagh), which reaches 12,200-13,000 ft. in its passes. On the S.W., the Nan-shan mountains consist of short irregular chains and broad plains. A higher terrace (12,000-13,500 ft.) contains Ritter's range, with passes at 15,990 ft. and 14,680 ft., and the Humboldt (or Ama-surgu) range. The latter includes several parallel ranges, such as the Suess, Semenov, Mushketov, Alexander III., and Bain-sarlyk.

## Other Global Ranges
### North America
British Columbia is characterized by the Cordillera or Cordilleran belt, consisting of parallel mountain ranges and narrow valleys. The two primary systems are the Rocky Mountains to the north-east and the Coast Range to the south-west. Between these lies the "Interior Plateau," an elevated tract of hilly country. Vancouver Island and the Queen Charlotte Islands are remnants of a submerged mountain range parallel to the coast.

### Assam
Assam is divided into three tracts, including the hill ranges between the Brahmaputra and Surma valleys. This elevated plateau, known as the Assam range, is composed of the Naga, North Cachar, Jaintia, Khasi, and Garo hills. The highest points are Japva peak (nearly 10,000 ft.) in the Naga hills, Shillong peak (6,450 ft.) in the Khasi-Jaintia hills, and Nokrek peak (4,600 ft.) in the Garo hills.

## Military Ranging
In a historical military context, "ranged" refers to the strategic arraying of forces. During the conflict at Plataia, Mardonios ranged his forces against the Hellenes by stationing the Persians opposite the Lacedemonians, followed by the Medes, Bactrians, Indians, and Sacans. The Boeotians, Locrians, Malians, Thessalians, and Phokians were ranged opposite the Athenians, Plataians, and Megarians, while the Macedonians and those from the borders of Thessaly were ranged opposite the Athenians.
