# The Bongo People

The Bongo (also referred to as Dor or Deran) are a tribe of Nilotic negroes inhabiting the south-west region of the Bahr-el-Ghazal province in the Anglo-Egyptian Sudan. They are believed to be related to the Zandeh tribes of the Welle district.

## Population and History
According to G.A. Schweinfurth, who spent two years living among the Bongo, the population numbered at least 300,000 before the arrival of slave-raiders around 1850. The population was significantly reduced by these slave-raiders and subsequently by the dervishes. Recovery of the population only became possible following the establishment of effective control by the Sudan government between 1904 and 1906. Historically, the Bongo resided in numerous peaceful and independent small communities; under the administration of the Sudan government, they have returned to managing their own affairs.

## Physical Characteristics
The Bongo are described as a race of medium height and a tendency to be thick-set. Their complexion is red-brown, which Schweinfurth compared to the soil of their residence, and they possess black hair. Regarding cranial structure, Schweinfurth declares their heads to be nearly round, stating that, to his knowledge, no other African race possesses a higher cephalic index.

In later life, Bongo women incline toward steatopygia. Schweinfurth noted that this deposit of fat, combined with the tail of bast worn by the women, gave them the appearance of "dancing baboons" while walking.

## Dress and Ornamentation
Historically, Bongo men wore only a loin-cloth and dozens of iron rings on their arms, which were arranged to function as a type of armour. Women formerly wore a simple girdle with an attached tuft of grass. In more recent times, both sexes have largely adopted cotton cloths for their dresses.

Tribal ornaments include plugs or nails passed through the lower lip. Women frequently wear a disk several inches in diameter in the lower lip, along with a bit of straw or a ring in the upper lip, a ring in the septum, and straws in the alae of the nostrils.

## Housing and Lifestyle
The Bongo construct well-built huts, some of which reach heights of 24 feet. Unlike other Negroes of the upper Nile, the Bongo are not great cattle-breeders. Instead, they dedicate their time to agriculture, primarily cultivating durra, sesame, tobacco, and sorghum.

Their diet is diverse, consisting of fungi, tubers, and fruits found in the region. They consume almost every type of creature, including reptiles, insects, beasts, and birds, with the sole exception of the dog. They do not despise any flesh, whether it is fresh or putrid, and they drive vultures from carcasses.
