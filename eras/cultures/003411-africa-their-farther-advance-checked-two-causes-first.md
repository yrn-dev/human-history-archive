# Africa

## Bantu Migrations and Populations
The study of Bantu languages suggests that approximately 3,000 years ago, a powerful tribe of negroes speaking the Bantu mother-language originated from the heart of Africa, specifically the region between the western Nile, Congo, and Shari watersheds. This group, physically allied to negroes of the southern Lake Chad basins and the south-western Nile—though impregnated with the Caucasian Hamite—pushed forcibly into the southern half of the continent.

At the time of this invasion, the southern half of Africa was likely sparsely populated, with the exception of the east, south, and north-west. The Atlantic seaboard of the Congo basin and the south-western Nile watershed were occupied by West Coast negroes, while the center was inhabited by Forest Pygmies and negroes of a low type. The East African coast region, extending from the eastern coasts of Victoria Nyanza down to opposite Zanzibar, was populated by Hottentot-Bushman and Nilotic-negro groups. From the Cape of Good Hope north-westward to Lakes Nyasa and Tanganyika, the population consisted of Nilotic-negro, Forest-negro, Bushman, and Hottentot. The Bantu swept over nearly all these areas, absorbing or assimilating the vast majority of these preceding populations.

Modern Bantu-speaking peoples exhibit significant diversity in physical characteristics, head shape, and outward appearance. While some resemblance exists among tribes such as the Ba-tonga, Zulu-Kaffirs, the people of the Great Lakes, the Ova-herero, the Awemba, and the Babangi of the upper Congo, the diversity is vast. Some Bantu-speaking tribes are dwarfish and belong to the Forest Pygmies; others show relationships to the Hottentots, while some are indistinguishable from the black West African negro. Those in the north are of Nilotic or Gala (Galla) origin.

## Carthage and Indigenous Conflict
In the period between 530 and 500 B.C., the Carthaginian leader Mago worked to extend the dominion of Carthage and organize its forces. Later, the sons of Hasdrubal, who remained powerful citizens in the state, engaged in hostilities against other indigenous Africans and the Moors. These conflicts forced the indigenous populations to relinquish the tribute that Carthage had previously paid for the land upon which the city was situated.

The power of Hasdrubal's family became so great that the state nominated the select One Hundred Senators to sit as judges for the first time to check their ascendency. These wars within Africa served to prevent the Carthaginians from interfering further in Sicily between 480 and 410 B.C.

## Carthaginian Military Expeditions
Carthage later launched a massive military effort against Sicily. A mercenary force was assembled during the winter, consisting of Numidians, Libyans, Campanians, Mediterranean islanders, and Iberians. Estimates of the force's size vary, with Ephorus citing 300,000 men, while Timæus and Xenophon cite 120,000. This host was united at Carthage and prepared for transport via 1,500 transports and 120 triremes.

To secure the landing, 40 Carthaginian triremes were sent to the Bay of Motyê. Syracusan leaders attempted to check the arrival of this armament by attacking with an equal number of triremes, destroying 15 and driving the rest back to Africa. However, Hannibal arrived with 50 fresh triremes, forcing the Syracusans to retire and allowing the mercenary army to disembark near the western cape of Sicily.

## World War I Colonial Campaigns
During the Great War, various military movements occurred across the African continent. In the west, east, and south, small armies moved in wide circles. In the Cameroons forests, handfuls of men operated until the land was secured. In the German South-West territory, mounted troopers converged upon the tin shanties of Windhoek.

In German East Africa, troops of all races advanced through dark green forests and mountain glens over a period of months and years. This campaign continued until the enemy's strength was reduced to a group of exiles located beyond the southern frontier.
