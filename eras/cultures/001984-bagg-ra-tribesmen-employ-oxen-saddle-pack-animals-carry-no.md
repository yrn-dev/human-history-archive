# Bagg[=a]ra

The Bagg[=a]ra, whose name means "Cowherds," are African "Arabs" of Semitic origin. They are characterized as great breeders and owners of cattle and are described as true nomad Arabs who have preserved most of their national characteristics, having intermarried little with the Nuba.

## Geography and Origins
The Bagg[=a]ra occupy the region west of the White Nile, situated between Dar Nuba and the Shilluk territory, with a primary presence in Kordofan. While the exact date of their arrival in the Sudan is uncertain, they appear to have drifted up the Nile valley, subsequently dispossessing the original Nuba population.

## Lifestyle and Economy
As a purely pastoral people, the Bagg[=a]ra move from pasture to pasture based on the availability of food. They are noted for their use of oxen, which they employ as pack animals and for the saddle. 

Beyond cattle breeding, the Bagg[=a]ra are famous as hunters of big game, utilizing spears and swords to attack animals as large as elephants. Historically, they have also been engaged in the slave trade from the earliest times.

## Physical and Mental Characteristics
Descriptions of the Bagg[=a]ra vary. G. A. Schweinfurth describes them as the best-looking of the Nile nomads, noting their athletic bodies, sinewy limbs, fine heads, and erect posture. Their skin colors range from deep black to dark red-brown, and while their features are regular and lack negro characteristics, there is little Semitic quality to their appearance. They are considered much superior in mental power to the indigenous races surrounding them. Additionally, they possess a passion for ornaments and fine clothes, often decorating themselves with rings and glass trinkets. Conversely, other descriptions characterize them as having savage eyes and sinister expressions.

## Warfare and Political History
The Bagg[=a]ra have a reputation for being resolute fighters. Their customary weapons are the sword and lance, and they do not carry shields, although many possess firearms. 

They were among the most fervent and earliest supporters of the mahdi during his 1882 revolt against the Egyptians. Due to their fanatical courage, they served as his primary fighting force and were instrumental in his victories. Their decision to follow the mahdi to Khartum led to the fall of that city. Following the mahdi, his successor, the khalifa Abdullah, was himself a Bagg[=a]ra; consequently, the tribe enjoyed the highest favor throughout his rule.
