# Formosa and Regional Topography

## Geography of Formosa
Formosa is located near the coast of China, situated under the northern tropic. The island is characterized by a high range of mountains that reach an elevation of nearly 13,000 ft. The geography of the island is divided by these highlands: the western side consists of open and well-cultivated plains, while the eastern side is mountainous.

## Population and Land Use in Formosa
The demographics and land utilization of Formosa vary by region. The western plains are occupied by an immigrant Chinese population. In contrast, the mountainous eastern region is occupied by independent indigenous tribes who are described as being in a less advanced state.

## Comparative Elevations in East Asia
Other regions in East Asia exhibit varying heights. The Philippine Islands, located between Borneo and southern China (between 5 deg. and 20 deg. N.), have a maximum land height of 10,250 ft. The islands of Japan, situated between the 30th and 45th parallels, are traversed by a line of volcanic mountains, some of which remain active. The highest point in the Japanese group is approximately 13,000 ft. above the sea.

## Other High-Altitude Regions
High elevations are found in several other global regions:
*   **Central Asia:** The Tian-shan (Celestial Mountains) form the northern boundary of the Gobi desert, with some summits said to exceed 20,000 ft. The Ala-tau range rises to 14,000 or 15,000 ft., and the Altai mountains reach 10,000 or 12,000 ft. In the Pamir region, the southern bank of Lake Sirikol features mountains 19,000 ft. above the sea, and the lake itself has a measured elevation of 15,600 ft.
*   **South America:** The Puna de Atacama, a broken and volcanic mountainous region in western South America, ranges from 7,000 to 13,500 ft. in general elevation.
*   **Western Asia:** The plateau west of British India includes various high regions. The mountain scarp flanking the Tigris and Mesopotamian plains rises in many points to 10,000 ft.
