# Goro Escarpment

The Goro Escarpment is a mountain range characterized by a steep escarpment facing south. It is situated between Lake Stefanie and the Daua tributary of the Juba river, running in a north-west and south-east direction. At its eastern extremity, the range serves as the boundary between Abyssinia and the protectorate.

## Geography and Surrounding Terrain
The region south-east of the Goro Escarpment consists primarily of waterless, level plains covered in bush. To the north and west of the general region, the landscape is varied. East of Lake Rudolf and south of Lake Stefanie lies a large volcanic waterless steppe featuring mountain ranges, the highest of which is Mount Kanjora, reaching an altitude of 6,900 ft. Further south of this arid, lava-strewn region are the Rendile uplands, which provide pasturage for thousands of camels.

## Inhabitants
The area around the Goro Escarpment is inhabited by various groups. The Borani, a section of the Hamites, live south of the Goro Escarpment, although their primary countries, Dirri and Liban, are located within Abyssinian territory. Other nearby populations include the Somali, who occupy the land between the Juba and Tana rivers. Among these are the nomadic Ogaden Somali and the Herti, who reside near the coast and are more or less stationary. Additionally, the Gurre tribe of the Somali occupies the region south of the lower Daua.

## Political Boundaries
The northern region of the protectorate was initially little known and lacked formal administration, leading to raids by Abyssinians and Gallas. Due to the absence of a defined frontier, the Abyssinian government extended its posts south to Lake Rudolf and took control of the Boran country. 

Following negotiations, an agreement regarding the boundary line between the river Juba and Lake Rudolf was signed in Adis Ababa on December 6, 1907. This frontier was subsequently delimited in 1908-1909 by an Anglo-Abyssinian commission, with Major C.W. Gwynn serving as the primary British representative. Under this agreement, Lake Stefanie was assigned to Abyssinia, while the British were assigned Lake Rudolf (except for its north-eastern extremity). The frontier was established from approximately 4° 20' N., following the Daua river to its junction with the Juba.
