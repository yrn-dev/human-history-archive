# Physical Features of British Central Africa

## Territorial Extent and General Geography
The territory of British Central Africa encompasses approximately 250,000 square miles. A significant portion of the region—more than two-thirds—is situated within the Zambezi basin. This area is further divided into the subordinate basins of the rivers Luangwa and Luengwe-Kafukwe, as well as the basin of Lake Nyasa. Other portions of the territory drain into Lake Tanganyika, the river Congo, and a half-dried swamp or small lake known as Chilwa.

## Lakes and Water Bodies
The region is characterized by several prominent lakes and sheets of water. These include:
*   **Lake Nyasa:** Two-thirds of this lake are located within the territory's limits.
*   **Lake Tanganyika:** The territory includes the southern end of this lake.
*   **Lake Mweru:** More than half of this lake is contained within the borders.
*   **Lake Bangweulu:** The entirety of this lake is within the territory.
*   **Lake Chilwa:** Also referred to by the Bantu word *Kilwa* (though sometimes wrongly styled as Shirwa), this salt lake lies on the borders of the Portuguese province of Moçambique. While it currently has no outlet, it is believed that in past ages it emptied into the Lujenda river and subsequently into the Indian Ocean.
*   **Other Water Bodies:** The territory contains the Great Mweru Swamp, located between Mweru and Tanganyika, and Lake Malombe, which is situated on the upper Shiré. Additionally, there is Moir's Lake, a small mountain tarn—potentially a crater lake—positioned between the Luapula and Luangwa rivers.

## River Systems
The territory's hydrology is defined by several major river courses:
*   **The Zambezi:** The northern bank of the Zambezi serves as the southern border of the territory, extending from the confluence of the Luangwa at Zumbo to the confluence of the Kafukwe. East of Zumbo, Portuguese possessions separate British Central Africa from the Zambezi.
*   **The Congo System:** The territory includes the whole of the river Chambezi, which is the most remote of the Congo river's headwaters. It also encompasses the east or right bank of the Luapula (the upper Congo) from where it exits Lake Bangweulu until it issues from the north end of Lake Mweru.
*   **Other Rivers:** The territory contains nearly the entire courses of the Luangwa (or Loangwa) and the Shiré rivers, as well as the full course of the Kafue (or Kafukwe).
