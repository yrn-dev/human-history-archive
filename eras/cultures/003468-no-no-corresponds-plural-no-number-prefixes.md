# Bantu Pronominal Prefixes

The Bantu languages utilize a system of pronominal prefixes to categorize substantives into various classes or genders. These prefixes are attached to the root of the noun and are used to indicate whether the noun is singular or plural. This system, referred to by Dr. Bleek as "concord," extends beyond the noun; the pronominal particle or prefix is also attached to the roots of verbs, pronouns, prepositions, and adjectives within a sentence that are connected to the governing noun.

## Structure and Function of Prefixes
In the Bantu system, prefixes generally function either in a singular or plural sense. With the exception of the "abstract" prefix *Bu* (No. 14), a prefix used for the singular cannot be used for the plural, and vice versa. There is a degree of correspondence between these pairs; for example, prefix No. 2 serves almost invariably as the plural to No. 3, and prefix No. 8 corresponds as the plural to No. 7.

While these particles must always precede nominal, adjectival, pronominal, and verbal roots, they may follow the roots of some prepositions and the auxiliary prefix-participles used in verb conjugation.

## The Sixteen Common Prefixes
There are approximately sixteen prefixes common to the entire Bantu group. In their most archaic forms, these prefixes may have consisted of two distinct particles. For instance, the 6th plural prefix *Nga-ma-* and the 1st and 3rd prefixes *Ngu-mu-* suggest a structure where the first syllable acted as a demonstrative pronoun and the second as an adjective or numeral. It is theorized that *Mu-* likely meant "one," while *Ma-* served as a collective numeral of indefinite number, applicable to herds of beasts, tribes of men, liquids (specifically water), or anything in the mass.

Over time, as Bantu dialects became more corrupt, these prefixes and their corresponding particles varied in form. In some tongues, the particle corresponding to the plural prefix *Ma-* shifted to *Ya-*, *Ga-*, or *A-*. Similarly, the particle corresponding to *Mu-* (Classes 1 and 3) may appear as *u-*, *gu-*, *gw-*, *wu-*, *yu-*, or *n-*.

## Specific Prefix Variations and Concord
Several prefixes exhibit distinct evolutionary paths across different dialects:

*   **Prefix No. 2 (*Ba-* or *Aba-*)**: In the most archaic speech (Mt. Elgon), this appears as *Baba-* (or *Ngaba* in Zulu-Kaffir). While the concord remains *-ba-* in less corrupt tongues, it can degenerate into *A-*, *Ma-*, *Wa-*, or *Va-*.
*   **Prefix No. 7 (*Ki-*)**: This prefix and its concord often transition through *Ci-* into *Si-* (or *Shi*).
*   **Prefix No. 8**: This is the invariable plural of the 7th prefix. Its oldest form is *Ibi-* or *Mbibi-*, but it varies widely across Bantu speech, appearing as *Zi-*, *Ri-*, *By-*, *Bzi-*, *Psi-*, *Zwi-*, *Fi-*, *Pi-*, *Vi-*, *Fy-*, *P[vs]i-*, *[vS]i-*, or *I-*.
*   **Prefix No. 10**: Originally *Ti-*, *Tin-*, *Zi-*, or *Zin-*, it has evolved into forms such as *Lin-*, *Din-*, *Rin-*, *Jin-*, or *[theta]on-*. The *n* in this prefix is actually the singular prefix No. 9. In some languages, such as the Swahili of Zanzibar, the prefix has fallen out of use as a definite article for nouns but persists in the concord (e.g., *Nombe hizi zangu*).
*   **Prefix No. 11 (*-Lu-*)**: Likely descended from *Ndu-*, it is commonly *Lu-*, though it may be pronounced *Du-* or *Ru-* in archaic dialects or become *U-*.
*   **Other Prefixes**: The 4th prefix *Mi-* has a concord of *-i-*, *-gi-*, *-ji-*, or *-mi-*. The 5th prefix, most commonly *Li-* (originally *Di-*), may become *Ni-*, *Ji-*, *I-*, or *Ri-*.

## Additional and Specialized Prefixes
Beyond the sixteen common prefixes, other specialized forms exist:
*   **Diminutive**: Prefix No. 17 (*Fi-* or *Vi-*) is used in the singular for diminutive sense in some north-western Bantu tongues and the northern Congo basin. Its plural is No. 12 (*Tu-*).
*   **Augmentative**: Prefix No. 18 (*Ogu-*) and its plural No. 19 (*Aga-*) are used for augmentative sense, primarily in Masaba and Luganda dialects, and possibly some Unyoro branches.
*   **Prepositional**: The 20th prefix, *Mu-*, is a preposition meaning "into" or "in." It is often combined with the suffix *-ni-*. Unlike other prefixes, it lacks a complete concord and has no pronominal accusative.

## Gender and Classification
The Bantu pronominal system does not recognize sexual gender in its pronouns or concord. Sexual gender is instead indicated by specific prefixes or suffixes. A male prefix often resembles a word for "father," while a feminine prefix (*na* or *nya*) relates to "mother," and suffixes like *kazi* or *ka* indicate "female" or "wife." Additionally, the 1st and 2nd prefixes are generally restricted to humanity and living beings.
