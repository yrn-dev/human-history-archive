# Fitton Bay and Walker Bay

Fitton Bay and Walker Bay are geographic features located within the Bonin Islands, a chain of small islands belonging to Japan. These islands, known in Japanese as Ogasawara-jima, stretch nearly due north and south between 26 deg. 35' and 27 deg. 45' N., situated a little east of 142 E. and approximately 500 miles from the Japanese mainland.

## Geographic Context
The Bonin Islands consist of twenty islands according to Japanese investigations, covering a superficies of 28.82 sq. m. with a total coastline of 174.65 m. Of these, ten islands possess appreciable size. These are, from north to south: Muko-shima (Bridegroom Island), Nakadachi-shima (Go-between Island), Yome-shima (Bride Island), Ototo-jima (Younger-brother Island), Ani-shima (Elder-brother Island), Chichi-jima (Father Island), Haha-jima (Mother Island), Mei-jima (Niece Island), Ani-jima (Elder-sister Island), and Imoto-jima (Younger-sister Island).

European geographers have traditionally categorized these islands into three distinct groups: the Parry Islands in the north, the Beechey Islands in the center, and the Coffin or Bailey Islands in the south.

## Nomenclature and Location
The names Fitton Bay and Walker Bay were established by Captain Beechey. In 1827, Beechey applied European names to several of the islands; he named Chichi-jima "Peel Island," Ototo-jima "Stapleton Island," and Ani-shima "Buckland Island."

As part of this nomenclature:
*   **Fitton Bay** is a large bay located on the south of Peel Island (Chichi-jima).
*   **Walker Bay** is a bay located on the south-west of Buckland Island (Ani-shima).

## Geological Nature
The islands of this chain are plainly volcanic in nature. This is exemplified by Port Lloyd, the chief anchorage on Peel Island. Commodore Perry, who visited the islands in 1853, observed that Port Lloyd appeared to have formerly been the crater of a volcano. He noted that the surrounding hills were thrown up from this crater and that the entrance to the harbour was a fissure through which lava once poured into the sea.
