# Starozitnosti Slovanske

*Starozitnosti Slovanske* (Slavic antiquities) is a scholarly work authored by Paul Joseph Safarik (1795–1861). Safarik was a prolific writer and one of four key figures—alongside Jungmann, Kolar, and Palacky—credited with the revival of the literature and language of Bohemia.

## Purpose and Scope
The primary objective of *Starozitnosti Slovanske* was to record the history and literature of the early Slavs, subjects which, at the time of the work's production, were almost unknown. Despite the age of the text, it is noted to still possess considerable value.

## Theories on Early Slavic Tribes
In his research, Safarik addressed the origins and identities of various ancient groups. Specifically, he challenged the views of Mannert, who believed the Budini to be a Teutonic tribe. Safarik provided more plausible grounds to argue that both the Budini and the Neuri belonged to the Slavic family. 

To support this conclusion, Safarik pointed to several pieces of evidence:
* **Etymology:** The names Neuri and Budini can be traced back to Slavic roots.
* **Urban Structure:** The wooden town described by Herodotus among the Budini serves as an exact parallel to primitive Slavic towns, a style of settlement that persisted into the twelfth century.
* **Geography:** The description of the surrounding environment—characterized by marshes and woods inhabited by otters and beavers—aligns more closely with the geography of Russia and southern Poland than with the neighborhood of the Ural mountains.
* **Physical Description:** While Mannert interpreted the color ascribed to the Budini as evidence of a Teutonic family, Safarik interpreted it in favor of a Slavic family.

## Historical Context of the Slavic Presence in Bohemia
The broader historical context of the Slavic people in Bohemia, as recorded in the archive, indicates that while the land was originally inhabited by the Celtic Boii and later the Teutonic Marcomanni, it was eventually conquered by Slavic tribes. The Cechs were the most important of these tribes. 

Although classical and Byzantine references are considered misleading, archaeological research has confirmed that Slavic inhabitants existed in Bohemia as early as the beginning of the Christian era. The Cechs are believed to have become the masters of the region in the 5th century. Early recorded Slavic leadership includes Samo, who defeated the Franks at Wogatisburg in 630 and overcame the Avars, a Turanian tribe. Later semi-mythic history attributes the foundation of a Bohemian principality to Krok and his daughter Libussa, who married a peasant named Premysl.
