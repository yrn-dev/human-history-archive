## Beltane Fires and the Connection to Baal
The festival of Beltane (also known as Beltene, Beltine, or Beal-tene) is a Celtic May-day celebration originally common to all Celtic peoples, with remaining traces found in Brittany, the Highlands of Scotland, and Ireland. The central ceremony of the festival in later centuries involved the lighting of "beltane fires," which are believed to represent the worship of the sun-god by Druids. 

Customs associated with these fires included:
* **The Beltane Cake:** A cake cooked at the fire and divided among those present. One piece was blackened with charcoal; the person who drew this piece became the *cailleach bealtine* (the beltane carline), a position of great reproach involving being pelted with egg-shells and being spoken of as dead for several weeks.
* **Health Rituals:** According to the 9th-century glossary of Cormac, archbishop of Cashel, it was customary to light two fires close together and drive cattle and men between them to ward off disease and promote health.
* **Agricultural Practices:** In Strathspey, sheep and lambs were driven through a hoop of rowan wood at sunset and dawn on Beltane day.

Historically, some theories suggested a connection between these fires and the Semitic deity Baal, based on the etymology of the word "beltane" as a combination of *teine* (fire) and the name of the god Bel, Bil, or Baal. However, scientific philologists have since repudiated this etymology. The *New English Dictionary* supports the view of Dr. Whitley Stokes that the Gaelic form of "beltane" has no connection to *teine*. Regarding the apparent points of connection between Baal and Beltane fires, Aug. Fick's *Vergleich. Worterbuch* suggests that the element *bel* actually derives from an old Celtic root meaning "shining."
