# Exploration and Geography of British East Africa

## Early Coastal Settlements and Political History
From the 8th century to the 11th century, Persians and Arabs established settlements along the coast. These groups achieved political supremacy in various locations, which led to the creation of the Zenj empire. Following this period, the history of the coastal towns was identified with that of Zanzibar until the eventual establishment of British rule.

## Exploration of the Interior
The interior regions of what is now British East Africa were first brought to light during the middle of the 19th century. This was achieved through the efforts of others, including Baron Karl von der Decken (1833-1865) and German missionaries Johannes Rebmann and Ludwig Krapf. 

Exploration of the Juba river in October 1865 resulted in the murder of Baron von der Decken and three other Europeans by Somali at a town known as Bardera. Later, in 1883-1884, the Scottish traveller Joseph Thomson became the first to traverse the entire extent of the countries located east of Victoria Nyanza, including Masailand. Further discoveries occurred in 1888 when Count S. Teleki, a Hungarian, discovered Lakes Stefanie and Rudolf.

## Education and Infrastructure
Education in the region is primarily managed by missionary societies. These societies maintain numerous schools that provide instruction in elementary education as well as handicrafts. In Nairobi, there are government schools designated for Indians and Europeans, while Arab schools are located in Mombasa.
