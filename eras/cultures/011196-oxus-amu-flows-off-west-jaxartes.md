# The Oxus and Jaxartes River Systems

The Oxus (also known as the Amu or Amu-darya) and the Jaxartes (also known as the Syr or Syr-Darya) are two rivers of ancient and modern renown that descend from the mountains of India toward the Caspian Sea. They serve as primary geographical markers for the region of Central Asia, flowing from the elevated plateaus of the east toward the west and north.

## Geographic Origins and the Pamir Plateau
The north-western extremity of the elevated Tibeto-Himalayan mountain plateau, situated at approximately 73 deg. E. and 39 deg. N., is known as the Pamir. This region possesses the characteristics of the highest regions of Tibet and is designated by Russians as a steppe. The Pamir plateau is described as a broad, rounded ridge extending north and south, characterized by thick mountain chains and elevated valleys. These valleys slope gently toward the east but are narrow and confined with a rapid fall toward the west.

The Pamir serves as a critical watershed. Most waters in this region collect in the Oxus, including the Múrgháb—which receives drainage from the Síríz and Alichór Pámírs—and the Áksú from the Little Pámír lake. Conversely, the eastern flow from the Tághdúngbásh stream leads into the Yarkand river. From the Pamir, the Oxus flows westward, while the Jaxartes flows northward through the Turki state of Khokand.

## The Oxus (Amu-darya)
The Oxus is the chief river of Bokhara, acting as a boundary that separates Bokhara from Afghanistan to the south before flowing along its south-west border. The river is navigable from the mouth of the Surkhan, with steamboats operating as far as Karki near the Afghan frontier.

In the region of Badakshan, the Oxus forms a great bend northwards. Near this bend, the Kokcha river—which drains the northern slopes of the Hindu Kush—intersects the area. The mountainous district of Darwaz, a small vassal state of Bokhara with a population of about 35,000 Moslem Tajiks and Galchas, is situated on the Panj where it makes a sharp westward bend.

Further downstream, the Oxus reaches the khanate of Khiva, a reduced state dependent upon Russia. Khiva is confined to the delta of the Amu-darya. The river's water is distributed via irrigation canals to the Khiva oasis, which covers 5,210 sq. m. The deposits of ooze and the river water render this oasis highly fertile, supporting the growth of cotton, vine, rice, wheat, barley, millet, flax, hemp, madder, and various fruits and vegetables.

## The Jaxartes (Syr-Darya) and Surrounding Plains
The Jaxartes flows north from the Pamir region. Historically, it passed through Fargana, described as a fertile province. The area between the northern border of the Persian highlands and the Caspian and Aral Seas is a nearly desert, low-lying plain. This plain extends to the foot of the Tibeto-Himalayan mountains and is prolonged eastward along the valleys of both the Oxus and the Jaxartes.

This tract is largely inhabited by nomadic tribes and includes Turkestan proper, Khiva, and Bokhara, where Uzbeg Turks are dominant. Because rainfall is insufficient for agriculture, the fixed population is small and restricted to towns and districts near the rivers where irrigation is possible.

## Regional Populations and Trade
The uplands draining toward the Oxus and Jaxartes are inhabited by the Kara-Kirghiz, a nomadic people exceeding 800,000 in number. They are divided into two sections: the "Right" (On) in the east and the "Left" (Sol) in the west. The Sol section occupies the region between the Oxus headstreams and the Talass in Bokhara and Ferghana (Khokand), where they encounter the Highland Tajiks (Galchas). The Kara-Kirghiz are primarily stock breeders, raising fat-tailed sheep, camels, goats, and a hardy breed of horses.

Historically, the region served as a vital corridor for movement and trade. A significant continental high-road connected Afghan Turkestan with Kashgar and China, running from the Oxus bend through Ishkashim, the Panja, and the Pamirs.

## Historical Significance
The rivers have been central to the movements of ancient empires. Yezdegerd, the last Sassanian king, fled beyond the Oxus as far as the Jaxartes, where he was entertained by Takhan, the prince of Fargana. During this period, the Chinese dynasty of the Tang, under the emperor Taitsong, maintained garrisons at Khoten and Cashgar, which kept frequent intercourse with the neighbors of the Oxus and Jaxartes. Additionally, the Saccæ, a nation of wandering Scythians, were known to encamp near the sources of both the Oxus and the Jaxartes.
