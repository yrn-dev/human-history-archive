# The Second Prefix in Bantu Languages

In the study of Bantu languages, the governing of unchangeable roots by prefixes is a leading feature. Among these, the second prefix—appearing as *Ba-* or *Aba-*—is a primary element used to indicate plural forms.

## Archaic Forms and Development
In the most archaic Bantu speech, specifically the languages of Mount Elgon, the second prefix appears as *Baba-* in its definite form. A similar form, *Ngaba*, is sometimes found in Zulu-Kaffir. It is possible that this prefix originated from *nga-ba-ntu* (which later became *na-ba-* or *aba-*), and the form *ngabantu* is explicitly met with in Zulu-Kaffir. Another theory suggests that *ba-* may have originally meant "two" (derived from the Bantu root *Bali*), serving as a dual form before becoming a plural.

In the less corrupt Bantu tongues, the concord for this prefix is *-ba-*. However, as various dialects became more "corrupt," this plural prefix degenerated into several different forms, including *Va-*, *Wa-*, *Ma-*, and *A-*. For example, in Luganda, the form *Wa-* is used, while in Lusoga (spoken on the north coast of Victoria Nyanza), *Wa-* becomes *[Gamma]a* (or *Gha*).

## Usage and Meaning
The first and second prefixes in Bantu languages are usually restricted to humanity and invariably indicate living beings. The term *Bantu* itself (literally *Ba-ntu*) is the most archaic and widely spread term for "people," "mankind," or "men." 

The meaning of the root is modified by the prefix applied to it. For instance, in purer forms of the language, the root *-ntu* (which originally meant "person" or "object") becomes *muntu* meaning "a man" when the first prefix is used, and *bantu* meaning "men" when the second prefix is applied.

## Concord and Syntax
The Bantu languages employ a system of concord, which is the repetition of pronominal particles. This is illustrated in the reconstruction of the original Bantu mother-tongue. In a hypothetical Old Bantu sentence, *Babo mbaba-ntu ba bi ba-bo-ta tu-ba-oga* translates to "They these-they person they bad they who kill we fear them." When rendered into the modern Luganda dialect, this becomes *Bo aba-ntu ba-bi ba-bota tu-ba-tia* ("They are bad people who kill; we fear them").

## Geographical Distribution of Archaic Dialects
The most archaic dialects and typical structures of Bantu speech are widely spread. The languages of Mount Elgon are noted as being among the most archaic at present. Other areas preserving archaic forms include:
* Ruwenzori, Unyoro, and Uganda.
* The north coast of Tanganyika and the Bemba country to the south-west of Tanganyika.
* The vicinity of Lake Bangweulu.
* The Nkonde and Kese dialects of the north and north-east coasts of Lake Nyasa.
* The Subiya speech of the western Zambezi.
* The Ci-bodzo of the Zambezi delta.

Additionally, Oci-herero of Damaraland contains many original Bantu roots and typical features, and Kimakonde on the east coast of Africa is considered a primitive Bantu tongue. While Ki-swahili of Zanzibar is primitive in its roots, it is not so in its prefixes.

Historically, Zulu-Kaffir was regarded as the most archaic type of Bantu speech until the languages of the Great Lakes were discovered; it is now deposed from that position as it is unusually divergent from the typical Bantu in some features and exhibits recent deviations in phonetics and vocabulary.
