# Sumatra and Cape Negrais

Sumatra and Cape Negrais are significant geographic landmarks in Southeast Asia, situated near the Bay of Bengal and the Indian Ocean. Cape Negrais serves as the south-western extremity of Burma, while Sumatra is a major island within the Malay Archipelago.

## Geographic Relationship and the Bay of Bengal
Cape Negrais and the northern end of Sumatra define a line across the Indian Ocean. The Andaman and Nicobar island groups are positioned along this line. It is suggested that these island groups may owe their existence to volcanic agencies that extend from Sumatra across this region of the ocean.

## Cape Negrais and the Geography of Burma
Cape Negrais is a pivotal point in the geography of Burma, a province of British India. It marks the southern extent of the Arakan division, a strip of country located on the seaward slopes of the Arakan Yomas mountain range. From Cape Negrais, the Arakan Yomas extend northwards, running more or less parallel to the coast until they join the Naga and Chin hills.

The Arakan division stretches from Cape Negrais in the south to the Naaf estuary in the north, which separates the region from the Chittagong division of Eastern Bengal and Assam. This area covers approximately 18,540 square miles. While the northern sections of this tract consist of barren hilly country, the southern and western portions feature rich alluvial plains containing some of the province's most fertile lands.

## Sumatra and the Malay Archipelago
Sumatra is one of the islands occupied by the Malays, a widely-scattered seafaring race. The distribution of the Malays across Sumatra, Java, Borneo, the Philippines, and the extremity of the Malay Peninsula suggests a history of exceptional dispersal from an island center, possibly Java.

The history of the Asiatic Malays in regions including Sumatra is characterized by three distinct periods:
1. A semi-barbarous first period.
2. A second period marked by the arrival of Hindu civilization, evidenced by the presence of Indian customs, numerous Indian words, and Hindu ruins.
3. A third period in which Hindu influence was superseded by Islam, which spread to the Malay Peninsula and the Malay Archipelago before the 16th century.

While the Arabic alphabet is used on the mainland, Indian alphabets continue to be used in Sumatra and Java.

## Regional Context
Burma, where Cape Negrais is located, is bounded to the south by the Bay of Bengal and the Siamese Malay States. The province extends from the Pakchan river in the south to the Kachin and Naga hills in the north. The total area of the province is estimated at 238,738 square miles, with Burma proper occupying 168,573 square miles.

Sumatra is part of a broader Asian landmass that, when viewed as a whole, resembles a great isosceles spherical triangle. One of the equal sides of this triangle extends from the north-eastern apex at East Cape in the Bering Strait to the extremity of the Malay peninsula.
