# Victoria Nyanza and the Uganda-Unyoro Group

The Victoria Nyanza (Lake Victoria) serves as a central geographical and linguistic landmark in East Africa. Its surrounding regions are characterized by a complex distribution of Bantu languages and a history of administrative shifts between protectorates.

## The Uganda-Unyoro Linguistic Group
The Uganda-Unyoro group encompasses a wide range of dialects across the equatorial lands. Geographically, this group is bounded to the north and east by Busoga and the Victoria Nile, to the west by the Congo Forest and the Ruwenzori range, and to the east coast of Lake Albert. Its southern boundaries extend from the south coast of the Victoria Nyanza to a line running from near the Emin Pasha Gulf to the Malagarazi river and the east coast of Tanganyika. To the south-west, the district is more or less bounded by the Rusizi river leading down to Tanganyika.

This group includes the district of Busoga in the north-east and all inhabited islands and archipelagoes of the Victoria Nyanza, extending as far east as Bukerebe, with the exception of islands located near the north-east coast. The languages of the west coast of Lake Victoria, the Sese Islands, and Busoga are closely related to the language of the kingdom of Uganda.

## Sub-sections of the Uganda-Unyoro Group
Within this broader group, there are distinct sub-sections:

*   **Unyoro:** This sub-section is allied to, but distinct from, the Uganda subjection. It includes dialects such as *Ru-toro*, *Ru-tusi*, *Ru-iro*, *Ru-songora*, and *Uru-hima* or *Ru-hinda* (the latter spoken by the Hima, described as the Hamitic aristocracy of these lands). It also encompasses kindred dialects of Bukerebe, Ruanda, Busiba, and Karagwe. Additionally, the languages of eastern Tanganyika down to Ufipa and *Ki-rundi* (from the Burundi country at the north end of Tanganyika) are closely allied to the Unyoro sub-section, though *Ki-rundi* may adhere more closely to group 12.
*   **Lu-konjo:** This independent sub-section is spoken on the southern flanks of the Ruwenzori Range, extending southwards to Lake Kivu and the eastern limits of the Congo Forest.

## Related Linguistic Groups
Other Bantu groups exist in proximity to the Victoria Nyanza. The Kavirondo-Masaba section includes *Lukonde*, *Luwanga*, *Lu-nyara*, and *Igizii*. These are spoken in the eastern and north-east shores of the Victoria Nyanza, as well as the Mount Elgon and northern Kavirondo territories. While this group is related to the Luganda section more than any other Bantu tongue, it remains a distinct division with highly archaic prefixes.

To the west of Ugogo, the Nyamwezi group includes dialects that extend as far north as the Victoria Nyanza, where they merge into the Uganda-Unyoro group.

## Regional Administration and Infrastructure
The areas adjoining the Victoria Nyanza have undergone administrative changes. The provinces of Kisumu and Naivasha, both of which adjoin the lake, were originally part of the eastern province of Uganda. However, on April 1, 1902, they were transferred to the East Africa protectorate.

Transport to the Victoria Nyanza is facilitated by the Uganda Railway, which connects the lake to the Indian Ocean port of Mombasa. In the broader region, the Nile is navigable up to Lake Albert, though the connection between Lake Albert and the Central and South African systems remains a logistical problem.

## Historical Population Movements
Study of Bantu languages suggests that approximately 3,000 years ago, a powerful tribe of negroes speaking the Bantu mother-language—physically allied to the negroes of the southern Lake Chad basins and the south-western Nile, but impregnated with the Caucasian Hamite—migrated from the heart of Africa. This group pushed into the southern half of the continent, assimilating or absorbing existing populations. Prior to this Bantu invasion, the eastern coasts of the Victoria Nyanza and the East African coast region down to opposite Zanzibar were likely populated by Hottentot-Bushman and Nilotic-negro peoples.
