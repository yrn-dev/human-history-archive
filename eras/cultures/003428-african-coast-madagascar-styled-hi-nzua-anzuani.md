# African Coast and Madagascar

The regions encompassing the East African coast and the island of Madagascar are characterized by a diverse array of linguistic groups, unique biological fauna, and a history of migration and cultural development.

## Linguistic Groups and Migrations
The Bantu peoples, whose legends and traditions indicate a northern origin, migrated down the east coast of Africa. During this migration, they expanded their reach by sea, occupying the islands of Zanzibar and Pemba. Their maritime travels extended further to the Comoro archipelago and the west coast of Madagascar. This invasion of Madagascar was considerable in number and is credited with the origin of the race of black people known traditionally to the Hovas as the Va-zimba.

In the Comoro Islands, located between Madagascar and the East African coast, the dialects are referred to as *Shi-ngazija* and *Hi-nzua* (or *Anzuani*). These dialects maintain a close relationship with Swahili. Along the coast between Ibo and Lindi, and the lower Ruvuma, the archaic *Mabiha* or *Makonde* are spoken, a group that may be attached to the Swahili branch. Further inland in German East Africa, the *Kaguru-Sagala-Kami* group occupies the territory between the Nyamwezi domain to the west and the Swahili coast dialects to the east. This inland group, which includes *Irangi* and *Kigogo*, is bounded to the south by the Ruaha river and to the north by the non-Bantu languages of the Taturu, Mbugu, and Masai.

## Biological and Regional Classification
From a zoological perspective, Africa and Madagascar are part of the "palaeotropical" region, a term adopted from Oscar Drude. Specifically, they comprise the Ethiopian Subregion, which includes all of Africa (excluding the Barbary States) and Madagascar, as well as Arabia. This subregion is naturally divided into two provinces: the Madagascar province and the African province.

The fauna of this region is notably rich due to the large portion of the Ethiopian subregion lying between the tropics. Among the characteristic birds are the *Ratitae*, specifically *Struthio* in Arabia and Africa and *Aepyornithidae* in Madagascar. Other characteristic birds include *Upupinae* (with *Upupa* found in Africa, Madagascar, and India), *Bucerotinae*, and *Pittidae*. The region hosts between fifty and sixty families of land birds, at least nine of which are peculiar. Typical genera of these peculiar birds include *Euryceros*, *Buphaga*, *Musophaga*, *Colius*, *Irrisor*, *Leptosoma*, *Serpentarius*, *Aepyornis*, and *Struthio*. Only *Philepitta*, *Euryceros*, and *Buphaga* belong to the order *Passeriformes*.

Other notable animals include the *Nandinia* (African spotted palm-civets), with *N. gerrardi* found in the eastern forest-region. In Madagascar, the *Eupleres goudoti* is a medium-sized, uniformly-coloured creature. Generally classed in the *Herpestinae*, it is characterized by reduced dentition resulting from insectivorous habits.

## Cultural Practices in Madagascar
Cultural observances in Madagascar include the Fandroana, or New Year's Festival, during which the queen and her guests eat a mixture of honey and rice. Honey also plays a role in religious rites, specifically as an ingredient in the sacred water of sprinkling used to bless children before they undergo circumcision.

## Geography and Geopolitics
The African coast is marked by significant maritime passages, such as the Bab-el-Mandeb (the "Gate of Tears"), the strait connecting the Indian Ocean with the Red Sea. This strait separates Africa from Arabia, stretching approximately 20 miles from Ras Siyan on the African coast to Ras Menheli on the Arabian coast. The British-possessed island of Perim divides this strait into two channels: the western Dact-el-Mayun and the eastern Bab Iskender. Near the African coast of this strait lies a group of islands called the "Seven Brothers."

In terms of geopolitical ambitions, historical records indicate German interests in uniting various territories under a single ownership to create a colonial empire. These desired territories included Madagascar, Zanzibar, German East Africa, Uganda, Mozambique, and Delagoa Bay, among others. Such a union of territories, totaling over 100,000,000 inhabitants, was viewed as offering immeasurable prospects for trade, settlement, and natural treasures.
