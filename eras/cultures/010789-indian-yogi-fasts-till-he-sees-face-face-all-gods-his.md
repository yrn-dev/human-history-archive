# Ecstatic Fasting and the Indian Yogi

## The Practice of Ecstatic Fasting
Fasting is recognized globally as a method for overcoming, consulting, or evoking the spirit world. One widespread reason for this practice, common among savages, is that famished individuals are prone to morbid excitement. This state can lead to imaginative ecstasies, during which the person may experience supernatural revelations, converse with angels and gods, or see spectres and visions.

Because of these associations, certain cultures place high value on asceticism. For example, the Zulus and other primitive races tend to distrust medicine men who are not lean from fasting. In the Semitic East, there is an old belief that fasting in the wilderness for forty days and nights grants a person power over the Djinns.

## Fasting in India
In India, fasting is utilized for both spiritual visions and practical magic. The Indian *yogi* engages in fasting to the extent that he sees all the gods of his Pantheon face to face. Additionally, the Indian magician practices fasting for a period of twelve days before attempting to work a cure or produce rain.

## Comparative Global Examples of Visionary Fasting
The pursuit of face-to-face visions through fasting is observed in various other traditions:
*   **North American Indians:** Ecstatic fasting is practiced regularly. The faster records his revelations and visions for an entire season. These records are then reviewed by tribal elders; if the events are verified, the individual is rewarded with chieftaincy and recognized as a supernaturally gifted being.
*   **The Bogomils:** This group fasted until they saw the Trinity face to face.
*   **Early Christian Traditions:** Fasting was practiced in the church for similar reasons. In the *Shepherd of Hermas*, prayer and frequent fasts are rewarded with a vision of the church. Extra-canonical sources state that James the Less vowed to fast until he was granted a vision of the risen Lord, which appeared to him after a rigorous and long fast. Similarly, some saints were granted glimpses of the beatific vision as a reward for fasting.

Dr. Tylor has noted that the act of eating bread and meat could have prevented these experiences, suggesting that opening the refectory door may have closed the gates of heaven to the gaze of the ascetic.

## Other Instances of Supernatural Visions
While fasting is a primary driver for these experiences, visions are recorded in other contexts. For instance, Titus Latinus, a respectable Roman citizen, dreamed he saw Jupiter face to face. In this vision, the god commanded him to inform the Senate that they had sent a dancer before his procession who was very displeasing and "bad."
