# The Region Between the Northern Persian Highlands and the Caspian and Aral Seas

## Geography and Topography
The area situated between the Caspian and Aral Seas and the northern border of the Persian highlands is characterized as a nearly desert, low-lying plain. This region extends eastward through the valleys of the Jaxartes (Syr-Darya) and the Oxus (Amu Darya), and continues northward across the country of the Kirghiz until it reaches the south western border of Siberia. To the west, the plain reaches the foot of the north-western extremity of the great Tibeto-Himalayan mountains.

## Climate and Agriculture
The tract is marked by a lack of rainfall sufficient for agricultural purposes. Consequently, agriculture is only possible through the use of irrigation. Due to these environmental constraints, the fixed population is comparatively small and is restricted primarily to the districts near the rivers and within the towns.

## Population and Demographics
The region is largely inhabited by nomadic tribes, who possess a reputation for being marauders and are described as the worst among a race of professed robbers. However, the tribes located to the north, who are subject to Russia, are naturally more peaceable and have been brought into some degree of discipline. 

The area includes Turkestan proper, as well as Khiva and Bokhara. In Turkestan proper, the Uzbeg Turks are the dominant group, although the fixed population of the region also contains a non-Turkish element.

## Regional Context
This plain sits north of an extensive plateau that covers an area as large as Tibet, though at a lower altitude. This plateau includes Iran (Persia), Afghanistan, Baluchistan, Armenia, and the provinces of Asia Minor. The northern border of this highland region follows nearly 36 deg. N. to the southern shore of the Caspian Sea. 

To the northeast, the Afghan table-land abuts on Tibet and the Himalaya. An unbroken range of mountains stretches from the intersection of the 36th parallel and the 71st meridian westward to the Caspian. The northeastern portion of this range is of great altitude and serves as a divide between the headwaters of the Indus and its Kabul tributary (which flow south into the Arabian Sea) and the headwaters of the Oxus, which run off toward the Aral Sea.
