# The Tshi-speaking Peoples and Colonial Administration

## Ethnology of the Tshi-speaking Peoples
The ethnology of the Tshi-speaking peoples of the Gold Coast is detailed in the work *The Tshi-speaking Peoples of the Gold Coast*, published in London in 1887 by Col. A.B. Ellis.

## Colonial Reporting and Administration
From 1906 onward, the Colonial Office in London issued Annual Reports regarding Ashanti. These reports serve as a resource for those seeking information on the region. Similarly, the British Colonial Office in London provided annual reports containing all statistical matter relating to the colony of British Honduras.

## Related Regional Documentation
Various other works provide historical and geographical context for the West African region. For a general survey of the country, resources include *Travels in Ashanti and Jaman* (1898) by R.A. Freeman and *Historical Geography of the British Colonies*, vol. iii. "West Africa" (1900) by C.P. Lucas. 

Early valuable works on the region include T.E. Bowdich's *A Mission from Cape Coast Castle to Ashantee* (1819) and J. Dupuis's *Journal of a Residence in Ashantee* (1824). General history of the Gold Coast of West Africa is covered by Col. A.B. Ellis in his 1893 publication, as well as by C.C. Reindorf, a native pastor of the Basel mission, in *History of the Gold Coast and Asante ... from about 1500 to 1860* (1895).
