# Gazaland, Lower Limpopo Valley, and North Transvaal

The regions of Gazaland, the Lower Limpopo valley, and specific patches of the North Transvaal (specifically *Shi-gwamba*) are characterized by their linguistic affiliations and their proximity to the geographical features of south-eastern Africa.

## Linguistic Characteristics
The languages spoken in Gazaland, the Lower Limpopo valley, Delagoa Bay, and patches of the North Transvaal are categorized as *Ronga* (*Tonga*) languages. These languages are found within Portuguese South-East Africa. Linguistically, the *Ronga* group is almost equally related to the *Nyanja* group and to *Zulu*. It is believed that these languages represent a mingling of these two influences, though the influence of *Zulu* is the predominating factor.

## Regional Geography and Hydrography
The broader region is defined by significant mountain ranges and river systems. The Drakensberg, known to natives as *Quathlamba* or *Kahlamba* ("heaped up and jagged"), is a mountain chain in south-eastern Africa that runs parallel to the coast for approximately 600 miles, extending from Basutoland to the Limpopo river. This range forms the eastern edge of South Africa's inner tableland.

In the north, the Drakensberg enters the Transvaal, where elevations are generally lower than in the south, although the Mauch Berg reaches approximately 8,500 feet. The range eventually joins the Zoutpansberg at its northernmost point.

The Komati river is a primary waterway of south-eastern Africa, rising in the Ermelo district of the Transvaal at an elevation of about 5,000 feet. It flows in a general north and east direction for some 500 miles before reaching the Indian Ocean at Delagoa Bay. The river descends the Drakensberg via a pass located 30 miles south of Barberton. At the eastern border of Swaziland, the Komati is deflected northward, running parallel to the Lebombo mountains.

## Tributaries and Drainage
The Komati is joined by a tributary also named the Crocodile (which rises as the Elands river in the Bergendal). After their junction, the combined stream is known as the Manhissa. This river passes through Komati Poort—a 626-foot-high cleft in the Lebombo mountains that marks the frontier between British and Portuguese territory—before entering the sea 15 miles north of Lourenço Marques. During periods of flood, there is a northward connection through swamps that links the Komati to the basin of the Limpopo.

## Related Transvaal Districts
Within the Transvaal, the district of Lydenburg is situated on the Spekboom tributary of the Olifants river at an altitude of 4,900 feet. The Mauchberg, the highest point in the Transvaal at 8,725 feet, is located some 15 miles east of the town. The region also includes the Lulu Mountains, a spur of the Drakensberg located approximately 40 miles northwest of Lydenburg.
