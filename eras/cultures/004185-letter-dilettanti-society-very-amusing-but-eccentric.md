# Letter to the Dilettanti Society

The *Letter to the Dilettanti Society* is a publication authored by Barry, a painter and former professor of painting. The work is characterized as an eccentric and amusing publication that expresses a strong enthusiasm for Barry's art while simultaneously conveying contempt for the professors of art who were living at the time.

## Context and Authorship
The author, Barry, had been chosen as a member of the Royal Academy following his return from the continent. In 1782, he was appointed as a professor of painting, succeeding Mr. Penny, with an annual salary of L30. During his tenure, Barry advocated for the purchase of a collection of pictures by the best masters to serve as models for students, specifically proposing several works from the Orleans collection. This recommendation was not well-received.

## Impact and Aftermath
The appearance of the *Letter to the Dilettanti Society* occurred shortly before Barry's expulsion from the Royal Academy in 1799. The loss of his professorship and subsequent salary led to financial difficulties. To provide relief, the earl of Buchan organized a subscription that raised L1000. These funds were used to purchase an annuity and to settle Barry in a larger house, which allowed him to finish his painting of Pandora.

Barry's later life was marked by illness, which seized him on February 6, 1806. He died on February 22 of that same year and was interred in St Paul's on March 14.

## The Dilettanti Society
While the *Letter* was a specific publication by Barry, the Dilettanti Society is also noted in archival records for its contributions to archaeology and history. The society is credited with the publication of *Ionian Antiquities*, produced between 1769 and 1840.
