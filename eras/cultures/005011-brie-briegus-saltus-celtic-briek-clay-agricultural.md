# BRIE

Brie, also referred to as *Briegus saltus* (derived from the Celtic word *briek*, meaning clay), is an agricultural district located in northern France. It is situated to the east of Paris and is defined by specific geographic boundaries: the Seine river bounds the district to the west and south, while the Marne river bounds it to the north.

## Geography and Administration
The district covers an area of 2,400 square miles. Administratively, it comprises the majority of the department of Seine-et-Marne, along with portions of several other departments, including Aube, Aisne, Marne, Seine, and Seine-et-Oise. The region is divided into two primary portions: the *Brie champenoise* in the east and the *Brie française* in the west.

The topography of the Brie consists of a plateau with few eminences. The altitude varies depending on the region, ranging between 300 and 500 feet in the western portion and between 500 and 650 feet in the eastern portion. The landscape is varied by the presence of forests of some size, the most prominent being the Forêt d'Armainvilliers, the Forêt de Crécy, and the Forêt de Senart.

## Hydrography and Climate
The region is not abundantly watered, and its rainfall is limited to between 20 and 24 inches. The primary rivers serving the district are the Yères, which is a tributary of the Seine, as well as the Petit Morin and Grand Morin, which are tributaries of the Marne.

## Geology and Soil
The subsoil of the Brie is composed of limestone. The surface soil consists of clay containing embedded fragments of siliceous sandstone. These sandstone fragments are utilized for constructional purposes and for the creation of millstones.

## Agriculture and Production
The Brie is renowned for its dairy products, specifically its cheeses, as well as its production of grain.
