# Fermentation Processes in Beverage and Food Production

Fermentation is a biological process utilized in the production of various alcoholic beverages and preserved foods. Depending on the substance being produced, the process may involve single or symbiotic organisms, varying temperatures, and multiple stages of development.

## Production of Saké
The preparation of the Japanese beer known as *saké* involves a three-stage process. The first stage is the creation of *Koji*, which is produced by treating steamed rice with a culture of *Aspergillus oryzae*. This specific mould is capable of performing the combined functions of fermentation and saccharification, replacing the yeast and diastase of malted grain used in European breweries. 

In the second stage, *Koji* is added to a vat containing a thin paste of fresh-boiled starch to create *moto*. At this point, fermentation is set up and lasts for a period of 30 to 40 days. The third stage consists of adding water, more *Koji*, and additional rice to the *moto*. This triggers a secondary fermentation that lasts between 8 and 10 days. The final product is filtered, heated, and run into casks to become *saké*.

## Beer Fermentation Systems
Brewing processes vary significantly by region and method, particularly between the English system and the "bottom fermentation" system used for lager-type beers.

### Bottom Fermentation and Lager
In the bottom fermentation system, the "pitching" and fermentation occur in small vessels at very low temperatures. Fermenting cellars are maintained at approximately 37-38° F, and the wort temperature does not exceed 50° F. The yeast remains at the bottom of the tun. Primary fermentation in this system lasts about eleven to twelve days.

Modern lager brewing, such as the process introduced by Messrs Allsopp in Great Britain, utilizes huge glass-enamel coated sheet-steel cylindrical vessels. The wort ferments under reduced pressure while a vacuum pump removes generated carbonic acid, which is replaced by cool sterilized air. In this specific system, fermentation is finished in about six days. Following this, the air supply is cut off, the green beer is cooled to 40° F, and it is moved to store tanks for a secondary fermentation lasting three weeks.

### Other Beer Varieties
*Weissbier*, largely produced in Berlin, is prepared from a mash of one part barley malt and three parts wheat malt. Its fermentation is symbiotic, involving both a fission fungus (*lactic acid bacillus*) and a yeast. This process is assisted by adding white wine to the yeast before fermentation begins.

Different systems for managing the fermentation and cleansing of beer include:
*   **Skimming System:** Fermentation occurs in wooden "squares" or "rounds" using a parachute or skimming device to remove yeast.
*   **Dropping System:** Main fermentation occurs in a "round" or "square," after which the beer is dropped into second vessels (such as "backs" or "pontos") for cleansing and completion.
*   **Burton Union System:** An improved ponto system using rotating casks with swan neck fittings and attemperators, often used to achieve the "Burton character."

## Other Fermented Products
Fermentation is also employed in the production of milk-based beverages and preserved vegetables.

### Milk-Wine and Kefir
*Koumiss* is a fermented alcoholic beverage made from camels' or mares' milk. The fermentation is initiated by adding a small amount of a finished product to fresh milk. This symbiotic process involves a true yeast and a fission fungus, converting milk sugar into alcohol and lactic acid. *Kefir* is a similar beverage usually made from cows' milk, where fermentation is brought about by "Kefir Grains" derived from a plant.

### Sauerkraut
In Germany, common or drumhead cabbage (*Brassica oleracea* var. *capitata*) is converted into *Sauerkraut*. This is achieved by placing alternate layers of salt and cabbage in a tub. An acid fermentation sets in and is completed after a few days, after which the vessel is tightly covered.
