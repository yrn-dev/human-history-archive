# Abo and Nkosi Dialects

The Abo and Nkosi dialects are linguistic varieties categorized within the Barondo-Bakundu group of Bantu languages. These dialects are characterized in archival records as being "much-debased."

## Geographic Distribution
The Abo and Nkosi dialects, along with other similar varieties in the Barondo-Bakundu group, are spoken across a specific region of West Africa. Their distribution extends across the eastern slopes of the Cameroon mountains and along the Cameroon river, also known as the Magombe. From this area, the reach of these dialects extends further to the Sanaga and Nyong rivers.

## Linguistic Classification and Affiliations
The Barondo-Bakundu group, which encompasses Abo and Nkosi, begins its northern limit at the Rio del Rey. This location is situated at the extremity of the Bantu field, near the estuary of the Cross river. In addition to Abo and Nkosi, this group may include the following:
* Barombi
* Bas[=a]
* Bonken

The Barondo-Bakundu group is situated adjacent to other linguistic classifications. To the east and north-east, there are languages described as "semi-Bantu," including Bafut, Bayon, Bati, Mbe, Nki, and Mbudikum. These semi-Bantu languages exhibit affinities with the Fan (a group of speech forms so corrupt they are only just recognizable as Bantu) as well as with speech forms found in the basin of the Central Benue river.

## Regional Context
The broader linguistic landscape of the region includes various other Bantu groups. To the north of the Cameroon delta (Ambas Bay) and on the west slopes of the Cameroon Mountains is the Isubu-Bakwiri group. On the estuary and delta of the Cameroon river, the Duala group is spoken, which is noted for being of a "much purer Bantu type" compared to other regional forms. Additionally, the Bantu dialects of Fernando Po—such as Bani, Bateti, and Ediya—are distantly allied to other groups in the region.
