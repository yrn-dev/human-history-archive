# Banka

## Geography and Geology
Banka (also known as Banca or Bangka) is an island located in the Dutch East Indies, situated off the east coast of Sumatra. It is separated from Sumatra by the Banka Strait, which reaches its narrowest point at approximately 9 miles wide. To the east, the island is separated from Billiton by the broader, island-studded Gaspar Strait. Banka measures 138 miles in length and has an extreme breadth of 62 miles, with a total area of 4,460 square miles, including a few small adjacent islands.

The island's geology resembles that of the Malay Peninsula. Its formations consist primarily of Devonian and Silurian slate, granite, and granite-based irregular hill-chains that extend from the west-north-west to the south-south-east. These formations are frequently covered by alluvium, laterite (a red ironstone clay of small fertility), and sandstone. Because the hill-chains generally lie near the east coast, the rivers on the west coast are longer. The highest point on the island is Maras in the north, reaching 2,760 feet. There are no volcanoes on Banka.

## Climate
The climate of Banka is characterized by two distinct periods driven by monsoons. From May to August, the south-east monsoon brings a hot and dry period. Conversely, the north-west monsoon accompanies the wet, cool season proper, which lasts from November to February. 

The island receives a mean annual rainfall of 120 inches, with rain occurring on an average of 168 days each year. This heavy rainfall is noted for being of great importance to the tin-streaming industry.

## Economy and Tin Production
Banka is principally known for the production of tin, a mineral discovered on the island in 1710. The production of tin is a government monopoly. The mineral is found in stream-tin and lodes. The industry is organized into nine mining districts containing approximately 120 mines under government control. These mines employ 12,000 workmen, who have produced as much as 12,000 tons of tin in a single year. The workforce consists of large numbers of Chinese who live in their own villages.

The island's chief rivers—the Waringin, Kotta, and Jering—are used for the transport of tin and are navigable for some 19 miles from their mouths. Regarding other natural resources, the surface of the island is largely covered with forests, where the logwood tree is especially abundant, though the soil is generally stony and dry.

## Population
As of 1905, the total population of Banka was 115,189. This population included 40,000 Chinese and 70,000 natives, the latter of whom were mainly composed of immigrant Malayan peoples.
