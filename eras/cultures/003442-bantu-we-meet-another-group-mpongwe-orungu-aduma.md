# Mpongwe-Orungu and Aduma Languages

The Mpongwe-Orungu and Aduma languages are a group of Bantu speech located in French Gabun. Within the broader geographical distribution of Bantu languages, this group is encountered when moving southwards from the north-westernmost limit of the Bantu field.

## Geographical Context and Affiliations
The Mpongwe-Orungu and Aduma group is situated in French Gabun and includes the tongues spoken on the Fernan Vaz promontory and in the Lower Ogowe region. Geographically, this group serves as a transition point in the Western group of Bantu languages. To the south, these languages shade into the Kakongo dialects of the Sete Kama and Loango coast. The Kakongo group includes various dialects such as Ba-kamba, Ba-vili, Ma-yombe, Ba-nyanga, Ba-kama, and Ka-kongo (Kabinda).

## Classification within the Bantu Domain
The Mpongwe-Orungu and Aduma languages are part of the Western group of Bantu speech. This overarching Western group encompasses all Cameroon and Central and Northern Congo languages, and likely includes group No. 2 of the Semliki river and Albert Nyanza. 

The domain of Bantu speech is broadly divided into four great sections:
*   The languages of the East Coast and Great Lakes, extending down to the Zambezi basin.
*   The South-Central group, which includes Zulu and Bechuana.
*   The South-West languages, ranging from the Angola-Congo coast and the southern Belgian Congo to Damaraland.
*   The Western group, which contains the Mpongwe-Orungu and Aduma languages.

## Characteristics of Bantu Speech
The Bantu family is noted for a striking homogeneity, which distinguishes it from the linguistic confusion found in Africa north of the Bantu borderland. A defining feature of the Bantu type of language is the use of pronominal-prefixes. 

While the exact birthplace of the Bantu languages remains a puzzle, it is suggested that they spread in comparatively recent times from a center of origin in west central Africa. This expansion effectively separated the Hottentots from other Hamitic peoples. The creation of the pronominal-prefix Bantu type is considered a much more ancient event than the subsequent exodus from the Bantu mother-land.

## Related Western Groups
The Mpongwe-Orungu and Aduma languages exist alongside several other distinct groups in the north-western region:
*   **The Duala group:** Located on the delta and estuary of the Cameroon river, these are described as being of a "much purer" Bantu type.
*   **The Fan (or Pangwe) forms:** Found in French Gabun and the interior of German Cameroon, extending to the Mbam, Sanaga, and Sanga rivers, as well as the hinterland of the Duala Cameroon. These are described as being so corrupt that they are only just recognizable as Bantu.
*   **The Barondo-Bakundu group:** Located near the estuary of the Cross river and the Rio del Rey, extending to the Nyong and Sanaga rivers.
*   **The Isubu-Bakwiri group:** Found on the west slopes of the Cameroon Mountains and the coast-lands north of the Cameroon delta (Ambas Bay).
