# Sphagnales

The Sphagnales are a group of mosses represented by the single genus *Sphagnum*. This genus occupies an isolated and distinct position among mosses. The species within this genus are commonly known as bog-mosses and are characterized by a high degree of similarity, requiring the use of minute structural characters for accurate identification.

## Distribution and Habitat
*Sphagnum* species are found in arctic and temperate climates. In tropical regions, they occur only at high levels. These plants typically grow in large patches of reddish or pale green color on moors. When they inhabit pools or small lakes, they can reach a length of several feet. The growth of these plants has been a significant factor in the formation of peat.

## Morphology and Structure
The plant begins as a protonema, which is a thalloid, lobed, and flat structure that uses rhizoids to attach itself to the soil. The plants emerge from marginal cells. The main shoot is characterized by numerous branches that appear to be arranged in whorls, although some of these branches bend downward and apply themselves to the surface of the main axis.

The internal structure of the leaves and stem is peculiar:
*   **The Stem:** A cross-section reveals a central tissue with thin walls, surrounded by a zone of thick-walled cells. The outermost layer consists of one to five layers of large, clear cells. These cells are dead and empty when mature, featuring round pores and walls strengthened by spiral thickening. Their primary function is the conduction and absorption of water via capillarity.
*   **The Leaves:** The leaves lack a midrib. They contain narrow cells containing chlorophyll, which form a green network interspersed with empty cells similar to those found in the stem.

## Sexual Generation and Reproduction
The sexual organs of *Sphagnum* differ from most other mosses in their placement; while most mosses bear sexual organs at the apices of branches or the main shoot, *Sphagnum* does not. The antheridia are globular with long stalks and are positioned beside leaves on special branches that are club-shaped. The archegonial groups are located at the apices of short branches.

The development of the sporogonium in *Sphagnum* is distinct from other mosses. In most mosses, the sporogonium develops via an apical cell that cuts off two rows of segments; however, *Sphagnum* does not follow this process. Additionally, while the archesporium in other mosses is the outermost layer of the endothecium, in *Sphagnum* (as well as in *Anthoceros*), the archesporium is derived from the amphithecium.

## The Sporogonium and Capsule
The mature sporogonium of *Sphagnum* consists of a globular capsule and a wide foot, which are separated by a constriction. It lacks a distinct seta. Instead, the capsule is elevated on a pseudopodium, which is a leafless outgrowth at the end of the branch.

The capsule is characterized by the following:
*   **Wall and Opening:** The wall contains rudimentary stomata. The capsule opens via a small operculum but does not possess a peristome.
*   **Internal Structure:** It contains a short, wide columella. A dome-shaped spore-sac extends over this columella, with no air-space existing between the wall and the spore-sac.

In the embryo, cells are first formed in a number of tiers, with the lower tiers forming the foot.
