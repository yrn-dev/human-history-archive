# Pamir

The Pamir is the region situated at the north-western extremity of the elevated Tibeto-Himalayan mountain plateau. Geographically, this area is located at approximately 39 deg. N. and 73 deg. E.

## Geographical Characteristics
The Pamir possesses all the characteristics associated with the highest regions of Tibet. Due to these features, it is designated as a "steppe" by the Russians. Despite its long-standing reputation as a geographical enigma, the region appears to have no special peculiarities that would explain such a designation.

The Pamir serves as a critical junction for several major water systems. To the west, the Oxus (also known as the Amu) flows off from this region. To the north, the Jaxartes (or Syr) flows through the Turki state of Khokand. To the east, waters run past Kashgar toward the central Gobi desert. These eastern waters unite with streams from the northern slope of the Tibetan plateau that traverse the Turki principalities of Khotan and Yarkand.

## Regional Connectivity and Boundaries
The Pamir is part of a broader mountainous belt that extends from the western end of the Yablonoi range on the 115th meridian. This belt is broken into several branches and encompasses various isolated drainage areas containing numerous lakes. 

At the Pamir, the Tibetan mountains unite with a line of elevation that stretches across the continent from the Pacific. This line of elevation serves as the boundary separating Siberia from the region commonly referred to as central Asia (Manchuria).

## Surrounding Lowlands and Populations
To the north-west of the Pamir's extremity lies a nearly desert, low-lying plain. This plain extends from the foot of the mountains and is prolonged eastward along the valleys of the Jaxartes (Syr-Darya) and the Oxus (Amu Darya), and northward across the country of the Kirghiz to the south-western border of Siberia. This area is bounded to the south by the northern border of the Persian high lands and to the north by the Caspian and Aral Seas.

This lowland region includes Turkestan proper, Khiva, and Bokhara, where the Uzbeg Turks are the dominant group. The area is primarily inhabited by nomadic tribes described as marauders and professed robbers, though those tribes to the north under Russian subject are more disciplined and peaceable. 

Agriculture in this tract is only possible through irrigation, as rainfall is insufficient. Consequently, the fixed population is relatively small and restricted to districts near rivers and within towns; this population includes a non-Turkish element.
