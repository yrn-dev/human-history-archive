# Pali Text Society

The Pali Text Society is an organization dedicated to the publication of Buddhist texts. It continues to publish two volumes per year.

## Contributions to Buddhist Literature
The society has been instrumental in making the Pali text of major Buddhist works accessible. Specifically, the Pali Text Society has published the entirety of the Pali text for the *Anguttara Nikaya*. This work is the longest book in the Buddhist Bible, spanning 1,840 pages in octavo format. While the society has published the full Pali text of this collection, only portions of it have been translated into English.

Additionally, the Pali Text Society has published the final of the four collections of Buddhist doctrine in five volumes. This collection organizes Buddhist doctrine by subject, consisting of 55 *Samyuttas* (groups) where suttas are grouped by subject or, in some instances, by the type of people to whom they were addressed. Only a few fragments of this five-volume set have been translated.

## Context of the Texts
The works published by the society were originally composed in northern India, specifically either north of the Ganges or a few miles south of it. These texts allow for the tracing of the main lines of Buddhist development through its first eight or nine centuries and provide an accurate idea of earliest Buddhism.

The *Anguttara Nikaya*, for example, organizes Buddhist doctrine into classes—starting with units, then pairs, then trios—to serve as a help to memory. This was essential in early Buddhist times when books were learned by heart and had not yet been written. This collection includes most of the ethics and psychology of Buddhism.

## Related Scholarly Works
In addition to the publications of the Pali Text Society, other scholarly efforts have expanded the archive of Buddhist and Indian history. The Russian Academy has started a series to publish important Sanskrit works that remain in manuscript form. Furthermore, fourteen volumes of commentaries by 5th-century scholars from Ceylon and south India are now accessible in Pali. These commentaries were primarily written by Dhammapala of Kancipura (the ancient name of Conjeeveram) and Buddhaghosa of Budh Gaya. These texts are noted for providing significant historical data regarding both the religious and social life of India during the periods they describe.
