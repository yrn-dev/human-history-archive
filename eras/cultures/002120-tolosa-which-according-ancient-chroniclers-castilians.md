# Las Navas de Tolosa

The battle of Las Navas de Tolosa was a significant military engagement fought in 1212. It took place in the neighborhood of Bailen, a town located in the province of Jaen in southern Spain.

## The Conflict and Outcomes
According to the accounts of ancient chroniclers, the Castilians, led by Alphonso VIII, achieved a victory in this battle. These chroniclers claim that the Castilian forces slew 200,000 Moors while suffering only 25 casualties themselves. Although this specific estimate is described as absurd, the victory for the Christians was complete.

## The Castilians
The Castilians are the people associated with the literary language of Spain, which is also spoken in Spanish America. This language is used in Aragon, Estremadura, Leon, and both Old and New Castile, though it undergoes modifications in accent and pronunciation within Andalusia. 

The Castilian is regarded as the typical Spaniard, as there is little difference in physical type, character, or racial origin among the inhabitants of these regions, with some exceptions in Estremadura and Andalusia. The national character is most evident among the Castilian peasantry, where foreign influence and education have not penetrated deeply. This character is defined by honesty, bigotry, intense pride, and a fatalistic indolence and ignorance, all of which are tempered by a keen sense of humour.

Beyond the peasantry, Castilians have been major contributors to Spanish literature and art, second perhaps only to the inhabitants of Andalusia, who are considered supreme in painting and architecture. Castilian influence was also pivotal in education; the university of Alcala de Henares belonged to Castile in all respects, and the university of Salamanca reached a status equal to Bologna, Oxford, or Paris under the influence of Alphonso X (1252-1284).

## Regional Comparisons
In contrast to the Castilians, the Asturians—who may be seen as the purest representatives of the Iberian race due to their relative immunity from alien domination (except for the Goths and Romans)—possess a different physical and spiritual profile. While Castilians are noted for their grace, Asturians are described as hardy mountaineers and fishermen who are finely built but rarely handsome. 

The Asturian dialect, *linguaje bable*, is considered closely akin to the parent speech of modern Castilian. However, it differs by retaining the original Latin *f*, which Castilian changes into *h*, and it remains free from Moorish idioms.
