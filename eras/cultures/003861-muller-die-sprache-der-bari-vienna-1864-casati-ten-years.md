# The Bari People

The Bari are a tribe of Nilotic negroes situated on the banks of the upper Nile, approximately 200 miles north of Albert Nyanza. Their geographic position places them in proximity to several neighboring groups: the Dinka reside to the north, the Madi to the south, and the Galla to the east.

## Physical Characteristics and Dress
The Bari exhibit distinct physical differences between the sexes; the men are described as tall and thin, while the women are fat and below middle height. Their skin color is a deep dead brown. In terms of attire, men and unmarried girls generally go naked. Married women wear goatskins that have been dyed red. The Bari also utilize red clay for body ornamentation and frequently extract their lower incisors.

## Diet and Economy
The sole form of wealth for the Bari is cattle. Their primary dietary staples are milk and blood; meat is consumed only on the occasion that a cow dies.

## Social Structure and Customs
The Bari reside in round grass huts featuring conical roofs. Within their society, rainmakers are the most important figures, receiving more reverence than the chiefs; these rainmakers are well-known among the tribes that surround them.

The tribe maintains specific customs regarding family and death:
* **Twins:** The birth of twins is regarded as unlucky. Consequently, the mother is divorced by her husband, and her family is required to refund a portion of the marriage-price.
* **Burial:** The dead are buried within the hut. A square grave is dug, and the body is placed in a sitting position with the hands tied behind the back.

## External Relations and History
Bari warriors have been recruited in significant numbers for the Egyptian army. Additionally, they were formerly employed by Arab traders to serve as slave-hunters.
