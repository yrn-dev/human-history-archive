# Hindu Inhabitants and the Baluch Population

## The Baluch Community
The Baluch are not a homogeneous race, but rather a community composed of various stocks, including people of Arab, Persian, and Dravidian extraction. The Arab influence is particularly strong in the ethnography of Baluchistan. All Rind tribes claim to be of Arab origin and Koraish extraction. The Arabs occupied southern Baluchistan and Seistan from an early date and eventually spread through the Sind valley, where they remained until the 12th century.

The Marri and Bugti tribes, located on the southernmost buttresses of the Suliman Mountains, are Rind Baluchis and are almost certainly of Arab extraction. These groups arrived in Sind either alongside or after the Arab conquerors and remained there, intermixing with the original Hindu inhabitants. The "Arab type" of Baluch is found at intervals throughout the entire country and is described as representing the finest and best of Baluch humanity.

## Characteristics of the Arab-type Baluch
When compared to the Afridi Pathan, the Baluch is considered easier to control and deal with due to his tribal organization and a lack of blind allegiance to priests or bigoted fanaticism. The Baluch is characterized as being less treacherous, less bloodthirsty, less turbulent, and less fanatical than the Pathan. 

Physically, the Baluch frame is more spare, wiry, and shorter than that of the Pathan, though they possess a bold and manly bearing. Baluch chiefs of the Bugti or Marri clans are noted for their imposing presence and stately dignity. Their Semitic features are similar to those of the Bedouin, and they carry themselves with the loftiness of an Arab gentleman.

In temperament, the true Baluch of the Derajat is described as frank, open, fairly truthful, temperate, enduring, and faithful to his word, viewing courage as the highest virtue. However, they are considered poor husbandmen because they lack industry and possess a pride that views manual labor as degrading, which makes them unsatisfactory revenue payers. They are expert riders, and the Baluch breed of horses is celebrated throughout northern India; horse-racing serves as their national amusement. While they are first-rate fighting men and bandits by tradition and descent, they rarely enlist in the Indian army. Nominally, they are Mahommedans.

## Other Populations of Baluchistan
Beyond the Baluch, other races inhabit the region. The central desert (known in Arabic medieval geography as the Kir, Kej, Katz, or Kash Kaian) contains scattered nomad tribes called Rekis. Among these, the Mohamadani are the most numerous and are likely of Arab origin.

In the Mashkel and Kharan deserts live the Naushirwanis, a purely Persian race that entered Baluchistan within historic times. They are identified with the Tahuki or Tahukani found in Perso-Baluchistan and are described as a fine, manly race. Additionally, an underlying Persian (Tajik) population is scattered throughout the country. This group often serves as the agricultural bondsmen or slaves of the general community, such as the Durzadas (referred to by Herodotus as *Derusiaei*) and the Dehwars or Dehkans; when serving as slaves, they are called Nakibs.

## Distribution of Hindu and Mahommedan Populations in India
In the broader context of India, the religion of the great bulk of the people is Hindu or Brahminical from the borders of the Sind desert and the river Sutlej as far as Ceylon and Burma. While Mahommedans are often numerous and sometimes hold a majority in certain places, the population west of the Sutlej is described as being wholly Mahommedan, with the exception of small areas in Syria and Asia Minor where Christians predominate.

The Aryans of India are considered among the most settled and civilized Asiatic races, found in their purest form in the north and north-west. In contrast, mixed races and the Australoid type are more prevalent in southern India and the peninsula.
