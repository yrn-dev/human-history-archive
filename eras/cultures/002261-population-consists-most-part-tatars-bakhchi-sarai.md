# Bakhchi-sarai

Bakhchi-sarai, a name derived from the Turkish words for "garden-palace," is a town located in Russia within the government of Taurida. It is situated in the Crimea in a narrow gorge, positioned 20 miles south-southwest of Simferopol by rail.

## History and Governance
From the end of the 15th century until 1783, Bakhchi-sarai served as the residence for the Tatar khans of the Crimea. This historical role is reflected in the town's streets, which maintain a decidedly oriental appearance. 

The town's primary architectural landmark is the palace, known as the *Khan-sarai*. This structure was originally built in 1519 by Abdul-Sahal-Ghirai. Although it was destroyed in 1736, the palace was later restored under the command of Potemkin to facilitate the reception of Catherine II. A mausoleum is attached to the palace, serving as the burial site for many of the khans.

## Demographics and Religion
As of 1897, the population of Bakhchi-sarai was 12,955. The inhabitants consist for the most part of Tatars. The town is a significant center of Islamic worship, containing no fewer than thirty-six mosques.

## Surrounding Landmarks
Several notable sites are located in the immediate vicinity of the town:

**Chufut-Kaleh:** Located two and a half miles to the east, this site is known as the "Jews' city." Situated on lofty cliffs that are almost inaccessible, it formerly served as the chief seat of the Karaite Jews of the Crimea. Currently, the site is deserted, with the exception of the rabbi.

**Uspenskiy Monastery:** This monastery is located between Bakhchi-sarai and Chufut-Kaleh. It is described as clinging to the face of the cliffs like a swallow's nest and serves as the location for a great pilgrimage held annually on August 15th (29th).
