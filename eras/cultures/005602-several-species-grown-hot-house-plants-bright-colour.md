# Greenhouse and Hot-House Plants

The cultivation of various plant species in controlled environments, such as greenhouses, hot-houses, and stoves, is practiced for a variety of purposes, including the appreciation of bright floral colors, the beauty of foliage, and the production of decorative cuttings.

## Plants Grown for Floral Color
Several plant families provide species specifically valued for their vibrant colors when grown in hot-houses or greenhouses.

**Bromeliaceae**
The Bromeliaceae are a natural order of Monocotyledons native to sub-tropical and tropical America. While the order includes the pine-apple and the rootless Spanish moss, several other species are grown as hot-house plants specifically for the bright colors of their flower-bracts or flowers. Examples of such species include those from the genera *Tillandsia*, *Aechmea*, and *Billbergia*.

**Daphne**
The genus *Daphne* (order Thymelaeaceae) contains approximately forty species native to temperate Asia and Europe. While some are cultivated in the open air, others are grown as greenhouse plants. These include *D. indica* from China, which produces red flowers, and *D. japonica* from Japan, which produces pinkish-purple flowers.

## Plants Grown for Foliage and Form
Some species are cultivated in controlled environments not for their flowers, but for the aesthetic qualities of their leaves and stems.

**Cordyline and Dracaena**
Many plants referred to as Dracaenas actually belong to the closely-allied genus *Cordyline*. These are utilized as summer greenhouse plants, decorative stove plants, or for table and room decoration due to their variegation, color, and beauty of form. One such species, *D. Goldieana* from west tropical Africa, is grandly variegated and requires a higher level of heat.

**Asparagus**
Certain climbing species of Asparagus are grown in greenhouses for their delicate and often feathery branches, which are also used for cutting. The South African species *Asparagus plumosus* is noted as being especially elegant.

## Cultivation and Care of Greenhouse Plants
The maintenance of these plants requires specific environmental controls regarding temperature and soil.

**Cordyline Propagation and Maintenance**
*Cordyline* species are propagated via cuttings in sandy soil, with the best time for this process being spring. The required night temperature for propagation is between 65 and 70 degrees. Once established, these plants thrive in fibry peat made porous with sand. Temperature requirements vary by season, with a day temperature of 75 degrees in the summer and 65 degrees in the winter. Plants intended for conservatories must be gradually inured to more air by midsummer while being protected from cold draughts.

## Other Specialized Cultivation
While not always kept in greenhouses for decoration, some plants are managed using heat-based methods for production.

**Forcing Asparagus**
To force *Asparagus officinalis*, growers may use a moderate hot-bed of stable litter with a bottom heat of 70 degrees, covered by a common frame. After the fermentation heat subsides, a layer of exhausted tan-bark or light earth is applied, and the roots of mature plants are placed within. A three-light frame can hold between 500 and 600 plants, providing a supply for several weeks.
