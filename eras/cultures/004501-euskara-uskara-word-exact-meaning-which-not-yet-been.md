# Eskuara

## Overview
Eskuara (also referred to as euskara or uskara) is the original and proper name of the language spoken by the Basques. The exact meaning of the word has not yet been ascertained, although it is probable that it corresponds to the concept of "clearly speaking."

## The Basque People
The Basques are a people who inhabit Navarre and the three Basque Provinces of Guipuzcoa, Alava, and Biscay in Spain, as well as Mauleon and the arrondissement of Bayonne in France. The Basques refer to themselves as *Eskualdunak*, a term meaning "those who possess the Eskuara," and they refer to their country as Eskual-Herria.

Historically, the word "Basques" is derived from *Vascones* (or *Wascones*), a term that also provided the name for the Gascons, who are a very different race. In terms of population, there are approximately 600,000 people considered pure Basques in Europe. Additionally, there are perhaps 100,000 emigrants in the Americas, primarily located in the region of La Plata in South America.

## Linguistic Characteristics
Eskuara is described as a highly interesting language that remains absolutely isolated from other European tongues. From a purely grammatical perspective, it recalls the Finnic and Magyar languages.

The language utilizes a polysynthetic, incorporating, and agglutinative system of speech. Within the general series of organized linguistic families, Eskuara would occupy an intermediate position between the Ugrian or Ugro-Altaic families on one side and the American languages on the other.
