# Bechuana Language Group

The Bechuana language group is a well-marked collection of Bantu dialects characterized by distinct features. This group encompasses all Bantu dialects located within the Bechuanaland protectorate to the west of the Guai river.

## Geographic Distribution and Dialects
The Bechuana dialects extend across a broad region, covering the Orange River Colony and Bechuanaland, as well as significant portions of the north and west of the Transvaal. The group's reach stretches from the Orange river in the south to the Zambezi in the north. Its westward extension reaches the domain of the Oci-herero, though this area is interrupted by the presence of Bushmen and Hottentot.

Specific dialects within this group include:
*   **Se-suto**: The language used in Basutoland.
*   **Se-rolon and Se-mangwato**: Languages of the Eastern Kalahri.
*   **Se-kololo**: The language utilized by the court of Barotseland.
*   **Ci-venda and Se-pedi (or Peli)**: The principal dialects found in the Transvaal.
*   **Se-[chi]lapi**: One of the dialects covering the north and west of the Transvaal.

## Related Languages of South-East Africa
In Portuguese South-East Africa—specifically within the Lower Limpopo valley, Gazaland, and patches of the North Transvaal (where Shi-gwamba is spoken), as well as Delagoa Bay—the Ronga (Tonga) languages are present. These languages are almost equally related to the Zulu language and the Nyanja group, likely representing a mingling of both influences, though the influence of Zulu predominates.
