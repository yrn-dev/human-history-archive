# Bolingbroke and the Art of Expression

## Career and Reputation
The writings and career of Bolingbroke have left a far weaker impression on posterity than they did on his contemporaries. His character and genius are described as superficial, with abilities that were applied to ephemeral objects rather than universal or lasting ideas. While George III and Bute derived their political ideas from his work *The Patriot King*, his influence on figures such as Burke, Gibbon, and Voltaire is considered problematical. 

Edmund Burke wrote *Vindication of Natural Society* as an imitation of Bolingbroke's style, though he did so to refute his principles. Burke later questioned who still reads Bolingbroke in full and denied that Bolingbroke's words left any permanent impression on his mind.

## Eloquence and Conversation
Bolingbroke's most brilliant gift was his eloquence, which Swift noted was acknowledged by men of all factions to be unrivalled. However, none of his great orations have survived. This loss was regretted by Pitt more than the loss of the missing books of Tacitus and Livy.

His social qualities, wit, and good looks allowed him to form firm friendships with men of opposite characters. Lord Chesterfield described Bolingbroke's conversation as possessing "such a flowing happiness of expression that even his most familiar conversations if taken down in writing would have borne the press without the least correction."

## Literary and Philosophical Works
Bolingbroke's political works are characterized by a style that is often spirited, dignified, and splendidly eloquent, though they are largely exceedingly rhetorical. His philosophical essays were written primarily to display his eloquence and were composed as an amusement during his retirement without serious preparation. 

Because of this focus on display, these writings are considered less readable for posterity and are deficient in permanent interest and solidity. His political writings are viewed as dealing with party questions without depth or sincerity, while his philosophical essays—specifically their feeble deism and attacks on theology and metaphysics—lack originality.
