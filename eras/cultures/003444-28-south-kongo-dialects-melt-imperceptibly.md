# The Kongo Language Group

The Kongo language group is a collection of dialects situated in Central Africa. Its geographic distribution extends along the lower course of the Congo river, stretching from the river's mouth to Stanley Pool. Additionally, the group encompasses the territory of the old kingdom of Congo, which is located north of the river Loje and south of the Congo river, extending from the coast eastwards to the longitude of Stanley Pool and the watershed of the river Kwango.

## Relations to the Mbundu Group
In the southern regions, the Kongo dialects melt imperceptibly into the Angola language, which is closely allied to the Kongo group. This related collection of languages may be generally styled as Mbundu. The Mbundu group includes several languages of Central Angola, specifically Songo, Ki-sama, Mbamba, Ki-mbundu, and U-mbangala.

The boundaries of the Mbundu genus are defined by specific geographic and coordinate markers. To the north, the river Loje serves as a frontier between the Mbundu and Kongo tongues. To the east, the boundary is likely the Kwango river, beyond which the Lunda languages begin. To the south, the boundary of the Mbundu group is approximately the 11th degree of south latitude.

## Linguistic Characteristics
Within the broader context of Bantu languages, the Kongo language provides linguistic evidence regarding the evolution of negative verbal particles. In the Bantu mother-tongue, the negative particle is traced back to original forms such as *ka*, *ta*, *sa*, *ki*, *ti*, or *si*. In most modern Bantu dialects, this particle has coalesced with the pronominal particle to the point where the two are no longer soluble. However, the Kongo language retains intermediate forms that allow linguists to guess at the original separation between the pronominal particle and the negative particle.

## Related Bantu Groups and Regions
The Kongo and Mbundu groups exist within a wider field of Bantu dialects across the continent. To the north and west, other groups include the Mpongwe-Orungu and Aduma languages of French Gabun, as well as the tongues of the Fernan Vaz promontory and the Lower Ogowe. These shade south into the Kakongo dialects of the Sete Kama and Loango coast, which include Ka-kongo (Kabinda), Ba-kamba, Ba-vili, Ma-yombe, Ba-nyanga, and Ba-kama.

Further north, the Bantu field includes the Isubu-Bakwiri group on the west slopes of the Cameroon Mountains and north of the Cameroon delta (Ambas Bay). The Barondo-Bakundu group begins at the Rio del Rey and may include dialects such as Abo, Nkosi, Bonken, Bas[=a], and Barombi, spoken on the Cameroon river (Magombe) and the eastern slopes of the Cameroon mountains, extending to the Nyong and Sanaga rivers.

In East Africa, other distinct Bantu groups are present, such as the Swahili language—an archaic dialect indigenous to the coast south of the Ruvu (Pangani) river—and the Kaguru-Sagala-Kami group, which occupies inland territories of German East Africa between the Nyamwezi domain and the Swahili coast.
