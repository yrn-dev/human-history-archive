# The Bongo

The Bongo (also known as the Dor or Deran) are a tribe of Nilotic negroes residing in the south-west region of the Bahr-el-Ghazal province within the Anglo-Egyptian Sudan. They are believed to be related to the Zandeh tribes of the Welle district.

## Population and History
According to G.A. Schweinfurth, who spent two years living among the tribe, the Bongo population numbered at least 300,000 prior to the arrival of slave-raiders around 1850. Their numbers were significantly reduced by these raiders and subsequently by the dervishes. The population only began to recuperate after the Sudan government established effective control between 1904 and 1906. Historically, the Bongo lived in numerous peaceful, independent small communities; under the administration of the Sudan government, they have returned to managing their own affairs.

## Physical Characteristics and Dress
The Bongo are described as a race of medium height and a thick-set build. Their complexion is red-brown, which Schweinfurth compared to the soil of their residence, and they have black hair. Schweinfurth noted that their heads are nearly round, stating that no other African race known to him possessed a higher cephalic index. In later life, Bongo women tend toward steatopygia; Schweinfurth observed that this deposit of fat, combined with a tail of bast worn by the women, gave them the appearance of "dancing baboons" while walking.

Traditionally, Bongo men wore only a loin-cloth and dozens of iron rings on their arms, which were arranged to function as a type of armour. Women traditionally wore a girdle with an attached tuft of grass. In more recent times, both sexes have largely adopted cotton cloths for their dress.

## Tribal Ornaments
The Bongo utilize nails or plugs passed through the lower lip as ornaments. Women frequently wear a disk several inches in diameter in the lower lip, along with a bit of straw or a ring in the upper lip. Additionally, they wear rings in the septum and straws in the *alae* of the nostrils.

## Housing and Agriculture
The Bongo construct well-built huts, some of which reach heights of 24 feet. 

Unlike other Negroes of the upper Nile, the Bongo are not significant cattle-breeders. Instead, they dedicate their time to agriculture. Their primary cultivated crops include durra, sesame, tobacco, and sorghum.

## Diet
The Bongo diet is diverse, consisting of fungi, tubers, and fruits found in their environment. They consume almost every type of creature, including reptiles, insects, beasts, and birds, though they do not eat dogs. They do not despise any flesh, whether it is fresh or putrid, and are known to drive vultures away from carcasses.
