# The Caspian Region and Associated Highlands

The Caspian region is characterized by a complex intersection of hydrography and extensive mountain systems that form part of a larger continental divide. This area serves as a boundary between various political territories, including Persia, Russian Transcaspian territory, and the highlands of Central Asia.

## Hydrography of the Caspian Sea
The Caspian Sea's basin is naturally divided into three distinct sections. The northern section is the shallowest, barely reaching a depth of 20 fathoms, and is being gradually silted by sedimentary deposits from the Terek, Ural, and Volga rivers. Its western shore, stretching 170 miles from the Volga delta to the mouth of the Kuma, is marked by thousands of narrow lagoons called *limans*, which are separated by sandbanks or chains of hillocks known as *bugors*. To the northeast, the coast is formed by low calcareous hills that act as the rampart of the Ust-Urt plateau, which separates the Caspian from the Sea of Aral. To the south, this northern gulf is backed by the Manghishlak and Busachi peninsulas, featuring the narrow, curving fjord of Kara-su or Kaidak.

The middle section of the sea begins south of the line joining the Manghishlak peninsula with the Bay of Kuma (at 44 deg. 10' N. lat.). In this region, the water deepens considerably; over half the area is 50 fathoms deep, with a maximum depth of 437 fathoms between 41 deg. and 42 deg. N. lat. This section extends to the Apsheron peninsula and receives drainage from the northern slopes of the Caucasus via the Terek and smaller streams such as the Samur and Sulak. At Derbent, a spur of the Caucasus creates a narrow passage known as the *Albanae Portae* or *Caspiae Pylae*, which has been fortified for centuries.

## The Continental Divide and Mountain Systems
The Caspian is part of a vast geographical system where a great central water-parting stretches from the Black Sea, through the Elburz and the Hindu Kush, to the Tian-shan mountains in the Pamir region. This divide is not always marked by steep ranges; in some areas, it consists of intermediate highlands.

An unbroken range of mountains stretches from the intersection of the 36th parallel and the 71st meridian, extending nearly due west as far as the Caspian. The western portion of this range was known to the ancients as Paropamisus Mons, and it diminishes in height west of the 65th meridian.

## The Elburz and Khorasan Highlands
The Elburz Mountains are a primary feature of the region's elevation. In the province of Astarabad, the Elburz forms the southern boundary, while the Caspian Sea and Russian Transcaspian territory lie to the north. 

In the province of Khorasan—a mountainous region originally representing the eastern quarter of the Sassanian monarchy—the north is occupied by an extensive highland system. This system consists of the Elburz and its continuation toward the Paropamisus, sometimes referred to collectively as the Kopet Dagh or Kuren Dagh. In the east, this system forms three ranges: the Jagatai, Binalud Kuh, and Hazar Masjed. These ranges enclose the Jovain plain and the Meshed-Kuchan valley. 

The northern highlands of western Khorasan develop into two branches:
1. The Ala Dagh, which continues from the Binalud Kuh and joins mountains between Astarabad and Bujnurd.
2. The Kuren Dagh, which stretches through the Little and Great Balkans to reach the Caspian at Krasnovodsk Bay.

These two branches enclose the valley of the Atrek River. Additionally, the Tejen river is formed by the junction of the Hari-rud and the Kashaf-rud (the river of Meshed); the Tejen sweeps toward the Caspian but disappears into the desert before reaching the sea.

## Regional Geography and Political Boundaries
The high region of Asia west of British India (excluding Syria and Arabia) forms an extensive plateau. Its northern border follows nearly 36 deg. N. to the southern shore of the Caspian and continues to the Sea of Marmora and the Black Sea. This region is nearly conterminous with Armenia, Iran (or Persia), Afghanistan, Baluchistan, and the provinces of Asia Minor.

The province of Astarabad is bounded by the Caspian and Russian Transcaspian to the north, the Elburz Mountains to the south, Mazandaran to the west, and Khorasan to the east. While the southern portion is mountainous and the valleys are fertile—producing wheat, rice, and other grains—the province has historically been subject to malarial fevers due to stagnant waters in its forests, as well as incursions by the Yomut and Goklan Turkomans.
