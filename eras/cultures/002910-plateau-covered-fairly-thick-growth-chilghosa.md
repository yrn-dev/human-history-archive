# The Takht-i-Suliman Plateau

## Geography and Topography
The Takht-i-Suliman, also known as "Solomon's throne," is a high tableland located in northern Baluchistan. It is a dominant feature of the region's geography, situated within the central range of the Suliman hills. The axis of this range lies slightly east of the 70° E. meridian. 

The plateau sits approximately 8,000 feet above sea level and is characterized as a massif—an oblong block of land—amongst neighboring ridges. It is bounded on its eastern and western edges by parallel ridges that are steep, rugged, and high. The western ridge reaches its highest point in the north at the peak of Kaisargarh, which rises to 11,300 feet. The eastern ridge culminates in the south at a detached headland or block reaching 11,070 feet, which serves as the site of a celebrated "zirat" or shrine.

## Geological Composition
Geologically, the central range of the Suliman hills consists of one or more great folds of the Cretaceous series. The Takht-i-Suliman tableland specifically is formed by a massive cap of coral limestone. According to Griesbach, this limestone cap is estimated to be between 4,000 and 5,000 feet in thickness.

The plateau is intersected at both ends by gorges that descend through stupendous precipices. To the north, these lead to the channel of the "Gat" (or Draband), and to the south, they lead to the Dhana. These channels allow mountain streams from the western slopes of the massif to flow across the axis of the mountains, passing through a barrier of minor ridges to reach the plains of the Indus.

## Flora and Exploration
The vegetation of the plateau is characterized by a fairly thick growth of the "edible" pine, known as the chilghosa. Additionally, the higher slopes of the massif feature a sprinkling of juniper.

The tableland remained unsurveyed and unascended until 1883. Among the local populations, the Sherani clansmen who occupy the area refer to the massif as "Kaisargarh."
