# ASHREF

## Geography and Location
Ashref is a town located in the province of Mazandaran in Persia. It is situated at the geographical coordinates 36 deg. 42' N. and 53 deg. 32' E. The town is positioned approximately 5 miles inland from the Caspian Sea and is about 50 miles west of Astarabad.

## Population and Demographics
The population of Ashref is approximately 6,000 people. This population consists of descendants of Georgians who were introduced to the area by Shah Abbas I (who reigned from 1587 to 1629), as well as a number of Gudars. The Gudars are described as a peculiar pariah race that is likely of Indian origin.

## Historical Development and Architecture
The town held little importance until 1612, when Shah Abbas began the process of laying out gardens and building palaces in the surrounding area. These sites are now known collectively as Bagh i Shah, which translates to "the garden of the shah." 

The palaces associated with this development were completed in 1627, though they currently exist in ruins. The original layout consisted of six separate gardens, all enclosed within one large perimeter wall, while high walls served to separate the individual gardens from one another. These gardens are noted for their luxuriant vegetation and the presence of gigantic orange and cypress trees.

## Notable Structures
The principal palace of the complex was the Chehel Situn, meaning "forty pillars." This structure was destroyed by the Afghans in 1723. Although Nadir Shah rebuilt the palace in 1731, it was already in ruins by 1743.

Additionally, the Safi-abad garden is located about 3/4 of a mile north of the town. This site features a palace built by Shah Safi (who reigned from 1629 to 1642) for his daughter. The palace is situated on a wooded hill and underwent repairs and partial renovation around 1870 under Nasiru'd-Din Shah.
