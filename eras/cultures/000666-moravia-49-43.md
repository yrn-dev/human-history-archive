# Moravia

Moravia is a region located in the south-east of Bohemia and the north of Lower Austria. It serves as a geographical link, providing access to Bohemia from the Danubian plain. Historically, it has been a territory of Austria and has played a significant role in the political and religious development of Central Europe.

## Early History and Christianization
In early history, the Marcomanni—a colony from the banks of the Rhine—occupied Moravia and Bohemia, establishing a formidable monarchy under King Maroboduus. 

The introduction of Christianity to the region occurred via Moravia. While early attempts to bring the faith to Bohemia from Germany were largely unsuccessful due to a distrust of the Germans, the doctrine was successfully taught in Moravia by Cyrillus and Methodius, who were Greek monks from Thessalonica. This influence led to the baptism of the Bohemian prince Borivoj by Methodius around the year 873, facilitating the rapid adoption of the Christian faith among the Bohemians. Later, during the reign of Boleslav II, his rule extended over both Bohemia and Moravia, as well as parts of Poland and Silesia.

## Political Conflicts and Governance
Moravia has frequently been a focal point of power struggles between competing rulers. In the 15th century, during the reign of King George of Podebrad, the king of Hungary, Matthias Corvinus, invaded Moravia and seized possession of Brunn, the capital of the country. Following this, Matthias was proclaimed king of Bohemia on May 3, 1469, at a meeting of Catholic nobles held at Olmutz in Moravia.

In the early 17th century, Moravia was again a site of political rupture during the conflict between Rudolph and his brother Matthias. When Matthias's forces entered Moravia, the estates of the country, led by Charles, lord of Zerotin, renounced their allegiance to Rudolph. This conflict eventually concluded on June 25, 1608, when Rudolph formally ceded the government of Moravia, Hungary, and Upper and Lower Austria to Matthias.

Additionally, the region's nobility has been involved in broader European dynastic struggles. John Henry, the margrave of Moravia and son of King John of Bohemia, was married to Margaret Maultasch, the heiress of Tirol. Later, Charles, the margrave of Moravia (who became Emperor Charles IV), attacked Tirol in 1346 after the emperor refused to accept his election as German king.

## Ethnic and Linguistic Tensions
In more recent history, Moravia was a primary site of vehemence in the contest between the German and Czech races. For twenty years, there was a constant encroachment of the Czech language upon the German. This shift was aided by government ordinances; an 1880 decree mandated that business brought before law courts or government offices in Bohemia and Moravia be handled in the language in which it was introduced. In 1886, this was extended to the high courts of Bruenn and Prague to avoid frequent translations.

While Czechs in Moravia were supported by some nobles, such as Count Belcredi, a strong German party remained present in Bruenn. These tensions were mirrored in the broader political demand by parties led by Palacky and Rieger for the restoration of the Bohemian monarchy to include Moravia and Silesia.

## Geography and Urban Centers
The town of Iglau (Czech: Jihlava) is one of the oldest towns in Moravia and serves as the center of a German-speaking enclave. Located on the Iglawa near the Bohemian frontier, it is 56 miles northwest of Brünn by rail. As of 1900, its population was 24,387, consisting of 4,200 Czechs and a German majority. 

Iglau is characterized by several principal buildings, including the town-hall, the churches of St. John, St. Paul, St. Ignatius, and St. Jakob, and barracks created from a monastery suppressed under Emperor Joseph II. The town's economy is diverse, featuring a state monopoly tobacco and cigar factory employing approximately 2,500 workers, as well as industries in glass, textiles, brewing, and pottery. Trade in cereals, timber, woollen goods, and linen is generally brisk, supported by periodic fairs.
