# BOII

The Boii were a Celtic people whose original homeland was Gallia Transalpina. The name "Boii" may mean "the terrible." They were known to the Romans at least by name during the time of Plautus, who referred to them contemptuously in the *Captivi*.

## Migrations and Settlements
At an early date, the Boii split into two primary groups: one migrated into Germany and the other into Italy. The group that entered Italy is said to have crossed the Pennine Alps via the Great St Bernard into the valley of the Po. Upon finding the area already occupied, they crossed the river and drove out the Umbrians and Etruscans, establishing themselves as far as the Apennines in the region of the modern Romagna.

Despite these migrations, some Boii remained in their original home. During the Second Punic War, a Boian prince named Magalus offered to guide Hannibal into Italy after the Carthaginian general had crossed the Pyrenees.

Additional settlements existed on the Danube, adjoining Raetia and Vindelicia on the land of their kinsmen, the Taurisci. While Strabo suggests the Boii were driven back across the Alps to settle there, most authorities believe a settlement existed on the Danube from very early times in part of modern Bohemia, which was known as *Boiohemum* ("land of the Boii"). Around 60 B.C., some Boii migrated to Pannonia and Noricum. During this period, 32,000 Boii joined a Helvetian expedition into Gaul, where they suffered defeat near Bibracte.

## Conflict with Rome
The Boii engaged in several wars with the Roman Republic. In 283, they were defeated alongside the Etruscans at the Vadimonian lake. Later, in 224, they were forced to submit following the battle of Telamon in Etruria.

Despite their submission, the Boii maintained a hatred for the Romans. They were further irritated by the Roman foundation of the colonies of Placentia and Cremona. Consequently, during the Second Punic War (218), they provided valuable assistance to Hannibal. The Boii continued their struggle against Rome until 191, when they were finally subdued by P. Cornelius Scipio Nasica and lost nearly half of their territory.

## Culture and Decline
Evidence from tombs at La Tene, Hallstatt, and other sites indicates that the Boii were fairly civilized. According to Cato, they were composed of as many as 112 different tribes.

In the region of Bohemia, the Boii established a capital called Boiohemum. However, they were weakened by continuous warfare with neighboring tribes. Around 12 B.C., they were subdued by the Marcomanni, a Teutonic tribe. The Boii who had settled in Noricum were later mingled with the Lombards and became known as the Boiovarii or Boio Arii (Bavaria), with "var" appearing in some German dialects to mean descendants or remains.

Following the period of Boian habitation, the Marcomanni were eventually expelled by other Teutonic tribes, and Bohemia was later conquered by Slavic tribes, most notably the Cechs, who became masters of the country in the 5th century.
