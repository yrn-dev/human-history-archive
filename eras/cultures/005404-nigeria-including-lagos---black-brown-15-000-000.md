# Nigeria

## Overview and Demographics
Nigeria, located in West Africa, is described as a well-populated region. According to archival estimates, the population of Nigeria, which includes Lagos, is 15,000,000 people categorized as black and brown. Within the broader context of West Africa, Nigeria represents the largest portion of the estimated 18,863,000 native population for that region.

The racial composition of the population is varied. While the aboriginal nations across Africa are mostly of the black negroid type, Nigeria is noted for having a large proportion of the population that is Arab and relatively light-skinned.

## Political History and Administration
The British presence in the region began with the cession of Lagos in 1861. Between 1884 and 1886, Nigeria was established through a combination of treaty, conquest, and settlement under a royal charter. In 1900, the territory of the chartered company was transferred to the crown, at which point the region was divided into North and South Nigeria. Later, in 1906, South Nigeria was amalgamated with Lagos to form the Colony and Protectorate of Southern Nigeria.

## The Province of Illorin
Illorin is a province within the protectorate of Nigeria in British West Africa, covering an area of 6,300 square miles. It has an estimated population of approximately 250,000 people consisting of various tribes, though the Yoruba currently predominate. Under British administration, the province is organized into three divisions:
*   **Illorin** (central)
*   **Offa** (southern)
*   **Patiji** (northern)

The province contains two minor emirates, Lafiagi and Shonga, as well as several semi-independent towns, including Patiji, Offa, Ajassa, and Awton. The capital is the town of Illorin, which is located 160 miles north-northeast of Lagos and 50 miles south-southwest of Jebba, a port on the Niger. The town is enclosed by a mud wall with a circuit of some 10 miles, though parts of the wall are in ruins.

## Economy and Trade
The region is rich in sylvan and agricultural products. Sylvan resources include rubber, palm-oil, and great quantities of shea. Agricultural products include kolas, ground-nuts, peppers, rice, cotton, and tobacco.

Illorin serves as a major trading center. Goods from central Africa are brought by Hausa caravans, and merchandise from the Mediterranean coasts is distributed from Illorin to Benin, Dahomey, and the Lagos hinterland. Trade from the Guinea coast is primarily managed by the Yoruba and arrives chiefly through Lagos.

The province also supports various manufacturing industries, including the production of pottery, embroidered work, finely plaited mats, carved wooden vessels, leather goods, and shoes made of red and yellow leather.

Historically, traders from the south were prohibited from entering the city of Illorin, with few exceptions; instead, Illorin middlemen handled all business between northern traders (who were barred from traveling south) and southern traders. Following the establishment of British authority, the town was opened to the public. This led to an influx of petty traders from Lagos, resulting in the issuance of 4,000 to 5,000 trade licenses annually. In 1904, the British resident estimated that at least 3,000 loads of British cotton goods, valued at £5 per load, were imported.

## Geography and Borders
Nigeria is bordered to the west by Dahomey, a French colony. The colony of Dahomey is bounded to the east by British Nigeria.
