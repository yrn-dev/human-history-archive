# Moldau

The Moldau, known in Czech as the *Vltava*, is the principal river of Bohemia from every point of view. While Bohemia belongs to the watershed of the Elbe, the hydrographic structure of the region indicates that the Moldau serves as the main stem, with the Elbe and other rivers acting as lateral branches. This is further evidenced by the fact that below Melnik—the point where the two rivers meet—the Elbe follows the general direction of the Moldau.

## Physical Characteristics and Basin
The Moldau has a length of 270 miles, making it 47 miles longer than the Upper-Elbe. Its basin covers 11,890 square miles, an area twice as large as that of the Elbe. The river is characterized by greater width and depth than the Elbe. 

The river's course includes a deep and narrow valley extending from above Budweis to approximately 15 miles south of Prague; this specific section of the valley is notable for having neither roads nor railways. The Moldau receives several tributaries, including the Luzniza and Sazawa on its right bank, and the Wottawa and Beraun on its left. The Beraun, formed by the union of the Radbusa, Angel, Uslawa, and Mies, is considered the third most important river in the country. Additionally, the Lainsitz is a tributary of the Moldau located in Lower Austria.

## Commercial and Navigational Importance
The Moldau serves as the principal commercial artery of Bohemia. Unlike the Upper-Elbe, the Moldau is navigable below Budweis. The town of Budweis is situated at the junction of the Moldau and the Maltsch, serving as a river port and a major commercial center for South Bohemia. Trade conducted via the river includes timber, salt, industrial products, corn, lignite, and beer, with the latter being exported largely to America.

Efforts have been made to integrate the Moldau into larger European navigable channels. The Schwarzenberg canal, located between the Moldau and the Great Muehl, establishes a direct navigable route between the Elbe and the Danube. Furthermore, projects have been proposed for a 125-mile canal connecting the Danube to the Moldau at Budweis. If completed and the Moldau regularized, this would form the final link in a 1,875-mile navigable channel stretching from Hamburg at the mouth of the Elbe to Sulina at the mouth of the Danube.

## Notable Locations and History
Several significant towns and landmarks are situated along the banks of the Moldau:

*   **Prague:** The imperial forces occupied the port of Prague, located on the left bank of the Vltava (Moldau), on the evening of November 8, 1620, following the defeat of the Bohemians at the "Bila Hora" (White Hill).
*   **Budweis:** A town and river port where the Moldau becomes navigable. Nearby in the Moldau valley stands the castle of Frauenberg, built in the mid-19th century by Prince Schwarzenberg on the site of a 13th-century castle.
*   **Krumau (Krumlov):** A town on the banks of the Moldau known for its ancient castle, which was the stronghold of the Rosenberg family (*pani z ruze*). The town was raised to the rank of a city by Peter I. of Rosenberg, who died in 1349.
