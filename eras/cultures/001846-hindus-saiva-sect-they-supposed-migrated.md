# Hindus of the Saiva Sect and the Badagas

The Saiva sect of Hinduism is associated with the Badagas, a tribe residing in the Nilgiri Hills of India. The term "Badagas" literally translates to "a Telugu man." While some authorities have debated their origins, declaring them not to be a jungle race or an aboriginal one, they are likely Dravidian by descent.

## Migration and History
The Badagas are believed to have migrated to the Nilgiris from Mysore around A.D. 1600. This movement occurred following the breaking up of the kingdom of Vijayanagar. 

## Society and Economy
The Badagas are an agricultural people and are characterized as the wealthiest and most numerous of the tribes inhabiting the hills. As part of their social and economic interactions with other groups, they pay a tribute in grain and other goods to the Todas. 

## Language and Population
The language spoken by the Badagas is described as a corrupt form of Kanarese. According to census data from 1901, the population of the Badagas numbered 34,178.

## Broader Context of Indian Populations
The Badagas' likely Dravidian descent places them within one of the strata of the Indian population. Dravidians, including the Kanarese and Tamils, are viewed by some as the earliest northern invaders who developed their own culture. In prehistoric times, Dravidians and Kolarians—who some authorities believe represent a single physical type—were spread across the whole of India. They were eventually driven toward the south and the center of the peninsula by the arrival of Aryans and potentially by Mongolian races from the north-west.

The Aryans, who divided into the Iranians and the Hindus, are believed to have migrated south-eastwards around 1500 B.C. from southern Russia or Asia Minor. By the 6th century B.C., they had established absolute monarchies in the Ganges valley, where the power of the king was tempered by the Brahmans, a hereditary sacerdotal class. This class is linked to the institution of caste, which divides the population based partly on racial distinctions.
