# Ethics and Oriental Religions

The study of ethics and the exploration of oriental religions are documented through various philosophical treatises and historical analyses. These fields encompass the study of moral theory, the behavior of ancient casuists, and the syncretic nature of religious beliefs in the Roman era.

## Philosophical Works on Ethics
The academic study of ethics is represented by several key texts and authors. T.H. Green authored *Prolegomena to Ethics* (Oxford, 1883), and James Martineau wrote *Types of Ethical Theory* (Oxford, 1885). Other significant contributions to the field of ethics include F.H. Bradley's *Ethical Studies* (1876), which is noted as a text that devotes attention to the matter of casuists. 

Further developments in ethical theory are found in the works of J. Dewey (*Ethics*, 1891), W.R. Sorley (*Ethics of Naturalism*, 2nd ed., 1904), J.S. Mackenzie (*Manual of Ethics*, 4th ed., 1900), and J.H. Muirhead (*Elements of Ethics*, 3rd ed., 1910). Additionally, Hastings Rashdall's *Theory of Good and Evil* (Oxford, 1907) serves as a resource for these studies.

## Casuistry and Moral Problems
The study of casuists—those who apply general ethical principles to specific cases—spans ancient, Catholic, and Anglican traditions. M. Raymond Thamin provided a study of ancient casuists in *Un Probleme moral dans l'antiquite* (Paris, 1884). 

Roman Catholic casuistry is examined in Dollinger und Reusch's *Moralstreitigkeiten im siebzehnten Jahrhundert* (1889), as well as in various articles on "Ethik," "Casuistik," and "Moralsysteme" within Wetzer and Welte's *Kirchenlexicon* (1880-1896). The *Provincial Letters* of Pascal, edited by John de Soyres and A. Molinier, also relate to these discussions. Anglican casuists are discussed in Whewell's *Lectures on Moral Philosophy* (London, 1862).

## Oriental Religions in Roman Paganism
Franz Cumont has contributed extensively to the understanding of oriental religions and their integration into Roman paganism. His work *Les Religions orientales dans le paganisme romain* (Paris, 1907) is described as an able and interesting study of syncretic religions that existed before Christianity. 

Within this work, Cumont specifically addresses the relationship between astrology and magic in chapter vii, titled "L'Astrologie et la magie." Cumont's other contributions to the study of ancient beliefs include *Mysteries of Mithra* (Chicago, 1903) and the *Catalogus Codicum Astrologorum Graecorum* (Brussels, 1898), the latter of which was published in seven parts up to 1909.

## Astrology and Related Beliefs
The history of astrology is studied in conjunction with its affinities to astronomy and other popular beliefs. Key references include A. Bouche-Leclercq's *L'Astrologie grecque* (1899) and *Histoire de la divination dans l'antiquite* (1879). Other scholarly works include Franz Boll's *Sphaera* (1903) and his research on ancient astrology published in *Neue Jahrbucher fur das klassische Altertum*.

Additional research into the astrology of Nineveh and Babylon is found in the reports of R.C. Thompson, while F.X. Kugler's *Sternkunde und Sterndienst in Babel* and Ch. Virolleaud's *L'Astrologie chaldeenne* provide further insights into cuneiform texts and Babylonian practices.
