# Simaruba excelsa

## Classification and Description
*Simaruba excelsa* is a plant known as the bitter ash of the West Indies. It is classified within the natural order Simarubaceae.

## Comparison with Other Ash Species
The bitter ash is distinct from several other trees that share the name "ash" or similar descriptors:

*   **Mountain Ash:** Known as *Pyrus Aucuparia*, this tree belongs to the natural order Rosaceae and the tribe Pomeae, which also includes pears and apples. In Scotland, it is called the rowan tree. It is characterized by succulent scarlet fruit, clusters of white blossoms, and pinnately compound leaves. Its resemblance to the true ash is found in its graceful ascending branches and smooth grey bark.
*   **Poison Ash:** This name refers to *Rhus venenata*, also known as the sumach or North American poison elder, which belongs to the Anacardiaceae (Cashew family).
*   **Cape Ash:** *Ekebergia capensis* is a large tree native to the Cape of Good Hope and belongs to the natural order Meliaceae.
*   **Prickly Ash:** *Xanthoxylon Clava-Herculis* is a small tree native to the south-eastern United States, belonging to the natural order Xanthoxyleae. It is distinguished by branches armed with sharp, stout, brown prickles and a trunk featuring corky tubercles.

## Etymology
The term "ash" (or "ashes" in the plural) is a common Teutonic word, with the German equivalent being *Asche*. It is connected to the Latin root *ardere*, meaning to burn, and is a homonym for the residue remaining after a body or other object has been burned.
