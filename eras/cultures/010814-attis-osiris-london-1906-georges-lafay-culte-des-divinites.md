# Attis

## Overview and Nature
Attis is identified as a sun-god and was a primary object of popular cult in Phrygia. In the religion of the Lydians, he stood alongside the supreme god Medeus. Within the mythological framework of these traditions, Attis is characterized as both the bridegroom and the son of Cybele (also referred to as Cybebe), who is known as the mother of the gods.

## Relation to the Great Mother and Other Deities
The relationship between Attis and the Great Mother goddess, Cybele, is comparable to the relationship between the Corybantes and the same deity. The Corybantes are described as half divine and half demonic beings of Asiatic origin who represent primitive gods of procreative significance. 

In Lydian tradition, the image of Cybele, carved by Broteas (the son of Tantalus), was adored on the cliffs of Sipylus. In Ephesus, Cybele was identified with the Greek Artemis and adored in the form of a meteoric stone. In the legend of Heracles, the figure of Omphale may have served as a Lydian title for Cybele.

## Rituals and Cult Practices
The cult of Cybele and Attis involved specific mysteries and rites. The Corybantic priests, and those representing Attis, were not originally priests but objects of worship. Their rites were characterized by an orgiastic nature, featuring:
* Frenzied dancing accompanied by flute music.
* The beating of tambourines and the clashing of castanets and cymbals.
* Wild cries and the self-infliction of wounds.

These activities culminated in states of exhaustion and ecstasy. The psychic effect of these dances was a popular feature of the cult, particularly during the initiation of the Cybele-Attis mysteries. It is suggested that such dances may have originated from a primitive belief in the ability of noise to avert evil.

Additionally, among Lydian girls, the practice of prostitution to gain dowries was considered a religious exercise marking their devotion to the goddess Cybele, a practice noted as being similar to that among the Semites.

## Cultural Context
The religion of the Lydians, which included the worship of Attis, was a form of nature worship that occasionally became sensuous. This religious system resembled that of other civilized nations in Asia Minor. It is possible that the cult was brought westward by the Hittites, who left memorials such as the figure of the Mother-goddess (the so-called Niobe) and the pseudo-Sesostris figures of Kara-bel.
