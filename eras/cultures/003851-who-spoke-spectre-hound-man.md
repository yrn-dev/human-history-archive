# The Spectre-Hound in Man

The "spectre hound in Man" refers to a specific figure from the folklore of the Isle of Man, where the creature is known as the Mauthe Doog. This entity is part of a broader tradition of spectre-hounds found throughout the British Isles and other regions.

## Regional Variations of the Spectre-Hound
The spectre-hound is a familiar element of folklore under various names depending on the location. In the north of England, particularly in Yorkshire, it is called the Barghest (also spelled Barguest or Bargest), described as a monstrous goblin-dog characterized by huge claws and teeth. Similar characteristics are shared by the Black Dog of Winchester, the Padfoot of Wakefield, and the Demon of Tedworth.

In other regions, the creature takes different forms:
*   **Wales:** The counterpart is Gwyllgi, known as the "Dog of Darkness," which is described as a frightful mastiff apparition with blazing red eyes and baleful breath. Another Welsh variant is the *Cwn Annwn*, or "dogs of hell."
*   **Lancashire:** The spectre-hound is referred to as Striker or Trash.
*   **Cambridgeshire and the Norfolk Coast:** It is known as Shock or Shuck. In Norfolk, the creature is believed to be amphibious, traveling lonely lanes at night after emerging from the sea.
*   **Isle of Man:** The creature is styled the Mauthe Doog.

## Literary Reference to the Mauthe Doog
The specific phrasing regarding who "spoke the spectre hound in Man" appears in the work of Sir Walter Scott. In "The Lay of the Last Minstrel," Scott describes a figure who was "speechless, ghastly, wan," comparing him to the individual from the stories who spoke the spectre hound in Man.

## Nature and Characteristics of the Barghest
The Barghest is essentially a nocturnal spectre, and its appearance is traditionally regarded as a portent of death. Belief in the spectre-hound persists in the wild areas of the north country of England; for example, in Nidderdale, Yorkshire, the name is used by nurses to frighten children.

The etymology of the word "barghest" is a subject of dispute. Some suggest it derives from *burh-ghest* ("town-ghost"), based on the northern English pronunciation of "ghost" as "guest." Other theories propose a German origin, suggesting *Berg-geist* ("mountain demon") or *Bar-geist* ("bear-demon"), the latter referring to claims that the creature sometimes appears as a bear.

## Related Folklore and Entities
The spectre-hound has parallels in other cultures, such as the *Rongeur d'Os* found in Norman folklore. Additionally, the Malay Peninsula has a legend of the Spectre Huntsman, a man who scours the sky with dogs in a vain search for a pregnant buck mouse-deer with male offspring. Unlike many spectre-hounds, the Spectre Huntsman is described as a living man rather than a spirit.
