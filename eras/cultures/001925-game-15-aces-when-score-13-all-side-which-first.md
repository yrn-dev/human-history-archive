# Scoring and "Setting" in Games of Aces

In certain competitive games played for aces, specific rules govern the conclusion of a game when the score reaches a tie near the winning threshold. These rules allow for the "setting" of the game, which alters the number of additional aces required to secure a victory.

## Setting the Game in 15-Ace Matches
In a game played for 15 aces, the option to "set" the game arises during two specific tied scores:

*   **At 13 all:** The side that first reaches the score of 13 has the option of "setting" the game to 5. If this occurs, the side that first scores 5 aces wins.
*   **At 14 all:** The side that first reaches the score of 14 has the option of "setting" the game to 3. In this instance, the side that first scores 3 aces wins.

## Variations by Game Type and Gender
The rules for setting vary depending on the category of the match and the total number of aces required for a win:

*   **Ladies' Singles:** In these games, which are played for 11 aces, the setting options occur earlier. When the score is "9 all," the side first reaching 9 may set the game to 5. When the score is "10 all," the side first reaching 10 may set the game to 3.
*   **Games of 21 Aces:** In matches played for 21 aces, the game may be set to 5 when the score is "19 all," and set to 3 when the score is "20 all."
*   **Handicap Games:** There is no "setting" permitted in handicap games.

## General Game Structure and Service
Games are typically played for 15 aces, though 21 aces are used in handicap contests and matches decided by a single game. A rubber consists of the best of three games. 

The initial service and choice of ends are determined by a toss. The winner of the toss may choose first service (leaving the other side to choose ends) or choose ends (leaving the other side to choose first service); alternatively, the toss winner may require the opponent to make the first choice.

### Changing Ends
The sides change ends at the start of the second and third games. In a third game, the timing of the change depends on the game type:
*   **15-ace games:** Sides change ends when the leading side reaches 8.
*   **11-ace games:** Sides change ends when the leading side reaches 6.
*   **Handicap games:** Sides change ends when either side's score reaches half the number of aces required to win.
*   **Single-game matches (21 aces):** Sides change ends when the leading side has scored 11 aces.

### Service Rules
The side that wins a game serves first in the subsequent game. In four-handed play, any player on the winning side may take the first service. The server begins in the right-hand half court, serving to the opponent in the opposite right-hand half court. 

Only the "in" (serving) side can score an ace. If the "in" side commits a fault, the server loses the "hand" to the opponent, but no score is recorded. If the "out" (non-serving) side commits a fault, the "in" side scores an ace, and the serving players change half courts. The server then serves from the left half court to the player in the opposite left half court who has not yet been served to.

In the first innings of a game, the starting side has only one "hand." In all following innings, every player on each side has a "hand," and partners serve consecutively.

## Service Interrupts and "Lets"
A stroke is considered good if the shuttle strikes the net but continues over. However, a "let" is called in the following service scenarios, meaning the stroke does not count and the server must repeat the service:
*   The shuttle strikes the net during service but is otherwise a good service (even if the opponent hits it).
*   The server misses the shuttle entirely while attempting to serve.
