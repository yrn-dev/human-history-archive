# Alopecia Seborrhoica

Alopecia is the technical term for baldness, derived from the Greek word *alopex* (meaning fox), as foxes frequently have bald patches on their coats. In humans, this condition results in the loss of hair, specifically on the scalp. Among the forms of alopecia that are remediable, alopecia seborrhoica is distinguished as a specific cause of premature baldness.

## Characteristics and Progression
Alopecia seborrhoica is a form of premature baldness commonly observed in young men. The condition is characterized by a steady advancement of hair loss that begins at the forehead and moves backwards. This progression continues until only a fringe of hair remains on the head. 

This specific type of hair loss is always caused by an underlying disease known as seborrhoea. While the condition progresses steadily if it is neglected, it is considered very amenable to treatment.

## Treatment and Remedies
Treatment for alopecia seborrhoica involves the application of specific drugs to the scalp. The two substances of greatest value for this condition are salicylic acid and sulphur. A recommended application consists of adding approximately eighteen grains of each of these drugs to one ounce of vaseline. This mixture should be rubbed thoroughly into the scalp on a daily basis for a prolonged period.

In cases where the greasiness of a vaseline-based application is objectionable, a salicylic lotion may be used as a substitute, although the vaseline application is likely of greater value. The composition of the salicylic lotion is as follows:
* Ac. salicyl.: i-iv dr.
* Ol. ricini: ii-iv dr.
* Ol. ros. geran.: x min.
* Spt. vini: up to vi oz.

Additionally, the head must be cleansed frequently. In very mild cases, a daily washing with soap spirit may occasionally result in a cure without further aid.

## Comparison with Other Forms of Hair Loss
Alopecia seborrhoica differs significantly from other types of hair loss described in the archive:

**Alopecia Areata**
Unlike the steady recession seen in alopecia seborrhoica, alopecia areata is characterized by the development of smooth, rounded, and somewhat depressed patches that are more or less completely denuded of hair. While most common on the scalp, it can occur anywhere hair naturally grows. Short broken hairs are typically visible at the margins of these patches. This condition is regarded as an epidemic disease and may be transmitted; it is associated with organisms called *Bacterium decalvens*, which can form a continuous sheath around the hair.

**Other Causes of Baldness**
The archive notes other causes of hair loss unrelated to seborrhoea. For example, loss of hair following acute fevers is treated with stimulating lotions, keeping the hair short, and attending to general hygiene. Additionally, historical records indicate that the baldness of the heads of the Babylonians was caused by the pressure of their helmets, serving as a badge of slavery during the siege of Tyre.

## Etymology
The adjective "bald" (Middle English "balled") is generally explained as meaning "round and smooth like a ball." However, it may also be connected to a stem *bal*, meaning shining or white, a derivation suggested by the Greek *phalakros*.
