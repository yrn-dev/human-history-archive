# Australia: Human and Natural History

## Indigenous Populations
The aboriginal tribes of Australia are described as a low-class black race. In their habits, they are generally characterized as inoffensive and peaceful. Within the broader context of Australasia and the Polynesian island groups, these populations are categorized into two main types: the dark and light Polynesians. The dark type is black and considered to be of a very low order; in some islands, this group still retains cannibal habits.

In terms of population estimates, the black population of Australia is listed at 200,000. Other groups in the region include 50,000 Chinese and half castes.

## Racial Classifications
Different historical frameworks provide various terms for the people of the Australian region. One classification identifies the black, primitive inhabitants of Australia and New Guinea as "Australoids." Another perspective suggests that Australia, along with South Africa and various tropical islands in southern Asia, contained remains of early negroid peoples.

It is noted that these racial labels are often loose-fitting generalizations or convenient terms for discussion rather than definitions of pure races. There is little to no proof that the black people of Australia, the negroes, and the "Asiatic negroids" (primitive black people found in the Andaman Islands or traces of negroid blood in south Persia and parts of India) derive from a single origin. Instead, it is suggested they may have lived under similar conditions for vast periods.

## Geography and Environment
Australia contains a desert, which is classified as a "hot desert." In such regions, the surface is unproductive because high temperatures and deficient rainfall cause evaporation to exceed precipitation. In Australia, as in Africa and America, the desert zone is influenced by the orientation of elevated land; where the line of elevation runs north and south, the desert zone is cut through on the windward side, while arid conditions are intensified on the lee side.

## Fauna
The region is home to specific animal species, including the Australian opossum. This marsupial—a class of animals generally not found outside of Australia—possesses a coat of close, even wool with sparse, fine top hair. The color of the fur varies by district:
* **Sydney:** Light clear blue.
* **Victoria:** Dark iron grey with stronger wool.
* **Adelaide:** Natural grey.
* **Other areas:** Varying from blue grey to yellow with reddish tones.

The Australian opossum is noted for being prolific and increasing in number. Its fur is used for items such as stoles, muffs, coat linings, and rugs.

Additionally, the region is home to specific avian species. The *M. isurus* (a type of kite) is peculiar to Australia. Other notable birds in the region include the black-winged kite congeners *E. axillaris* and *E. scriptus*.
