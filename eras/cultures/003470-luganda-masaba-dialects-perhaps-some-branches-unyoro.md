# Luganda, Masaba, and Unyoro Dialects

The Luganda, Masaba, and Unyoro dialects are members of the Bantu language family, characterized by the use of prefixes to govern unchangeable roots. These languages are primarily located in the region of the Great Lakes in East Africa.

## The Uganda-Unyoro Group
The Uganda-Unyoro group encompasses a wide geographical area. Its boundaries extend from the Victoria Nile and Busoga in the north and east to the Congo Forest and the Ruwenzori range in the west. To the south and south-east, it reaches the south coast of the Victoria Nyanza and extends to the east coast of Tanganyika and the Malagarazi river. This group also includes the inhabited islands of the Victoria Nyanza as far east as Bukerebe, though it excludes certain islands near the north-east coast.

Within this group, the language of the kingdom of Uganda is closely related to the dialects of the west coast of Lake Victoria, the Sese Islands, and the district of Busoga. While allied to this subsection, the Unyoro group is considered quite distinct. The Unyoro classification includes several kindred dialects, such as:
*   **Hima dialects:** Including *Uru-hima* and *Ru-hinda*.
*   **Other regional dialects:** *Ru-songora*, *Ru-iro*, *Ru-toro*, and *Ru-tusi*.
*   **Related tongues:** Dialects of Bukerebe, Businja, Ruanda, Busiba, and Karagwe.

Additionally, *Ki-rundi* (spoken in the Burundi country) and other languages of eastern Tanganyika down to Ufipa are closely allied to the Unyoro sub-section, although they may adhere more closely to the Tanganyika group. A third independent sub-section of the Uganda-Unyoro group is *Lu-konjo*, spoken on the southern flanks of the Ruwenzori Range, extending south to Lake Kivu and the eastern limits of the Congo Forest.

## The Kavirondo-Masaba Section
The Kavirondo-Masaba section is a distinct division of Bantu tongues. It is more closely related to the Luganda section than to any other Bantu group, though it is noted for having the most archaic prefixes. This section includes:
*   Languages spoken along the western flanks of Mount Elgon.
*   The languages of Bantu Kavirondo.
*   Languages of the eastern coast-lands of the Victoria Nyanza, specifically *Igizii*.
*   Other dialects of the north-east and eastern shores of the Victoria Nyanza, including *Luwanga*, *Lukonde*, *Lu-nyara*, and *Igizii*.

## Linguistic Characteristics and Prefixes
A defining feature of these languages is the system of prefixes. While sixteen prefixes are common to nearly all members of the Bantu family, certain specific prefixes are found in the Luganda and Masaba dialects, and potentially some branches of the Unyoro language. These include:
*   **Prefix No. 18 (*Ogu-*)** and **Prefix No. 19 (*Aga-*)**: These are used in an augmentative sense. They are regular prefixes, as they are supplied with the concord *-gu-* and *-ga-*.

In Luganda, the prefix *Wa-* is used, whereas in Lusoga (on the north coast of the Victoria Nyanza), this becomes *Gha* (represented as *[Gamma]a*). 

The Luganda language also demonstrates the "concord" system, where pronominal particles are repeated. For example, a reconstructed sentence in Old Bantu—*Babo mbaba-ntu babi ba-bo-ta tu-ba-oga*—is rendered in modern Luganda as *Bo aba-ntu ba-bi ba-bota tu-ba-tia*.

## Vocabulary and Archaism
The dialects of Uganda and Unyoro, along with those of Mount Elgon and Ruwenzori, are considered among the most archaic Bantu dialects existing today. 

Vocabulary comparisons show consistency across these regions. For instance, the word for "fowl" is *enkoko* in Luganda, other languages of Uganda and the Victoria Nyanza, and in Runyoro on the Victoria Nile. This differs from the Ki-Swahili word *kuku* or the Zulu *inkuku*.

## Terminology and Nomenclature
In historical and popular usage, the terms "Uganda" and "Unyoro" have been used to refer to the countries and their respective languages. However, these are noted as popular mis-spellings; the correct names for the countries are Buganda and Bunyoro, and the correct names for the languages are Luganda and Runyoro.
