# Le Lys dans la vallée

*Le Lys dans la vallée* is a literary work by the author Balzac. Within the context of Balzac's life and creative process, the work is noted for its connection to the author's personal relationships and the real-life inspirations for his characters.

## Inspiration and Character Origins
The character of Mme de Mortsauf in *Le Lys dans la vallée* was based on Madame de Berny. Madame de Berny was a woman connected with the court of the *ancien regime* and the mother of nine children. She was introduced to Balzac in 1821 and became known to him as *La dilecta*. 

Madame de Berny was one of three women of a higher social rank than Balzac who served as his "Egerias." The influence of these women was so significant that it is suggested Balzac would not have been the author he became without them.

## Context of Balzac's Work
*Le Lys dans la vallée* exists within a vast body of work produced by Balzac, whose materials were extensively collected by M. de Lovenjoul. Balzac's professional life was characterized by a lifelong financial pressure resulting from various irregularities, including speculations, travel, the collection of curiosities, and costly production methods, such as writing his novels on a succession of printed revises.

Balzac's output during the 1830s included several other notable works:
* **1830:** *La Maison du chat qui pelote* and *La Peau de chagrin* (which was not fully published until 1831).
* **1831:** *Le Chef-d'oeuvre inconnu*, *Le Requisitionnaire*, *Les Proscrits*, *Maitre Cornelius*, and *Jesus Christ en Flandre*.
* **1832:** *Le Colonel Chabert*, *Le Cure de Tours*, *Louis Lambert*, *La Bourse*, *La Femme abandonnee*, *Madame Firmiani*, *Le Message*, *La Grenadiere*, and *Les Marana*.
* **1833:** *Eugenie Grandet* (considered a universally admitted masterpiece), *L'Illustre Gaudissart*, *Le Medecin de campagne*, and the first part of *L'Histoire des treize* titled *Ferragus, chef des devorants*.
