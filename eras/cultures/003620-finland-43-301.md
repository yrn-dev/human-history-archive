# Finland

## Geography and Geology
Finland is located in Northern Europe, bordered by the Baltic basin. It is separated from Esthonia by the Gulf of Finland, an arm of the Baltic that runs eastwards for approximately 250 miles. To the west, an unbroken ridge extending from Stockholm to Hango separates the Baltic basin from the Aland Haf. Further north, Finland is bordered by the Bothnian Sea and the Gulf of Bothnia, which are separated by the Quarken (or Kvarken) Strait.

The geological history of the region includes a period of glacial subsidence where an arm of the sea extended across Sweden and submerged much of the littoral. Later, the area was covered by the Ancylus lake, a vast freshwater lake that included a large part of Finland and Lake Ladoga. Following subsequent periods of subsidence and elevation, the land reached its present form. Dr. R. Sieger's research on isobasic lines indicates that while elevation movement is nearly nil along the Gulf of Finland, it increases in amplitude toward the Gulf of Bothnia.

## Major Cities and Ports
Several key urban centers serve as administrative, religious, and commercial hubs:

*   **Borga (Porvoo):** A seaport in the province of Nyland located at the entrance of the river Borga into the Gulf of Finland. It is the seat of a court of appeal and a Lutheran bishopric covering the provinces of St Michel and Viborg, as well as parts of Nyland and Tavastehus. The city features a cathedral and a high school where the poet Runeberg once lectured. Its primary industries include the manufacture of tobacco and the weaving of sail-cloth, while its chief trade articles are furs, butter, and wood.
*   **Kotka:** A seaport located in the province of Viborg on an island at the mouth of the Kymmene river. As of 1904, its population was 7,628. It serves as a center for the timber trade and is the primary port for imports and exports for east Finland.
*   **Kuopio:** The capital of the province of the same name, situated on Lake Kalla-vesi. With a population of 13,519 in 1904, it is a significant trading center due to its steamer communication with the sea via the Saima Canal and middle Finland. It is the seat of a bishop and contains a cathedral, two gymnasia, two lyceums, and various professional and commercial schools, with an agricultural school located nearby at Leväis.

## History and Governance
In 1809, the estates of Finland met in a special diet at Borga to determine the future of the country. During this meeting, in the Borga cathedral, Emperor Alexander I pledged himself as the grand duke of Finland to maintain the liberties and constitution of the grand duchy.

Historical accounts also note the birth of Gustavus Adolphus in Finland. According to a prediction by the astronomer-royal of Denmark, Tycho Brahe—who studied the comet of 1577—a prince would be born in Finland who would lay waste Germany and vanish in 1632, which aligned with the life and death of Gustavus Adolphus.

Earlier chronicles describe conflicts involving the Finlanders, including an account of a king who sailed to Finland to plunder. While the king's forces moved inland to a valley called Herdaler, they were attacked by people rushing from the woods. The account claims the Finlanders used witchcraft to conjure a dreadful storm and bad weather at sea to hinder the king's retreat, though the king eventually escaped to the open sea.
