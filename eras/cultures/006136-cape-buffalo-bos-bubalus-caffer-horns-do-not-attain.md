# Buffaloes

Buffaloes are heavily built oxen characterized by broad muzzles, long tufted tails, large ears, and massive angulated horns. They possess 13 pairs of ribs, a feature that makes them resemble typical oxen. Their skin is sparsely haired. While the term "buffalo" is used in popular American parlance to designate the American bison, it properly refers to a species of cattle native to India and Africa.

## The Cape Buffalo (_Bos (Bubalus) caffer_)

The Cape buffalo is found in Central and Southern Africa, where it typically remains in the near vicinity of water. In South Africa, these animals frequent reedy swamps, associating in herds of fifty to a hundred or more individuals, though old bulls may be found alone or in small parties ranging from two or three to ten. While they formerly roamed the plains in large herds, their numbers have greatly diminished; however, the government protects some herds in the eastern forest-districts of Cape Colony.

The species is brown rather than black and has never been domesticated. Like the Indian buffalo, the Cape buffalo is fond of water, visiting it at regular intervals throughout the day. To protect itself from the bite of gadflies, which cause considerable annoyance despite the animal's thick hide, it plasters itself with mud. This mud protects the animal once it has been hardened by the sun.

The Cape buffalo also maintains a relationship with the red-beaked rhinoceros bird (_Buphaga erythrorhynca_). These birds feed on parasitic ticks common to thick-skinned animals, often perching on the buffalo's horns or moving across its back. These birds are known as "the buffalo's guardian birds" because they provide timely warnings to the buffalo regarding the proximity of hunters, though hunters also use the birds to locate the animals.

### Physical Variations in Africa
The horns of the Cape buffalo do not reach an excessive length. However, in old bulls, the horns are thickened and expanded at the base, creating a helmet-like mass that protects the forehead. Local races vary by region:
* **Eastern Africa (_B. caffer aequinoctialis_):** These buffaloes have smaller horns that do not meet at the middle line.
* **West Africa (_B. nanus_):** Known as the red dwarf buffalo, this animal is approximately two-thirds the size of the southern variety and possesses relatively small, flattened horns that curve upward.

## The Indian Buffalo (_Bos (Bubalus) bubalis_)

The typical Indian buffalo is restricted to Ceylon and India, though local races may exist in the Islands and Malay Peninsula. This species has been introduced to Italy, Egypt, and Asia Minor.

### Distinctions from African Species
The Indian buffalo is distinguished from African forms by several physical markers:
* **Horns:** The horns are larger and more widely separated.
* **Head and Ears:** The head is narrower and more elongated, and the ears are less thickly fringed.
* **Hair:** The hair on the fore-part of the back is directed forwards, whereas in African buffaloes, the hair on the back is directed backwards.

### Local Races and Behavior
In Assam, two distinct races have been noted: _B. bubalis macrocercus_, which has immense horns directed mainly outwards rather than curving circularly upward, and _B. bubalis fulvus_, which is characterized by tawny hair and hide instead of black.

Indian buffaloes inhabit grass-jungles near swamps where grass can exceed 20 feet in height. In these areas, they create covered pathways that keep them completely concealed. They are fond of wallowing in marshes and are known to cause damage to crops. 

### Reproduction and Strength
The rutting season occurs in autumn, during which a single male is followed by several females. The gestation period lasts ten months, resulting in the birth of one or two calves. The bull is noted for its immense strength, said to be capable of overthrowing an elephant and generally superior to the tiger in combat. Tame buffaloes with massive horns are used by Indian drivers to protect against tigers in the jungle.

## Other Species and Related Cattle

The tamarao, or Philippine buffalo (_Bos (Bubalus) mindorensis_), is a smaller animal that serves as an intermediate form between the dwarf anoa (Celebes buffalo, _B. depressicornis_) and the Indian buffalo. In the Indian region of Asia, other species of true _Bos_ include _B. sondaicus_ and _B. frontalis_.

Historical records indicate that in the sixth century, a large wild species of horned cattle—referred to by various names including bubalus, aurochs, and bison—was hunted in the Ardennes and the forests of the Vosges in Lorraine. Additionally, ancient accounts from the land of the nomads in Libya mention the existence of buffaloes among other wild animals.
