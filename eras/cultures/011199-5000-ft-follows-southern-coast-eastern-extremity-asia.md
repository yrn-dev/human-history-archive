# The Stanovor Range and Eastern Asian Geography

## The Stanovor Range
The Stanovor is a range of mountains located at the eastern extremity of Asia. These mountains rise to heights of 4,000 or 5,000 feet and follow the southern coast of the region, extending from Kamchatka to the borders of Manchuria. This range reaches as far as the 135th meridian at 55 degrees North latitude.

## Adjacent Mountain Ranges and Drainage
Continuing in the same direction as the Stanovor is the Yablonoi range. This range serves as a drainage divide between two major river systems: the river Lena, which flows through Siberia into the Arctic Sea, and the river Amur, which falls into the North Pacific. The basin of the Amur river and its affluents constitute the region of Manchuria.

Further south, from the north of Manchuria, the Khingan range stretches toward the Chinese frontier near Peking. The drainage to the east of the Khingan range falls into the Yellow Sea and the Amur, while the region to the west is an almost rainless area that inclines toward Mongolia, the central area of the continent.

## Continental Outline and Boundaries
When viewed on a globe, Asia takes the form of a great isosceles spherical triangle. Its north-eastern apex is located at East Cape (Vostochnyi) in the Bering Strait. The two equal sides of this triangle are approximately 6,500 miles in length; one extends west to the southern point of Arabia, and the other extends east to the extremity of the Malay peninsula. The base of the triangle occupies about 60 degrees of a great circle, or 4,500 miles, and is deeply indented by the Bay of Bengal and the Arabian Sea on either side of the Indian peninsula.

The boundaries of the continent are defined as follows:
*   **North:** Formed by the Arctic Ocean, with the coastline falling between 70 and 75 degrees North. The extreme northern point is Cape Sivero-Vostochnyi (Chelyuskin) at 78 degrees North.
*   **East:** The most easterly point is East Cape (Vostochnyi or Dezhnev) at 190 degrees East. The boundary from this point to the Malay Peninsula follows the China Sea and the Northern Pacific, featuring the recession of the Yellow Sea and the Sea of Okhotsk, as well as the projections of Korea and Kamchatka.
*   **South:** The coastline is irregular, with the China Sea, the Bay of Bengal, and the Arabian Sea reaching approximately to the northern tropic. The Malay peninsula extends to within a degree and a half of the equator, while the peninsulas of Cambodia, Hindostan, and Arabia descend to about 10 degrees North.
*   **West:** The extreme western point is Cape Baba on the Mediterranean shore at 26 degrees East. The boundary extends from here through the Red Sea to the southern point of Arabia at the strait of Bab-el-Mandeb (45 degrees East), or alternatively through the Black Sea, along the Caucasus range (approximately 40 degrees North) to the Caspian, and then north along the Ural Mountains to the Arctic Ocean.

## Regional Plateaus and Elevations
West of British India (excluding Syria and Arabia) lies an extensive plateau. Its eastern flank consists of a line of hills located a short distance from the Indus, between the Himalaya and the mouth of the river, roughly on the 72nd meridian. While these hills generally do not exceed 5,000 or 4,000 feet in elevation, some summits reach 10,000 feet or more.

To the south and southwest, the plateau's face follows the Persian Gulf coast from the mouth of the Indus. Further west, a mountain scarp flanks the Mesopotamian plains and the Tigris, rising in many points to 10,000 feet and extending through Armenia and Kurdistan to nearly the 40th meridian, before turning along the Taurus range.

## Climate and Environment
Northern Asia contains some of the lowest temperatures on the globe, with the mean temperature of the north coast of eastern Siberia being only a few degrees above zero Fahrenheit. In contrast, southern Asia contains some of the highest temperatures, with the highest mean temperature not exceeding 82 degrees Fahrenheit by much.

The continent features diverse environmental zones:
*   **Rainless Regions:** An almost rainless region exists between the margin of the temperate alpine zone of north China and the Himalaya, encompassing Mongolia, Turkestan, western Manchuria, and central Asia. This area is characterized by intense summer heat and extreme winter severity.
*   **Hot and Dry Tracts:** An almost unbroken desert extends from Arabia through Baluchistan and south Persia to Sind.
*   **Humid Regions:** India, southern China, the Indo-Chinese peninsula, and the western Malay Archipelago are characterized by high temperatures and heavy periodical summer rains.
