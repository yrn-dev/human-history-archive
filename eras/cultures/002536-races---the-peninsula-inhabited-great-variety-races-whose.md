# Races

The distribution of human races is characterized by complex migrations, linguistic shifts, and ethnic admixtures, often resulting in populations whose ethnological limits do not correspond with existing political boundaries.

## Global Racial Divisions and the Aryan Group
Early racial divisions of mankind include the brownish peoples of the Heliolithic culture, who are the ancestors of the bulk of the inhabitants of the Mediterranean world, the Egyptians, the Berbers, and much of the population of South and Eastern Asia. This group includes various divisions such as the Dravidians, the Maoris, Polynesian races, and the "dark-white" Iberian or Mediterranean race of the Atlantic and Mediterranean coasts.

The term "Aryan" refers to races speaking languages derived from or akin to ancient Sanskrit. They occupy a temperate zone extending from the Mediterranean, through Persia and Afghanistan, to India. The "Semitic" races, speaking languages akin to ancient Assyrian (represented mainly by Arabic), occupy North Africa and countries south-west of Persia, including Arabia and Syria. Both Aryans and Semites are considered branches of the Melanochroi, modified by admixture with the Xanthochroi, Australoids, and Mongols.

Within the Aryan group, Nordic peoples pressed into the Italian and Greek peninsulas, developing Greek and Latin tongues. Other Nordic Aryan tribes moved toward the Baltic and Scandinavia, speaking varieties that became Gothic, Low and High German, and ancient Norse (the parent of Icelandic, Norwegian, Danish, and Swedish). The Angles and Saxons belonged to the Teutonic stock, a branch of the Indo-Germanic (Indo-European) family also represented by the people of Germany, Holland, Denmark, and England.

## Populations of the Balkan Peninsula
The Balkan Peninsula is inhabited by a variety of races. The Slavonic population, including Bulgars and Serbo-Croats, is the most numerous, exceeding 10,000,000. Serbo-Croats settled in the peninsula in the 7th century. While a distinction between Croats of the north-west and Serbs of the central region was established by the 12th century, this is based on religious difference rather than racial or linguistic ones. Roman Catholic Croats predominate in north-western Bosnia and Dalmatia due to Latin civilization and intercourse with Italy, while Orthodox Serbs inhabit Montenegro, Herzegovina, parts of Bosnia, the kingdom of Servia, and Old Servia.

The Turkish population, totaling scarcely over 1,800,000, consists of descendants of 14th and 15th-century Ottoman invaders and later colonists from Asia. Their numbers declined during the 19th century; they have disappeared entirely in the rest of Greece and almost entirely in Servia, though they remain in north-eastern Bulgaria and the region between the Sea of Marmora, the Black Sea, and Adrianople.

In Bulgaria, the national element is strongest in the Rhodope and Sredna Gora. The Pomaks, or Mahommedan Bulgarians, are considered some of the most genuine representatives of the race; they are Muslims who retained their Bulgarian speech and national usages. Other groups include the Gagäuzi, Turkish-speaking Christians of Turanian origin descended from the ancient Kumani, and the Shôp tribe of the Sofia, Radomir, and Breznik mountain tracts, who may be remnants of an aboriginal race. Additionally, a Vlach or Ruman colony exists in the Danubian districts, having sought refuge from the tyranny of nobles in the 18th century.

## Macedonia and the Slavic Migrations
The bulk of the rural population in Macedonia speaks a Slavonic dialect. Slavs first crossed the Danube in the 3rd century, with major immigrations occurring in the 6th and 7th centuries. They drove the Albanians into the Mirdite country, the Greeks to the Aegean shores, and the latinized population into highland districts like Olympus, Agrapha, and Pindus. In the 7th century, the Bulgarians—an Ugro-Finnish horde—conquered the Slavs of the central and eastern portions, though the Bulgarians were eventually absorbed by the larger subjected race. The Macedonian Slavs exhibit a physical type resulting from the admixture of Turkish, Albanian, Hellenic, and Vlach blood.

## Asian and Pacific Races
The Malays are a widely scattered seafaring race occupying the Malay Peninsula, the Philippines, Borneo, Sumatra, Java, and Madagascar. They are described as Mongols modified by maritime life and climate. Their history includes three periods: a semi-barbarous first stage (seen in tribes like the Dyaks), a second stage influenced by Hindu civilization (Brahminical Indians), and a subsequent adoption of Islam before the 16th century.

In the region of Bundelkhand, the Bundelas are the race that gave the country its name; they serve as chieftains and disdain cultivating the soil. In the territory behind the Kowloon peninsula, the population consists of the agricultural Punti (Cantonese), the hardy and frugal Hakka of the hill districts, and the Tanka, who are the floating boat population.
