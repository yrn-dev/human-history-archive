# Stassfurtite and Related Mineral Forms

In the study of mineralogy, certain substances appear in both crystalline forms and massive varieties. One such example is stassfurtite, which is the massive variety of the mineral boracite.

## Boracite and Stassfurtite
Boracite is a magnesium borate and chloride with the chemical formula $\text{Mg}_7\text{Cl}_2\text{B}_{16}\text{O}_{30}$. It is characterized by its insolubility in water, though it is soluble in hydrochloric acid. The mineral is subject to slow alteration through the absorption of water by magnesium chloride, a process that results in an altered form known as parasite.

Boracite typically appears as small, transparent to translucent crystals with a vitreous lustre, often appearing colourless or white, though they may be tinged with green, yellow, or grey. These crystals are found embedded in anhydrite and gypsum within the salt deposits at Luneburg in Hanover. They are noted for their optical anomalies and pyroelectric properties; for instance, a cube of boracite becomes positively electrified on four corners and negatively on the four opposite corners when heated.

In addition to these embedded crystals, boracite occurs in a massive variety known as stassfurtite. This variety is found as nodules in the salt deposits at Stassfurt in Prussia. The appearance of stassfurtite varies depending on its geological layer:
* **Carnallite layer:** The variety is compact, white or greenish in colour, and resembles fine-grained marble.
* **Kainite layer:** The variety is yellowish or reddish in colour and is characterized as soft and earthy.

## Other Massive and Crystalline Varieties
The distinction between massive and crystalline forms is common across various mineral species in the archive.

### Calcite
Calcite, consisting of calcium carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$), is one of the most widely distributed minerals. In its massive condition, it forms large rock-masses such as limestone, chalk, and marble. These are often organic in origin, formed from the remains of crinoids, corals, and molluscs. Calcite also appears in other forms based on its state of aggregation, such as the finely fibrous satin-spar or the soft, dull white earthy varieties known as rock-meal, rock-milk, or agaric mineral.

### Corundum
Corundum is composed of native alumina ($\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$). While its transparent and finely coloured varieties are known as sapphire and ruby, its impure massive and granular forms are known as emery.

### Datolite
Datolite is a basic calcium and boron orthosilicate ($\text{Ca}(\text{BOH})\text{SiO}_4$). While it often occurs as glassy crystals with pentagonal outlines, it also appears as masses with a compact or granular texture. When the mineral is compact, its fractured surfaces resemble porcelain.

## Embedded Crystals in Geological Matrices
The archive records several instances where crystals are embedded within other mineral matrices:
* **Danburite:** In Danbury, Connecticut, crystals of danburite occur with oligoclase and microcline embedded in dolomite.
* **Dolomite:** Crystallized masses of dolomite are found embedded in talc-schist, serpentine, and other magnesian silicate rocks.
* **Granite:** In rocks such as granite, crystals of hornblende, mica, or felspar are embedded in a quartz matrix.
* **Amorphous Ground-mass:** In complex mixtures like slags or acid lavas containing high silica, crystals of various sizes may remain embedded in an amorphous matrix that sets as the mother liquor increases in viscosity during cooling.
