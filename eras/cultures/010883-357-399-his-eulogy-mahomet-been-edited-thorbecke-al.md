# A'SHA (MAIMUN IBN QAIS)

A'sha, born as Maimun ibn Qais, was an Arabian poet whose life spanned the period before and during the mission of Mahomet. Born in the village of Manfuha, located in al-Yemama in the center of Arabia, A'sha lived as a wandering singer. His travels took him across the breadth of Arabia, ranging from al-Hira in the north to Hadramut in the south. During these travels, he frequently visited the annual fair at Okaz (Ukaz).

## Poetic Works and Style
The poetry of A'sha is noted for its technical skill and variety. His works are praised for their use of diverse metres and his proficiency in both satire and praise. Specifically, his poems are recognized for their descriptions of the wild ass and for the praise of wine. In his romantic poetry, A'sha devoted his love poems to a black female slave named Huraira.

Among his body of work, his most well-known poem is a eulogy written in praise of Mahomet.

## Beliefs and Influences
Records indicate that A'sha held monotheistic beliefs and believed in the last judgment and the resurrection even before the time of Mahomet. It is suggested that these specific convictions may have resulted from his interactions with the `Ibadites (Christians) of al-Hira and the bishop of Nejran (Najran). A'sha lived long enough to accept the mission of the prophet.

## Archival Collections and Editions
The poems of A'sha have been gathered from various sources and are included in L. Cheikho's *Les Poetes arabes chretiens*, published by the Jesuit press in Beirut in 1890, specifically on pages 357-399. Additionally, his eulogy of Mahomet was edited by H. Thorbecke in the work *Al Asa's Lobgedicht auf Muhammad*, published in Leipzig in 1875.
