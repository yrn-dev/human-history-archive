# New Guinea

## Geography and Location
New Guinea, also referred to as Papua, is an island located in the Pacific Islands region. It extends almost to the same meridian as the eastern coast of Australia. The two landmasses are separated by the Torres Straits, which lie at the north point of Australia. On its north coast, the island features a cape and an island (also known as Kairu) that bear the name of D'Urville.

The interior of New Guinea is largely unknown. However, it is reported that the mountains in the interior rise to heights of 20,000 ft. and appear to be permanently covered with snow. The general surface of the island seems to be clothed in thick wood.

## Inhabitants
The inhabitants of New Guinea are described as being of the Negrito type, characterized by bushy, curly, or crisp hair. There is a distinction in the social conditions of the population based on geography: those residing on the west coast have had more communication with traders from other islands and are considered fairly civilized. In contrast, many of the tribes located further eastward are described as barbarous savages.

## Fauna
The biological characteristics of New Guinea mark a transition in regional fauna. Deer do not extend into the island. Additionally, the genus *Sus* appears to reach its eastern limit within New Guinea.

## Political Status
New Guinea is listed as one of the notable exceptions within the British Empire where the land is not suitable for white settlement, unlike the greater portion of the empire which lies within temperate zones.
