# Fula Language and People

The Fula (also known as Fulbe, Fellatah, or Peuls) are a numerous and powerful African people distributed across an immense region extending from Senegal nearly to Darfur. While they have no country of their own and do not form the entirety of the population in any single area, they are nearly always the dominant native race. Their populations are most dense in the north and west, and more scattered toward the south and east.

## Distribution and Political History
Fula populations are most numerous in Upper Senegal and the regions south of Senegambia, specifically Futa Jallon. Under French control, they rule Massena and Segu on both banks of the upper Niger, southwest of Timbuktu. Large populations also exist within the great bend of the Niger. In the region east of the Niger, Fula princes rule Sokoto and its tributary emirates under British Nigerian administration. Their most southerly emirate is Adamawa, located on both sides of the upper Benue. Additionally, Fula are settled in Wadai, Bornu, Bagirmi, and the upper Nile Valley, though they possess no political power in these specific countries.

Originally herdsmen in the central and western Sudan, the Fula extended their influence east of the Niger during the early 19th century under the leadership of Othman Dan Fodio. After subduing the Hausa states, they established the empire of Sokoto, which included vassal emirates such as Nupe, Gando, Kano, and Adamawa.

## Linguistic Characteristics
The Fula language (also referred to as Fulfulde) has not yet been placed within any specific African linguistic family. In its rudiments, it is akin to the Hamito-Semitic group. The language is characterized by several distinct grammatical and phonetic features:

*   **Gender:** It possesses two grammatical genders: the human and the non-human.
*   **Agreement and Inflection:** Euphony is a significant factor in nominal and verbal inflections, and the adjective agrees in assonance with its noun.
*   **Structure:** The language utilizes suffixes, a feature that betrays non-Negroid influences and distinguishes it from certain Negro dialects.

## Comparative Linguistics
In the study of African languages, Fula is often analyzed in relation to the Bantu type of language. While the exact origin of Bantu remains a puzzle, some suggest that a form of speech like Fula, Kposo of northern Togoland, or Kiama (Tern) may have been adopted by Nilotic, Hamite, or ancient Libyan conquerors and cast into the Bantu type. 

Fula shows some resemblance to Bantu in its grammatical construction, specifically where suffixes take the place of prefixes. Furthermore, Fula possesses a form of concord considerably similar to that of the Bantu. There are also affinities found in a few verbal, pronominal, and nominal roots, as well as in the numerals 3 and 4.

## Nomenclature and Physical Description
The name for the Fula people varies across different languages:
*   **Fulbe or Fula:** The Mandingan name (singular: Pullo or Peul).
*   **Follani:** The Hausa name.
*   **Fellatah:** The Kanuri name.
*   **Fullan:** The Arab name.
*   **Fulde:** The name used on the Benue.

These variations, along with the name "Abate" (meaning "white") used in Kororofa, appear to refer to the light reddish hue of the people's complexion.
