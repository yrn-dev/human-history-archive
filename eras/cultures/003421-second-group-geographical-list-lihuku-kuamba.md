# Lihuku-Kuamba

The Lihuku-Kuamba group constitutes the second category on a geographical classification of Bantu speech. These dialects are described as separate and somewhat peculiar, lingering in territories located to the south and south-west of Albert Nyanza, specifically within the Mboga country.

## Dialect Composition and Distribution
The group is composed of two primary dialects: Lihuku and Kuamba.

*   **Lihuku:** Also referred to as Libvanuma, Lihuku is characterized as a very isolated type of Bantu. Despite being in close juxtaposition with the Uganda-Unyoro groups, it is quite apart from them and exhibits no special affinity with those groups.
*   **Kuamba:** This dialect is spoken on the north and west slopes of the Ruwenzori mountains.

While Lihuku and Kuamba are grouped together, the alliance between the two is noted as not being very close.

## Linguistic Affinities
The Lihuku-Kuamba tongues show a marked relationship with languages found on the Aruwimi and northern Congo. This relationship is more evident in their vocabulary than in their grammar. Additionally, Lihuku shows affinities with the degraded Bantu dialects of the Ituri-Aruwimi forests, such as Ki-bira.

Further analysis indicates that the Lihuku-Kuamba section is connected with the Manyema (or Baenya) group. The Manyema group comprises most of the corrupt Bantu dialects located between the main stream of the Luapula-Congo and the western watershed of Tanganyika, extending further north to include languages of the Aruwimi basin, such as Lokele, Soko, Kusu, Tu-rumbu, and Yalulema. The Kibira dialects of the Ituri district in the north-eastern Congo Forest may potentially be placed within this Manyema section.

## Regional Classification
In a broader classification of Bantu speech, the domain is divided into four great sections. The Lihuku-Kuamba group (identified as group No. 2 of the Semliki river and Albert Nyanza) is likely included in the Western group. This Western group encompasses all Cameroon and Central and Northern Congo languages.
