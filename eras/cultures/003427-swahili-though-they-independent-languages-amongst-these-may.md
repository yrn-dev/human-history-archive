# Swahili

Swahili is a form of speech and a commercial language widely spread across Central and Eastern Africa. It serves as the *lingua franca* of eastern Africa in the region between the Zambezi and the White Nile.

## Origins and Distribution
Swahili is characterized as a somewhat archaic Bantu dialect. It is believed to have originated on the mainland, distinct from the original local dialects found on Pemba and Zanzibar. Its probable indigenous origin is the East African coast south of the Pangani (Ruvu) river. Through intermixture with Arabic, it developed into its current form.

The language is spoken in Zanzibar and on the opposite coast. Swahili-speaking colonies are established in several locations, including:
* Mombasa, Malindi, and Lamu.
* The coast of Portuguese and German East Africa, extending as far south as Angoche.
* As far north as the Shebeli river in Somaliland.
* The Angoji coast-region.

## Linguistic Relationships
In the coast-lands between the Kilwa settlements to the south and the Pangani or Ruvu river to the north, there are several local languages and dialects that are more or less related to Swahili, though they remain independent languages. These include *Ki-zaramo*, *Ki-mrima*, *Zeguha*, *Nguru*, *Shambala* (located north of the Ruvu), and *Bondei*.

Other languages exhibit close relationships to Swahili. The dialects of the Comoro Islands—specifically *Shi-ngazija* and *Hi-nzua* (or *Anzuani*)—are somewhat closely related. Additionally, the *Mabiha* or archaic *Makonde* of the coast between Ibo and Lindi, as well as the lower Ruvuma, could conceivably be attached to the Swahili branch.

## Linguistic Characteristics
Swahili is a Bantu language, a group defined by the governing of unchangeable roots by prefixes. While it is considered primitive in its roots, it is not so in its prefixes. 

A notable feature of Bantu languages is the terminal vowel *a* of the common root, which almost invariably terminates verbal roots. Departures from this rule in Swahili are generally the result of borrowed foreign words from English, Portuguese, or Arabic. 

Swahili demonstrates the Bantu ability to create nouns from verbs by changing the terminal vowel of the verbal root and potentially adding a personal prefix. For example, using the verbal root *patani[vs]a* (meaning "conciliate"), Swahili forms the noun *upatani[vs]o* for "conciliation" and *mpatana[vs]i* for "conciliator."

## Vocabulary and External Influence
The influence of other languages is evident in Swahili's vocabulary. For instance, the word for "fowl" in Ki-Swahili of Zanzibar is *kuku*. 

The language has also influenced external terminology. The word "dengue" (and "dandy") is identified by the *New England Dictionary* as a corruption of the Swahili word *denga* or *dinga*, which means a sudden attack of cramp. The Swahili name for the infectious fever known as dengue is *ka-dinga pepo*.
