# Bantu Languages of Cameroon and the Congo

The languages of Cameroon and the Congo are part of the broader Bantu family, a group of Negro languages characterized by a striking homogeneity. The term "Bantu" (literally meaning "men," "mankind," or "people") describes the leading feature of these tongues: the governing of an unchangeable root by prefixes. For example, in purer forms of these languages, the root *-ntu* (originally meaning "person" or "object") combines with prefixes to create different meanings: *muntu* (a man), *bantu* (men), *kintu* (a thing), *bintu* (things), and *kantu* (a little thing).

## Classification and Geographical Distribution
The domain of Bantu speech is broadly divided into four great sections. The Western group includes all the languages of Cameroon and the Central and Northern Congo. Within this region, various groups and dialects are identified:

*   **Cameroon Groups:** The *Duala* group, spoken on the estuary and delta of the Cameroon river, is noted as being of a much purer Bantu type. Other groups include the *Isubu-Bakwiri* of the west slopes of the Cameroon Mountains and the coast-lands north of the Cameroon delta (Ambas Bay). The *Barondo-Bakundu* group extends from the Rio del Rey to the Sanaga and Nyong rivers, including dialects such as *Barombi*, *Bas[=a]*, *Bonken*, *Abo*, and *Nkosi*. In the interior of German Cameroon and French Gabun, the *Fan* or *Pangwe* forms of speech are found, though they are described as so corrupt as to be only just recognizable as Bantu. Additionally, the *Di-Kele* and *Benga* dialects are spoken on the Batanga coast of German Cameroon and in Spanish Guinea.
*   **Congo Groups:** The *Bangala-Bobangi-Liboko* group comprises commercial languages of the Upper Congo, such as *Ngala*, *Bangi*, *Liboko*, *Poto*, *Ngombe*, and *Yanzi*. This group extends from the junction of the Sanga in the west to the Rubi and Lomami rivers in the east. The *Bateke* (*Batio*) group includes dialects west of the Sanga river and south of the Upper Ogowe and Ngoko rivers. In the south-central basin of the Congo, the *Rua-Luba-Lunda-Marungu* group (including *Kanyoka*, *Lulua*, and *Ki-tabwa*) is situated between the Kasai and Kwango streams and the south-west coast-line of Tanganyika. The *Bakuba* assemblage includes languages between the Lomami river and the Kwa-Kasai and Upper Kwilu.

## Linguistic Features and Vocabulary
A defining characteristic of these languages is the use of prefixes. While sixteen prefixes are common to nearly all members of the family, a seventeenth prefix, *Fi-* or *Vi-*, is found primarily in Cameroon and the northern half of the Congo basin; this prefix is used in the singular number to convey a diminutive sense.

Vocabulary comparisons reveal the relationships between these regional tongues. For instance, the word for "fowl" varies across the Bantu domain:
*   **Cameroon:** *lokoko*, *ngoko*, and *ngok*.
*   **Congo:** *nkogo* and *nsusu*.
*   **Other regions:** *enkoko* (Uganda), *kuku* (Ki-Swahili), *inkuku* (Zulu), and *nkuku* (Zambezi and South Africa).

In the Cameroon region, the root *-koko* exists alongside *kuba*, a form found in some languages of the Gold Coast and Lower Niger.

## Non-Bantu and Semi-Bantu Regions
Not all languages in these regions are classified as Bantu. In the north-eastern bend of the Congo, between Stanley Falls and the Rubi river, the *Bondonga* and *Wamanga* languages are not Bantu; they are allied with the *Mundu* of the Egyptian Sudan and the *Mbuba-Momfu* of the Nepoko and Ituri.

Furthermore, some languages are described as "semi-Bantu." In Cameroon, languages such as *Mbe*, *Bati*, *Nki*, *Mbudikum*, and *Bafut* exhibit affinities with the *Fan* forms of speech and the languages of the Central Benue river basin.

## Pronouns and Borrowings
In the north-eastern Congo and some coast Cameroon languages, the word for "I" or "me" is *mbi* or *mba*. This specific form appears to be borrowed from the Sudanian Mundu tongues. The first-person possessive pronouns in these languages are derived from forms such as *-ami* and *-angi* (including variations like *-am*, *-angu*, *-anji*, and *-ambi*).
