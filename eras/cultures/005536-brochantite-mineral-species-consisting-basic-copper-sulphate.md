# BROCHANTITE

Brochantite is a mineral species composed of a basic copper sulphate with the chemical formula $\text{Cu}_4(\text{OH})_6\text{SO}_4$. It crystallizes within the orthorhombic system.

## Physical Characteristics
The crystals of brochantite are typically small and exhibit either an acicular or prismatic habit. They possess a vitreous lustre and range in colour from emerald-green to blackish-green. In terms of transparency, the mineral is translucent to transparent. Brochantite has a specific gravity of 3.907 and a hardness rating of 3½-4. A notable physical property of the mineral is its perfect cleavage, which runs parallel to the face lettered $a$ in archival figures.

## Discovery and Nomenclature
The mineral was first discovered in the copper mines of the Urals, where it was found associated with native copper and malachite. It was named in 1824 by A. Lévy in honour of A.J.M. Brochant de Villiers.

## Varieties and Related Minerals
Several varieties of brochantite have been identified that differ slightly in their crystalline form. While some were originally described as distinct species, they were later proven to be essentially identical to brochantite. These include:
* **Königine** (from the Urals)
* **Brongniartine** (from Mexico)
* **Krisuvigite** (from Iceland)
* **Warringtonite** (from Cornwall)

Brochantite is distinct from langite, another orthorhombic basic copper sulphate. While langite shares general characters with brochantite, it differs by containing water of crystallization and possessing a fine blue colour; its composition is $\text{CuSO}_4 \cdot 3\text{Cu}(\text{OH})_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$.

## Occurrences and Localities
Brochantite is found in various global locations, often in association with other minerals:
* **Arizona, USA:** In the Clifton and Morenci district, microscopical examination shows that brochantite is extremely common. It is mostly intergrown with malachite, which often masks its presence. It is suggested that malachite in other localities may similarly be intergrown with brochantite.
* **Cumberland, England:** At Roughten Gill in the Caldbeck Fells, small brilliant crystals occur in quartzose rock associated with chrysocolla and malachite.
* **Chile:** In the Atacama, it is found with atacamite, a mineral that closely resembles brochantite in general appearance.
* **Other sites:** The mineral is also found in the Tintic district of Utah and at Rézbánya in the Bihar Mountains of Hungary.
