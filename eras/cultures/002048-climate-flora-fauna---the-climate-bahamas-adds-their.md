# Climate, Flora, and Fauna

The distribution of plant and animal life across various global regions is closely dependent upon climatic conditions. This relationship is evident in the diverse environments of the Americas, Africa, Asia, and Europe, where temperature, rainfall, and altitude dictate the characteristics of the local flora and fauna.

## The Americas

### The Bahamas
The climate of the Bahamas is characterized by a mean temperature of 88°F during the hottest months (June to September) and 66°F during the coldest (January to March). Rainy months occur from May to October, with hurricanes appearing between July and October. In 1901, Nassau recorded 63.32 inches of rainfall. The soil is generally thin but fertile where it covers rock. Three soil types are distinguished: a white soil along shores, a reddish clay, and a black soil of decayed vegetable matter found under forests. New Providence contains a "pine barrens" area named for the tree growing in its rocky soil. 

The islands are rich in flora, particularly in the Abaco Islands and Andros Island, which possess large timber such as iron wood, bullet wood, mastic, mahogany, and lignum vitae. Though valuable, a lack of roads and labour has hindered the use of this timber. The region produces a vast array of fruits and spices, including pine-apples, oranges, lemons, limes, citrons, pomegranates, bananas, tamarinds, olives, figs, sapodillas, sour-sops, melons, yams, potatoes, gourds, cucumbers, pepper, cassava, prickly pears, sugar-cane, ginger, coffee, indigo, Guinea corn, and pease. Tobacco and cascarilla bark also flourish. Cotton is indigenous and was used by aborigines to weave cloth, though it suffers from insect pests. Sisal is grown in increasing quantities. The fauna is poorer than the flora; indigenous species include the guana and a small coney, and aborigines reportedly had a breed of dogs that did not bark.

### Central America and the United States
In Honduras, there are two seasons: a wet season from May to November and a dry season from November to May. The climate is oppressive on the Atlantic coast lowlands but delightful in the interior highlands. At Tegucigalpa, temperatures range from a maximum of 90°F in May to a minimum of 50°F in December.

In Idaho, the mean annual temperature from 1898 to 1903 was 45.5°F, with distinct climate zones. The Snake river basin (between Twin Falls and Lewiston) and the valleys of the Weiser, Payette, and Boisé rivers are the mildest regions, with a mean annual temperature of 52°F in 1902. Conversely, the Lost river regions, Camas prairie, and Upper Snake basin are much colder; in 1902, temperatures there ranged from a high of 101°F to a low of -35°F. The state's mean annual rainfall in 1903 was 16.60 inches, with the highest recording (37.70 inches) at Murray in Shoshone county and the lowest (5.69 inches) at Garnet in Elmore county.

## Africa

### British East Africa
Climate and vegetation in British East Africa are arranged in zones parallel to the coast. The coast is hot but healthy due to dry soil and constant breezes from the Indian Ocean, with annual rainfall of approximately 35 inches. Inland, the outer plateaus and plains are more arid. The highlands (above 5000 ft.) are very healthy, with an average temperature of 66°F in the cool season and 73°F in the hot season. Above 7000 ft., the climate is colder and experiences frosts. Rainfall in the highlands ranges between 40 and 50 inches. The region bordering Victoria Nyanza is typically tropical with over 60 inches of annual rainfall. The hottest period is December to April, and the coolest is July to September. "Greater rains" occur from March to June, while "smaller rains" fall in November and December.

### Benin
The region of Benin, formerly ruled by the king of Benin city, shares the physical characteristics, fauna, flora, and climate of southern Nigeria. The coastline consists of a huge mangrove swamp intersected by creeks. Inland, the rising ground is covered in dense forests where mahogany and cotton trees are conspicuous.

## Asia and Europe

### Northern Asia and the Carpathians
The flora of northern Asia is essentially the same as that of northern Europe, though it lacks heaths and oak east of the Ural. Principal mountain trees include larch, birch, and pines, with the latter reaching a northern limit of 70 deg. N. in Siberia. Lower ground features poplars, alders, and willow. A European-character flora extends from the Mediterranean to the Himalaya, with Japanese and Chinese forms becoming more numerous in the eastern Himalaya.

In the Carpathians, the mountains act as a protective wall, creating a milder climate for regions to the south. The mountain vegetation is divided into four zones: beech (up to 4000 ft.), Scottish fir (up to 5000 ft.), pine (which dwarfs and disappears at 6000 ft.), and a final zone of bare rock or moss and lichen. The High Tatra and Transylvanian Mountains have flora similar to the middle region of the Alps. Transylvania also features sea-shore flora in its marshes, ponds, and salt-impregnated lakes. Fauna includes birds of prey, lynxes, wolves, and bears, as well as a mollusc fauna containing species found only in the Alpine region.

### The Balearic Isles
The climate of the Balearic archipelago is generally healthy and mild, though it varies based on shelter from wind and altitude. Its flora and fauna resemble those of the Mediterranean coasts of France and Spain.

## Historical Flora and Fauna
During the Eocene period in Northern Europe, early plants such as laurels, chestnuts, willows, and oaks indicated a temperate climate. By the Middle Eocene, the climate shifted, allowing palms, cinnamon, figs, sequoia, magnolia, cypress, ferns, and cactus to flourish in Hampshire, the Isle of Wight, and the adjacent continent. The late Eocene flora of Europe was very similar to modern Australasia. Fauna of the Eocene included the primitive opossum (_Didelphys_) and large flightless birds such as _Gastornis_ and _Eupterornis_, alongside ancestral forms of owls, eagles, buzzards, herons, albatrosses, and gulls. Reptiles were limited primarily to large snakes, turtles, tortoises, and crocodilians.
