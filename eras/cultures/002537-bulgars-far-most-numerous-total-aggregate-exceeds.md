# The Bulgars

## Origins and Early History
The Bulgars were a Turanian race, related to the Finns, Avars, Huns, and Tatars. Their original homeland was located in the region between the Volga and the Ural mountains, where the kingdom of Black (or Great) Bolgary existed until the 13th century. Described as a horde of fierce and barbarous wild horsemen, the Bulgars practiced polygamy and were governed by a despotic system led by nobles known as *bolyars* (or *boyars*) and chiefs called *khans*.

In the latter part of the 7th century, the Bulgars appeared on the banks of the Pruth. In 679, under the leadership of Khan Isperikh (or Asparukh), they crossed the Danube. After subjugating the Slavonic population of Moesia, the Bulgars advanced toward Salonica and the gates of Constantinople. This expansion forced the East Roman emperors to pay an annual tribute and cede the province of Moesia to them.

## Assimilation and Cultural Evolution
Although the Bulgars established a political organization and gave their name to the region, the invading horde was not numerous. Over the following two centuries, the Bulgars gradually merged into the Slavonic population they had conquered. Similar to the Franks in Gaul, the Bulgars adopted the local institutions, customs, and language of the more civilized race. Consequently, there is no trace of Finnish or Ugrian elements remaining in the Bulgarian speech.

## Demographics of the Balkan Peninsula
In the context of the broader Balkan Peninsula, the Slavonic population—which includes the Bulgars and the Serbo-Croats—is the most numerous group, with a total aggregate exceeding 10,000,000. In contrast, the Turkish population of the Peninsula, which consists of colonists introduced from Asia and descendants of 14th and 15th-century Ottoman invaders, scarcely exceeds 1,800,000.

The Turkish population has seen a considerable decline during the 19th century, particularly in areas removed from the authority of the sultan. While the Turkish population continues to decrease in Bulgaria despite efforts by authorities to stop emigration, they are still found in compact masses in the region between the Sea of Marmora, the Black Sea, and Adrianople, as well as in north-eastern Bulgaria.

## Military Characteristics
Accounts of Bulgar combatants highlight a proficiency in close-quarters fighting. Specifically, the Bulgar is noted as being "a great man with the knife," a reputation reflected in parodies and songs performed by British "Tommies" at Saloniki, such as those referencing "Boris the Bulgar," described as the "Taker of Life" and the "Pride of Sofia."
