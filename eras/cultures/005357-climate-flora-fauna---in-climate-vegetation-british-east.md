# Climate, Flora and Fauna

The distribution of life and the characteristics of the environment vary significantly across different global regions, often dictated by altitude, proximity to oceans, and geological formations.

## British East Africa
In British East Africa, climate and vegetation are arranged in zones parallel to the coast. The coastal region is tropical and hot, though it is generally healthier than other tropical coast lands due to dry soil and constant breezes from the Indian Ocean. Annual rainfall on the coast is approximately 35 inches. Moving inland, the plains and outer plateaus are more arid.

The highlands, defined as districts over 5,000 feet high, are very healthy, and fever is almost unknown there. In these regions, the average temperature is 73° F in the hot season and 66° F in the cool season. Above 7,000 feet, the climate becomes distinctly colder and frosts occur. Average rainfall in the highlands ranges between 40 and 50 inches. The region bordering Victoria Nyanza is typically tropical with annual rainfall exceeding 60 inches, making it unsuitable for Europeans.

The hottest period in the protectorate occurs from December to April, while the coolest is from July to September. Rainfall is irregular compared to other tropical countries, and severe droughts occasionally occur. The "greater rains" fall between March and June, and the "smaller rains" occur in November and December.

## The Carpathians and Northern Asia
The Carpathians act as a protective wall, ensuring that regions to the south enjoy a milder climate than those to the north. The mountains feature four vegetation zones based on altitude:
*   **Beech:** Extends to 4,000 feet.
*   **Scottish fir:** Extends to 5,000 feet.
*   **Pine:** Grows above the fir but becomes dwarfed and disappears at approximately 6,000 feet.
*   **Lichen and moss:** Found above 6,000 feet on almost bare or covered rock.

The Transylvanian Mountains and High Tatra possess flora similar to the middle region of the Alps. Transylvania also features remarkable sea-shore flora in its marshes, ponds, and salt-impregnated lakes. The fauna includes birds of prey, wolves, lynxes, and numerous bears. The mollusc fauna is characteristic, containing species found in the Alpine region but not in neighbouring areas. Additionally, sheep and cattle are pastured in great numbers on the slopes.

In Northern Asia, the flora is essentially the same as that of northern Europe, though species vary. Pines, birch, and larch are the primary mountain trees, while poplars, alders, and willow grow on lower ground. The pine's northern limit in Siberia is roughly 70 deg. N. East of the Ural, heaths and oak are absent. A European-character flora extends from the Mediterranean to the Himalaya; while many European species reach the central Himalaya, Japanese and Chinese forms are more numerous in the eastern Himalaya.

## The Americas
### Bolivia
Bolivia's flora represents every climatic zone due to altitude variations, ranging from tropical forests in the Amazon basin to scanty Arctic vegetation in the lofty Cordilleras. The puna region is characterized by quinoa, oca, the potato, and coarse grasses. In temperate and warm regions, Indian corn was cultivated prior to the arrival of Europeans, who later introduced oats, rye, wheat, pease, beans, and Old World fruits and vegetables.

Indigenous plants in tropical and sub-tropical zones include peanuts, cassava, the sweet potato, guava, pineapple, pawpaw, chirimoya, vanilla, sarsaparilla, and rubber-producing trees. Hardwoods such as cedar, rosewood, mahogany, jacaranda, walnut, colo, and quebracho are also present. Coca is cultivated on the eastern slopes of the Andes at 5,000 to 6,000 feet, where frosts are unknown. Quina (calisaya) is found between 3,000 and 9,000 feet; its bark is a significant foreign trade item, though natural sources are diminishing due to destructive collection methods.

### The Bahamas and Honduras
The Bahamas experience a mean temperature of 88° F during the hottest months (June to September) and 66° F during the coldest (January to March). Rainy months occur from May to October, with hurricanes appearing from July to October. In 1901, Nassau recorded 63.32 inches of rainfall. 

The vegetation includes "pine barrens" on New Providence. Andros and the Abaco Islands are noted for large timber, including bullet, iron, lignum vitae, mastic, and mahogany. The islands produce a vast array of fruits and spices, such as pomegranates, citrons, lemons, oranges, olives, tamarinds, pine-apples, bananas, and others. Indigenous cotton was woven by aborigines, though it suffers from insect pests. The fauna is poorer than the flora; mentions include a small coney, the guana, and a breed of non-barking dogs used by aborigines.

Honduras has two seasons: wet (May to November) and dry (November to May). The climate is oppressive on the Atlantic coast lowlands but delightful in the interior highlands. In Tegucigalpa, temperatures range from a minimum of 50° F in December to a maximum of 90° F in May.

### Idaho
Idaho's fauna and flora generally resemble other north-western United States. The mean annual temperature from 1898 to 1903 was 45.5° F. The Snake river basin and the valleys of the Weiser, Payette, and Boisé rivers are the mildest regions (mean annual temperature of 52° F in 1902). Conversely, the Lost river regions, Camas prairie, and Upper Snake basin are much colder. In 1903, the state's mean annual rainfall was 16.60 inches, ranging from 5.69 inches at Garnet to 37.70 inches at Murray.

## Europe and the Mediterranean
### Denmark
Denmark's climate is similar to Great Britain's at the same latitude, though winters are colder and summers slightly warmer. The mean average temperature for the country is 45.14° F. The islands are generally warmer than Jutland. Annual rainfall varies between 21.58 and 27.87 inches, with the highest amounts on the west coast of Jutland. September is the wettest month and April the driest. In winter, ice often lines the eastern coasts, sometimes making the Great Belt and the Sound impassable.

### Balearic Isles
The climate of this archipelago is generally healthy and mild, though it varies based on shelter from wind and altitude. The flora and fauna are similar to those found on the Mediterranean coasts of France and Spain.
