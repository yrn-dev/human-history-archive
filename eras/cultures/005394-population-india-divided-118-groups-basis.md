# Population Groups of India

The population of India is characterized by a complex intersection of linguistic, racial, and social divisions. According to census data from 1901, the native population of India was recorded at 294,191,379. This population is described as being mainly of a "high type, brown" race.

## Linguistic Divisions
The population of India is divided into 118 groups based on language. These numerous groups can be further categorized into several principal linguistic families:
*   **Indo-European:** Specifically the Indo-Aryan sub-family.
*   **Dravido-Mu[n.][d.][=a]:** This includes the Dravidian group and the Mu[n.][d.][=a] (Kolarian) group.
*   **Indo-Chinese:** This is further subdivided into Siamese-Chinese, Tibeto-Burman, and Mon-Khmer.
*   **Other Groups:** The population also includes Malayo-Polynesian, Semitic, and Hamitic groups, as well as unclassed groups such as the Gipsy.

## Racial Strata and Origins
The population is believed to comprise at least three distinct strata:
1.  **Uncivilized Aborigines:** This group includes the Santhals and the Kols.
2.  **Dravidians:** Including the Kanarese and Tamils, these people may represent the earliest northern invaders and attained a degree of culture independently. While the Dravidians and Kolarians are viewed by some recent authorities as representing a single physical type, they possess different languages and exhibit different stages of civilization.
3.  **Aryans:** Migrating south-eastwards around 1500 B.C. from an original home likely in Asia Minor or southern Russia, the Aryans divided into the Iranians and Hindus. They drove the previous inhabitants toward the south and center of the peninsula.

Additionally, invasions by "so-called Mongolian races" from the north-west are believed to have profoundly affected the composition of the population in Bengal, which is considered Mongolo-Dravidian.

## Physical Classifications
From a physical standpoint, the people of the Indian peninsula and certain hill tribes of central India (the Dravidians) are believed to be based on the Australoid type, characterized by dark skin, dark soft hair, broad flat noses, and heavy jaws. The "ordinary population" of the Indian provinces is thought to have arisen from the admixture of this Australoid type with the Melanochroic group. The Melanochroi—dark-haired people extending from southern Europe through North Africa, Syria, Arabia, and Persia to India—are themselves considered by Professor Huxley to be a result of the admixture of the Xanthochroic race (fair-haired northern Europeans) with the Australoid type.

## Social and Religious Divisions
The population is further divided by social and religious structures:
*   **Caste:** The institution of caste is a division of the population into groups founded partly on racial distinctions. This system is connected to the hereditary sacerdotal class known as the Brahmans, who held extraordinary influence over the absolute monarchies of the Ganges valley by the 6th century B.C.
*   **Religion:** Islam has been a significant transforming force in India, particularly in the north and center, though it is weaker in the south. Approximately one-fifth of the total population is Moslem. Historically, under the Moguls, a ruling caste of Moslems was formed.

## Regional Distribution and Administration
In the Bombay Presidency, the population is distributed across British districts and native states. As of 1901, the total population of the presidency was 25,468,209, with 18,515,587 residing in British territory and 6,908,648 in native states. The region is divided into four commissionerships: Sind, the southern (Carnatic), the central (Deccan), and the northern (Gujarat).

In terms of urban centers, Bombay City serves as a major seaport and the capital of the Bombay Presidency. By 1901, its population was 776,006, making it the second most populous city in the Indian empire after Calcutta.
