# Backgammon: Opening Moves and Gameplay

Backgammon is a game played on a special board with draughtsmen, where movement is determined by the throw of dice. It is believed to have been invented around the 10th century, though the Romans played a similar "twelve-line game" known as *Ludus duodecim scriptorum*. The name is likely Saxon, derived from *baec* (back) and *gamen* (game), referring to the possibility of players being sent back. In French, the game is called *trictrac*, an imitation of the sound of rattling dice.

## Movement and Board Mechanics
Players move their men from point to point alternately. White moves from the inner table of black, through black's outer table, and then through white's own outer table to reach white's inner table. Black moves in the opposite direction. A player may move a man the number of points indicated by the dice, provided the destination is not "blocked" by two or more of the adversary's men.

A throw can be used to move one man the total sum of the dice or two separate men according to the individual numbers on each die. If doublets are thrown (such as two sixes), the player makes four moves of that number.

## Strategic Principles and Opening Moves
Movement is guided by two primary principles: making points to obstruct the opponent and, when forced to leave "blots" (single men), placing them where they are least likely to be hit. The probability of a blot being hit decreases if it is left nearer to the adversary's man or if it can only be reached with double dice. If a point is blocked by adverse men, the chance of being hit is further reduced.

The most valuable points to secure are the five-point in both the player's own inner table and the adversary's inner table. These are followed in importance by the player's own bar-point and the four-point in their own inner table.

At the commencement of a game, the best moves for specific throws are:
*   **Aces:** Considered the best of all throws; move two on the five-point and two on the bar-point.
*   **Sixes:** The second-best throw; move two on the player's own bar-point and two on the adversary's bar-point.
*   **Ace, trey:** Make the five-point in the player's inner table.
*   **Ace, six:** Make the bar-point.
*   **Deuces:** Move two on the trey-point in the opponent's inner table and two on the four-point in the player's inner table.
*   **Deuce, four:** Make the four-point in the player's own table.
*   **Threes:** Make the bar-point, or play two on the four-point of the adversary's inner table and two on the five-point of the player's inner table.
*   **Trey, five:** Make the trey-point in the player's own table.
*   **Trey, six:** Move a man from the adversary's ace-point as far as possible.
*   **Fours:** Move two from the five-point in the adversary's outer table and two on the five-point in the adversary's inner table.
*   **Four, five and four, six:** Move a man from the adversary's ace-point as far as possible.
*   **Fives:** Move two men from the five in the adversary's outer table to the trey-point in the player's inner table.
*   **Five, six:** Move a man from the adversary's ace-point as far as possible.

## Advanced Play and Winning
To carry men home efficiently, players should move the most distant man to the adversary's bar-point, then to the six-point in the outer table, and finally to the six-point in the inner table. Players are advised to avoid clustering many men on the deuce or trey-points of their own tables, as these men become out of play.

The game concludes when a player moves all men into their inner table (home) and begins to "bear" them off the board. A man is removed from a point corresponding to the number thrown. If a player is hit on a blot after they have started bearing off, they must re-enter on the adversary's inner table and return that man to their own inner table before continuing to bear.

Victory is categorized by the state of the adversary's men:
*   **Single game (hit):** The adversary has already begun bearing.
*   **Double game (gammon):** The adversary has not yet borne a single man.
*   **Triple game (backgammon):** The winner bears their last man while the adversary has not borne any men and either has a man "up" or a man in the winner's inner table.

## Variations and Special Rules
In *Russian Backgammon*, men are entered into the same table by dice throws, and both players move in the same direction. Rules vary; some allow players to take up blots during entry, though a man "up" must generally be entered before other moves can be made.

Regarding doublets, some rules require players to play the number thrown and then the corresponding number on the opposite side of the dice. If a player cannot complete the entire throw, they may be barred from taking the corresponding doublets or receiving another throw.
