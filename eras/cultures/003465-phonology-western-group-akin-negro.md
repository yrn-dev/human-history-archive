# Phonology of the Western Bantu Group

## Linguistic Affinities and Characteristics
The phonology of the Western group (d) of Bantu languages is described as being akin to that of the Negro languages found in West-Central and Western Africa. This group is distinguished by the use of specific West African combinations, namely *kp* and *gb*, which are characteristic of African speech located between the Guinea coast and the Upper Nile. These particular combinations are not adopted by the South-Central (b) or other related groups (a and c), except occasionally on the borders of the Western group.

In contrast, the South-Central group, specifically _Zulu_, has incorporated clicks, which may have been borrowed from the Bushmen and Hottentots.

## General Features of Bantu Languages
The Bantu languages are defined by several specific structural and phonological propositions:

*   **Construction:** They are agglutinative. Their syntax is primarily formed by adding prefixes and suffixes to a root. They do not utilize infixes, meaning no mutable syllable is incorporated into the middle of the root-word.
*   **Root Stability:** The root remains practically unchanging, with the exception of its terminal vowel. However, the pronunciation of the first or penultimate vowel or consonant may be modified by a preceding prefix, and the last vowel may be similarly modified by a succeeding suffix.

## Distribution and Related Language Groups
The linguistic landscape of the region includes various distinct groups and families:

*   **The Oci-herero Group:** This group is very distinct from _Ki-mbundu_ speech, although connecting forms exist. It includes several languages: _Herero_ (Damaraland), _Umbundu_ (Bihe highlands of south Angola), _Nano_ (Benguela coast), and _Si-ndonga_, _Ku-anyama_, and _Oci-mbo_ (northern German South-West Africa and southern Portuguese Angola). These languages likely extend to the Zambezi watershed, including the Kubango and Kwito rivers.
*   **The Kiboko Family:** Also known as _Kibokwe_ or _Kioko_, this eastern Angola group shows affinities to the languages of the Upper Zambezi and the aforementioned _Oci-herero_ and _Ki-mbundu_ groups. It extends into the south-western Belgian Congo and includes the _Lubale_ of northern Barotseland and the Zambezi sources, and potentially the _Gangela_ of south-western Angola.
*   **Other Influences:** The Fula language, while not part of any African linguistic family and akin in its rudiments to the Hamito-Semitic group, resembles Negro dialects in some ways, though it shows non-Negroid influence through its use of suffixes.

## The Bantu Expansion and Population
The study of Bantu languages suggests that approximately 3,000 years ago, a powerful tribe of negroes speaking the Bantu mother-language migrated from the heart of Africa—the region between the western Nile, Congo, and Shari watersheds. This group was physically allied to the negroes of southern Lake Chad basins and the south-western Nile, though they were impregnated with the Caucasian Hamite.

As they pushed into the southern half of the continent, they assimilated or absorbed the majority of existing populations. These preceding populations included:
*   **Atlantic seaboard:** West Coast negroes.
*   **Central region:** Forest Pygmies and negroes of a low type.
*   **East African coast and Victoria Nyanza:** A population that was partly Hottentot-Bushman and partly Nilotic-negro.
*   **Cape of Good Hope to Lakes Nyasa and Tanganyika:** Forest-negro, Nilotic-negro, Bushman, and Hottentot.

## Diversity Among Bantu Speakers
It is noted that speaking a Bantu language does not necessarily mean a person belongs to a specific, definite type of negro. While there is some physical resemblance among certain tribes—such as the Ova-herero, the Ba-tonga, the people of the Great Lakes, the Babangi of the upper Congo, the Awemba, Zulu-Kaffirs, and some East Coast tribes—there is significant diversity in physical characteristics and head shape among negroes in Bantu Africa. Some Bantu speakers are Forest Pygmies (dwarfs), some show relationships to Hottentots, some are indistinguishable from the most exaggerated types of black West African negroes, and those in the north may be of Nilotic or Gala (Galla) origin.
