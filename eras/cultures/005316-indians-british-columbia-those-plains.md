# Indians of British Columbia

The Indian population of British Columbia was recorded as 25,488 in the census of 1901. Within this population, 20,351 individuals professed Christianity, while 5,137 were pagans.

## Tribal Groups and Distribution
The Indians of the province are divided into numerous tribes identified by local names. On linguistic grounds, these tribes are categorized into several large groups based on their geographic location:

*   **The Interior:** The southern part of the interior is occupied by the Kootenay and Salish. The northern interior is inhabited by the Athapackan people, also known as the Tinneh.
*   **The Coast:** Coastal groups include the Tsimshian, Haida, Nootka, and Kwakiatl. Additionally, various tribes related to the Salish proper are located around the Gulf of Georgia.

## Specific Tribes and Stocks
Certain tribes are noted for their specific linguistic stocks and populations:

*   **Kutenai (Kutonaga):** Forming the distinct stock of Kitunahan, this group's former range was along the Kootenay river and lake in British Columbia. They were noted for their honesty and friendliness toward white people. As of 1904, there were some 550 Kutenai in British Columbia.
*   **Bellacoola (Bilqula):** A tribe of Salishan stock inhabiting the coast of British Columbia, numbering approximately 300.
*   **Bellabella:** This is the common name—derived from an Indian corruption of Milbank—for a tribe of Kwakiutl Indians located at Milbank, British Columbia. This group includes the subtribes Ocalitk, Oetlitk, and Kokaitk. They number about 300 and were converted to Christianity by Protestant missionaries.

## Land Title and Government Relations
The legal status of land for the Indians of British Columbia differs from that of the Indians of the plains. In British Columbia, there is no treaty for the relinquishment of their title to the land. Despite the absence of such treaties, the government provides them with assistance.
