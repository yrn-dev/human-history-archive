# Sumatra

Sumatra is the largest island within the southern group of the Malay Archipelago. This group of islands, which includes Java and Timor, extends in a circular arc between 95 and 127 degrees East and from 5 to 10 degrees South.

## Geography and Environment
Sumatra is characterized by a low population density. Much of the island's surface is covered by dense forest, leaving a comparatively small area for cultivation. These cultivated lands are primarily confined to the lowlands and are concentrated chiefly in the volcanic region located near the center of the island. The central part of the southern Malay Archipelago is a volcanic region with many active volcanoes, some of which reach summits of 10,000 feet or more.

The island's geographical influence extends to other nearby landmasses. The Nicobar and Andaman island groups in the Bay of Bengal are positioned on a line connecting Cape Negrais (the south-western extremity of Burma) and the north end of Sumatra; it is possible that these groups exist due to volcanic agencies extending from Sumatra across the Indian Ocean. Additionally, the island of Banka is located off the east coast of Sumatra, separated from it by the Banka Strait, which reaches a minimum width of approximately 9 miles.

## People and Culture
Sumatra is inhabited by the Malays, a seafaring race known for exceptional powers of dispersal. While some theories suggest they spread from an island center such as Java, another theory proposes a larger linguistic group—including the Munda, Monkhmer, Polynesian, and Micronesian languages—that immigrated from the extreme west.

The history of the Asiatic Malays, including those on Sumatra, is traced through three periods:
1. A semi-barbarous first period.
2. A second period marked by the arrival of Hindu civilization. The strength of this influence is evidenced by the presence of Indian customs, numerous Indian words, and Hindu ruins.
3. A third period in which Hindu influence was superseded by Islam, which spread to the Malay Peninsula and the Malay Archipelago before the 16th century.

Despite the spread of Islam, Indian alphabets continue to be used in Sumatra and Java, while the Arabic alphabet is used on the mainland.

## Arts and Drama
The drama of the Malay populations on Sumatra and the neighboring island of Java resembles Indian drama, which likely influenced its development. In both Java and Sumatra, comic drama is frequently performed by troupes of strollers consisting of a woman and two men, a group size sufficient to represent a wide variety of plots.

## Historical Perspectives
In ancient times, the islands of Sumatra and Ceylon were often confounded by ancients such as Solinus, Pliny, and Salmas. Pliny referred to the region as Taprobane, though the Christian topographer Cosmas Indicopleustes provided a clearer, albeit exaggerated, description of its dimensions.

In more recent history, the East Indies—which include various islands and possessions—were held by Holland.
