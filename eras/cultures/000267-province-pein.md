# THE PROVINCE OF PEIN

The province of Pein is a territory located between the east and north-east, described as being five days in length. It is part of a larger region known as Great Turkey, which encompasses various provinces from Cascar forward to the city of Lop. The province is under the sovereignty of the Great Kaan.

## Geography and Natural Resources
The province contains a number of villages and towns, the most noble of which is the capital city, Pein. The region is characterized by the presence of rivers where quantities of Chalcedony and Jasper are found. The land is productive, providing the inhabitants with plenty of all products, including cotton.

The terrain surrounding and leading away from Pein toward the province of Charchan is notably sandy. In these areas, much of the water available is described as bad and bitter, although fresh and sweet water can be found in certain locations.

## Economy and Society
The people of Pein are worshippers of Mahommet. Their economy is primarily based on trade and manufactures.

The province is noted for a specific matrimonial custom: if a husband departs on a journey and remains away for more than 20 days, the woman is permitted to marry another man once that term has passed. Simultaneously, the husband is permitted to marry whomever he pleases. Some archival notes suggest this may be related to the practice of temporary marriages found in various Central Asian caravan halting-places, where alliances may be formed for short durations, such as a month, a week, or twenty-four hours.

## Identification and Location
Historical and geographical analysis has sought to identify the exact location of Pein. Some manuscripts suggest the name may have been miscopied from "Pem" or "Peym." Based on the reports of Johnson regarding journeys to Khotan and the itineraries of Hiuen Tsang, Pein is identified as being identical to Pima. 

Pima was the first city reached by Hiuen Tsang upon his return to China after leaving Khotan. It is situated 330 *li* east of Khotan, though some accounts place it at 500 *li*. The city is described as standing on the banks of a river that flows from the east and enters the sandy desert. Other records, such as the Turkish map of Central Asia printed in the *Jahán Numá*, place the town of Pím slightly north of Khotan.

## Regional Context
Pein exists in a region where shifting sands have historically overwhelmed prosperous communities. To the north-east of Pima, the ancient kingdoms of Shemathona and Tuholo once held flourishing cities that have since been buried by the desert. 

The province is situated along a route that connects Khotan to Cathay. While ancient travelers could once make this journey in approximately 14 days through a series of towns and villages, later travelers were forced to take a circuitous route of 100 days due to the fear of the Kalmaks.
