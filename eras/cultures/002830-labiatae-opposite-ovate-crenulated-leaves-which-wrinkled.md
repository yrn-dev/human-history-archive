# Labiatae

The Labiatae is a natural order of plants that includes various aromatic herbs and undershrubs. In certain regions, such as south-eastern Asia (including the Malayan peninsula, islands, and India), the Labiatae are listed as one of the principal orders of indigenous plants, although their presence varies across different environments, being deficient in some areas.

## Notable Species and Genera

### Balm
Balm (_Melissa officinalis_) is a fragrant herb of the Deadnettle order (Labiatae). It is characterized by opposite, ovate, and crenulated leaves that are wrinkled on the upper side. Its flowers are small and may be white or spotted with rose. Native to southern and central Europe, the plant is frequently grown in gardens and has become naturalized in the south of England. It also grows wild in North America as a garden escape. The name is derived from the Greek word *melissa*, as bees visit the plant. An allied southern European species found in south-west and south England is Bastard Balm (_Melittis Melissophyllum_).

### Horehound
The order includes both common (white) and black horehound.

*   **Common or White Horehound** (_Marrubium vulgare_): A perennial herb reaching approximately 1 foot in height, featuring thick stems and a short, stout rootstock. Both the stems and branches are coated in a hoary white felt. Its leaves are rhombic-ovate or roundish with long petioles and bluntly toothed margins; they are downy and pale green above, while being white, woolly, and much wrinkled below. The flowers are small, dull-white, and sessile, appearing in dense clusters or whorls with a 10-toothed calyx. It is found in West Asia, North Africa, and Europe, and has been naturalized in parts of America. In Britain, it typically grows on dry chalky or sandy ground. Chemically, it contains resin, a volatile oil, and a crystallizable bitter principle known as *marrubiin*, giving the plant a persistent bitter taste and an aromatic odour. It was formerly official in British pharmacopoeias, and its syrup, confection, or infusion has been used popularly to treat various affections.
*   **Black Horehound** (_Ballota nigra_): A hairy perennial herb reaching 2 to 3 feet in height. It is characterized by a foetid odour and roundish-ovate, toothed leaves that are stalked. Its flowers appear in dense axillary clusters with a pale red-purple corolla and a purplish or green calyx. It occurs in West Asia, North Africa, and Europe, and is found in Britain south of the Clyde and the Forth. It has also been introduced to North America.

### Marjoram
Marjoram is the common name for several aromatic undershrubs or herbs in the genus _Origanum_. 

*   **Wild Marjoram** (_O. vulgare_): A perennial common to England's hedge-banks and dry copses. It possesses many stout stems (1 to 3 feet high) with clusters of purple flowers and somewhat ovate, short-stalked leaves.
*   **Cultivated Marjoram**: Both pot marjoram (_O. Onites_) and sweet or knotted marjoram (_O. Marjorana_) are grown for their aromatic leaves, which are used for culinary purposes in either dry or green form. The tops of these plants are cut and dried slowly in the shade as they begin to flower.
