# Rhamnus

The genus *Rhamnus*, belonging to the natural order Rhamnaceae, comprises various species of shrubs and trees known for their medicinal properties and their utility in the production of dyes.

## Botanical Characteristics and Species
A primary species of the genus is *Rhamnus cathartica*, commonly known as buckthorn. This much-branched shrub can reach a height of 10 feet and is characterized by blackish bark, spinous branchlets, and ovate leaves that are sharply serrated, measuring between 1 and 2 inches in length. Its small green flowers are regular with parts in fours, and the plants are dioecious, meaning male and female flowers are borne on different plants. The fruit of *R. cathartica* is globose, black, and succulent, containing four stones.

*Rhamnus cathartica* is native to north Asia, north Africa, and Europe, including England, where it occurs primarily in thickets and woods on chalk. While it is found in some parts of eastern North America as a naturalized species, it is rare in Ireland and does not grow wild in Scotland.

Other species within the genus include:
*   ***Rhamnus Frangula***: Common in England and known as black alder or berry-bearing. It differs from *R. cathartica* by having bisexual flowers with parts in fives, non-serrated leaves, and an absence of spiny branchlets.
*   ***Rhamnus purshiana***: Also known as *Cascara sagrada*, this species is found on the Pacific coast and produces cascara bark.
*   ***Rhamnus Caroliniana***: Known as the alder-buckthorn.
*   ***Rhamnus Alaternus***: A Mediterranean species grown in shrubberies in several varieties.

## Economic and Medicinal Uses
Various species of *Rhamnus* are utilized for their chemical properties. The fruit of *Rhamnus cathartica* possesses strong purgative properties, and its bark is used to produce a yellow dye. Similarly, the fruits of *Rhamnus Frangula* are purgative; when unripe, these fruits yield a green dye. The wood of *R. Frangula*, described as soft and porous and referred to as black dogwood, is used in the manufacture of gunpowder.

The genus is also significant in the commercial dye industry. Dyes are obtained from the bark and fruits of several species, including *R. infectoria*, *R. tinctoria*, and *R. davurica*. Specifically, *R. tinctoria* and *R. davurica* are used to produce the "China green" of commerce.

## Biological Interactions
In the study of Uredineae, *Rhamnus* serves as a host for certain fungi. Specifically, the species *Puccinia coronata* produces aecidiospores on *Rhamnus*.

## Distinctions from Related Terms
The archive distinguishes *Rhamnus* from other plants with similar common names. Sea-buckthorn (*Hippophae rhamnoides*) is a willow-like shrub with orange-yellow fruits and silvery undersides to its leaves, found on sandy seashores from Sussex to York. Additionally, *Bumelia lycioides* (or *lanuginosa*) is popularly referred to as "southern buckthorn," though it is distinct from the *Rhamnus* genus.
