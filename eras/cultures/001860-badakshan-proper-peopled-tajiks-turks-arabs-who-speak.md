# Badakshan

Badakshan is a region that was formerly part of Greek Bactria. Its history is marked by various conquests and a long-standing royal tradition, while its population is characterized by a diverse mix of ethnic and linguistic groups.

## History and Governance
The region was visited by Hsuan Tsang in 630 and 644. By the 10th century, Arabian geographers noted the presence of large towns such as Khotl and Waksh, as well as the region's mines of lapis lazuli and ruby. Marco Polo and his companions stayed in Badakshan between 1272 and 1273.

For several centuries, the country was governed by kings who claimed descent from Alexander the Great. This Alexandrian pedigree was also claimed by the chiefs of several other regions, including Wakhán, Roshán, Shighnán, Darwáz, Karátegín, Gilgít, Chitrál, Swát, and Khapolor in Bálti. The royal line of these kings continued until the middle of the 15th century with the death of Shah Mahommed, who left only married daughters. 

In the early 16th century, the Usbegs seized possession of the country but were soon expelled, after which the region was governed by descendants of the old royal dynasty through the female line. By the middle of the 18th century, the current dynasty of Mirs replaced the extinct old dynasty. The region faced significant instability in later years: it was invaded and ravaged by the ruler of Kabul in 1765, and during the first three decades of the 19th century, it was depopulated and overrun by Murad Beg and Kohan Beg, chiefs of the Kataghan Usbegs of Kunduz. Following the death of Murad Beg, power shifted to another Usbeg, Mahommed Amir Khan. In 1859, the Kataghan Usbegs were expelled, and Mir Jahander Shah was reinstated at Faizabad under Afghan supremacy. He was later replaced in 1867 by Mir Mahommed Shah and other representatives of the same family after being expelled by Abdur Rahman.

## Demographics and Ethnicity
The population of Badakshan proper consists of Arabs, Turks, and Tajiks. The Tajiks are identified as part of the underlying population of Afghanistan, Badakshan, Persia, and Baluchistan. These Tajiks are recognized as part of the Aryan population, related to the ancient Scyths or Sacae, and are found in their purest form in the glens and inaccessible mountains of the central highlands. Their language in central Asia contains words of Sanskrit or Aryan derivation not found in Persian. While Turkish conquests and immigration dispossessed them of much of their country, they are noted for retaining an intellectual superiority over the surrounding mixed and Turkish tribes.

## Language and Religion
The inhabitants of Badakshan proper speak the Turki and Persian languages and follow the Sunnite sect, professing the orthodox doctrines of the Mahommedan law. 

In contrast, the mountainous districts are inhabited by Tajiks who profess the Shi'ite creed. These populations speak distinct provincial dialects or languages that differ materially from one another, although those in the principal places often combine these dialects with a knowledge of Persian. Specific dialects include:
* **Shighnáni** (or Shighni): spoken in Roshán and Shignán.
* **Ishkáshami**: spoken in Ishkásham.
* **Wakhi**: spoken in Wakhán.
* **Sanglichì**: spoken in Zebák and Sanglich.
* **Minjáni**: spoken in Minján.
