# Mammals and Flora of Asia

The biological landscape of Asia is characterized by a diverse array of fauna and flora, with northern regions often sharing mammals with Europe, though the Palaearctic fauna in Asia is enriched by numerous additional species.

## Mammals of Northern and Central Asia

Many mammals in northern Asia are forms restricted to temperate and cold regions. This environmental specialization results in a nearly nonexistent presence of the Quadrumana (monkeys), with only one species found in Japan and one or two others located in Tibet and northern China. In contrast, other groups are numerous:

*   **Carnivora:** This group is well-represented, particularly animals suited for cold climates, including foxes, wolves, weasels, and bears.
*   **Insectivora:** Hedgehogs, shrews, and various forms of moles prevail in this region.
*   **Rodents:** This order includes mice, squirrels, and hares. Specific characteristic forms of northern Asia include pikas (tailless hares, *Lagomys*) and marmots (*Arctomys*). Additionally, the common dormouse (*Muscardinus avellanarius*) ranges through Asia, Russia, and England.
*   **Ungulata:** This order is represented by several peculiar forms of antelope, such as the yellow sheep (*Gazella gutturosa*) and the saiga (*Saiga tatarica*). The mountain chains of Asia are home to as many as twelve or ten wild species of sheep (*Ovis*).

## Deer and Other Specialized Mammals

Within the Ungulata, northern Asia features characteristic forms of deer, particularly those belonging to the typical genus *Cervus*. One of the most peculiar types restricted to northern Asia is the musk deer (*Moschus*).

Other specialized mammals found across the continent include:
*   **Badgers:** The European badger (*Meles taxus* or *M. meles*) is found across northern Asia and Europe. In eastern Asia, two species occur: the Chinese badger (*M. chinensis*) and the white-tailed badger (*M. leucurus*). Japan is home to *M. anacuma*, while the Persian badger (*M. canescens*) replaces the European species in eastern Persia.
*   **Mongooses:** The genus *Herpestes* is common to the warmer parts of Asia and Africa, including the ordinary Indian mongoose (*H. mungo*).
*   **Bandicoot-Rats:** The *Nesocia bandicota*, a large rat measuring 12 to 15 inches to the root of the tail, inhabits Ceylon and India. Other species of this genus are found in central Asia and from Formosa to Palestine.

## Marine Life of Asia

The seas of Asia host a wide variety of fish. The *Percidae* (perches) are largely represented, with the genus *Serranus* being very numerous and featuring large forms in Asia. In the seas of southern Asia, particularly near coral reefs, the Squamipennes (scaly-finned fishes) are principally found. 

Other notable marine species include:
*   **Mullidae:** Red mullets represented by genera that differ from those found in Europe.
*   **Polynemidae:** These range from the Pacific through the Indian Ocean to the Atlantic. One species, the mango fish, is considered a delicacy and inhabits the seas from Siam to the Bay of Bengal.
*   **Sciaenidae:** These extend from China to the Bay of Bengal.
*   **Stromateidae:** Pomfrets that extend to the Pacific and China.
*   **Other species:** The seas of south Asia are also distributed with scabbard fishes (*Trichuridae*), lancet fishes (*Acanthuridae*), sword fishes (*Xiphidae*), as well as mullets, blennies, gobies, and mackerels.

## Botanical Species

Asia is home to several significant genera of shrubs and trees:

*   **Maple (*Acer*):** This genus includes approximately sixty species native to Asia, North America, and Europe. In Asia, they are especially prevalent in China, Japan, and the Himalayas. Many species from Japan have been introduced for ornamental use.
*   **Daphne:** This genus of shrubs contains about forty species native to temperate Asia and Europe. Specific Asian species include *D. japonica* from Japan, which has pinkish-purple flowers, and *D. indica* from China, which features red or white flowers. Both are greenhouse evergreens. Additionally, *D. mezereum* is native to north Asia and Europe.
