# Scandinavians and the Aryan Linguistic Group

The Scandinavians are identified in historical and philological records as speakers of languages belonging to the Aryan, Indo-European, or Indo-Germanic family. Specifically, the people of the Scandinavian peninsula, Denmark, and Iceland speak tongues derived from the Teutonic or Germanic branch of this family.

## Linguistic Classifications
The term "Aryan" has been applied in various ways by philologists. Max Muller utilized the term as a convenient shorthand for the entire body of Indo-European or Indo-Germanic languages. In this framework, "Aryas" are defined strictly as those who speak Aryan languages, regardless of their blood, color, or hereditary background. According to Muller, calling a group "Aryan" predicates nothing about them except that the grammar of their language is Aryan. He explicitly argued against using the term for ethnological purposes, asserting that an ethnologist who speaks of "Aryan race" or "Aryan blood" is as mistaken as a linguist who speaks of a "brachycephalic grammar."

In a broader linguistic context, the Aryan group is distinguished from other primary language groups such as the Semitic (which includes Hebrew and Arabic) and the Hamitic. While Aryan, Semitic, and Hamitic languages all share the feature of grammatical gender, they are otherwise diverse. The Semitic and Aryan tongues are believed to have been developed by human communities that were separately and independently formed, remaining in almost complete separation for thousands of years during early Neolithic times.

## Origins and Migration
The original speakers of the fundamental Aryan language, approximately 2000 to 3000 B.C., are described as a specialized Nordic race of fair white men who were accustomed to cattle and forests. These peoples wandered through the Danube valley, the Balkan peninsula, Asia Minor, and eastward toward the Central Asian Sea. 

As these populations spread, they encountered and mixed with other races, including Mediterranean and Iberian peoples. However, those who spread northward into Europe moved into areas that were previously uninhabited. Consequently, these northern groups remained racially more purely "Nordic blonds." These Aryan peoples reached Scandinavia many centuries B.C.

## Development of Northern Tongues
Certain Aryan tribes drifted toward the Baltic and into Scandinavia. Their speech evolved into ancient Norse, which served as the parent language for several modern tongues:
*   Swedish
*   Danish
*   Norwegian
*   Icelandic

This same Teutonic stock and form of speech are also represented by the people of Germany, Holland, and England. The Angles and Saxons, who belonged to the Teutons, are recognized as being of Aryan descent.

## Ethnological Perspectives
Ethnologists generally place Aryan-speaking peoples, along with Semitic and Hamitic-speaking peoples, within the Caucasian group of races. Within this group, the original Aryan speakers are associated with the Nordic race, while original Semites are associated with the Mediterranean type.

Some historical accounts suggest that the original Aryan speakers were a "forest people" rather than a "steppe people," and were characterized as a "cattle people" rather than a "horse people." While some Aryan-speaking invaders who spread north and east of the Assyrian and Babylonian empires may have mixed with Mongolian elements, those who moved into the northern reaches of Europe maintained a more distinct Nordic physiognomy.
