# Altyn Tagh

The Altyn Tagh is a mountain range located in Central Asia, serving as a significant geographical feature between the Tibetan plateau and the low-lying sands of the Gobi. It is part of a broader system of elevated valleys and ranges that form an intermediate steppe between the highest regions of the continent and the lowest.

## Geographical Position and Extent
The Altyn Tagh is situated east of Lop Nor. It branches off as a minor system to the north-east from the Kuen-lun mountains at approximately 83° E. longitude. From this point, the range extends eastwards, acting as a continuous water-divide that reaches the Nan Shan mountains, which are located north of the Koko Nor basin.

This system of mountains and elevated valleys, including the Tsaidam region, reaches altitudes of 8,000 to 9,000 feet above sea level. The presence of this elevated terrain between Tibet and the Gobi desert explains why the primary trade routes from Peking to the west were pushed significantly to the north.

## Nomenclature and Related Ranges
In geographical literature, the Altyn Tagh is associated with several other names and nearby ranges:
* **Astin-tagh:** Etymologically meaning the "Lower or Nearer Mountains," the Astin-tagh is identified as the westward continuation of the Nan-shan (Southern Mountains).
* **Ustun-tagh:** This term, meaning "Higher or Farther Mountains," appears on some maps (such as Stieler's Hand-atlas) as an alternative name for the Altyn Tagh.
* **Akato-tagh:** Also known as the Altun Range of Carey or the Chimen-tagh on Russian general staff maps, this parallel range stretches toward the E.N.E. and is composed of granite and schists. It is separated from the Astin-tagh by the Kakir valley, a sterile expanse of clay.

## Exploration and Discovery
The Altyn Tagh was discovered in 1876 by the Russian explorer Nicolai Prjevalsky. Prjevalsky's explorations of the region were extensive; during his fourth journey between 1883 and 1885, he crossed the Altyn Tagh to reach Lop Nor, traveling from Sining through northern Tibet.

Other explorers and travelers have also documented the range in their itineraries. Bonvalot and Prince Henri d’Orléans followed a route that took them from Lob-nor through Charkalyk and the Altyn Tagh in a nearly straight line toward Tengri Nor.

## Regional Context
The Altyn Tagh exists within a complex network of Central Asian highlands. To its south lies the Chang Tang, the elevated highland steppes of Tibet, for which the Kuen-lun mountains serve as the northern limit. To the north lies the Gobi depression. 

The broader Kuen-lun system extends from the Pamirs in the west to 113° E., where it merges with the escarpments of the Mongolian plateau. In a narrower sense, the Kuen-lun refers to the ranges separating the Takla-makan desert from the Tibetan plateau. The Altyn Tagh represents the eastward extension of this rugged mountainous environment, contributing to the hydrographic barriers that separate the central depressions of Asia from the low plains of China, India, and Siam.
