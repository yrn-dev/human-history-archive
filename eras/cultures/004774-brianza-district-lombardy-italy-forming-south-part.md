# BRIANZA

## Geography and Characteristics
Brianza is a district located in Lombardy, Italy. It forms the southern portion of the province of Como and is situated between the two southern arms of the lake of the same name. The region is characterized by its hilly terrain and is noted for being remarkably fertile and thickly populated. Due to its geography, Brianza serves as a favorite summer resort for the people of Milan.

## Regional Context: Lombardy
Brianza is part of the broader region of Lombardy in Northern Italy. Historically, before the Roman conquest, the area now known as Lombardy was not considered part of Italy; it was occupied by a powerful colony of Gauls who settled along the banks of the Po, extending from Romagna to Piedmont and diffusing their presence from the Apennine to the Alps.

The region's later history was marked by the establishment of the Lombards in North Italy around 570, following a great pestilence and the overthrow of the East Gothic kings by Justinian. The Lombards remained a source of fear and danger to the Popes, leading to an alliance between the Frankish King Pepin and the Papacy against them. Eventually, Charlemagne completely subjugated Lombardy in 774, sending his father-in-law, the King of Lombardy, to a monastery. In 781, Charlemagne had his son, Pepin, crowned King of Italy in Rome.

## Surrounding Areas and Infrastructure
The province of Como, which encompasses Brianza, also includes other towns such as Castiglione Olona. This town is located 27 miles northeast of Milan by rail and is home to the collegiate church erected around 1428 by Cardinal Branda Castiglione, which features frescoes by Masolino of Florence.

To the west of the Lake of Como lies the Lake of Lugano (also called Ceresio), one of the smaller lakes in Lombardy. It is an irregularly shaped lake with a surface 899 feet above sea-level and a maximum depth of 945 feet. The lake is situated among the outer spurs of the Alps, which separate the Adda basin from the Ticino (Tessin) basin. While a portion of the lake's area is in the Swiss Canton of Ticino—formed in 1803 from conquests made by the Swiss from the Milanese in 1512—the remainder is located in Italy.

## Administrative and Linguistic History
In the context of the Austrian Empire, the Italian districts, including Lombardy and later Venetia, held a distinct status regarding language. While German was the ordinary language of the government, the army, and the central public offices in Vienna, Italian continued to be used for the internal service of government offices in the Italian districts.
