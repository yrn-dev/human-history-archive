# Barabra

## Definition and Ethnology
The Barabra are complex Nubian races located in the Egyptian Sudan. Their original stock is identified as Hamitic-Berber, though this has been long modified by negro crossings. A specific tribe by this name resides on the banks of the Nile in the region between Assuan and Wadi Haifa.

## Etymology and Historical Records
The origin of the name "Barabra" is subject to various derivations. It is suggested to be derived from *Berberi* (meaning "people of Berber"). There is evidence that it is a real ethnical name that was later confused with the Greek and Roman term *barbarus*, only to be revived in its original meaning following the Moslem conquest.

Historical inscriptions support the antiquity of the name:
*   **Tethmosis I:** An inscription on a gateway identifies *Barabara* as one of 113 tribes conquered by the pharaoh.
*   **Rameses II:** An inscription at Karnak, dating to approximately 1300 B.C., refers to a southern conquered people as *Beraberata*.

## Assuan and the Southern Frontier
Assuan (also known as Aswan) is a town in Upper Egypt located on the east bank of the Nile, facing Elephantine Island below the First Cataract. It serves as the capital of the southernmost province of Egypt and is situated 590 miles south of Cairo by rail. 

Historically, the area served as a critical frontier. In ancient times, the island of Elephantine housed the city of Yeb, the capital of the frontier nome. Later, the city of *Swan* (the Mart), which became the Greek *Syene* and Arabic *Aswan*, grew near the granite quarries. Syene was a strategic base for Eratosthenes' measurements of the earth and was strongly garrisoned during Roman times to resist attacks from desert tribes.

By the 12th century, the town was raided and ruined by Nubian and Bedouin tribes. In the 16th century, following the Turkish conquest of Egypt, Selim I established a garrison there, and the descendants of these soldiers make up part of the modern population. In times of peace, Assuan functioned as an entrepot for trade with Abyssinia and the Sudan, with trade valued at £2,000,000 annually in 1880.

## Regional Geography and Infrastructure
The landscape around Assuan is defined by the Nile and its cataracts. Three and a half miles above the town, the Assuan Dam was completed in December 1902. This engineering work created a vast lake above the dam, resulting in the partial or total submergence of several islands, including Philae. 

The village of Shellal, located on the east bank opposite Philae, serves as the starting point for steamers heading to the Sudan and marks the southern terminus of the Egyptian railway system.

## Nubian Context
The region south of Egypt has been inhabited by various groups, including the Blemmyes and the Nobatae. The Nobatae converted to Christianity around the middle of the 6th century. A chieftain named Silko later founded a state with its capital at Old Dongola, which lasted for eight or nine hundred years. 

The relationship between Egypt and Nubia was often characterized by conflict and tribute. After the Moslems captured Dongola in 652, the country was placed under a tribute known as *bakt*, requiring the annual sending of 400 men to Egypt. By the 12th century, the Nubian kingdom was in a flourishing state, extending from Assuan southward to the 4th cataract. However, the region later saw the rise of the Funj (or Funniyeh), a mixed negroid race that became the dominant power in Sennar during the 15th century before being destroyed by the Egyptians in 1821.
