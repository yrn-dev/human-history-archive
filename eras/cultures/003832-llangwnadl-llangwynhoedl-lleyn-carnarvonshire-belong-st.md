# Llangwnadl (Llangwynhoedl)

## Overview and Ownership
Llangwnadl, also referred to as Llangwynhoedl, is a location situated in Lleyn, within South Carnarvonshire. The rectory of Llangwnadl belongs to St John's College, Cambridge. This ownership extends to other rectories as well, specifically those of Penmachno and Aberdaron.

## Regional Context: Aberdaron and Bardsey
Llangwnadl is associated through its rectory ownership with Aberdaron, which is located on the mainland opposite the island of Bardsey. Bardsey, known in Welsh as *Ynys Enlli* ("isle of the current"), is an island positioned at the northern extremity of Cardigan Bay. While traditionally associated with South Wales, it is included in Carnarvonshire, North Wales.

The island of Bardsey measures approximately 2-1/2 miles in length and 3/4 miles in breadth, covering an area of some 370 acres. Its geography is characterized by high cliffs on the northwest side and a fairly deep harbour on the southeast. Roughly one-third of the island's terrain is hilly, and the land is used for growing oats and barley.

## Religious and Historical Significance of Bardsey
The island was made famous by St Dubricius, who died there in 612. Bardsey served as a burial site for monks whose friends could afford to transport their bodies there, with all the great abbeys of England sending their quota. A significant portion of the island, spanning about 3 to 4 acres, remains a great cemetery featuring close-set, rough graves topped with slabs.

In the northeast of the island lie the ruins of the tower of St Mary's abbey, which dates to the 13th century. Historically, priests fled to the island via the road of the Rivals (*Yn Eifl*) hill in South Carnarvonshire following the great massacre of Bangor-is-coed in 613, perpetrated by Ethelfride of Northumbria. Access to the island is facilitated by roads running from the north, east, and south, featuring monks' wells placed at intervals of 7 to 9 miles.

## Local Administration and Land Tenure
Although the rectory of Aberdaron does not belong to the island of Bardsey, the farm known as "Cwrt" (Court)—the site where the abbot held his court—remains associated with Bardsey. Following the Civil War and the partial sack of Norwich by the Puritans, Bardsey was granted to John Wynn of Bodvel, Carnarvonshire. 

The property subsequently passed through Mary Bodvel to her husband, the earl of Radnor, who then sold it to Dr Wilson of York. Dr Wilson later sold the property to Sir John Wynn of Bodfean Hall and Glynllifon, Carnarvonshire.

## Current Status of Bardsey
The inhabitants of Bardsey are farmers and fishermen. There is no Anglican church on the island, as the residents are Dissenters. The island is also known for its lighthouse, which stands 140 feet high with a fixed light visible for 17 miles.
