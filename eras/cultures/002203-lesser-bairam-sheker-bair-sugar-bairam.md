# Lesser Bairam

Lesser Bairam, also known as *Kutshuk Bairam* or *Sheker Bairam* ("Sugar Bairam"), is one of the two principal festivals of Islam. The term "Bairam" is a Perso-Turkish word meaning "festival." Among Arabic-speaking Moslems, the occasion is referred to as *'Id al-Fitr* ("Festival of Fast-breaking") or *Al-'id al-saghir* ("Lesser Festival").

## Calendar and Timing
The festival is governed by a lunar calendar, which causes it to move through the solar year every thirty-two years. Lesser Bairam follows immediately after the ninth month, known as the fasting-month of Ramadan. It occupies the first three days of the tenth month, Shawwal. Due to this timing, the festival is also referred to by Turks as *Ramazan Bairam*.

## Observances and Traditions
Lesser Bairam is characterized by more outward signs of rejoicing than the "Greater Festival." Common practices associated with the celebration include:

*   **Social Interactions:** The holding of official receptions and the payment of private visits. Friends use the occasion to congratulate one another.
*   **Gift-Giving and Attire:** The giving of presents and the wearing of new clothes.
*   **Commemoration:** The visiting of the graves of relatives.

## Comparison with Greater Bairam
While Lesser Bairam focuses on the conclusion of the fasting month, the second principal festival is the "Greater Festival," known to Turks as *Qurban Bairam* ("Sacrifice Bairam") and to Arabic speakers as *Al-'id al-kabir* or *'Id al-adha* ("Festival of Sacrifice"). 

The Greater Festival occurs on the tenth and the following two or three days of the final month, *Dhu-l-hijja*. Its primary religious observance involves the sacrifice of a legal animal—such as a cow, a he-goat, a ram, or a camel—by those who can afford it. This act commemorates the ransom of Ishmael with a ram. Pilgrims perform this sacrifice in the valley of Mina, while others throughout the Moslem world either consume the meat or provide it to the poor. Aside from the sacrifice, the Greater Festival is celebrated similarly to the Lesser Festival, though it is noted to be observed with less ardour.
