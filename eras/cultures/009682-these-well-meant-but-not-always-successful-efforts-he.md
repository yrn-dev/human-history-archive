## The Reign of King Joseph in Spain
King Joseph was called away by Napoleon in May 1808 to take the crown of Spain. Despite his benevolent intentions, which he announced in a proclamation dated June 23, 1808, reconciliation between the French and the Spaniards proved impossible due to Napoleon's treatment of the de facto king, Ferdinand VII. Joseph's sovereignty was largely titular, and he was forced to flee Madrid in August 1808 following a Spanish victory at Baylen.

Although reinstated by Napoleon at the end of 1808, Joseph remained in a subordinate position. He offered to abdicate on four separate occasions, but Napoleon ignored these requests and ordered him to govern with more energy. Between February and May 1810, Napoleon further diminished Joseph's authority by placing the north-eastern and northern provinces under the command of French generals as military districts. 

Joseph's adviser, Miot de Melito, noted that while Joseph attempted to be a constitutional king, he could only succeed in the Peninsula as an "instrument of a military power." By April 1811, facing an exhausted treasury, Joseph traveled to Paris to seek better terms or offer his abdication. He returned with a monthly subsidy of 500,000 francs and control over the army of the centre, the smallest of the five French armies. However, following Wellington's victory at Salamanca on July 22, 1812, Joseph was compelled to leave his capital.

## The Gallipoli Expedition
By September 1915, it became evident that the Gallipoli expedition was unable to succeed. Throughout the summer, a hopeless struggle persisted for the peninsula's heights. In August, new divisions and the Anzacs (the Australian and New Zealand Army Corps) launched an attack, with the left wing at Suvla Bay attempting to turn the enemy flank, but this effort failed.

The entry of Bulgaria into the war prevented the arrival of further reserves, as the Allies were required to protect the northern frontier of Greece and assist Serbia by sending troops to Salonika. Consequently, the only remaining option was evacuation. After discussions, it was decided to evacuate the Anzac and Suvla positions while retaining those at Cape Helles. This move was expected to result in heavy losses; some estimates predicted a 15 per cent loss, while others anticipated the loss of at least one division, as embarkation in the face of the enemy typically involved many casualties and a stiff rearguard fight.

## Military Conflicts in Japan
In 1570, Nobunaga sought to test his fortunes by declaring war against Asakura Yoshikage, who had refused to recognize the new shogun. While the Owari forces saw early success, the situation shifted when Asai Nagamasa marched from Omi to threaten Nobunaga's rear. 

Although Nobunaga had previously married his sister to Nagamasa to secure friendship, Nagamasa had maintained a close amity with Yoshikage and had stipulated from the start that Nobunaga should not wage war against him. Unprepared for this contingency, Nobunaga found himself caught between Yoshikage's army in front and Nagamasa's in the rear. Following the advice of Tokugawa Ieyasu, who had joined the expedition into Echizen, Nobunaga decided to countermarch rapidly for Kyoto. To hold Yoshikage's forces in check, Hideyoshi was left with three thousand men.

## Naval Technical Efforts in the British Empire
During a period of naval expansion, an effort was made to secure 15-inch guns for all battle-cruisers and battleships in a new programme. Lord Fisher strongly advocated for this "big punch," arguing that failing to do so would be treason to the Empire. 

The project presented significant risks and difficulties: enlarging the guns required enlarging the ships, which increased costs. Furthermore, the redesign had to be completed without delay so that the guns would be ready simultaneously with the turrets. At the time, no modern 15-inch gun existed, though the previous move to 13.5-inch guns had improved power, accuracy, and longevity. The Ordnance Board produced a design, and the firm Armstrongs agreed to execute it in secrecy. Rear-Admiral Moore, the Director of Naval Ordnance, was willing to stake his professional existence on the project, though there remained uncertainty regarding new stresses that might develop in the 15-inch model.

## Greek Political and Historical Efforts
In Athens, the statesman Demosthenes emphasized that upholding Hellenic liberty against foreigners was an obligation for all Greeks, particularly Athens. He argued that success depended on earnest and persevering individual efforts involving money and arms, and he worked to shame Athenians out of their habit of avoiding personal military service.

In other contexts, historical critics have employed various "efforts of ingenuity" to reconcile the Egyptian chronology of Manetho with biblical chronology. Some suggested that dynasties mentioned as successive were actually simultaneous, while others proposed that the years listed were not full years, but rather months of one or three.
