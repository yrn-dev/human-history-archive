# The Tanganyika Languages

The Tanganyika languages constitute a group of Bantu dialects spoken primarily in the regions of Tanganyika. This linguistic group includes languages such as Kiguha, Ki-rega, and Kabwari.

## Geographical Distribution
The Tanganyika languages are chiefly spoken in the regions to the north, east, and west-north-west of Tanganyika. Their northern boundary extends to the vicinity of Lake Albert Edward, while their southern limit reaches the Lukuga outlet of Tanganyika. To the west, these languages are bounded by the Manyema genus and the Congo Forest.

On the east coast of Tanganyika, languages such as Kigeye and Ki-rundi are spoken. While these eastern dialects appear to be more closely connected with the Uganda-Unyoro group (Group No. 1), they are often more conveniently included within the Tanganyika group.

## Linguistic Characteristics and Concord
The Tanganyika languages belong to the Bantu family, a group of tongues characterized by the governing of unchangeable roots by prefixes. A primary feature of these languages is the "concord," which involves the repetition of pronominal particles.

The function of the concord can be illustrated by comparing a reconstructed "Old Bantu" sentence with a modern dialect. In Old Bantu, the sentence *Babo mbaba-ntu ba bi ba-bo-ta tu-ba-oga* (They these-they person they bad they who kill we fear them) is rendered in the modern Kiguha dialect of North-West Tanganyika as *Umuti guno gugwa ugumona?* (The tree falls; dost thou see it?), demonstrating the persistence of the concord system in the region's speech.

## Related Linguistic Groups
The Tanganyika languages exist in proximity to several other Bantu groups:

*   **Uganda-Unyoro Group:** This group includes dialects between the Victoria Nile, Busoga, and the east coast of Lake Albert. Ki-rundi, spoken in the Burundi country at the north end of Tanganyika, and other languages of eastern Tanganyika down to Ufipa, are closely allied to the Unyoro sub-section, though they may adhere more closely to the Tanganyika group.
*   **Manyema (Baenya) Group:** Located between the main stream of the Luapula-Congo and the western watershed of Tanganyika, this group includes languages such as Soko, Lokele, Kusu, Yalulema, and Tu-rumbu. The Manyema genus merges into the "much purer" Bantu dialects of the Tanganyika and Uganda-Unyoro groups on its eastern side.
*   **Bemba (Emba) Group:** This genus occupies the area between Lake Mweru, the Upper Chambezi river, and the south and south-west coasts of Tanganyika. This domain includes the locally-modified Ki-mambwe and Ki-lungu of South and South-East Tanganyika.
*   **North Nyasa (Nkonde) Group:** This group comprises dialects of the north and north-west coasts of Lake Nyasa, such as Iki-nyikiusa and Ici-wandia. It extends to the Nyasa-Tanganyika plateau and perhaps as far north-west as the shores of Lake Rukwa and the Fipa country. Iki-fipa specifically shows affinities with the Tanganyika and western Victoria-Nyanza languages.

## Broader Context of Bantu Speech
The languages of the Tanganyika region are part of a wider distribution of Bantu speech across the continent. Some of the most archaic dialects currently spoken are found on the north coast of Tanganyika, as well as in the Bemba country to the south-west of the lake.

These languages are connected to the Bantu languages of Uganda through dialects spoken to the north and west of Tanganyika. Furthermore, there is a slight resemblance between these languages and Zulu-Kaffir, suggesting that the Zulu-Kaffir race may have migrated from countries to the north-east of Tanganyika, crossing the Zambezi to reach their home.
