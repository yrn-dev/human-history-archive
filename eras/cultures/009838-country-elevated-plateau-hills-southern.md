# Bondu

## Geography and Topography
Bondu is a French protectorate located in West Africa, dependent on the colony of Senegal. It is situated between 13 and 15 degrees North latitude and 12 and 13 degrees West longitude, positioned between the upper course of the Gambia and the Faleme river. 

The country is characterized as an elevated plateau. Hills are located in the central and southern parts of the region; these areas are generally unproductive and covered with stunted wood. In contrast, the lower country is fertile and features various valuable fruit-trees, as well as the tamarind and the baobab. The region is traversed by torrents known as *marigots*, which flow rapidly during the rainy season but remain empty during the dry season.

## Population and Culture
The inhabitants of Bondu are mostly Fula, although the Mandingos largely control the trade. The laws and religion of the country are Mahommedan, though the precepts of the faith are not observed with great rigor.

## Political History
European contact with Bondu includes the visit of Mungo Park in 1795, the first European traveler to visit the country, who faced many exactions from the reigning prince. At that time, the royal residence was located at Fatteconda. By 1818, when Major W. Gray, a British officer attempting to solve the Niger problem, visited the region, the royal residence had been moved to Bulibani. Bulibani is a small town with a population of approximately 3,000, enclosed by a strong clay wall.

In August 1845, the king of Bondu signed a treaty that recognized French sovereignty over the country. Although the natives disregarded this treaty, Bondu came definitely under French control in 1858. Since that time, the country has experienced considerable prosperity.
