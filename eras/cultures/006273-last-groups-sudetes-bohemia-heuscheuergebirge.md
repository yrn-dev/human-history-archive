# The Heuscheuergebirge and the Sudetes of Bohemia

The Heuscheuergebirge is one of the final groups of the Sudetes mountain range located within Bohemia. Reaching an altitude of 2,532 ft., it forms part of the broader mountain system that defines the north-east frontier of the region.

## The Sudetes Range
The Sudetes comprise several distinct mountain groups that border Bohemia to the north-east. These include the Lausitzergebirge, which reaches 2,500 ft., and the Jeschkengebirge, which attains an altitude of 3,322 ft. Also part of this system is the Isergebirge, featuring the Tafelfichte as its highest peak at 3,683 ft.

The Riesengebirge (known in Czech as *Kroknose*) are among the highest mountains in central Europe, surpassed only by the Alps. The highest point of the Riesengebirge is the Schneekoppe, which reaches an altitude of 5,264 ft. The Sudetes, along with the parallel Bohmerwald range, generally run in a direction from the south-east to the north-west.

## Regional Orography
Bohemia is shaped like an irregular rhomb and is characterized as a separate massif from an orographic perspective. It is bordered on three sides by mountain ranges:
* **North-West:** The Erzgebirge (Czech *Rudo Hori*), which have an average altitude of 2,600 ft. and a highest point at the Keilberg (4,080 ft.).
* **North-East:** The Sudetes, including the Riesengebirge and the Heuscheuergebirge.
* **South-West:** The Bohmerwald (Bohemian Forest), which is divided by the pass of Neumark. The southern part of this range, known as *Sumava*, contains the Plockenstein (4,513 ft.) and the Osser (4,053 ft.), though its highest peak, the Arber (4,872 ft.), is located in Bavaria.

The fourth side of the Bohemian rhomb is formed by the Bohemian-Moravian Hills, a broad series of low hills or a plateau composed of primitive rocks that reach altitudes of 2,500 ft. in certain areas.

## Geological and Mineral Characteristics
The region is noted for its diverse mineral deposits. While salt is entirely absent, Bohemia contains nearly every other useful mineral. Lignite and coal are of primary importance; some of Europe's richest lignite fields are found in the north-east corner of Bohemia near Teplitz, Dux, Ossegg, Falkenau, and Brux. 

Other significant minerals include:
* **Metals:** Silver is extracted at Joachimsthal and Pribram, while lead is also found at Pribram. Tin is found at Graupen in the Erzgebirge, the only location in Austria where it occurs. Iron ores are located at Nucic and Krusnahora.
* **Rare Elements:** Uranium and radium are extracted near Joachimsthal.
* **Other Minerals:** Graphite is found near Budweis and Krumau, and porcelain-earth is located near Carlsbad. The region is also known for building stone and the Bohemian garnet (pyrope).

In terms of rock formations, Bohemia contains leucite-basalts and the rare melilite-basalts (a lime-alumina-silicate). Additionally, marcasite is found in the plastic clay of the Brown Coal formation at Littmitz, near Carlsbad, where it has been mined for the production of ferrous sulphate and sulphur.
