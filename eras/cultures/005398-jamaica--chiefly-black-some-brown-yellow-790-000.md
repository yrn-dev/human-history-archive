# Jamaica

## Population and Demographics
Jamaica is one of the colonies in the West Indies, a group consisting of seventeen islands or groups of islands along with the continental colonies of Honduras and British Guiana. Within this region, Jamaica possesses one of the largest coloured populations, totaling 790,000 people. This population is described as being chiefly black, with some brown and yellow individuals.

The demographic composition of Jamaica is varied and consists largely of imported African negroes, who contribute four-fifths of the population. Additionally, the island is home to a considerable number of imported Chinese and East Indian coolies. The aboriginal races of the region include Caribs and American Indians from the mainland. These aboriginal groups have experienced intermixture with Portuguese and Spanish blood, resulting in the appearance of many mixed types.

## Regional Context
Jamaica is part of a broader West Indian coloured population that totals approximately 1,912,655. Other colonies in this group with significant coloured populations include British Guiana, with 286,000 black and brown inhabitants, and Trinidad and Tobago, with 250,000 black and brown inhabitants. Together, these three colonies account for 1,326,000 of the region's coloured population.

On a wider scale, the West Indies and Bermuda have a white population of 100,000. This is part of the empire's total white population of over 53,000,000 (as of 1901), which is primarily of Anglo-Saxon race but includes Spanish, Dutch, and French peoples.

## Natural Resources and Industry
Jamaica is a source of fustic, a yellow dyestuff derived from the wood of the dyer's mulberry tree (*Morus tinctoria*). Fustic remains an important and widely used dyestuff because it is inexpensive and the resulting colours are satisfactorily fast to other influences and light.

The colouring properties of fustic vary depending on the mordant used:
*   **Aluminium:** Produces yellow.
*   **Tin:** Produces a brighter yellow.
*   **Chromium:** Yields an "old-gold" shade or olive-yellow.
*   **Iron:** Produces olive-green.

Fustic is primarily used in the dyeing of wool, often in combination with other dyestuffs to provide the yellow component of compound shades. The wood contains two colouring principles, morin and maclurin, though morin is the most important and is generally considered the true colouring matter.
