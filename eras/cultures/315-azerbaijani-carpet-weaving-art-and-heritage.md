# Azerbaijani Carpet Weaving: A Tapestry of Art and Heritage

Azerbaijani carpet weaving is not merely a craft; it is a profound visual language that encodes the history, spirituality, and geography of the South Caucasus. For centuries, these textiles have served as an artistic chronicle of the Azerbaijani people, blending ancient nomadic traditions with the sophisticated aesthetics of settled urban centers. In 2010, this legacy was formally recognized by UNESCO, which inscribed Azerbaijani carpet weaving on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

### Overview
The art of the Azerbaijani carpet is characterized by a diverse array of schools—most notably the Guba, Shirvan, Baku, Ganja, and Karabakh schools. Each region developed a distinct stylistic identity. For instance, the Guba carpets are renowned for their geometric precision and intricate patterns, while the Karabakh carpets often feature larger, floral compositions and vibrant palettes reflecting the lushness of the region.

Central to this art is the use of natural dyes derived from plants, minerals, and insects, which grant the carpets their legendary longevity and depth of color. The motifs used are rarely purely decorative; they are symbolic. The "Buta" (a stylized flame or almond shape) is perhaps the most iconic, representing fire, eternity, and the spiritual longing of the weaver.

### Evolution of the Craft

| Era | Significance |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Ancient Era** | Early evidence of weaving; integration of Zoroastrian and pagan symbols. |
| **Islamic Golden Age** | Refinement of floral patterns and geometric symmetry; growth of trade routes. |
| **Safavid Period** | Peak of royal workshops; carpets become luxury exports to European courts. |
| **19th - 20th Century** | Transition to industrialization, though traditional village weaving persists. |
| **Modern Era** | Institutional preservation and UNESCO recognition as global heritage. |

### Key Facts
*   **The Loom:** Traditional Azerbaijani carpets are woven on vertical or horizontal looms using a symmetrical knot (the "Ghiordes" knot), which ensures the durability of the pile.
*   **Regional Schools:** The **Shirvan** school is praised for its fine weave and small-scale patterns, whereas the **Baku** school often incorporates elements of urban luxury and courtly elegance.
*   **Materiality:** High-quality Azerbaijani carpets are primarily made from wool, though silk is frequently used in "Palas" or "Kelaghayi" styles to add luster and detail.
*   **Symbolism:** The colors are deeply intentional: red often symbolizes fire and vitality, blue represents the sky and peace, and yellow evokes the sun and prosperity.

### Did You Know?
1. **The Flame Symbol:** The prevalence of flame-like patterns in Azerbaijani carpets is a nod to the region's ancient identity as the "Land of Fire," rooted in the natural gas vents of the earth.
2. **A Mathematical Art:** The complex geometric patterns found in Guba carpets are often based on sophisticated mathematical symmetries that predated formal geometry in the region.
3. **Royal Prestige:** During the Safavid dynasty, Azerbaijani carpets were so highly prized that they were frequently used as diplomatic gifts to foreign monarchs to signal the empire's wealth and cultural sophistication.
