# Items Subject to Criticism and Reduction

In the records of human history, the term "item" is applied to a diverse array of subjects, ranging from financial accounts and military logistics to historical facts and philosophical concepts. Depending on the context, these items may be subjected to critical analysis, mathematical reduction, or administrative decrease.

## Financial and Administrative Items
In the context of state finance, a budget is defined as an account of the ways and means used to balance income and expenditure over a definite period. These accounts are generally managed by a finance minister. In the United Kingdom, the chancellor of the exchequer presents statements to the House of Commons regarding the results of the previous finance year and estimates for the current year. 

These financial items are divided into two primary categories:
*   **Consolidated fund services:** This includes the debt charge, pensions, courts of justice, and the civil list.
*   **Supply services:** This encompasses the remaining national expenditures, such as the revenue departments, the civil service, the army, the navy, the post-office, and telegraph services.

When estimated revenue shows a large surplus over estimated expenditure, there is room for the reduction or remission of certain taxes. Such relief is typically limited to the amount of the surplus realized in the previous year. Similarly, in administrative restructuring, such as the formation of a new division within a war staff, personnel may be reduced from existing divisions, such as the Mobilisation and Operations Divisions.

## Logistics and Material Items
In naval warfare, the cost of fuel is considered a relatively small item compared to the immense cost of constructing and maintaining a modern warship's fighting efficiency. Consequently, the increase or decrease in fuel costs is often viewed as a negligible quantity. However, the physical properties of fuel items can be reduced to improve efficiency. For example, changing the type of fuel can reduce its weight by 30%. 

Such a reduction in weight not only affects the fuel itself but also reduces the consumable stores required for stokers. If the crew is halved through the abolition of stokers, the radius of effective action regarding consumable stores is doubled. Furthermore, using liquid fuel instead of coal increases the radius of effective action by 50% for the same weight of fuel. In terms of space, 36 cubic feet of stored oil possess the same practical calorific value as 67 cubic feet of coal.

## Historical and Textual Items
In the study of ancient texts and chronicles, "items" often refer to specific facts, figures, or entries in a list. These are frequently subject to criticism and mathematical reduction to ensure accuracy.

In the analysis of Babylonian talents, historians have encountered figures that are irreconcilable with total sums. To resolve these discrepancies, separate items may be reduced by specific proportions—such as 70:60 or 78:60—to determine if they align with the total sum provided in the text.

Similarly, in the recording of Athenian defeats at the territory known as "Nine Ways" (the site where Amphipolis was later built), historians categorize specific events as "items" in a catalogue. For instance, the capture of Amphipolis by Brasidas is listed as the third item, while the defeat of Kleon by Brasidas is the fourth.

Critics of historical narratives also examine how poets use "items of fact." Some narratives blend fragments of truth from different centuries with fiction, selecting specific items of fact only because they suit the purposes of the story rather than because they are attested by historical evidence.

## Philosophical and Intellectual Items
The application of criticism to intellectual items is a hallmark of the development of the Grecian mind. Early social philosophy consisted of homely sayings or admonitions that were accepted without inquiry. However, this changed as figures like Simonidês of Keôs began to severely criticize the authors and songs of predecessors like Kleobulus.

This evolution culminated in the approach of Sokratês, who subjected all social and ethical doctrines to the scrutiny of reason. This analytical interest in verifying and proving truth is described as a "capital item of human progress." In the dialogues of Plato, specifically the *Protagoras*, Sokratês is depicted as considering right conduct to be a matter of justly calculating items of pleasure and pain against one another, a process referred to as ἡ μετρητικὴ τέχνη.
