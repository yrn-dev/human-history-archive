# The General Assemblage of Animals and Plants of Northern Asia

The biological landscape of northern Asia is characterized by a strong affinity with the adjacent regions of Europe and the specific climatic conditions of the Asian interior. The distribution of flora and fauna in this region is largely dictated by temperature, rainfall, and elevation.

## Siberian and Central Asian Flora and Fauna
In Siberia, north of the 50th parallel, the general assemblage of plants and animals closely resembles that found in adjacent parts of Europe with similar climates. While the winters in Siberia are more severe and the summers hotter than in similarly situated European regions, the moderate rainfall is sufficient to support abundant vegetation and maintain the water supply of the great rivers that flow toward the Arctic Sea.

South of the Siberian border lies the region commonly referred to as central Asia, which includes Mongolia, Turkestan, and western Manchuria. This area is characterized as almost rainless, experiencing intense summer heat and extreme winter severity. Consequently, the plants and animals of central Asia possess a special character suited to these peculiar conditions. These species are more closely allied to those of the adjacent northern Siberian tract than to other bordering regions.

## Regional Affinities and Connections
The biological character of northern and eastern Asia shows various connections to other continents and regions:

*   **North America:** Many temperate forms of life from the American continent extend to north China and Japan. Some of these special forms extend as far as the Himalaya, reaching almost to Kashmir.
*   **Japan and China:** North China and Japan share many forms of life. While the interior of northern China remains under-explored, it is probable that a botanical connection exists between it and the temperate region of the Himalaya.
*   **The Himalaya and Tibet:** On the Tibetan plateau, increased dryness causes the flora to assume many features of the Siberian type, including various Siberian genera and species. Some of these Siberian forms extend into the plains of upper India and the rainy parts of the mountains. Additionally, marine plants are found at elevations of 14,000 to 15,000 feet above the sea, particularly near salt lakes.

## Specific Animal Species
The fauna of northern and central Asia includes specialized mammals adapted to the cold and arid environments:

*   **The White Fox:** This species inhabits the extreme northern sections of Siberia, as well as regions like Greenland and Hudson Bay. The Siberian variety is described as more woolly and whiter than the silky, creamy-colored Canadian variety. Specimens found in central Asia, near or within Chinese territory, are yellowish and of poorer quality.
*   **Reindeer:** In northern Siberia, reindeer are utilized for both draught and as saddle animals.
*   **Other Domesticates:** While horses are perfected in the hot, dry countries of western Asia and Arabia, ponies are most highly esteemed from the hilly tracts and wetter regions of the east.

## Comparative Vegetation and Climate Zones
The northern regions contrast with the temperate and tropical belts of Asia. A temperate zone extends from the Mediterranean across to northern China and the Himalaya, embracing northern Persia, Afghanistan, and Asia Minor. This belt maintains a vigorous vegetation due to sufficient rainfall and a climate where winters and summers, though severe or hot, are not extreme. The plants and animals here show a marked similarity to those of southern Europe.

In the higher, cooler ranges of the Himalaya, there is a closer general approach to European forms. At the greatest elevations, many alpine plants are believed to be identical to species found in the north Arctic regions. In contrast, the tropical flora of eastern India and the Malayan peninsula is dominant in the south-eastern regions, though it becomes less marked as one moves westward toward regions of increased winter cold and diminished rainfall.
