# Tarkani Pathans

The Tarkani (also referred to as Tarkalanri) are a Pathan race of Afghan origin primarily inhabiting the district of Bajour in the North-West Frontier Province of India.

## Distribution and Demographics
The Tarkani Pathans inhabit Bajour almost exclusively. Within this district, they are sub-divided into three primary groups: the Mamunds, the Isazai, and the Ismailzai. Together with a small number of Utmauzais and Mohmands, the total population of these groups in the region is approximately 100,000.

## Geography of Bajour
Bajour is a small district measuring roughly 45 miles in length and 20 miles in width. It is situated at a high elevation to the east of the Kunar valley. The district is separated from the Kunar valley by a continuous line of rugged frontier hills that serve as a barrier, though this barrier is passable at one or two specific points. Historically, the old road from Kabul to India utilized this route before the Khyber Pass became the primary path.

The topography of the district is characterized by a gradual downward slope that begins at the dividing ridge overlooking the Kunar and terminates at the Panjkora river to the east. The drainage of the district flows eastwards. Bajour is bordered to the south by the mountain district of the Mohmands (another Pathan race) and to the east, beyond the Panjkora river, by the hills of Swat, which are dominated by a different Pathan race. To the north, a watershed separates Bajour from the small state of Dir.

## Political History and Administration
Nawagai serves as the chief town of Bajour. To ensure the safeguarding of the road to Chitral, the khan of Nawagai remains under British protection. 

Jandol, located in one of the northern valleys of Bajour, lost its political significance following the efforts of its chief, Umra Khan, to seize control of Dir, Bajour, and a significant portion of the Kunar valley. The conflict between Umra Khan and the amir of Kabul, who claimed sovereignty over these same areas, resulted in two major events:
1. The demarcation agreement of 1893, which established the boundary of Afghanistan in Kunar.
2. The 1895 invasion of Chitral and the subsequent siege of the Chitral fort by Umra Khan, who had not been a party to the 1893 boundary settlement.

## Tribal Affiliations
The Tarkanis are categorized under the term "Bar Durani." This label is sometimes applied to independent Pathan tribes inhabiting the Salt Range, parts of the Indus valley, the range of Suliman, and the hill districts south of the Hindu Kush. These areas were first conceded to these tribes by Ahmad Shah Durani, the founder of Afghan national independence. Other tribes included in the Bar Durani group are the Mohmands, Afridis, Shinwaris, Orakzais, and Yusafzai, as well as the Pathan tribes of the plains of Peshawar and those of Khattak and Bangash, though the archive notes that the derivation of some of these tribes from true Durani stock is considered doubtful.
