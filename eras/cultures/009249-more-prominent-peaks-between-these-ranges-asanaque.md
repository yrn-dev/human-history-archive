# Mountain Ranges and Prominent Peaks

The Earth's geography is characterized by various mountain systems, ranging from the high-altitude massifs of the Andes and the Himalayas to the volcanic ranges of Europe and Asia. These formations often serve as physical barriers, influence regional climates, and define political or tribal boundaries.

## The Andean System in Bolivia
In Bolivia, approximately two-fifths of the total area is comprised of the Andean cordilleras. These are divided into two great parallel chains that form the massif of the Andean system, consisting of parallel ranges that enclose deep ravines and elevated plateaus. To the north-east of Lake Titicaca lies the Nudo de Apolobamba, a knot of intersecting ridges containing some of South America's highest peaks.

The western range, known as the Cordillera Occidental, forms the western rampart of the Bolivian tableland (alta-planicie). This plateau extends 500 meters in length and 80 meters in breadth, with an average elevation of about 12,650 feet near Lake Titicaca. The northern region of the plateau is called the puna, while the southern part is the "desert of Lipez." The highest peak on the plateau is the Cerro de Tahua at 17,454 feet. Overlooking the plateau from the west are several snow-clad peaks, including Sajama (21,047 ft.), Huallatiri (21,654 ft.), Parinacota (20,918 ft.), and Pomarape (20,505 ft.), as well as volcanic peaks such as Licancaur (19,685 ft.), San Pedro y Pablo (19,423 ft.), and Oyahua (19,226 ft.).

## Asian Mountain Systems
### The Himalayas and Tibet
The Himalayan mass occupies an area approximately 2,000 meters in length and between 100 to 500 meters in width. These mountains include the highest peaks in the world, many of which exceed 20,000 feet. Because they rise far above the line of perpetual snow, they act as an almost impassable barrier to animal and vegetable life. They also significantly impact atmospheric conditions, obstructing nearly three-fourths of the air mass and moisture in the region.

In Northern Tibet, the Kuen-lun system includes the Kalta-alaghan (also known as the Columbus or Chimen-tagh Range). This range increases in elevation toward the east and features towering, snow-clad peaks with passes reaching altitudes such as 14,560 feet and the Avraz-davan pass at 15,700 feet. Other associated features include the Muzluk-tagh and the isolated Mount Kreml, which reaches 15,055 feet.

### Southeast Asia and India
Burma is encircled by mountain ranges on three sides. The Arakan Yomas extend northwards to join the Naga and Chin hills, with the peak of Liklang rising nearly 10,000 feet. In the eastern Kachin hills, the peaks of Sabu and Worang reach 11,200 feet. Other notable peaks include Loi Ling in the Northern Shan States (9,000 ft.) and Shwedaunggyi in the Kumon range (5,750 ft.). The Pegu Yomas are smaller in proportion, though they include Popa, an extinct volcano rising nearly 5,000 feet.

In Assam, the region is divided by hill ranges between the Brahmaputra and Surma valleys. These ranges are named after inhabiting tribes: the Naga, North Cachar, Jaintia, Khasi, and Garo hills. The highest points include Japva peak in the Naga hills (nearly 10,000 ft.), Shillong peak in the Khasi-Jaintia hills (6,450 ft.), and Nokrek peak in the Garo hills (4,600 ft.).

### Baluchistan
In Baluchistan, the area around Quetta is overlooked by several grand peaks and precipices ranging from 10,500 to 11,500 feet, including Takatu to the north, Khalifat to the east, Chahiltan to the south-west, and the square-headed Murdar to the south.

## North American and European Ranges
### The Rocky Mountains
The Rocky Mountains consist primarily of palaeozoic sediments. In Canada, the range culminates between latitudes 51° and 53°. The highest peak in British Columbia is Mount Fairweather (15,287 ft.), while the highest in the Canadian Rockies is Mount Robson (13,700 ft.). Other significant peaks include Columbia (12,740 ft.) and Forbes (12,075 ft.). The range is traversed by several passes, such as the South Kootenay (7,100 ft.) and the Peace River Pass (2,000 ft.).

### The Carpathians and Alps
The eastern wooded Carpathians are a sandstone range covered in forests. The culminating peak of this range is Cserna Gora (6,505 ft.), with other principal peaks including Pop Ivan (6,214 ft.) and Bistra (5,936 ft.). The Vihorlat range, of volcanic origin, reaches 3,495 feet.

In Carniola, the region is occupied by the Alps in the north and the Karst Mountains in the south. The Julian Alps feature the Triglav or Terglou (9,394 ft.), which serves as a dividing range between the Alps and the Karst Mountains. Other high peaks include the Mangart (8,784 ft.) and the Jaluz (8,708 ft.). The Karawankas' highest peak is the Stou or Stuhlberg (7,344 ft.), while the Steiner Alps culminate in the Grintovc (8,393 ft.).
