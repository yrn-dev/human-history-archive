# Mount Mlanje and the Former Existence of Unmixed Bushman Tribes

## Mount Mlanje
Mount Mlanje is located in the extreme south-eastern corner of the protectorate and is the highest mountain within those limits, reaching a peak altitude of 9,683 feet. Geologically, it is described as an isolated "chunk" of the Archean plateau that experienced a volcanic outburst of basalt at a later date, resulting in several craters on its sides and summit. The mountain is noted for its picturesque appearance and high rainfall, which can often reach 100 inches per year.

## Historical Presence of Bushman Tribes
In certain mountain districts, specifically Mount Mlanje, there are legends regarding the former existence of unmixed Bushman tribes. This aligns with broader evidence suggesting that the Bushmen once extended considerably further north than the areas they occupy in the memory of white men. Indications of their former presence have been found as far north as the Nyasa and Tanganyika basins. 

Specifically, rounded stones with a hole through the center have been discovered at the south end of Lake Tanganyika. These implements are similar to the "graaf stock" (digging sticks) used by Bushmen in the south to weight their tools. It is believed that the earliest known race of man inhabiting what is now British Central Africa was akin to the Bushman-Hottentot type of negro.

## Displacement and Interaction
The current population of British Central Africa is linguistically exclusive to the Bantu family. It is probable that the Bantu tribes drove the Bushmen south, outflanking them and confining them to less fertile tracts of country. While the current population is generally of a black or chocolate-coloured Negro type, some Bantu races in certain parts exhibit signs of Bushman or Hottentot intermixture.

## Characteristics of the Bushmen
The Bushmen, also known as Bosjesmans, are regarded as the aboriginal element of the population of South Africa. They often call themselves *Saan* (a Hottentot name) or *Khuai*, the latter of which may mean "small man." Other names for them include *Abatwa* (used by Kaffirs) and *Masarwa* or *Maseroa* (used by Bechuana).

### Physical Traits
The most striking physical feature of the Bushman is shortness of stature. Measurements by Sir John Barrow during the first British occupation of Cape Colony recorded the tallest man at 4 ft. 9 in. and the tallest woman at 4 ft. 4 in., though later measurements by Selous in Bechuanaland showed some individuals reaching 5 ft. 4 in. or even 6 ft. 

Physically, they are described as having a dirty yellow color, a long and low skull, prominent cheek-bones, and deeply set eyes. Their build is typically slim and lean, with very little fat under the skin, leaving the skin dry and folded around the joints and stomach. Unlike the characteristic fetor of the negro, this is not found in the Bushman. Women may exhibit considerable steatopygy and a prolongation of the nymphae.

### Social Structure and Culture
The Bushmen are a nomadic race living in widely scattered groups with practically no tribal organization. While individual families may temporarily appoint a chief, these arrangements are not permanent. Marriage is conducted via offer and acceptance, ratified by a feast; in some tribes, a youth must prove himself an expert hunter. 

The social position of women is low; they serve as beasts of burden, carrying children and property, and are responsible for supplying the encampment with water. Mothers feed their children chewed roots and meat within the first few days of birth and teach them to chew tobacco at an early age.

### Beliefs and Customs
The Bushmen do not have a concrete idea of a God but believe in supernatural interference and evil spirits. They utilize amulets and practice forms of totemism, refusing to eat certain foods, such as goat's flesh or caterpillars (N'gwa), depending on the group. 

Their funerary customs involve anointing the corpse's head, smoke-drying it, and laying it in a grave. The family then deserts the site after raising a pile of stones. Cairns of stone are thrown on the graves of chiefs; the Bushman believes that failing to throw a stone on these mounds will result in evil spirits twisting his neck. As a sign of repentance or mourning, the Bushman may cut off the joints of his fingers.

### Subsistence and Technology
The Bushmen possess no domestic animals (except some half-wild dogs) and no knowledge of agriculture. They live by hunting, following antelope migrations. Their primary weapon is a bow made of a stout bough and twisted sinew, firing reed arrows. These arrows are coated with a gummy poison derived from the milky juice of *Euphorbia arborescens* or *Amaryllis toxicaria*, often mixed with snake venom, spider venom (*Mygale*), or the entrails of the N'gwa caterpillar.

For gathering, women use the *graaf stock*, a sharpened wooden spike with a perforated circular stone secured by a wedge, used to uproot tuberous roots and dig pitfalls.
