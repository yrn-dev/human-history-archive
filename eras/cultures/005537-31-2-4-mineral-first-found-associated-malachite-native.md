# Brochantite

Brochantite is a mineral species characterized as a basic copper sulphate with the chemical formula $\text{Cu}_4(\text{OH})_6\text{SO}_4$. It crystallizes within the orthorhombic system.

## Physical Properties
The crystals of brochantite are typically small and exhibit either an acicular or prismatic habit. These crystals possess a vitreous lustre and range in colour from emerald-green to blackish-green. They are translucent to transparent and feature a perfect cleavage parallel to the face lettered *a* in the archive's accompanying figures. The mineral has a specific gravity of 3.907 and a hardness rating of 3½-4.

## Discovery and Nomenclature
The mineral was first discovered in the copper mines of the Urals, where it was found associated with native copper and malachite. In 1824, A. Lévy named the mineral in honour of A.J.M. Brochant de Villiers.

## Varieties and Related Minerals
Several varieties of brochantite have been identified. While some were originally described as distinct species, they were later proven to be essentially identical to brochantite. These include:
* **Königine**: found in the Urals.
* **Brongniartine**: found in Mexico.
* **Krisuvigite**: found in Iceland.
* **Warringtonite**: found in Cornwall.

## Global Occurrences
Brochantite is found in various localities worldwide:
* **United Kingdom**: In Cumberland, specifically at Caldbeck Fells and Roughten Gill, where small brilliant crystals occur in quartzose rock alongside chrysocolla and malachite.
* **Hungary**: In the Bihar Mountains at Rézbánya.
* **Chile**: In the Atacama, where it is found with atacamite, a mineral that closely resembles brochantite in its general appearance.
* **United States**: In the Tintic district of Utah and the Clifton and Morenci district of Arizona. 

Microscopical examinations of secondary green copper ores in the Arizona districts of Morenci and Clifton indicate that brochantite is extremely common. In these areas, it is mostly intergrown with malachite, which effectively masks the presence of the brochantite. It is suggested that malachite in other localities may similarly be intergrown with brochantite upon closer examination.
