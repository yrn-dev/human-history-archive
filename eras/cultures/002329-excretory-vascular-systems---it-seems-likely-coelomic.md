# Excretory and Vascular Systems

The excretory and vascular systems vary significantly across different biological groups, ranging from the specialized structures of the Enteropneusta to the complex renal and circulatory systems of lower vertebrates.

## Systems of the Enteropneusta
In the Enteropneusta, such as *Balanoglossus*, the vascular system is characterized by channels and lacunae that lack endothelium. These are located within the mesenteries, the gut-wall, and the basement-membrane of the body-wall. The blood is a non-corpuscular fluid. It is moved forward by a contractile dorsal vessel and gathered into a central blood-sinus situated over the stomochord. This sinus is surrounded on three sides by the pericardium (*Herzblase*), a closed vesicle with contractile walls. The pulsation of the pericardial vesicle drives blood into the glomerulus, from which it exits via efferent vessels to join the ventral (sub-intestinal) vessel in the trunk.

Regarding excretion, it is likely that coelomic pore-canals originally served as excretory organs. However, in existing Enteropneusta, these have either become vestigial or acquired new functions; for instance, the collar-pores are used to inhale water to keep the collar turgid during progression. Excretion is now primarily handled by the glomerulus, a vascular complex located on either side of the anterior portion of the stomochord and projecting into the proboscis-coelom.

## Renal Systems in Fishes
The kidney system in fishes, known as the *holonephros* or *archinephros*, consists of segmentally arranged tubes. These tubes lead from the coelom into a longitudinal duct that opens at the hinder end of the enteron. The system develops from the head backward in a non-regular sequence:
*   **Pronephros:** A small number of enlarged tubules at the head end that meet excretory needs during larval existence.
*   **Mesonephros:** The tubules of the rest of the series that form the functional kidney of the adult.

The adult fish kidney is typically a compact gland located immediately dorsal to the coelomic cavity, extending in an anteroposterior direction.

## Mechanisms of Excretion
Kidney tubules perform excretory functions through two primary methods. First, the tubule wall extracts nitrogenous products from the blood (supplied by the posterior cardinal vein) and passes them into the tubule lumen. Second, a ciliated open funnel called a nephrostome at the coelomic end allows coelomic fluid to enter and flush the tubule.

Secretory activity is concentrated near the nephrostomes in areas ensheathing a glomerulus—a blood vessel coiled into a skein. In the pronephros, these glomeruli are usually fused into a single glomus. In the mesonephros, they remain separate, and the surrounding coelom may be nipped off to form a Malpighian body. If this separation is incomplete, the Malpighian coelom remains connected to the general coelom via a narrow peritoneal canal, the splanchnocoelic end of which is a ciliated peritoneal funnel.

## Vascular Systems of Lower Vertebrates
The blood vascular system in lower vertebrates comprises three main components:
1.  A ventral vessel located beneath the enteron.
2.  A series of paired, hoop-like aortic arches that connect the ventral and dorsal vessels around the sides of the pharynx.
3.  A single or double dorsal aorta situated between the notochord and the enteron.

Blood flows forward toward the head through the ventral vessel, moves dorsalwards through the aortic arches, and travels tailwards via the dorsal aorta. In other vertebrate examples, such as birds, the heart is positioned in the middle line of the body with its long axis parallel to the trunk. In these cases, the right and left halves are completely divided by septa, preventing the mixture of arterial and venous blood.

## Related Mesodermal and Coelomic Structures
The splanchnocoel, or general coelom, arises from the ventral portion of the trunk mesoderm. In most fishes (except Myxinoids), the anterior part of the splanchnocoel separates into a pericardiac cavity. In adult Selachians, this separation is incomplete, and the two cavities communicate via a pericardio-peritoneal canal.

Additionally, the spleen develops from the mesenchyme in the dorsal mesogastrium. Microscopically, it features a fibro-elastic coat containing involuntary muscle and trabeculae that divide the organ into compartments. These compartments contain highly vascular red spleen pulp, which includes Malpighian corpuscles (small spherical masses of adenoid tissue) located on the terminal branches of splenic blood vessels. The splenic arteries may end in capillaries or open into blood spaces (lacunae) from which veins arise.
