# General Pitt-Rivers (Lane Fox)

General Pitt-Rivers, also referred to as Lane Fox, was a contributor to the fields of archaeology and anthropology, producing works focused on primitive warfare and the study of ancient settlements.

## Archaeological Contributions
Pitt-Rivers conducted extensive research into the history of Roman Britain. His work specifically addressed the study of villages, for which he authored the four-volume set *Excavations in Cranborne Chase, &c.*, published between 1887 and 1908. His findings on this subject were also presented in the *Proc. Soc. of Ant.* xviii.

## Anthropological Studies and Primitive Warfare
In his work *Anthropological and Archaeological Fragments*, specifically within the section titled "Primitive Warfare," Pitt-Rivers examined the utility and nature of the boomerang. The archive notes that the war boomerang is a deadly weapon when used by an expert, and the lighter hunting boomerang is likewise effective. In contrast, the return boomerang is primarily used for killing birds or as a plaything; the sources indicate that this type of boomerang is often as dangerous to the person throwing it as it is to the intended target.

Beyond his fragments, Pitt-Rivers' research on these topics appeared in several other publications, including:
* *Journ. Royal United Service Inst.* xii. No. 51
* *British Ass. Report* (1872)
* *Catalogue of Bethnal Green Collection* (p. 28)
