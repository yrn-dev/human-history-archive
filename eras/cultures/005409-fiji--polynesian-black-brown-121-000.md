# Fiji

## Population and Demographics
Fiji is located within the region of Australasia. The population of the islands is estimated at 121,000 people. The inhabitants are described as being of Polynesian origin, consisting of black and brown racial types.

## Racial Classifications in the Polynesian Region
The native races found within the Polynesian groups of islands and Australia are categorized into two primary types: the dark Polynesian and the light Polynesian. 

The dark type is characterized as black and is described as being of a very low order. In some of the islands, this group is noted to still retain cannibal habits. In contrast, the white Polynesian races are described as a very superior type. An example of this high-order characteristic is found in the Maoris of New Zealand.

Fiji is part of a broader demographic landscape in Australasia that includes other groups such as the black race of Australia, which is described as a low-class race that is generally inoffensive and peaceful in its habits, and the population of Papua (New Guinea). The natives of Papua are described as being in a very low state of civilization and, like Fiji, are composed of Polynesian black and brown types. The population of Papua is estimated at 400,000, though this figure is approximate as no census has been conducted.
