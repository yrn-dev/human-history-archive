# The Barotse

The Barotse are a paramount tribe located in the Upper Zambezi basin of South Central Africa. Originally known as the Aalui, they have occupied the extensive plain through which the Zambezi passes from 14deg 35' S. to 16deg 25' S. since approximately the beginning of the 17th century, a period spanning the reigns of twenty-two successive paramount chiefs.

## Origins and Migration
Before settling in their current territory, the Aalui lived for an indefinite period on the Kabompo river, situated 200 miles to the northeast. A section of the tribe that did not migrate to the Zambezi plain remained there; these people are known as the Balokwakwa (men of the ambuscade) and were formerly called the Aalukolui. Vague tradition and similarities in language and type to tribes in the far north-east suggest an even more remote emigration from that direction. While some writers have assumed the Barotse are an offshoot of the Mashona because natives from Mashonaland can understand those at the Barotse capital, Lialui, the homogeneity of the Barotse compared to the variety of type among the Mashona suggests the opposite conclusion.

## The Makololo Conquest
In the early 19th century, a section of the Basuto tribe called the Makololo migrated from the south of the current Orange River Colony, traveling through the Kalahari and Bechuanaland to subdue the Barotse. Their leader, Sebituane, was a general and administrator who established the rule of his house for roughly forty years. Around 1865, an organized rebellion by the Barotse led to the reinstatement of the original dynasty and the near-total extinction of the Makololo oligarchy. It was the Makololo who provided the name "Barotse," derived from *Rotse* (plain), *Bu-rotse* (country of the plain), *Mu-rotse* (man of the plain), and *Ma-rotse* (people of the plain), the latter of which was inaccurately rendered as *Ba-rotse*.

## Social Structure and Governance
The Barotse proper are small in number but have historically ruled a territory two and a half times the size of Great Britain. Physically, they are characterized as tall, very black, deep-chested, and refined in feature. Their society functioned as an oligarchy with a feudal-style system of grades:

*   **Chiefs:** The highest rank, consisting of three grades.
*   **Freeborn:** Descendants of chiefs who did not acquire chief's rank themselves.
*   **Slaves:** This class included prisoners of war and members of subject tribes. Slavery was graded, allowing slaves to own other slaves, with the highest grade reporting to a Barotse chief.

The paramount chief could occasionally grant chief's rank to non-Barotse individuals for ability or gallantry, granting them the privileges and name of the Barotse. In 1906, the paramount chief abolished slavery by proclamation, though the rights to chieftainship and the predominant position of the Barotse remained.

Political authority is shared between the paramount chief and a queen known as the *Mokwai*, who is the ruling chief's eldest sister rather than his wife. Her privileges end upon the chief's death. While theoretically equal—neither can issue a national decree without the other's consent—each maintains absolute authority in their own province and their own capital town and councillors. They hold joint authority over all other provinces. Their legal code is noted for its advancement, including the right for an accused chief to be tried by his peers.

## Barotseland and Administration
The term "Barotseland" refers to the country over which the paramount chief exercises authority, encompassing the Barotse proper and fifteen subject tribes. The territory extends from the Kwito river in the west to the Kafue river in the east, and from the Congo-Zambezi watershed in the north to the Zambezi and Linyante (or Kwando) river in the south. It is divided into three administrative groups:
1.  **Central provinces:** Administered by the paramount chief from Lialui, the Mokwai from Nalolo, and two chiefs of the blood from Sesheke.
2.  **Outlying provinces:** Administered by Barotse chiefs under the direction of the paramount chief.
3.  **Tributary tribes:** Local chiefs retain their positions provided they pay annual tribute and perform homage at Lialui.

## British Protectorate
The Barotse kingdom became part of the British sphere of influence following a treaty between Great Britain and Portugal on June 11, 1891. The British South Africa Company, granted a charter in October 1889, established rule in the regions north of the Middle Zambezi. The company appointed an administrator, established a native police force under a British officer, and appointed district commissioners and magistrates, though they did not interfere in internal Barotse affairs.

The first Westerners in the region were missionaries. F. S. Arnot visited from 1882 to 1884, and in 1884 the Societe des Missions Evangeliques de Paris established a mission led by Francois Coillard. Although Coillard initially advised delaying the arrival of white men to protect his mission, the paramount chief Lewanika petitioned the "Great White Queen" for a protectorate.

The western limits of the territory were settled by the king of Italy in an arbitration award on May 30, 1905. This award fixed the frontier at the Kwando river as far north as 22deg E., then along that meridian to 13deg S., following that parallel east to 24deg E., and then north to the Belgian Congo frontier. This shifted the political boundaries, placing some sections of Barotseland outside the British sphere of influence. The area under British protection is approximately 182,000 square miles. In 1907, the region became known as North Western Rhodesia.
