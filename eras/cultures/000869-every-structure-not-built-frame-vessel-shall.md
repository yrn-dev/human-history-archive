# Structures Not Built in with the Frame of the Vessel

In the context of maritime regulations and the management of cargo, the distinction between the permanent frame of a vessel and external additions is critical for determining financial liability and the classification of ship components.

## Classification of Deck Structures
According to established maritime rules, any structure that is not built in with the frame of the vessel is considered to be a part of the deck of the vessel. This classification is specifically relevant to the rules governing the jettison of deck cargo. Under Rule I, no jettison of deck cargo is made good as General Average (G.A.).

## General Average and Ship Sacrifice
While deck cargo jettisons are excluded from General Average, other forms of damage and sacrifice are eligible for compensation under specific rules:

*   **Common Safety:** Damage to a ship or its cargo resulting from a sacrifice made for the common safety is made good as G.A. This includes damage caused by water entering through openings made for the purpose of making a jettison for the common safety, or water entering through ship's hatches that were opened for the same purpose.
*   **Fire Suppression:** Damage caused by water or other means during the extinguishing of a shipboard fire is made good as G.A. This extends to damage resulting from scuttling or beaching a burning ship. However, compensation is not provided for separate packages of cargo, bulk cargo, or portions of the ship that were actually on fire.
*   **Wreck Removal:** Loss or damage caused by the cutting away of remains of spars or other items that had previously been carried away by sea-peril is not made good as G.A.
