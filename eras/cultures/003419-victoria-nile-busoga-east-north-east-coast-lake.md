# Victoria Nile, Busoga, and the East African Lake Regions

The geographical area encompassing the Victoria Nile, the district of Busoga, and the eastern and northern coasts of the great lakes is a region of significant linguistic diversity and complex topography. This area is situated within the broader context of British East Africa, a territory under British influence located between Abyssinia and the Anglo-Egyptian Sudan to the north and German East Africa to the south.

## Geography and Topography
The continental area of this region is characterized by the great plateau of East Africa, where elevations rarely fall below 2,000 feet and frequently reach heights of 6,000 to 8,000 feet. The landscape is marked by volcanic action and the eastern "rift-valley," which contains various lakes without outlets. 

To the west, a basin of lower elevation is partially occupied by Victoria Nyanza, which drains northward into the Nile. Further inland, the terrain rises again toward a second volcanic belt, which culminates in the Ruwenzori range. In the north, the Nile is navigable from Nimule up to Lake Albert.

## Linguistic Groups and Dialects
The region is home to the Uganda-Unyoro group of Bantu languages. This group is defined by specific geographical boundaries:
*   **East and North:** Bounded by the Victoria Nile and Busoga.
*   **West:** Bounded by the east coast of Lake Albert, the Ruwenzori range, and the Congo Forest.
*   **South and South-East:** Bounded by the south coast of the Victoria Nyanza and a line extending from near the Emin Pasha Gulf to the east coast of Tanganyika and the Malagarazi river.
*   **South-West:** Bounded approximately by the Rusizi river down to Tanganyika.

Within this group, the dialects of the west coast of Lake Victoria, the Sese Islands, and the district of Busoga (located in the north-east) are closely related to the language of the kingdom of Uganda. The group also includes all inhabited islands and archipelagoes of the Victoria Nyanza as far east as Bukerebe, excluding those near the north-east coast.

Distinct from the Uganda subjection is the Unyoro sub-section. This includes the dialects of the Hima (the Hamitic aristocracy of these lands), such as *Uru-hima* and *Ru-hinda*, as well as *Ru-songora*, *Ru-iro*, *Ru-toro*, and *Ru-tusi*. Kindred dialects are found in Bukerebe, Businja, Ruanda, Busiba, and Karagwe. Additionally, the *Lu-konjo* language is spoken on the southern flanks of the Ruwenzori Range, extending south to Lake Kivu and the eastern limits of the Congo Forest.

## Population and Migration
The peoples of the Great Lakes are among those who speak Bantu languages. While there is a general physical resemblance among Bantu-speaking tribes, there is great diversity in physical characteristics. Some Bantu speakers are Forest Pygmies, while others show relationships to Hottentots or resemble West African negroes. In the north, some are of Nilotic or Gala (Galla) origin.

Historical analysis suggests that approximately 3,000 years ago, a powerful tribe of negroes speaking the Bantu mother-language—physically allied to the southern Lake Chad and south-western Nile basins and impregnated with the Caucasian Hamite—migrated from the heart of Africa. They pushed into the southern half of the continent, assimilating or absorbing previous populations. Before this invasion, the eastern coasts of Victoria Nyanza and the East African coast down to Zanzibar were populated by Hottentot-Bushman and Nilotic-negro peoples.

## Administration and Infrastructure
The East Africa protectorate is divided into several provinces. Two provinces, Naivasha and Kisumu, which adjoin the Victoria Nyanza, were originally part of the eastern province of Uganda before being transferred to the East Africa protectorate on April 1, 1902.

Communications in the region are facilitated by the Uganda Railway, which runs from the Indian Ocean port of Mombasa to the Victoria Nyanza. Mombasa serves as the chief port, with the railway terminating at the harbour of Kilindini. Other significant towns along the railway include Nairobi, the administrative capital chosen in 1907, and Naivasha, located in the rift-valley.

## Exploration
The lake regions of equatorial Africa were explored in the mid-19th century. In February 1858, Richard Francis Burton and John Hanning Speke discovered Lake Tanganyika. Subsequently, Speke discovered Victoria Nyanza. These expeditions provided the incentive for further explorations by Stanley, Baker, Livingstone, and Grant.
