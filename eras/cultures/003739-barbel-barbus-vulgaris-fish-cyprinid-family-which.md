# BARBEL

The barbel (*Barbus vulgaris*) is a fish belonging to the Cyprinid family. It is primarily an inhabitant of the rivers of central Europe, though it is found in England with a very local distribution.

## Physical Characteristics
The barbel is characterized by the presence of four barbels, which are fleshy appendages that hang from the mouth (the term derives from the Latin *barba*, meaning beard). Its short dorsal fin features a first ray that is strong, serrated behind, and spine-like. In terms of size, the barbel can attain a weight of 50 lb on the European continent.

## Classification and Related Species
The barbel serves as the type for its genus, which is very large and contains approximately 300 species distributed across Africa, Asia, and Europe. One notable member of this genus is the mahseer (or mahaseer), described as the great sporting fish of India.

The broader family, *Cyprinidae*, is extremely numerous with about 1,400 known species. These fish are dispersed globally, with the exception of Australasia, Papuasia, Madagascar, and South America. They are almost entirely confined to fresh water and feed on small animals or vegetable substances. The *Cyprinidae* are divided into four subfamilies:
*   **Cyprininae**: The largest group, which more or less conforms to the type of the carp.
*   **Catostominae**: Found mostly in North America, with some species in eastern Siberia and China; these are characterized by pharyngeal teeth that form a single comb-like series and maxillary bones that help border the mouth.
*   **Cobitinae**: Known as loaches, found in Asia, Europe, and Abyssinia.
*   **Homalopterinae**: Mountain forms found in south-eastern Asia and China, which possess a rudimentary air-bladder and are allied to loaches.

A specific member of the *Cyprinidae* discovered in Natal by Max Weber, named *Barbus viviparus*, is a small barbel known to be viviparous.

## Distribution and Regional Variations
The distribution of the genus *Barbus* varies significantly by geographical zone:
*   **Palaearctic Region (Europaeo-Asiatic)**: *Barbus* and *Cobitinae* are numerous.
*   **Nearctic Region (North American)**: *Barbus* and *Cobitinae* are absent.
*   **Southern Asia**: The *Cyprinidae* are largely represented here and can reach sizes unknown in Europe. For example, a *Barbus* taken from the Tigris river has reached a weight of 300 lb.

Generally, the chief development of the *Cyprinidae* family in terms of number of forms and size occurs in mountain regions with temperate climates, while smaller species are found in low-lying rivers and hotter regions.
