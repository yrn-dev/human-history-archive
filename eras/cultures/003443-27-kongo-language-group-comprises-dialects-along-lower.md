# The Kongo Language Group

The Kongo language group is a collection of dialects situated in the region of the Congo river and the territory of the former kingdom of Congo. Its geographic and linguistic boundaries are defined by specific river systems and landmarks.

## Geographic Distribution
The Kongo language group comprises the dialects found along the lower course of the Congo river, extending from the river's mouth to Stanley Pool. Additionally, the group encompasses the territory of the old kingdom of Congo. This area lies south of the Congo river and north of the river Loje, stretching from the coast eastwards to the watershed of the river Kwango and approximately to the longitude of Stanley Pool.

## Linguistic Boundaries and Relations
The boundaries of the Kongo group are marked by transitions into other allied language groups:

*   **The South:** In the southern region, the Kongo dialects melt imperceptibly into the Angola language. This closely-allied group is generally styled as Mbundu and includes languages such as Songo, Ki-sama, Mbamba, and Ki-mbundu. The river Loje serves, to some extent, as a frontier between the Mbundu tongues and the Kongo group to the north.
*   **The East:** The boundary of the Mbundu genus to the east is likely the Kwango river. Beyond this river, the Lunda languages begin.

## Related Dialects and Groups
While the Kongo group is distinct, the archive identifies other dialect groups in the broader region. To the north and west, there are various Bantu and "semi-Bantu" groups. For example, the Kakongo dialects—which include Ka-kongo (Kabinda), Ba-kamba, Ba-vili, Ma-yombe, and Ba-nyanga, Ba-kama—shade into other groups on the south. Further north, the Bangala-Bobangi-Liboko group comprises commercial languages of the Upper Congo, such as Liboko, Ngala, Poto, Ngombe, and Bangi.
