# A'SHA

A'sha, also known as Maimun ibn Qais, was an Arabian poet whose life spanned the era preceding and following the mission of Mahomet. Born in Manfuha, a village located in al-Yemama in the center of Arabia, he lived long enough to accept the prophet's mission.

## Life and Travels
A'sha lived as a wandering singer, traveling extensively throughout the Arabian peninsula. His journeys took him from Hadramut in the south to al-Hira in the north. During his travels, he frequently attended the annual fair at Okaz (Ukaz). At this fair, the poet Nabigha Dhubyani is said to have ranked A'sha as the first among the poets present, placing him above other figures such as Hassan ibn Thabit and the poetess Khansa.

## Beliefs and Influences
Even before the time of Mahomet, A'sha is said to have been a monotheist who believed in the last judgment and the resurrection. These specific beliefs may have resulted from his interactions with the 'Ibadites (Christians) of al-Hira and the bishop of Nejran (Najran).

## Poetic Works and Style
A'sha's poetry is noted for its technical skill and variety of metre. His work is particularly praised for its descriptions of the wild ass, its praise of wine, and his proficiency in both satire and praise. His love poems are dedicated to the praise of Huraira, who was a black female slave. Among his body of work, his best-known poem is a eulogy in praise of Mahomet.

## Archival Records
The poems of A'sha have been collected from various sources in L. Cheikho's *Les Poetes arabes chretiens*, published by the Jesuit press in Beirut in 1890. Additionally, his eulogy of Mahomet was edited by H. Thorbecke in the 1875 work *Al Asa's Lobgedicht auf Muhammad*.
