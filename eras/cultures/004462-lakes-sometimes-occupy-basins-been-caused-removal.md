# Lake Basins and Their Formation

In geology, a basin is defined as a structure proper to the bed rock of a district, equivalent to a broad shallow syncline. This structural basin is composed of rock beds that exhibit a centroclinal dip. It is distinct from a physiographic river basin, although the two may occasionally coincide.

## Geological Formation of Lake Basins
Lake basins can be formed through several distinct geological processes:

*   **Chemical Solution:** Lakes sometimes occupy basins created by the removal in solution of more soluble constituents in the underlying strata, such as rock salt.
*   **Crustal Movements:** In some instances, lake basins have been formed directly by movements of the earth's crust.
*   **Glacial Action:** "Rock-basins" are steep-sided, comparatively small depressions scooped out of solid rock in mountainous regions, primarily through the agency of glaciers.
*   **Tectonic Activity:** In rift-valleys, transverse ridges can divide a generally level floor into a series of basins, some of which contain lakes without outlets.
*   **Volcanic Activity:** The elevation of volcanic peaks can dam back water, creating a lake by blocking a former outlet or extension.
*   **Drainage Issues:** Some shallow lakes fill slight depressions caused by defective drainage or obstructed outlets.

## Examples of Basins and Lakes

### Africa
In British East Africa, the rift-valley contains a sequence of basins. Lake Naivasha, a small fresh-water lake 6,135 ft. above sea level, is closed to the north by the ridge of Mount Buru. Beyond this are the basins of Lakes Elmenteita (5,860 ft.) and Nakuro (5,845 ft.), followed by the basins of Lakes Baringo and Hannington. Further north, the valley drains into Lake Sugota and the larger Lake Rudolf.

In British Central Africa, Lake Bangweulu occupies the north-west part of a central basin in an extensive plateau, situated about 3,700 ft. above the sea. The land slopes gently into this depression from the north, east, and south. In South-West Africa, the Kwamatuo flows into Etosha, a lake that occupies a depression in the inner table-land at an elevation of approximately 3,400 ft.

Lake Kivu is located in the Central African (or Albertine) rift-valley. It is roughly triangular, approximately 4,830 ft. above sea level, and is separated from the Nile basin to the north by a line of volcanic peaks.

### South America
In Bolivia, a great lacustrine basin exists between the Mamore and the Beni rivers, containing lagoons and lakes such as Lake Rogagua and the Roguaguado marshes. In the south-east, lakes like Gaiba, Uberaba, Mandiore, Caceres, and Bahia Negra fill slight depressions. The plateau region features Lake Titicaca, one of the world's most elevated lakes at 12,644 ft. above sea-level.

### Europe
The lakes of Killarney are situated in a basin surrounded by lofty mountain groups. This system includes the lower lake (Lough Leane), the middle lake (Torc lake), and the upper lake, which are connected by a winding channel known as the Long Range.

## Man-Made Basins
Historical records describe the creation of artificial basins for urban utility. In Babylon, a basin was dug to create a lake. During this process, the stream of the river Euphrates was diverted into the excavated area. This allowed the ancient river bed to dry, enabling the construction of a bridge using stones bound with lead and iron, as well as the construction of baked brick edges and descent points for the city's gateways. Once the bridge was complete, the river was conducted back into its original channel, leaving the excavated area as a swamp.
