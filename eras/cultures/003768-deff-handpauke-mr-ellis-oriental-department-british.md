# Deff (Handpauke)

The *deff*, also referred to as a *Handpauke*, is a musical instrument identified in Persian musical traditions. It is categorized among the instruments used by singers during guest meals.

## Classification and Context
The *deff* appears in historical references to Persian musical instruments alongside other instruments such as the *barbiton* (or *barbut*), the *lyre* (or *chang*), the *lute* (or *rubab*), and the *flute* (or *nei*). The identification of the *deff* as a *Handpauke* is noted in German translations of these Persian references.

## Related Musical Instruments
The *deff* exists within a broader nomenclature of Persian and Arab musical instruments where slight deviations in an instrument's construction often resulted in the assignment of a new name. Other instruments associated with this musical tradition include:

*   **Barbut (Barbiton):** An instrument associated with a famous musician who lived during the time of Chosroes II (A.D. 590-628).
*   **Rubab:** A lute-like instrument. As late as the 19th century in Persia, the *rubab* and the *barbut* were considered different instruments.
*   **Rebab:** A pear-shaped instrument. The *barbat* was a variety of *rebab* that served as a bass instrument, differing from the standard *rebab* only in its size and the number of strings it possessed.

Before the introduction of the bow, the archetypes of the lute and the pear-shaped *rebab* shared similar characteristics: both featured gut strings played by the fingers, vaulted backs, and a construction where the neck and body were one.
