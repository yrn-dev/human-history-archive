# Baboons

Baboons are large terrestrial monkeys characterized by long, naked, dog-like muzzles with nostrils pierced in the truncated extremity. They typically possess short or medium-sized tails and short, thick limbs that are better adapted for walking than for climbing. Physical characteristics of baboons include large cheek-pouches and large, often brightly coloured, naked callosities located on the buttocks.

## Distribution and Behavior
Baboons are currently confined to Arabia and Africa, although in past times their range extended into India. They generally frequent barren rocky districts, where they live in large droves. These animals are described as being exceedingly fierce and dangerous to approach. Their diet is opportunistic, consisting of practically everything eatable that they are able to kill or capture.

## Classification and Species
The genus *Papio* (formerly known as *Cynocephalus*) is divided into several groups:

**Subgenus *Choeropithecus***
This group includes several species:
*   **The anubis baboon (*P. anubis*):** Ranging from Egypt through tropical Africa, this species includes the subspecies *neumanni*, *pruinosus*, *heuglini*, and *doguera*.
*   **The Abyssinian baboon (*P. lydekkeri*)**
*   **The chacma (*P. porcarius*):** Found in the Cape.
*   **Other members:** *P. sphinx* and *P. olivaceus*.

**Subgenus *Hamadryas***
This group is typified by the South Arabian *P. arabicus* and the Arabian baboon (*P. hamadryas*), the latter of which is found in Arabia and North Africa.

**Subgenus *Maimon***
This group consists of the mandrill (*P. maimon*) and the drill (*P. leucophaeus*), both of which are found on the west coast.

The typical representative of the genus as a whole is the yellow baboon (*P. cynocephalus*, or *babuin*), which is distinguished by its small size and grooved muzzle. Its range extends from the Zambezi to Abyssinia.

## Historical Significance
The term "baboon" is derived from the French *babuin*, which originates from *Babon*, an Egyptian deity to whom the monkey was sacred. The Arabian baboon (*P. hamadryas*) was dedicated by ancient Egyptians to the god Thoth. Additionally, frescoes indicate that the ancient Egyptians tamed anubis baboons and trained them to pluck sycamore-figs from trees.
