# Occupancy of America and Related Antiquities

## Archaeological Investigations in the Americas

Adolph Francis Alphonse Bandelier (born August 6, 1840, in Bern, Switzerland) was a prominent American archaeologist and ethnologist who emigrated to the United States as a youth. After 1880, Bandelier focused his research on the Indians of Mexico, South America, and the south-western United States. He established himself as a leading authority on the history and prehistoric civilization of the south-western United States, specifically conducting studies in New Mexico, Arizona, and Sonora.

Bandelier's work in the south-west included the *Report on the Ruins of the Pueblo of Pecos* (1881) and a *Final Report of Investigations among the Indians of the South-western United States* (1890-1892). His research also extended to the social and military structures of ancient Mexicans, producing studies on the mode of warfare, the customs of inheritance and land tenure, and the mode of government and social organization of the ancient Mexicans between 1877 and 1879.

In 1892, Bandelier shifted his focus to Peru, Bolivia, and Ecuador. During this period, he worked for the American Museum of Natural History in New York and Henry Villard. His investigations in South America led him to challenge and demonstrate the falsity of several historical myths, particularly those concerning the Inca civilization of Peru. His written works also include *The Gilded Man (El Dorado) and other Pictures of the Spanish Occupancy of America*.

## Colonial Boundaries and Administration

In Central America, the British crown colony of British Honduras (formerly known as Belize or Balize) maintained specific frontiers defined by conventions between Great Britain and Guatemala in 1859 and 1893. The colony is bounded by the Mexican province of Yucatan to the north and northwest, the Bay of Honduras (an inlet of the Caribbean Sea) to the east and northeast, and Guatemala to the south and west.

The frontier established by these conventions begins at the mouth of the river Sarstoon (or Sarstun) in the Bay of Honduras. It follows the river to the rapids of Gracias à Dios, then runs in a straight line to Garbutt's Rapids on the Belize river. From there, the boundary proceeds north to the Mexican frontier, following the river Hondo to its mouth in Chetumal Bay. As of 1905, the population of British Honduras was 40,372, covering an area of 7,562 square miles.
