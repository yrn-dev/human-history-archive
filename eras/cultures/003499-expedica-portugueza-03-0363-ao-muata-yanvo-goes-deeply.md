# Muata Yanvo and the Lunda Empire

## The Lunda Empire and the Muata Yanvo
The Lunda empire was a significant political entity founded by peoples connected to the Ba-Luba, a Bantu negroid race. The empire's territory is currently divided between Angola and the Belgian Congo. The Lunda empire was ruled by a monarch who held the title of Muata Yanvo (also referred to as Jamvo).

## Connection to the Ba-Luba
The Ba-Luba are described as one of the most cultivated and important peoples of Central Africa. They are a Bantu negroid race consisting of several subdivisions, distributed across eight degrees of longitude. Their geographic range extends from the Kasai in the west to Lakes Bangweulu, Mweru, and Tanganyika in the east.

In the eastern regions, where the Ba-Luba are found in their greatest racial purity, they established the states of Uguha, Urua, and Katanga. In the west, the Ba-Luba intermixed to some extent with the Ba-Kete aborigines. During this process, the Ba-Luba partially dispossessed the Ba-Kete, dividing them into northern and southern portions. The Ba-Luba located in the west are known by the name Ba-Shilange.

## Migration and State Formation
The movement of the Ba-Luba toward the west occurred in relatively recent history, specifically during the beginning of the 19th century or the end of the 18th century. Shortly after this westward movement, a chief named Kalamba Mukenge founded a large state.

## Politico-Religious Developments
In 1870, a remarkable politico-religious revolution took place. This event led to the establishment of a secret society known as the *Bena Riamba*, which was associated with a cult of hemp-smoking. Members of this society abandoned their previous fetish worship in favor of a form of communism. The central tenet of this system was the blood-brotherhood of all its members. The practice of hemp-smoking is noted to be less common as one moves toward the east.
