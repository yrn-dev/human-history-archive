# BANSHEE

The banshee (Irish *bean sidhe*; Gaelic *ban sith*, meaning "woman of the fairies") is a supernatural entity found in general Celtic and Irish folklore. She is primarily recognized as a harbinger of death, whose presence is marked by a mournful screaming or "keening" at night. This sound is believed to foretell the death of a member of the household that the spirit has visited.

## Regional Variations and Related Figures
While most closely associated with Ireland, similar ghostly informers appear in the folklore of other Celtic regions. In Brittany, a similar figure exists. In Wales, the banshee is known as the *gwrach y Rhibyn* (the witch of Rhibyn). Additionally, Welsh folklore contains a groaning spirit called the *cyhyraeth*, which is frequently confused with both the banshee and the *gwrach y Rhibyn*. In Scotland, Sir Walter Scott has noted a belief in the banshee within the highlands.

## Cultural Associations in Ireland
In Irish tradition, the banshee is specifically linked to certain families. The records of these families include chronicles of periodic visits from the spirit. Furthermore, the Irish banshee is considered to be a distinction reserved exclusively for families of pure Milesian descent.

## Theoretical Origins and Classifications
Within the study of folklore and animistic creeds, the banshee is categorized among various local spirits. Some theorists suggest that the banshee may be connected to house spirits or ancestral spirits. She is part of a broader mass of "British goblindom," though unlike certain other figures—such as the Gabriel hounds, the Seven Whistlers, or the Wild Huntsman—the banshee cannot be traced back to specific actual phenomena or savage analogues.
