# Rhamnus Frangula

*Rhamnus Frangula* is a plant species allied to the buckthorn (*Rhamnus cathartica*). It is common in England, where it is known by the names black alder or berry-bearing.

## Botanical Characteristics
*Rhamnus Frangula* is distinguished from the buckthorn by several specific physical and biological traits. Unlike the buckthorn, it lacks spiny branchlets and possesses leaves that are non-serrated. Its flowers are bisexual with parts arranged in fives, whereas buckthorn flowers are regular with parts in fours and are borne on separate male and female plants.

## Utility and Properties
The species provides several materials for practical use:
*   **Medicinal:** The fruits of *Rhamnus Frangula* possess purgative properties.
*   **Dyes:** When unripe, the fruits yield a green dye.
*   **Industrial:** The wood of the plant is soft and porous; referred to as black dogwood, it is utilized for the production of gunpowder.

## Related Species and Genus
*Rhamnus Frangula* belongs to the natural order Rhamnaceae. Other species within the *Rhamnus* genus are also utilized for dyes obtained from their bark and fruits, including *R. tinctoria*, *R. davurica* (the latter two of which produce the commercial China green), and *R. infectoria*. Additionally, *R. Alaternus* is a Mediterranean species grown in shrubberies.

Other related plants include:
*   **American Buckthorns:** These include *Rhamnus Caroliniana* (alder-buckthorn) and *Rhamnus purshiana* (also known as *Cascara sagrada*), the latter of which produces cascara bark from the Pacific coast.
*   **Other Related Plants:** *Bumelia lycioides* (or *lanuginosa*) is commonly referred to as "southern buckthorn."

Furthermore, the dried unripe fruits of various *Rhamnus* species growing in the Levant are known as Persian Berries. These contain the coloring matter xanthorhamnin, which yields the dyestuff rhamnetin through acids and fermentation. When used with a tin mordant, these berries produce a particularly brilliant orange color.
