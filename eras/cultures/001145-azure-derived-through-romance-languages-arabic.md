# AZURE

## Etymology and Meaning
The term "azure" refers to the color blue. It is derived from the Arabic word *al-lazward*, which refers to the precious stone lapis lazuli. In the transition from Arabic to the Romance languages, the initial letter *l* was dropped, resulting in the modern term. Consequently, "azure" can refer both to the lapis lazuli stone itself and to its characteristic blue color.

## Linguistic Context
The Arabic language, from which "azure" originates, is classified by philologists as part of the Semitic group of languages. This group is distinct from the Aryan languages, possessing different root words, different ways of expressing relationship, and different fundamental grammatical ideas. The Semitic group includes Arabic, Hebrew, Abyssinian, ancient Phoenician, and ancient Assyrian. 

The Semitic-speaking peoples are believed to have lived separately from Aryan-speaking peoples for thousands of years during early Neolithic times, likely residing in north-east Africa or south Arabia. While both groups are classed as Caucasians, the original Semites are characterized as being of the Mediterranean type, whereas the original Aryan speakers are seen as being of the Nordic race.

## Geographic and Cultural Influence
Arabic is one of the languages used in a broad tract of Asia. West of the Indus, dialects approach Persian, which then meets Arabic and Turki west of the Tigris and along the Caspian and the Turkoman desert. In these regions, a common set of letters is used for Persian, Arabic, and Turkish, written from right to left.

The influence of Arabic culture and language extended significantly through trade and interaction:
* **India:** In northern India, the mixed language of the Mahommedan conquerors, Urdu or Hindostani, utilizes the Persian character for writing. In the west, beyond the river Sutlej, the population is described as wholly Mahommedan, with the exception of some areas in Syria and Asia Minor.
* **Southeast Asia:** The Malays, who occupy the peninsula and the Archipelago, adopted Mahommedanism and experienced a process of partial civilization through intercourse with the Arabs.
* **Intellectual Exchange:** During its best period, the Arab mind was in effective contact with Indian ideas and Sanskrit literature. Similarly, the Arab and Jewish minds reacted upon one another for mutual benefit; many Jewish writers, including Maimonides, produced great religious literature in Arabic.

## Literary and Linguistic Characteristics
In the context of literary history, Arabic is noted for a specific divide between its written and spoken forms. Some Oriental nations, including the Arabs, are described as having a literary language—specifically "dead Old-Arabic"—that is not suited to modern requirements, creating a situation where the written language is divorced from the spoken vernacular.
