# ARVERNI

The Arverni were an ancient Gaulish tribe situated in the region of Auvergne, located on the Upper Loire. In the era of Julius Caesar, they existed as a powerful confederation. The region of Auvergne, which still bears the name of the tribe, is characterized by fertile valleys such as the Limagne, vast pasture-lands, numerous medicinal springs, and mountains of volcanic origin, including the Mont Dore, Plomb du Cantal, and Puy de Dome.

## Conflict with Rome
The Arverni are noted for resisting Julius Caesar longer than most other Gaulish tribes. Early in 52 B.C., following the massacre of Roman traders at Cenabum (Orléans), the Arverni revolted under the leadership of Vercingetorix, who is recognized as the most famous of the Gallic chieftains. Vercingetorix was joined in this revolt by other tribes, most notably the Bituriges, whose capital was Avaricum.

Recognizing that Caesar could not be defeated in open battle, Vercingetorix opted to concentrate his forces in a few strong positions. Caesar first besieged and captured Avaricum, resulting in a massacre of its occupants. He then invested Gergovia, the capital of the Arverni located near the Puy-de-Dome. However, Caesar suffered a severe repulse at Gergovia and was forced to abandon the siege.

The conflict eventually shifted westward. Caesar defeated Vercingetorix near Dijon and trapped him within Alesia (Mont-Auxois) using lines of circumvallation. Despite a desperate struggle and an attempt at relief by Vercassivellaunus, Vercingetorix surrendered. The resistance concluded in 51 B.C. with the capture of Uxellodunum (Puy d'Issolu), where the defenders had their hands cut off. Following these victories, Caesar established Gaul as a province and set a tribute of 40,000,000 sesterces, though he dealt liberally with the conquered tribes by leaving their cantons intact.

## Culture and Religion
Once the Arverni were vanquished, they readily adopted Roman civilization. The tribe possessed a tribal deity, the god of the mountain, specifically the Puy de Dome. In Roman terminology, this deity was rechristened as Mercurius Dumias. This god was famous far beyond the borders of the Arverni territory, and portions of his temple have been recently excavated.

## Agriculture and Roman Influence
Under the Roman Empire, the territory of the Arverni became part of *Prima Aquitania*. The region was known for its agriculture; Pliny the Elder, writing in A.D. 77, noted that a specific kind of grape (*vitis picata*) and wine (*vinum picatum*), which grew naturally in the district of Vienne, had been transplanted into the country of the Arverni, as well as into the regions of the Helvii, Burgundy, and Franche Compte.

## Legacy and Later History
The population of the Auvergne region has retained strongly-marked Celtic characteristics into the present day. Following the Roman period, the district shared in the fortunes of Aquitaine during the Carolingian and Merovingian periods. By the end of the 8th century, Auvergne served as the seat of a separate countship, with William the Pious becoming the first hereditary count in 886.

The region later underwent various political divisions and transitions. Through the marriage of Eleanor of Aquitaine to Henry Plantagenet, the countship fell under the suzerainty of the English kings. Internal family disputes between William VII ("the Young") and his uncle William VIII ("the Old") led to the division of the domain. This conflict prompted the intervention of King Philip Augustus of France, who annexed a large portion of the country as the *Terre d'Auvergne*. This area eventually became a duchy in the peerage of France in 1360 under King John II.
