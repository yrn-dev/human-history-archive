# The Tamarao

## Overview and Classification
The tamarao, also known as the Philippine buffalo, is scientifically classified as *Bos (Bubalus) mindorensis*. It is a smaller animal that is considered intermediate in many respects between the dwarf anoa (the Celebes buffalo, *B. depressicornis*) and the Indian buffalo.

## Comparison with Other Buffalo Species
The tamarao exists within a broader group of bovine animals often referred to as buffaloes. These animals are generally characterized as heavily built oxen featuring broad muzzles, large ears, long tails with tufts, and massive angulated horns. They typically possess 13 pairs of ribs, a trait they share with typical oxen.

The tamarao is distinct from other species such as the Cape buffalo (*Bos (Bubalus) caffer*) of Africa. While the Cape buffalo is known for the helmet-like mass of horns found on old bulls, the tamarao is smaller in stature. It also differs from the typical Indian buffalo (*Bos (Bubalus) bubalis*), which is restricted primarily to Ceylon and India. Asiatic buffaloes, including the Indian variety, are distinguished from African forms by the direction of the hair on the fore-part of the back, which points forwards.

## Terminology
In popular parlance, particularly in America, the term "buffalo" is almost universally used to designate the American bison. However, in a strict biological sense, the term pertains to aberrant species of cattle, such as the tamarao and its relatives, which have been kept in states of domestication in Egypt and India from time immemorial.
