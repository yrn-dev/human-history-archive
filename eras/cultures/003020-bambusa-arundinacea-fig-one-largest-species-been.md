# Bambusa arundinacea

## Overview
*Bambusa arundinacea* is a species of Indian bamboo. It is categorized as one of the largest species within the tribe of grasses known as *Bambuseae*. Members of this tribe are characterized as large, often tree-like plants featuring woody stems.

## Physical Characteristics
The stems of *Bambusa arundinacea* and related bamboo species spring from an underground root-stock and frequently grow in crowded, dense clumps. The largest species in the tribe can reach heights of 120 feet. The stems are slender and hollow, featuring well-marked nodes or joints where the internal cavity is closed by a strong diaphragm.

The plant possesses numerous branches, which may be spiny in certain species. Its leaves consist of a narrow, often short, leaf-blade that is usually jointed at the base and attached to a long sheath by a short stalk.

## Flowering and Life Cycle
The flowering process of *Bambusa arundinacea* is notable for its rarity and scale. While some bamboo species bloom annually, others do so only at intervals of many years, often with individuals of the same species blooming simultaneously over large areas. On the west coast of India, *Bambusa arundinacea* has been observed to bloom simultaneously at intervals of thirty-two years.

The flowers of the *Bambuseae* tribe typically differ from most other grasses by having six stamens and usually three lodicules. The spikelets are variously arranged in panicles or racemes and are usually many-flowered. Following the ripening of the seed, the leafless flowering culms always die down.

## Related Genera and Fruit
The fruit of the *Bambusa* genus, as well as *Arundinaria*, resembles the grain characteristic of grasses. This differs from other genera such as *Dendrocalamus*, where the fruit is a nut, or *Melocanna*, where the fruit is fleshy and resembles an apple in appearance and size.

## General Uses of Bamboo
In Oriental countries, the various parts and products of bamboo are used in nearly endless ways. The soft, succulent shoots are harvested as they begin to spring and are served at the table similarly to asparagus. To extend their fitness for consumption, these shoots are earthed over; they are available throughout the year, though most abundant in autumn. Additionally, they are prepared as pickles by salting and eating them with rice, or preserved in sugar as candied treats.

As the plant ages, a fluid is secreted within the hollow joints. This leads to the development of a concrete substance known as *tabascheer* or *tabaxir*. This substance is a purely siliceous concretion with peculiar optical properties and was once highly valued in the East for medicinal qualities. However, as a medicinal agent, bamboo is considered entirely inert and has not been included in the European materia medica.
