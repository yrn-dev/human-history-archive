# Java and Regional Wind Patterns

## Wind Systems of the Malay Archipelago
The Malay Archipelago is situated on the equator, a position that modifies the directions of prevailing winds. In this region, the force of the monsoons is significantly interrupted. Specifically, the southerly summer winds that characterize the Asiatic seas between the tropic and the equator do not reach the coasts of Java. Instead, the south-easterly trade winds are developed there in the usual manner.

## Monsoons of the Asiatic Seas
The progression of periodical winds generally follows the seasons, though local influences often disguise these general laws. In the Arabian Sea, the south-west monsoon begins with north-westerly and west winds. As the year progresses, these shift to the south-west, before falling back to north-west and north during the autumn.

In the Bay of Bengal, the south-west monsoon is characterized more by winds from the south and south-east. After October, these are succeeded by north-east winds, which eventually give way to north-westerly and northerly winds as the year advances. The China Sea is fully exposed to both monsoons, with normal directions that nearly coincide with the center of the channel between the eastern islands and the continent of Asia.

## Continental Influence on Wind Flow
The south-west monsoon typically does not extend over land in its character as a south-west wind. As the current of air flows from the sea, it is dissipated, loses much of its original force, and is diverted toward areas of least pressure. 

Winds passing northward over India are transformed into easterly and south-easterly winds over the north-eastern part of the Gangetic plain, and blow as south winds up the Indus. By the time these winds reach the northern extremity of the great Indian plain, their northward velocity is almost entirely exhausted. Consequently, they hardly penetrate the Indus basin or the ranges of the Himalaya and are not felt on the table-lands of Afghanistan. The mountains of the Himalaya and those branching into the Malay peninsula prevent these winds from continuing in their original direction.

## Cyclones and Storms of the Hotter Seas
The hotter seas of Asia experience revolving storms or cyclones during the hot months. In the China Sea, these are known as typhoons. 

*   **Bay of Bengal Cyclones:** These appear to originate over the Nicobar and Andaman islands. They commonly propagate in a north-westward direction, striking the east coast of the Indian peninsula before often advancing over land with an easterly tendency and passing violently across the Ganges delta. These occur from June to October, and more rarely in November, appearing to be caused by south-west monsoon currents meeting adverse currents from the north.
*   **China Sea Cyclones:** These occur in the same months and latitudes as those in the Indian Sea but advance from north-east to south-west (and occasionally east to west). They originate near the island of Formosa and extend to approximately the 10th degree of N. lat.

In both regions, storms tend to advance toward the area of greatest heat, and the wind invariably circulates from south to east, through south, and from north by west.
