# Celebes

Celebes is an island located in the Dutch East Indies. It is separated from Borneo by the Macassar Strait, which serves as a physical line of separation known as Wallace's line. This boundary, indicated by the naturalist A.R. Wallace, marks a transition in flora and fauna; to the west of the line, types are essentially Asiatic, while to the south and east, the Australian element becomes distinctly marked and eventually predominant.

## Geography and Natural Characteristics
Celebes resembles Borneo in its natural characteristics and general condition, although it is less known than the latter. The island's topography includes volcanic peaks, with the highest known elevations reaching 8000 ft. 

## Flora and Fauna
The island is situated within the Australian zoological region, a classification that includes Papua and other islands east of Java. This regional placement is evidenced by the presence of mammals of the Marsupial order, which are absent from adjacent islands like Borneo and Java. Conversely, Celebes lacks various mammals common to the western Archipelago, such as Ruminants, Insectivora, Carnivora, and Quadrumana.

Notable wildlife unique to Celebes includes the *Anoa*, a singular ruminant, and a peculiar form of baboon called *Cynopithecus*. These animals appear to have no relation to Asiatic animals, showing instead an alliance with those found in Africa. Some naturalists include the black ape of Celebes (*Cynopithecus niger*) within the genus *Macacus*. Additionally, the island is home to species of birds akin to those of Africa and a Megapode, the latter indicating the influence of the Australian realm.

In terms of ichthyology, Celebes is noted for the absence of Cyprinid fishes, which are found in Borneo and Bali but are not known from Celebes, Lombok, or islands further east. Other fauna present on the island include species of the Asiatic palm-civet genus *Paradoxurus*.

Regarding botany, the durian tree (*Durio zibethinus*) is cultivated on Celebes. This tree can reach heights of 70 to 80 ft. and produces a spherical, thorny fruit with a cream-colored, glutinous pulp.

## Macassar
Macassar (also spelled Makassar or Mangkasar) is the chief town of the Dutch government of Celebes and the capital of a district of the same name. It is located on the west coast of the island's southern peninsula, near the southern end of the Macassar Strait.

The town is divided into two primary areas: the Malay town, which is situated inland, and the Dutch town and port, known as Vlaardingen. As of the archive's records, the population of Macassar was 17,925, consisting of 2,618 Chinese, 940 Europeans, and 168 Arabs.

Macassar serves as a significant commercial hub with an annual trade volume of approximately £1,250,000. The primary commodities traded include:
* Valuable timber
* Spices
* Gums
* Copra
* Trepang
* Coffee
