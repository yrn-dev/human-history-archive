# Ndu-

## Overview and Origin
In the study of Bantu speech, *Ndu-* is identified as an older form from which the 11th prefix, *Lu-*, is believed to have descended. This prefix system is part of a broader structure of linguistic prefixes and concords found across various Bantu languages.

## Variations and Forms
The 11th prefix manifests in several different forms depending on the dialect and the age of the speech:
* **Common Form:** The most frequent type of this prefix in present use is *Lu-*.
* **Degenerate Form:** In some instances, the prefix loses the *L* and becomes *U-*.
* **Archaic Form:** In dialects that are more archaic, the prefix is usually pronounced as *Du-* or *Ru-*.

Additionally, the archive records the form *Ndu-du-* in relation to Class 11.

## Context within Bantu Prefixes
The 11th prefix exists alongside a complex system of other prefixes and their corresponding concords. For example, the 2nd prefix (*Ba-* or *Aba-*) is found as *Baba-* in the most archaic Bantu speech of Mt. Elgon, while the 5th prefix is commonly *Li-* today, though *Di-* is considered the older and more correct form. Similarly, the 7th prefix (*Ki-*) and 8th prefix (originally *Ibi-* or *Mbibi-*) exhibit significant variability across non-related dialects.

The 10th prefix, originally *Ti-*, *Tin-*, *Zi-*, or *Zin-*, demonstrates how concords can persist even after a prefix has fallen out of use as a definite article. This is exemplified in the Swahili of Zanzibar, where the word *Nombe* (cows) no longer uses the plural prefix *zinombe*, yet the *Zi-* particle reappears in the concord as seen in the phrase *Nombe hizi zangu* (These cows are mine).
