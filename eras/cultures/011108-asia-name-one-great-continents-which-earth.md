# ASIA

Asia is one of the great continents into which the earth's surface is divided. It embraces the north-eastern portion of the land mass generally known as the Old World, with Africa forming the south-western region and Europe forming the north-western region.

## Geography and Boundaries
The continent is bounded to the east and south-east by several important groups of islands. The Philippines, Formosa, Luchu, the Japanese group, and the Kurile islands are regarded as unquestionable outliers of Asia. To the south-east, certain islands link Asia to the Pacific islands and Australia. 

A physical line of separation, known as Wallace's line, exists along the Straits of Macassar (approximately 120 deg. E.) between Borneo and Celebes. This boundary, indicated by the naturalist A.R. Wallace, marks a transition where flora and fauna to the west are essentially Asiatic, while those to the south and east show a predominant Australian element. While old geographers established no distinctly marked boundary between the Asiatic continent and the Malay archipelago (from Sumatra to New Guinea), modern science utilizes this line of separation.

In the extreme west lies Asia Minor, a peninsula forming part of the empire of Turkey. It is bounded by the Mediterranean to the south, the Aegean to the west, and the Black Sea to the north. It is separated from Europe only by the Dardanelles and the Bosporus. Its eastern limit is roughly defined by a line from the Giaour Dagh, along the eastern Taurus to the Euphrates near Malatia, and north to the Black Sea via the Churuk Su. The term "Asia Minor" was not used by classical geographers, appearing first in the 5th century A.D. in the work of Orosius. Natives use the term *Anadol* (Anatolia) for the western part of the peninsula.

## Fauna and Climate
The zoological provinces of Asia correspond closely with its botanical provinces. Dr. Sclater's primary divisions categorize the continent as follows:
* **Palaearctic region:** The northern portion of Asia, extending as far south as the Himalaya, which is not zoologically distinct from Europe.
* **Indian region:** The south-eastern portion of Asia, including the islands of Borneo, Java, Sumatra, and the Philippines.
* **Ethiopian region:** The extreme south-west, comprising southern Persia, Palestine, and Arabia, reaching to Sind and Baluchistan.

The continent's climate is characterized by alternating heating and cooling of the air. During summer months, great heat expands the air upwards, causing a diminution of barometric pressure and an outflow of upper strata. In winter, the cold causes an accumulation of air and an increase in barometric pressure.

## Peoples and Cultures
The northern half of Asia is inhabited largely by Mongolians, who are primarily shepherds, herdsmen, and nomadic hunters. Those in Siberia are described as the most peaceful and least advanced. Between the 40th and 50th circles of N. lat., the region is occupied by tribes known as Tatars (and to ancients as Scythians), which include the Mongols proper, the Moguls, the Manchus, and the Turks. 

The Turks are Mahommedans, with tribes extending to the Caspian, the borders of Persia and Afghanistan, and into Russia (where they are known as Kirghiz). Their language is spoken across much of western Asia, and they use Persian letters. The Manchus and Mongols are chiefly Buddhist and use letters derived from ancient Syriac; the Manchus are noted to be losing their nomadic habits and language under the influence of Chinese civilization. In contrast, the Chinese branch of the Mongolian family are settled traders and agriculturists who are partially Buddhist and possess a unique uninflected, monosyllabic language using symbols rather than letters.

## Historical Influences
European influence on Asia has been particularly strong during two periods: following the conquests of Alexander the Great and from the 16th century onwards. Alexander's conquests established Perso-Greek kingdoms that hellenized their regions and influenced the religion and art of China and India. Later, the Crusades maintained communication with the Levant and established the power of the Roman Church, though this occurred to the detriment of oriental Christianity.

While western Europe has asserted power in eastern and middle Asia through improved communication, Asiatic influences have historically prevailed in the region of western Asia and eastern Europe, where Islam is paramount.

## Global Context
In historical art, such as a 200-year-old oak pulpit carved by van der Voort, Asia was one of the four continents depicted as supporting the platform of the gospel. By 1914, the world was recognized as having five continents. During the conflict of that era, Asia was represented in the trenches by Hindus and Ghurkas from India.
