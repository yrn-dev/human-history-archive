# Events and Developments of 1908

The year 1908 was marked by significant advancements in linguistic research, infrastructure development in South America, financial stabilization in the United States, and discoveries in astronomy.

## Linguistic and Archaeological Research
In the field of Indo-European languages, the history regarding the separation of the Aryan group remains unclear. However, discoveries by German excavators at Boghaz Keui—identified as Pteria in Cappadocia—have provided new evidence. Treaties from the beginning of the 14th century B.C. between the king of the Hittites and the king of Mitanni appear to contain the names of Indra, Varuna, and Mitra, deities belonging to early Aryan mythology.

Additionally, research into Tocharish, a previously unknown Indo-European language, began to emerge. Preliminary accounts provided up to 1909 suggest that Tocharish may be more closely related to Western groups than to Aryan, though further investigation is required. Scholars have also looked toward the collections of the German expedition to Turfan in Turkestan for further insight. In other linguistic studies, G. A. Grierson published work on suffixes in modern Indo-Aryan vernaculars in 1903, which continues to be referenced in comparative grammar.

## Infrastructure in Buenos Aires
The port of Buenos Aires underwent extensive modernization to improve shipping facilities. While improvements began in 1872 at the "Boca" on the Riachuelo—costing nearly £1,500,000 for dredging and landing facilities—these were eventually deemed insufficient. In 1887, plans by Sir John Hawkshaw were implemented to create two basins and four docks in front of the city, occupying 3 meters of reclaimed shore-line.

By 1908, the total cost of these new port works reached approximately £8,000,000 sterling ($40,000,000 gold). The facilities included 24 warehouses, hydraulic cranes, two dry docks in the north basin, and 28 meters of railway connections. Due to the high volume of silt deposited by the river, the channels require constant dredging; consequently, an expenditure of £560,000 was voted in 1908 for this and related purposes. Furthermore, the congress decided in September 1908 to borrow an additional £5,000,000 for further required extensions.

## Financial Stability in New York
The beginning of 1908 saw the gradual resolution of a financial panic that began in New York on October 28, 1907. The crisis was triggered by over-speculation by national banks and the suspension of the Knickerbocker Trust Company, which held deposits of $48,000,000. This led to bank runs and a deficit in New York bank reserves of $38,838,825 during the week of November 2.

To combat the panic, arrangements were made to import nearly $100,000,000 in foreign gold. Between the treasury's transfer of $72,000,000 to banks and an autumn increase in national bank circulation of $77,000,000, the total currency added or disappeared during the panic exceeded $275,000,000. While the bank-note circulation rose to an abnormal $687,340,835 by December 28, 1907, this amount was reduced during the spring of 1908.

## Astronomical Discoveries
The study of the solar system continued to expand through the identification of new satellites. In 1908, a third remote and faint member of the Jovian system was photographed at Greenwich. This followed previous discoveries, such as the 1904-1905 photographs of two other Jovian satellites by Professor C.D. Perrine using the Crossley reflector. These findings contributed to a total of 26 discovered satellites in the solar system.
