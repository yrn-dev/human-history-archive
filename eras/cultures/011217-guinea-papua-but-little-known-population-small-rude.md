# Papua and New Guinea

Papua, also referred to as New Guinea, is a region in the Pacific Ocean that extends almost to the same meridian as the eastern coast of Australia. It is separated from the northern point of Australia by the Torres Straits. The region is characterized by a lack of detailed knowledge regarding its interior.

## Geography and Natural Features
The surface of New Guinea is generally clothed in thick wood. Its mountains are said to reach heights of 20,000 ft. and appear to be permanently covered with snow. 

The region is part of the Papuan Subregion, which also includes the Moluccas, Celebes, and the Timor group of islands. Geologically, strata from the Eocene period occur in New Guinea. In terms of fauna, the region is home to the bandicoot (genus *Perameles*), a small ground-living marsupial that builds nests of sticks, grass, and dried leaves in hollow places and forms burrows.

The area is particularly noted for its avifauna, which is characterized by brilliance of plumage or metallic coloring. The birds of paradise are almost peculiar to this subregion, featuring extraordinary and highly specialized forms. Other characteristic birds include the great crowned pigeons (*Goura*), various Megapodes, racquet-tailed kingfishers (*Tanysiptera*), and a specialized development of cassowaries, parrots, pigeons, honey-suckers, and flycatchers. However, the region lacks certain birds found in Australia, such as emeus, diurnal birds of prey, weaver birds (*Ploceidae*), and babblers (*Timeliidae*).

## The Louisiade Archipelago
Extending south-eastward from the easternmost promontory of New Guinea is the Louisiade Archipelago, a chain of over eighty islands interspersed with reefs. These islands are included in the Australian territory of Papua (British New Guinea). The archipelago is rich in tropical forest products. Gold has been discovered on Misima (St Aignan) and on the chief island, Tagula (South-east), which covers an area of 380 sq. m. The islands were likely observed by Torres in 1606 and were named in 1768 by L. A. de Bougainville after Louis XV.

## Population and Culture
The population of Papua is described as being in a very low state of civilization. While no census has been taken, the population of Papua is estimated at approximately 400,000. 

The inhabitants are categorized as being of the Negrito or Papuan type, characterized by black or brownish skin and curly, crisp, or bushy hair. Within the region, there are varying levels of social development:
* **West Coast:** Inhabitants have had more communication with traders from other islands and are considered fairly civilized.
* **Eastward:** Many tribes are described as barbarous savages.
* **Louisiade Archipelago:** The natives of this chain of islands practice cannibalism.

More broadly, the native races of the Polynesian groups of islands and Australia are divided into dark and light Polynesian types. The dark type is black and of a very low order; in some islands, this group retains cannibal habits.

## Language
The Papuan speech of New Guinea is regarded as one of a few remote and isolated patches of language.
