# Spectre-Hounds of the British Isles

The folklore of the north of England and surrounding regions includes various accounts of a monstrous spectre-hound. These apparitions are characterized by frightening physical traits, such as huge claws and teeth, and are known by different names depending on the specific geographic locale.

## Regional Variations and Names

In the north of England, particularly within Yorkshire, this creature is referred to as the Barghest, Barguest, or Bargest. The Barghest is described as a monstrous goblin-dog. Similar characteristics are attributed to other regional entities, including the Padfoot of Wakefield, the Black Dog of Winchester, and the Demon of Tedworth.

In other parts of the British Isles, the spectre-hound appears under different titles:
* **Wales:** The counterpart to the Barghest is Gwyllgi, known as "the Dog of Darkness." This apparition is described as a mastiff with blazing red eyes and baleful breath.
* **Lancashire:** The creature is called the Striker or Trash.
* **Cambridgeshire and the Norfolk Coast:** In these areas, the entity is known as Shock or Shuck.
* **Isle of Man:** On the island, the spectre-hound is styled as Mauthe Doog.

## Literary References

The folklore surrounding the spectre-hound of the Isle of Man was noted by Sir Walter Scott in his work "The Lay of the Last Minstrel." In the text, Scott describes a figure who was "speechless, ghastly, wan," comparing him to the subject of the story regarding the spectre-hound in Man.
