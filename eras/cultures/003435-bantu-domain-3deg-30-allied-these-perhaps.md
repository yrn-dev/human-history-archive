# Bantu Languages

The Bantu languages constitute a striking and homogeneous family of speech characterized by a pronominal-prefix structure. This homogeneity is noted as being particularly distinct when compared to the linguistic confusion found in Africa north of the Bantu borderland. While the exact birthplace and origin of the Bantu family remain a puzzle, the creation of the Bantu language type is considered an event more ancient than the exodus from the Bantu mother-land.

## General Classification and Domain
The domain of Bantu speech is broadly divided into four primary sections:
*   **The Great Lakes and East Coast:** Extending down to and including the Zambezi basin.
*   **South-Central Group:** Including Bechuana-Zulu.
*   **South-West Group:** Ranging from the southern part of the Belgian Congo to the Angola-Congo coast and Damaraland.
*   **Western Group:** Comprising the languages of Cameroon and the Central and Northern Congo, and likely including group No. 2 of the Semliki river and Albert Nyanza.

The north-eastern extremity of the Bantu domain is where languages are generally closest to the hypothetical parent speech. However, certain areas are explicitly excluded from the Bantu field. For instance, the Congo's northern and north-eastern bend between Stanley Falls and the Rubi river lies outside this field; the Wamanga and Bondonga languages, as well as the Mundu of the Egyptian Sudan (extending south to 3deg 30' N), are not Bantu.

## Western and Central Congo Groups
The Western group contains several distinct subdivisions:
*   **Bangala-Bobangi-Liboko:** This group includes commercial languages such as Ngala, Bangi, Liboko, Poto, Ngombe, and Yanzi. They are spoken along the main stream of the Congo from the Sanga junction in the west to the Lomami and Rubi rivers in the east, and north up the Ubangi river to the Bantu domain limit at approximately 3deg 30' N.
*   **Bateke (Batio):** This group generally includes Bantu dialects east of the Atlantic coast-lands, south of the Ngoko and Upper Ogowe rivers, northwest of the Lower Congo, and west of the Sanga river.
*   **Central Congo Assemblages:** The Bakuba assemblage (including Songe, Babuma, and Shilange) occupies the area between the Upper Kwilu and Kwa-Kasai in the west and the Lomami river in the east. The Rua-Luba-Lunda-Marungu group (including Lulua, Kanyoka, and Ki-tabwa) is located in the south central Congo basin, bounded by the Zambezi watershed to the south and the south-west coast of Tanganyika to the east.
*   **Balolo Group:** These languages are found in the Northern Congo bend, bounded by the main stream of the Congo.

## Cameroon and Gabun Dialects
The Bantu presence in the west varies in "purity" and form:
*   **Duala Group:** Described as a purer Bantu type, spoken on the delta and estuary of the Cameroon river.
*   **Fan or Pangwe:** These forms of speech are so corrupt they are only just recognizable as Bantu, occupying the interior of French Gabun and German Cameroon.
*   **Barondo-Bakundu:** This group begins at the Rio del Rey on the northern extremity of the Bantu field.
*   **Other Groups:** These include the Isubu-Bakwiri of the Cameroon mountains and coast-lands, the dialects of Fernando Po (such as Bani and Ediya), and the Mpongwe-Orungu and Aduma languages of French Gabun.

## Eastern and Southern Groups
The eastern domain is characterized by extensive cognate groups:
*   **Nyanja:** Perhaps the most extensive group, associated with the shores of Lake Nyasa, the Shire valley, and the Zambezi basin down to its delta. Principal dialects include Cinyanja, Ci-peta, Ci-maravi, and Ci-podzo.
*   **Tanganyika and Manyema:** The Tanganyika languages (such as Ki-rega and Kabwari) are spoken around Lake Tanganyika. The Manyema (Baenya) group includes corrupt dialects between the Luapula-Congo and the western watershed of Tanganyika, including languages of the Aruwimi basin like Soko and Lokele.
*   **South-West:** Oci-herero of Damaraland is noted for containing many original Bantu roots despite some strange divergencies.

## Linguistic Features and Archaic Types
The most archaic dialects—those preserving typical structures and original roots—are found in several specific regions: Mount Elgon, Ruwenzori, Uganda, Unyoro, the north coast of Tanganyika, the Bemba country, and the vicinity of Lake Bangweulu. Other archaic types include the Subiya speech of the western Zambezi, Kimakonde on the east coast, and Ci-bodzo in the Zambezi delta. While Zulu-Kaffir was once thought to be the most archaic, it is now deposed from that position, as it exhibits recent deviations in phonetics and vocabulary.

The archive notes affinities between Bantu and other non-Bantu languages. For example, the word for "fowl" (_kuba_) in some Gold Coast and Lower Niger languages may be derived from the Bantu _kuku_. Additionally, Fula shows some resemblance in grammatical construction (though it uses suffixes instead of prefixes) and possesses affinities in numerals 3 and 4.
