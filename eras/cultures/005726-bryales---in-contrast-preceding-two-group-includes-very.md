# Bryales

The Bryales represent a substantial group of mosses, characterized by a very large number of species and genera. In Britain alone, more than one hundred genera are represented, encompassing between five hundred and six hundred species. These plants are found in a wide variety of environments, including rocks, soil, and trees, and in some instances—such as *Fontinalis*—they occur in water.

## General Structure and the Protonema
While the Bryales exhibit a broad range of size and structural complexity across both generations, they generally conform to a common type, with *Funaria* serving as a representative example. The protonema is typically filamentous. In some of the simplest forms, the protonema is long-lived, and the small plants that grow upon it primarily function to protect the sporogonia and sexual organs.

Examples of this relationship include *Ephemerum*, which grows on the damp soil of clayey fields, and *Buxbaumia*, which is found on humus-rich soil and may be partially saprophytic. In *Buxbaumia*, the filamentous protonema is capable of assimilation; however, the leaves of the small plants lack chlorophyll and are therefore dependent on the protonema. The male plant of *Buxbaumia* lacks a definite stem and consists of a single concave leaf that protects the antheridium. The female plant is more highly organized, featuring a short stem with a few leaves surrounding the group of archegonia.

In other species, the protonema is highly developed. *Georgia*, *Oedipodium*, and *Diphyscium* possess protonemata with leaf-like assimilating organs. In *Ephemeropsis*, which grows on the leaves of living plants in Java, the persistent protonema is likely an adaptation to its specific living conditions.

## The Sporogonium
With the possible exception of *Archidium*, the sporogonium in the Bryales follows a consistent construction plan. In most Bryales, there is a definite columella extending from the base to the apex of the capsule. The archesporium is derived from the outermost layer of cells of the endothecium, and an air space is formed between the wall and the spore-sac. In the Polytrichaceae, an additional air space separates the columella from the spore-sac.

The seta varies in length and is occasionally practically absent. The apophysis usually bears stomata and serves as the primary organ of assimilation. This region is expanded in the Splachnaceae and constitutes most of the long pale stalk supporting the capsule in *Oedipodium*.

The capsule typically opens via a distinct operculum, which is detached with the help of the annulus. More commonly, a peristome consisting of one or two series of teeth is present. This structure narrows the opening to ensure that spores are shed gradually during dry weather.

## Variations and Special Cases
*Archidium* is a small moss found on the soil of wet fields that differs from other Bryales in several ways. Its protonema is not persistent, and its sporogonium has a small foot and almost no seta. It lacks a sterile columella and a distinct archesporium; instead, a variable number of cells in the endothecium become spore-mother-cells while others provide nourishment. The spores are large and thin-walled, escaping when the capsule ruptures the archegonial wall irregularly. Despite these peculiarities, which may have arisen by reduction, it is generally retained within the Bryales.

Other forms are described as cleistocarpous, meaning the capsule does not open to release spores. These include *Phascum*, where the columella persists but arrangements for dehiscence are absent, and *Ephemerum* (and the related *Nanomitrium*), where the columella is absorbed during spore development.

## Classification
Modern classifications of the Bryales rely primarily on the construction of the peristome. Previous methods of subdivision are now considered artificial. These include:
*   The division into Musci Cleistocarpi (lacking an operculum) and Musci Stegocarpi (possessing an operculum).
*   The grouping of stegocarpous forms into acrocarpi (where the archegonial group terminates a main axis) and pleurocarpi (where it is borne on a lateral branch).
