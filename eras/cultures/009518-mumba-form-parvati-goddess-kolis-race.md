# Mumba

## Overview and Etymology
Mumba is a goddess identified as a form of Parvati. She is the deity of the Kolis, a group of people described as a race of fishermen and husbandmen. The name of the city and island of Bombay is derived from this goddess.

## The Kolis
The Kolis are a tribe or caste located in Western India, though their exact origin remains uncertain. One theory suggests their name may be derived from the Turki word *kuleh*, meaning a slave; this theory further proposes that the term "cooly," used for agricultural laborers, may have originated from this name. 

The Kolis are distributed across several regions:
* **Gujarat:** They constitute the primary portion of the inferior agricultural population. In the past, they were notorious as robbers.
* **Konkan and Deccan:** The population of Kolis also extends into these areas.
* **District of Broach:** In this district, which consists mainly of a rich, highly cultivated alluvial plain at the mouth of the river Nerbudda, there are specific Koli villages.

In 1901, the total number of Kolis in India was reported as nearly 3¾ million, although this figure includes a distinct weaving caste known as Koris or Kolis located in northern India.

## Geography and Early History of Bombay
The area of Bombay originally comprised seven islands, referred to by Ptolemy as the *Heptanesia*. The Kolis are the earliest known inhabitants of these islands, having likely occupied them around the beginning of the Christian era.

The islands were part of the territories held by various successive dynasties dominant in western India, including the Rashtrakutas, Chalukyas, Mauryas, and Satavahanas. Between 450 and 750, during the Chalukya and Maurya periods, the principal location in the Bombay harbour was the city of Puri, situated on Elephanta Island.

## Urban Development and Political Transitions
The first town established on Bombay Island was Mahikavati (also known as Mahim). It was founded by King Bhima, who was likely a member of the house of the Yadavas of Deogiri. This foundation occurred as a result of a raid into the Deccan by Ala-ud-din Khilji in 1294.

Mahikavati remained under Hindu rule until 1348, when it was captured by a Mahommedan force from Gujarat. Consequently, the islands became part of the kingdom (or province) of Gujarat. This arrangement continued until 1534, at which point Sultan Bahadur ceded the islands to the Portuguese.
