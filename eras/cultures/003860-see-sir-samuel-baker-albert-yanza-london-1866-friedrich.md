# The Bari People

The Bari are a tribe of Nilotic negroes situated on the banks of the upper Nile, approximately 200 miles north of Albert Nyanza. They are bordered by the Galla to the east, the Madi to the south, and the Dinka to the north.

## Physical Characteristics and Dress
The Bari are characterized by a deep dead brown skin color. There is a distinct physical difference between the genders: the men are thin and tall, while the women are under middle height and fat. In terms of attire, unmarried girls and men go practically naked. Married women wear goatskins that have been dyed red. The Bari also ornament their bodies with red clay and frequently extract their lower incisors.

## Social Structure and Customs
The Bari reside in round grass huts featuring conical roofs. Within their society, rainmakers are the most important figures; they are reverenced more than the chiefs and are famous among the tribes that surround them.

The tribe maintains specific customs regarding family and death. Twins are viewed as unlucky; consequently, a mother who bears twins is divorced by her husband, and her family is required to refund a portion of the marriage-price. When a person dies, they are buried inside the hut. A square grave is dug, and the deceased is placed in a sitting position with their hands tied behind their back.

## Economy and Diet
The sole form of wealth for the Bari is cattle. Their primary diet consists of blood and milk. Meat is not a staple and is only consumed in the event that a cow dies.

## External Relations and History
Bari warriors have been recruited in significant numbers for the Egyptian army. Additionally, Arab traders formerly employed them as slave-hunters.
