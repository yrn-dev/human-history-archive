# Alligators and the Paraguay River Basin

## Distribution and Habitat
Alligators are found within the lower tropical districts of Bolivia, specifically inhabiting the lagoons and tributaries of the Paraguay river. They are part of a well-represented group of amphibia and reptiles in these regions, which also include numerous turtles, lizards, and various species of batrachians. The area is further characterized by a high population of snakes, including the great boa-constrictors of the Amazon region and rattlesnakes.

## Biological Characteristics
As members of the reptile genera, alligators—along with crocodiles, snakes, turtles, tortoises (Chelonia), and lizards—are creatures that require warmth throughout the entire year. They are unable to withstand exposure to cold, a limitation shared by reptilian beings of the Mesozoic. This requirement for warmth characterizes them as a "hothouse fauna."

## Geography of the Paraguay River System
The Paraguay river serves as a central drainage point for several regions. In Bolivia, the south-east drainage basin discharges into the Paraguay. This basin extends from the Cordillera Oriental in the east to the Sierras de Chiquitos in the south, reaching the Argentine frontier. 

The Bolivian tributaries of the upper Paraguay are generally considered small and unimportant. Notable streams in this region include:
* **The Otuquis:** Formed by the Tucabaca and San Rafael, which drain the Cerro Cochii range; however, it is lost in great marshes approximately 50 meters from the Paraguay.
* **The Parapiti:** Rising on the eastern slopes of the Sierra de Misiones, it flows north-east for about 150 meters before being lost in the marshy districts of the Bolivian plain.

Further downstream, the Paraguay river reaches a junction with the Upper Parana. Near this confluence lies the city and river port of Corrientes in the Argentine Republic. Corrientes is situated on the left bank of the Parana, 20 meters below the junction of the Paraguay and Upper Parana.

## Regional Fauna and Environment
The environment where alligators are found is part of a broader indigenous fauna that closely corresponds to the neighboring districts of Peru, Brazil, and Argentina. In the tropical forests, numerous species of monkeys coexist with the jaguar, puma, tapir (or *anta*), coati, sloth, ant-bear, capybara, and paca (*Coelogenys paca*). 

Other notable wildlife in the surrounding regions includes:
* **Andean Foothills:** The rare spectacled bear (*Ursus ornatus*).
* **Plateau Regions:** The chinchilla (*C. laniger*), prized for its fur, and the viscacha (*Lagidium cuvieri*).
* **Waterways:** The South American otter (*Lutra paranensis*), which is hunted for its skin.
* **Open Country:** The peccary, which ranges from the densely wooded north to the Chaco.
* **Marshes:** The large marsh deer of the Chaco, though deer are not numerous in the region.
* **General Distribution:** Armadillos, skunks, ferrets, and opossums are widely distributed.
* **Avian Life:** The neotropical region is home to the macaw (genus *Ara*). These large, long-tailed birds are especially abundant in Bolivia, where nearly one half of the known species have been found.

## Etymology
The word "alligator" is one of several Spanish and American words in Spanish forms that were introduced into the English language by the voyages of English navigators during the latter part of the 16th century.
