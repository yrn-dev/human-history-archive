# AVALON

Avalon (also appearing as Avallon, Avollon, Avilion, and Avelion) is a term associated with Welsh mythology, Arthurian romance, and a specific geographic location in France.

## Mythology and Romance
In Welsh mythology, Avalon was originally the kingdom of the dead. It later evolved in tradition into an earthly paradise located in the western seas. Within the Arthurian romances, it is characterized as the abode of heroes; it is the place to which King Arthur was conveyed following his final battle.

The Welsh name for the location is *Ynys yr Afallon*, which is generally interpreted as the "Isle of Apples." This derivation comes from the Welsh word *afal*, meaning apple. Because no other large fruit was well known to the races of northern Europe, the apple is likely intended to symbolize the enjoyments and feasting of elysium. An alternative theory suggests the name may be connected to Avalloc (Afallach in Welsh), a king over the dead in Celtic tradition.

Other names for this location include *Ynysvitrin* and *Ynysgutrin*, meaning "Isle of Glass." These terms appear to be identical to Glasberg, the Teutonic kingdom of the dead.

## Geographic Identifications
There have been various attempts to identify Avalon with specific locations in Wales and England. The name "Isle of Avalon" was applied to a low ridge in central Somersetshire that culminates in Glastonbury Tor, and Glastonbury itself became known as Avalon. This identification may have resulted from a confusion between the Anglo-Saxon Glaestinga-burh and either *Ynysvitrin* or Glasberg.

In literature, the island appears in the *Lais* attributed to Marie. In the fairy story *Lanval*, the hero eventually vanishes to the island of Avallon (or Avilion) along with his fairy princess.

## Avallon, France
Separate from the mythological isle, Avallon is a town in central France. It serves as the capital of an arrondissement in the department of Yonne, situated 34 miles south-south-east of Auxerre on a branch of the Paris-Lyon railway. As of 1906, the population was 5,197.

The town is situated on a promontory, with the Cousin river washing its southern base and small streams to the east and west. Its architecture includes:
* **The Church of St Lazare:** Dating from the 12th century, it features two western portals with ornate Romanesque sculpture. Its left facade tower was rebuilt in the 17th century.
* **The Tour de L'Horloge:** A 15th-century structure containing a museum on the second floor, with a gateway that allows the Grande Rue to pass through.
* **Fortifications:** Seven flanking towers from the ancient fortifications remain.

The town also features a statue of the military engineer Vauban. Public institutions in Avallon include a communal college, a tribunal of first instance, and the subprefecture. The local economy involves the manufacture of farm implements, leather, and biscuits and gingerbread, as well as trade in wine, wood, and agricultural produce and livestock from the surrounding region.

Historically, Avallon (known as *Aballo*) was the seat of a viscounty dependent on the duchy of Burgundy during the middle ages. Following the death of Charles the Bold, it passed under royal authority.
