# Gobi and the Tibetan Plateau Drainage

The Gobi is a great desert of central Asia, forming a vast interior space that is almost waterless. It is bounded to the north and north-west by the Yablonoi Mountains and their western extension in the Tian-shan, to the south by the northern face of the Tibetan plateau, and to the east by the Khingan range. Geographically, its eastern portion is nearly conterminous with south Mongolia, while its western portion forms eastern or Chinese Turkestan.

## Hydrography and Water Systems
The Gobi exists as a central depression that divides Asia laterally along the 40th parallel north. This depression extends westwards from the east of the Gobi through Mongolia to Chinese Turkestan. In the central portions of the Gobi, specifically around Lop Nor, research indicates the land may actually be below sea-level, though it is generally noted that no part of the great central Asiatic desert is less than 2,000 ft. above sea level.

The region serves as a basin where local drainage is lost in swamps or deserts. To the west of Kashgar, this depression is limited by the great elevation of the Pamir and the meridional range of Sarikol. From the Pamir—located at approximately 73 deg. E. and 39 deg. N.—waters flow in several directions: the Jaxartes (Syr) flows north through the Turki state of Khokand, and the Oxus (Amu) flows west. To the east, waters run past Kashgar toward the central desert of the Gobi. In this area, these waters unite with streams descending from the northern slope of the Tibetan plateau, traversing the Turki principalities of Khotan and Yarkand.

## Topography and Elevation
The Tibetan plateau, which borders the Gobi to the south, has a mean elevation of 15,000 ft. above the sea. The northern slope of this plateau, which descends toward the central plains of the Gobi, is generally referred to by the Chinese designation Kuen-lun. 

Within the Gobi region, elevations vary significantly:
* **Kashgar and Yarkand:** The plains in these areas range from 4,000 to 6,000 ft.
* **Mongolia:** The more northern parts are between 4,000 and 6,000 ft., with no part of the route between Kiakhta and the Chinese frontier falling below 3,000 ft.
* **Mountain Ridges:** While the precise positions of ridges traversing the central area are not fully known, many points are known to exceed 10,000 or 12,000 ft.

The Gobi is further characterized by specific mountain ranges, such as the Pe-shan in the desert of Gobi and the Arka-tagh (the Remoter Mountains). Other designations include the Astin-tagh (Lower or Nearer Mountains) and the Ustun-tagh (Higher or Farther Mountains), the latter of which may designate the more southerly of the twin border-ranges of the Tibetan plateau.

## Regional Context and Climate
The Gobi is part of the interior or inland basins of Asia, which include the Tibetan plateau and the Iranian uplands, totaling approximately 3,141,500 sq. m. This is about half the extent of the northern or southern divisions of the continent.

The environment of the adjacent Tibetan plateau is defined by extreme rigour, combining great drought with great cold, rendering the country essentially a desert with very scanty vegetation. Trees are rarely found except in sheltered spots where they are artificially planted. This harsh climate dictates the lifestyle of the population; while those in warmer, lower valleys practice agriculture, those in the higher regions are nomadic shepherds.
