# African and Global Populations: Black and Brown Estimated Groups

## Populations of Africa
Across the African continent, the aboriginal nations are mostly of the black negroid type, though their varieties are only imperfectly known. In South Africa, the aboriginal races include the Hottentots and Bushmen, both of which are rapidly diminishing and are expected to become extinct in British South Africa during the twentieth century. The majority of the native population in South Africa consists of dark-skinned negroids of Bantu stock, which include tribal groups such as the Damara, Bechuana, Zulu, and Kaffirs.

In Central Africa, many tribes exhibit racial characteristics similar to those in the south, though some possess an admixture of Arab blood. In East Africa, estimated populations include 4,000,000 natives (black and brown) in the Protectorate, 200,000 black and brown individuals in Zanzibar, and 3,200,000 in Uganda. In West Africa, the estimated populations are 15,000,000 in Nigeria (including Lagos), 2,700,000 in the Gold Coast and hinterland (chiefly black), 1,000,000 in Sierra Leone (chiefly black), and 163,000 in Gambia (chiefly black).

General estimates for other regions of Africa describe the population as chiefly black, with 5,211,329 in the South and 2,000,000 in the Central region. While the negroid type is prevalent, there is a wide admixture of Arab blood from east to west, producing a light-brown type. This is particularly evident in Nigeria and Uganda, where a large proportion of the population is relatively light-skinned and Arab. Conversely, some lower negroid types have drifted toward the west coast, where they continue to practice fetish rites and cannibalism.

## The West Indies and the Americas
The populations of the West Indies are diverse and composed largely of imported African negroes. In Jamaica, these individuals contribute four-fifths of the total population. Other groups in the islands include Chinese and imported East Indian coolies, as well as aboriginal races such as Caribs and American Indians of the mainland. Intermixture with Portuguese and Spanish blood has resulted in many mixed types. The total European population in this group of colonies is over 80,000, with an additional 15,000 in Bermuda.

In Costa Rica, the black population is remarkably small, amounting to only some 1,200 people. This is attributed to the fact that only 200 negroes were settled there when slavery was abolished in 1824, and there was no significant increase through immigration.

## Australasia and the Pacific
The native races of Australia and the Polynesian islands are categorized into dark and light Polynesian types. The dark type is black and considered of a very low order; in some islands, this group still retains cannibal habits. The aboriginal tribes of Australia are described as a low-class black race, though they are generally inoffensive and peaceful. The estimated population of black, very low-type individuals in Australia is 200,000.

In other regions of Australasia, populations include:
* **Fiji:** 121,000 (Polynesian, black and brown)
* **Papua:** 400,000 (Polynesian, black and brown)
* **New Zealand:** 53,000 (Maoris, brown, Chinese and half castes)

The natives of Papua (New Guinea) are noted to be in a very low state of civilization. The population estimates for this region are approximate because no census has been conducted.

## Mixed Populations and Other Groups
In South Africa, a hybrid population known as the Mixed Races has emerged over the last two hundred years. This group resulted from the intermarriage of natives with Indians and Malays introduced from Mozambique, Madagascar, Ceylon, Java, and British India. Their skin colors vary from dark brown to yellow.
