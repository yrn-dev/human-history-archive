# Basque Postpositional System and Suffixes

The Basque language utilizes a highly developed postpositional system in place of declension. In this system, declensional suffixes or postpositions are added to the noun. When a noun is definite, these suffixes are postponed to the definite article, which is itself a postposition: *a* for the singular and *ak* for the plural (for example, *zaldi* meaning "horse," *zaldia* meaning "the horse," and *zaldiak* meaning "the horses"). These postpositions may be added to one another, similar to the function of prepositions.

## Principal Suffixes and Meanings
The Basque language employs a variety of principal suffixes to indicate grammatical relationships and direction. These include:

*   **k**: Marks the plural and the singular nominative agent.
*   **n**: Indicates "of" and "in."
*   **i**: Indicates "to."
*   **z**: Indicates "by."
*   **ik**: Indicates "some."
*   **ko**: Indicates "from" or "of" (corresponding to the Latin *a*).
*   **tik**: Indicates "from" (corresponding to the Latin *ex*).
*   **tzat**, **kotzat**, and **tzako**: Indicate "for."
*   **kin** and **gaz**: Indicate "with."
*   **gatik**: Indicates "for the sake of."
*   **gana**: Indicates "towards."
*   **ra** and **rat**: Indicate "to," "into," "at," and similar directions.

Depending on the noun, some of these suffixes are joined to the indefinite noun, some to the definite noun, and some to both.

## Phonetics and Orthography
Basque does not possess its own graphic system and instead employs the Roman character, using either French or Spanish conventions. Modern writings use accented or dotted letters to indicate specific sounds. The alphabet varies across different dialects.

Prince L. L. Bonaparte identified thirty-eight simple consonants and thirteen simple vowels in the language. Certain dialects contain nasal vowels and "wet" consonants, such as *dy*, *ty*, and *ny*. In current speech, most soft consonants are dropped, and the doubling of consonants is not permitted. Additionally, the letter *r* cannot begin a word; consequently, the word *rationem* is written as *arrazoin* in Basque.
