# Balalaika

The balalaika is a stringed musical instrument described as having retained its primitive form unchanged. It is particularly popular among the peasants of Russia, with a notable prevalence in Ukraine.

## Physical Characteristics and Playstyle
The instrument is characterized by a triangular soundboard with a vaulted back glued to it, creating a body with a triangular base that allows the instrument to stand upright. Attached to this body is a fretted neck. The balalaika is strung with either two, three, or four strings. These strings are generally tuned to produce a minor chord when played together.

While the strings are typically plucked with the fingers, peasants utilize a technique of sweeping the strings lightly one after another with the side of the hand or the fingers to create "glissando" effects.

## Cultural Usage and Distribution
The balalaika is common to the Slav races, who employ the instrument to accompany their dances and folk-songs. Beyond the Slav populations, the instrument is also used by gipsies during fairs and rural festivities.

The instrument's use extends to other groups in Asia. Among the Kazaks, the balalaika—along with the flute—is used to accompany songs during nighttime feasting and the recital of tales during the summer months. Additionally, the instrument is associated with the Kalmaks, where it is described as a two-stringed lyre and is noted as the most common instrument among that people.
