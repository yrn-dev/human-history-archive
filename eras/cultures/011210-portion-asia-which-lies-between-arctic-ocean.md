# Siberia

Siberia is the portion of Asia situated between the Arctic Ocean to the north and a mountainous belt that bounds Turkestan, Mongolia, and Manchuria to the south. 

## Geography and Topography
The region is characterized by an immense high and broken plateau that extends from the south-west toward the north-east. As this plateau advances north-east, it loses both altitude and width. High border ridges fringe the plateau on either side; to the north-west, these ridges subside into high plains reaching altitudes of 1,500 to 2,000 feet, which eventually drop to lowlands a few hundred feet above sea level.

The Ural Mountains form part of the regional geography, with an average altitude between 2,000 and 3,000 feet. The highest summits of the Urals do not exceed 6,000 feet, and one of the mountain passes is as low as 1,400 feet. The southern half of the Ural range contains the primary mining districts of Russia.

## Hydrography
Siberia is traversed by some of the largest rivers in the world, specifically the Ob, the Yenisei, and the Lena. The Ob river basin is the largest in Asia, exceeding 1,000,000 square miles in extent. 

In a broader hydrographic context, the northern division of Asia—which includes the drainage falling into the Aralo-Caspian depression (or the Mediterranean), the Arctic Sea, and the Mediterranean—covers an area of approximately 6,394,500 square miles. Of this total, the Arctic river basins account for 4,367,000 square miles and the Aralo-Caspian basin accounts for 1,759,000 square miles.

## Climate and Ecology
The region experiences very great extremes of both heat and cold. North of the 50th parallel, the climate is similar to that of similarly situated portions of Europe, although the summers are hotter and the winters more severe. While rainfall is not heavy, it is moderate and sufficient to support abundant vegetation and maintain the water supply of the great rivers flowing to the Arctic Sea.

The surface of the land is frequently peaty or swampy. Vegetation varies by terrain: pine forests commonly clothe the mountain-sides, while the plains are covered with shrubs or grasses. The general assemblage of plants and animals in northern Asia closely resembles that found in adjacent parts of Europe with similar climates.

## Population and Civilization
The population of Siberia is very scanty. Cultivated tracts are restricted to the more settled districts and are relatively small in extent. The towns within the region are entirely Russian. The indigenous races are nomadic Mongols who are described as being of a peaceful character, though in a very backward state of civilization.

## Historical Context
The region east of the Ural Mountains, north of the Caspian Sea, was part of a wide area where the Aryan group of languages likely became distinct. This region, which also included the main rivers of the Danube, Dnieper, Don, and Volga, was inhabited by speakers who were in or past the Neolithic stage of civilization. Their language possessed complex verbal devices and grammatical forms, making it less crude than the expressions of earlier Neolithic kitchen-midden people or Palæolithic peoples such as the Azilians.
