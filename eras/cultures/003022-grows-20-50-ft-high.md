# Plants and Biological Growth

The archive contains various records regarding the growth habits, dimensions, and cultivation of diverse biological entities, ranging from microscopic bacteria to large forest trees.

## Forest Trees and Large Herbaceous Plants
Among the tallest plants recorded in the archive is the common white or silver birch (*B. alba*). This short-lived tree is typically between 40 and 50 feet high, with a trunk diameter that seldom exceeds 1 foot. It is found across North America, Siberia, Asia Minor, and much of Europe, where it reaches the extreme northern limits of forest vegetation. The silver birch is characterized by a slender grey or white bark, drooping branches, and leaves that are bright green in spring. It flourishes in light soils and is capable of growing among heather.

The banana, belonging to the genus *Musa*, is described as a gigantic herbaceous perennial. It produces an apparent stem that reaches 15 to 20 feet in height, which is composed of closely-enveloped leaf-sheaths. The blades of these leaves can sometimes reach 10 feet in length. During the flowering period, a true stem grows through the hollow tube of the sheaths to bear tubular flowers within protecting bracts.

## Garden Plants and Wild Flora
Various smaller plants are documented with specific height and growth characteristics:
*   **Lupines:** The perennial species *L. polyphyllus*, native to western North America, grows from 3 to 6 feet high. The tree lupine (*L. arboreus*), a Californian bush, reaches 2 to 4 feet in height and produces fragrant yellow flowers.
*   **Cabbage:** The wild or sea cabbage (*Brassica oleracea*), found on the Mediterranean coast and near the sea coasts of continental Europe and England, is a comparatively insignificant plant growing from 1 to 2 feet high.
*   **Saccharus (Hyoscyamus):** This herb is described as often reaching a tallness of above three spans. It possesses a root similar to a turnip and leaves resembling mint.
*   **Rhubarb:** The real rhubarb (*Rheum palmatum*) grows wild on very high mountains, specifically in the high range dividing the head waters of the Min-Kiang, Yalung, and Hwang-Ho. While an inferior kind is grown in fields in Szechwan, the genuine variety defies cultivation.

## Agricultural Cultivation
The cultivation of barley is primarily determined by the requirements of the maltster for uniformity of sample, as grains must germinate simultaneously. Consequently, the best barley is grown on light, open, and preferably calcareous soils. If the soil is too rich, the crop may grow too rapidly, resulting in a coarse and uneven sample; in such cases, wheat is often grown before the barley to reduce the soil's condition.

## Microscopic and Animal Growth
The archive also notes the growth and resistance of bacteria. Thermophilous bacteria are capable of growing at high temperatures, while other forms develop on ice. Certain bacilli can withstand exposure to liquid air (-192°C to -183°C) for seven days and liquid hydrogen (-252°C) for ten hours, after which they can grow again when returned to normal conditions.

Additionally, the archive mentions the Indian *Sáras* (also known as *Grus antigone* or Cyrus), which grows to 52 inches high.
