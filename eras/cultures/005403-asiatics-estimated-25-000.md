## Regional Centers and Demographics
Various towns and states across Asia exhibit specific demographic and economic characteristics. In Asiatic Turkey, the town of Kerkuk (or Qerquq) is located in the Mosul vilayet. Its population is estimated between 12,000 and 15,000, consisting chiefly of Mahommedan Kurds. The town is noted for its petroleum and naphtha springs, as well as a Jewish quarter located beneath the citadel.

In Asiatic Russia, the town of Khabarovsk (known as Khabarovka until 1895) serves as the capital of the Maritime Province and the Amur region. As of 1897, its population was 14,932. It functions as a trade center, particularly for sables, and an entrepôt for goods from the Usuri and Sungacha rivers.

In India, the feudatory state of Khairagarh in the Central Provinces had a population of 137,554 in 1901. This figure represented a 24% decrease over the previous decade due to famine. The state is ruled by a raja descended from the old Gond royal family.

## Other Asiatic Populations
In Korea, the native population is described as absolutely homogeneous.
