# Pulosari

## Geography and Location
Pulosari is an inactive volcano located in the residency of Bantam on the island of Java in the Dutch East Indies. It is situated within a hilly group in the middle of the northern portion of the residency. This northern region is characterized as the most fertile part of the residency and is generally flat. Pulosari is found alongside another inactive volcano known as Karang.

## The Residency of Bantam
The residency of Bantam is the westernmost residency of Java. Its boundaries are defined by the Java Sea to the north, the Strait of Sunda to the west, the Indian Ocean to the south, and the residencies of Preanger and Batavia to the east. In addition to the mainland, the residency includes several smaller islands along the coasts, as well as Dwars-in-den-weg (meaning "right-in-the-way") Island and Princes Island in the Sunda Strait.

The residency is divided into five native territorial divisions or regencies: Lebak, Charingin, Pandeglang, Anjer, and Serang. Serang serves as the capital of the residency and is located 2 1/2 miles from Bantam Bay. Other principal towns include Rangkas Betug, Menes, Pandeglang, and Chilegon.

## Demographics and Population
As of 1897, the population of Bantam was 709,339. This total included 1,959 Chinese, 302 Europeans, and 89 other Asiatic foreigners, including Arabs. The native population consists of Sundanese, although the northern division of Serang is inhabited by Javanese.

## Natural Features and Land Use
The coast of the residency is frequently marshy and low-lying. While the center of the residency contains various cold and warm sulphur springs and geysers, the southern portion is covered by the Kendang Mountains, which extend into the Preanger. On a ridge of the Karang Mountain, there is a large crater-lake called Dano; the government drained a great part of this lake in 1835 to facilitate rice cultivation.

The primary products of cultivation in the region are coffee, rice, and pulse (_kachang_). Historically, under government culture, indigo, sugar, and especially pepper were grown extensively.

## Economic and Environmental History
The residency's prosperity and trade have been impacted by several disasters. In 1879, a cattle plague occurred, which was followed by a fever epidemic that resulted in the deaths of 50,000 people. Due to these factors, there is a considerable emigration of natives, except during the rice season.

The fishing and coasting trade, which was once considerable, was ruined by the 1883 eruption of Krakatoa. This event caused an inundation that destroyed the seaport towns of Anjer and Charingin, along with a large stretch of the coastline.
