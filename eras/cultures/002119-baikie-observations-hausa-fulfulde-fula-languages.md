# Baikie's Observations on the Hausa and Fulfulde (i.e. Fula) Languages

William Balfour Baikie (1824–1864) was a Scottish philologist, naturalist, and explorer who produced significant linguistic work regarding the languages of West Africa. A graduate of medicine from Edinburgh and a former surgeon and naturalist for the 1854 Niger expedition, Baikie later served as a British consul. During his time in the region, he established a base of operations at Lokoja after concluding a treaty with the Fula emir of Nupe.

## Linguistic Publications
Baikie authored a work titled *Observations on the Hausa and Fulfulde (i.e. Fula) Languages*, which was privately printed in 1861. In addition to this study, he translated the Psalms into the Hausa language, a work published by the Bible Society in 1881.

## The Fula Language
The Fula language (also referred to as Fulfulde) is spoken by a powerful and numerous African people spread across a vast region from Senegal to Darfur. The language is characterized by several distinct linguistic features:

*   **Classification:** The Fula language has not yet been placed within any specific African linguistic family, though in its rudiments, it is akin to the Hamito-Semitic group.
*   **Grammar and Structure:** It utilizes two grammatical genders—human and non-human—rather than the masculine and feminine distinction. The language employs suffixes, which betrays non-Negroid influences and resembles some aspects of Bantu grammatical construction, where suffixes take the place of prefixes.
*   **Phonetics and Inflection:** Euphony is a significant factor in nominal and verbal inflections, and adjectives agree in assonance with their corresponding nouns.
*   **Comparisons:** Fula shows some resemblance to Bantu in its use of concord and offers affinities in a few verbal, pronominal, and nominal roots, as well as in the numerals 3 and 4.

## Terminology and Nomenclature
The name of the Fula people and their language varies depending on the source or the group providing the name. These variations include:
*   **Fulbe or Fula:** The Mandingan name (with *Pullo* or *Peul* as the singular).
*   **Follani:** The Hausa name.
*   **Fellatah:** The Kanuri name.
*   **Fullan:** The Arab name.
*   **Fulde:** The name used on the Benue.

These various names, along with the term *Abate* (meaning "white") used in Kororofa, are believed to refer to the light reddish hue of the people.

## Context of the Fula and Hausa Peoples
The Fula were originally herdsmen in the central and western Sudan. Under the leadership of Othman Dan Fodio in the early 19th century, they extended their influence east of the Niger and subdued the Hausa states, leading to the foundation of the empire of Sokoto. This empire included vassal emirates such as Nupe, Kano, Gando, and Adamawa.

In the province of Nupe, Fula rule was established in 1859, leading to the founding of the walled town of Bida. While Bida served as the capital of the province, its inhabitants were mostly Hausa, who became noted for their glass factory and their extensive trade in embossed copper and brass work.
