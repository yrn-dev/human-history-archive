# Professor A. Dumeril and the Axolotl Breeding Program

## Acquisition of Specimens
In late 1863, thirty-four axolotls, believed to have originated from the vicinity of Mexico City, arrived at the Jardin d'Acclimatation in Paris. These specimens, which included one albino, were sent to the institution by order of Marshal Forey. Forey had served as the commander-in-chief of the French expeditionary force to Mexico following the defeat of General Lorencez at Puebla on May 5, 1862, and returned to France at the conclusion of 1863 after transferring command to General (later Marshal) Bazaine.

From this group, the Societe d'Acclimatation provided six specimens—consisting of one female and five males—to Professor A. Dumeril. Dumeril served as the administrator of the reptile collection at the Jardin des Plantes. At the time of the axolotls' arrival, the living specimens of this collection were housed in a structure described as very miserable, located a short distance from a more sumptuous building that would eventually open to the public in 1874.

## Breeding and Larval Production
Initial attempts at breeding at the Jardin d'Acclimatation were unsuccessful because the eggs were not removed from the aquarium and were subsequently eaten by other occupants. However, at the Jardin des Plantes, the single female axolotl spawned twice in succession, and a large number of young were successfully reared.

The original six specimens were kept carefully apart from their progeny and remained in a branchiate condition. Between 1865 and 1868, they bred eleven times, and bred again in 1870 following a two-year period of rest. According to a report by Aug. Dumeril, these original specimens and their offspring produced between 9,000 and 10,000 larvae during this timeframe.

## Distribution and Observation
The high volume of axolotls produced allowed the Paris Museum to distribute more than one thousand examples to private individuals, dealers, and other institutions. These specimens spread throughout Europe, and numerous examples were maintained in England from 1866 onward. While specimens were exhibited in the London Zoological Gardens as early as August 1864—likely originating from the original stock received by the Societe d'Acclimatation from Mexico—those specific animals do not appear to have bred.

## Morphological Transformation
The breeding program provided data regarding the perennibranchiate nature of these Batrachians. By the time the second generation reached sexual maturity, new broods were produced. Some of these individuals underwent a transformation, losing their dorsal crest and gills, developing movable eyelids, and changing their dentition. These individuals also acquired yellow spots, assuming the characteristics of *Amblystoma tigrinum*. 

Between 1865 and 1870, twenty-nine of these transformed salamanders were obtained. Despite the free breeding of their branchiate counterparts, these transformed individuals did not breed. It was not until 1876 that Professor E. Blanchard reported the first instance of an axolotl in its *Amblystoma* state spawning in the reptile house of the Jardin des Plantes; these were the offspring of several generations of perennibranchiates.
