# The Languages of Mount Elgon

The languages spoken in the territories of Mount Elgon are categorized as part of the Bantu group of tongues. Specifically, they are included within the Kavirondo-Masaba section, a distinct division that encompasses languages spoken along the western flanks of Mount Elgon, as well as those of Bantu Kavirondo and the eastern coast-lands of the Victoria Nyanza (Igizii). This section also includes the Lukonde, Luwanga, Lu-nyara, and Igizii of the eastern and north-east shores of the Victoria Nyanza.

## Linguistic Classification and Archaism
The Kavirondo-Masaba group is more closely related to the Luganda section than to any other group of Bantu tongues. It is distinguished by its prefixes, which are considered the most archaic. In a broader context, the dialects of Mount Elgon are identified as being among the most archaic Bantu dialects existing at the present day, alongside those of Ruwenzori, Uganda, Unyoro, the Bemba country, the north coast of Tanganyika, the Subiya speech of the western Zambezi, and various dialects near Lake Bangweulu and Lake Nyasa.

## Prefix Structures and the Second Prefix
A leading feature of the Bantu group of languages is the governing of an unchangeable root by prefixes. In the most archaic Bantu speech, such as that of Mount Elgon, the second prefix appears as *Ba-* or *Aba-*. In its definite form, this prefix is *Baba-*.

The archive notes that in "less corrupt" Bantu tongues, the concord for this prefix is *-ba-*. However, in other dialects, this plural prefix degenerates into various forms, including *Va-*, *Ma-*, *Wa-*, and *A-*. For example, in Luganda, the form is *Wa-*, while in Lusoga (located on the north coast of Victoria Nyanza), *Wa-* becomes *Gha* (or *[Gamma]a*).

## Hypothetical Forms and Definite Articles
The languages of Mount Elgon (classified as Group No. 3) and the Subiya group (No. 32) retain forms that closely resemble hypothetical full forms of prefixes when joined with definite articles. These forms include:
*   *Ngumu*
*   *Mbaba*
*   *Ngimi*
*   *Ngama*

Regarding the definite form of the second prefix, the archive mentions that while *Baba-* is the form in Mount Elgon languages, the form *Ngaba* is sometimes found in Zulu-Kaffir. Additionally, the form *ngabantu* is met with in Zulu-Kaffir, which may have evolved from *nga-ba-ntu* (later becoming *na-ba-* or *aba-*).

## Comparison with Other Bantu Dialects
The archaic nature of the Mount Elgon languages stands in contrast to other Bantu tongues. For instance, while Ki-swahili of Zanzibar is considered primitive in its roots, it is not primitive in its prefixes. In Swahili, the 10th prefix (originally *Ti-*, *Tin-*, *Zi-*, or *Zin-*) has fallen out of use as a definite article for nouns in the plural, although it persists in the concord. An example is the phrase *Nombe hizi zangu* ("Cows these mine"), where *Nombe* is no longer *zinombe* in the plural, but the *Zi-* particle reappears in *hizi* and *zangu*.
