# Buddhist Birth Stories

The *Buddhist Birth Stories* is a translation of the *Jātaka*, a collection of verses attributed to the Buddha. The first volume of this translation was published in 1880 by T. W. Rhys Davids. Subsequent volumes (i.–vi.) were published between 1895 and 1897 by Rouse, Francis, Neil, and Chalmers.

## Nature and Content of the Jātaka
The *Jātaka* consists of 550 folk-tales that were current in India at the time the Buddhist canon was being formed. These stories are presented as verses supposed to have been uttered by the Buddha regarding his previous births. In each of these tales, the Buddha is identified as the hero of the story.

The archive notes that the *Jātaka* is the most authentic, complete, and oldest collection of folklore currently extant. While the verses themselves are central, the prose of these stories is preserved only within the commentary. Due to the nature of these fables, many of the stories migrated to Europe and can be found in medieval story-books, poems, and homilies. A detailed account of this migration is provided in the introduction to Rhys Davids' *Buddhist Birth Stories*.

## Related Canonical and Translation Works
The *Jātaka* exists alongside other foundational Buddhist texts. The philosophy and ethics of early Buddhists are derived from these works, including the *Sutta Nipāta*—which contains fifty-five poems, mostly short lyrics—and the *Vinaya*, which provides a complete statement of the rules of the order. 

Various translations of these texts have been published in the "Sacred Books of the East" series, including:
* *Vinaya Texts* (1881–1885) by Oldenberg and Rhys Davids.
* *Dhammapada* (1881) by Max Müller.
* *Sutta Nipāta* (1881) by Fausböll.
* *Buddhist Suttas* (1881) by Rhys Davids.
* *Questions of King Milinda* (1890–1894) by Rhys Davids.
* *Buddhist Mahāyāna Texts* (1894) by Max Müller and Cowell.

Additionally, a full translation of the *Jātaka* book has been published under the editorship of Professor Cowell through the Cambridge University Press.

## The Life of the Buddha in the Canon
While the *Jātaka* focuses on previous births, other canonical Pali texts (which reached their present shape before the 4th century B.C.) provide episodes from the Buddha's final life. These include:
* **Birth:** Accounts found in the *Majjhima Nikāya* and *Anguttara Nikāya*.
* **Adoration of the babe:** An old ballad found in the *Sutta Nipāta*.
* **Youth and Departure:** Accounts of his youth at home (*Anguttara Nikāya*) and "the going forth" (*Sutta Nipāta* and *Dīgha Nikāya*).
* **Attainment:** The period of study, penance, and the attainment of Buddhahood and Nirvāna (*Majjhima Nikāya* and *Vinaya*).
* **Final Days:** The *Mahāparinibbāna Suttanta* in the *Dīgha Nikāya* details his last days.

## The Birth at Lumbini
Specific details regarding the Buddha's birth are preserved in the *Sutta Nipāta* and the commentaries on the *Jātakas*. The Buddha was born in a grove or garden called Lumbini, located in the Sakiya land. 

According to the commentaries, the mother of the Buddha was traveling from Kapilavastu, the Sakiya capital, to her mother's home in Devadaha, the capital of the Koliyas. While on the way, she turned aside into the grove at Lumbini, where she gave birth to her son. This account is repeated in all later Buddhist Sanskrit and Pali sources.

## T. W. Rhys Davids
The translation of the *Buddhist Birth Stories* was conducted by T. W. Rhys Davids, M.A., LL.D., PH.D. He served as the Professor of Comparative Religion at the University of Manchester and was formerly the Professor of Pali and Buddhist Literature at University College, London. He was also a Fellow of the British Academy and served as the Librarian and Secretary of the Royal Asiatic Society from 1885 to 1902. His other contributions to the field include the works *Early Buddhism* and *Buddhist India*.
