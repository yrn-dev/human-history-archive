# Vegetable Life and Environmental Diversity

The diversity of vegetable life is closely linked to variations in climate, soil, and altitude. In certain regions, the range of flora is as extensive as the extremes of the environment it inhabits, spanning from sparse vegetation in high-altitude zones to lush forests in tropical basins.

## Flora of Bolivia
In Bolivia, the diversity of vegetable life is as great as the diversity of soil and climate. Because of varying altitudes, the country's flora represents every climatic zone. This ranges from the luxuriant tropical forests found in the Amazon basin to the scanty Arctic vegetation of the lofty Cordilleras. Due to the inaccessibility of these inland regions, the flora of Bolivia has been studied less than that of neighboring republics.

The vegetation varies significantly by region:
*   **Puna Region:** This area is characterized by coarse grasses, quinoa (*Chenopodium quinoa*), oca (*Oxalis tuberosa*), and the potato. Barley, an exotic plant, is grown widely here for fodder.
*   **Temperate and Warm Regions:** Indian corn was cultivated in these areas before Europeans arrived. The Europeans later introduced oats, rye, wheat, pease, beans, and various Old World vegetables and fruits.
*   **Sub-tropical and Tropical Zones:** Indigenous plants include peanuts, pineapple, cassava (*Manihot utilissima* and *M. aipi*), the sweet potato, pawpaw (*Carica papaya*), guava, chirimoya (*Anona cherimolia*), vanilla, sarsaparilla, *ipecacuanha* (*Cephaelis*), copaiba, tolu (*Myroxylon toluiferum*), false jalap (*Mirabilis jalapa*), cotton, and rubber-producing trees. This zone also features numerous beautiful hardwoods, including cedar, mahogany, rosewood, walnut, quebracho, colo, and jacaranda.

Common fruits in these zones include exotics such as the date, fig, grape, lime, lemon, and orange. The origin of the aguacate (avocado or alligator pear), caju (cashew), and banana is disputed. Additionally, quina or calisaya is found in the eastern Andes at altitudes between 3,000 and 9,000 feet; its bark is a significant item in foreign trade, though destructive collection methods are reducing natural sources. Coca is cultivated on the eastern slopes of the Andes at altitudes of 5,000 to 6,000 feet, where frosts are unknown and temperatures remain uniform.

## Vegetation of Asia
Vegetable life in Asia varies by region and is heavily influenced by geography and climate.

### Northern and Southern Asia
The plants and animals of northern Asia resemble those of adjacent European parts with similar climates. In Siberia, north of the 50th parallel, abundant vegetation is supported by moderate rainfall and rivers flowing to the Arctic Sea, despite hotter summers and more severe winters than in similar European regions. Similarly, a belt extending from the Mediterranean across to northern China and the Himalaya—including Afghanistan, northern Persia, and Asia Minor—possesses a temperate climate and sufficient rainfall to maintain vigorous vegetation. This region shows a marked similarity to south Europe.

### Central Asia and Tibet
The dry regions of central Asia are remarkable for a high relative number of Polygonaceae (including frequent leafless forms) and Chenopodiaceae, such as *Salicornia* and other salt plants. These, along with peculiar forms of Leguminosae, extend into Tibet. In Tibet, a summer climate similar to the plains of central Asia is produced by the combination of extreme drought and a nearly vertical, hot sun.

### The Himalayan Barrier
The mountain mass of the Himalaya and its connected elevated areas—approximately 2,000 miles long and 100 to 500 miles wide—acts as a barrier to living creatures. These mountains reach altitudes where vegetable life ceases. The range creates a complete separation of atmospheric conditions between its opposite flanks, obstructing the movement of moisture and air.

## Other Regional Observations
In the United States, a country of over 3,000,000 square miles, there is a great variation of climatic conditions. This has led American gardeners to focus on producing or finding fruits, flowers, and vegetables that can flourish in different parts of the country, with temperature ranges between the extreme northern and southern states varying by as much as 120 degrees F.

In ancient Greece, thinkers such as Aristotle, Hippocrates, and Herodotus viewed the climate of Asia as more favorable to vegetable and animal life than that of Greece, though they considered the Asian climate more enervating.

## Geological Context
The history of the geological record is described as a story of widening range. While species and genera appear and disappear, the overall range of life widens. During the Mesozoic time, lush vegetation and steamy air characterized the lagoons and great river plains where monsters once lived.
