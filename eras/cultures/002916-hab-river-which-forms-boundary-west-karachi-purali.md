# Hab River and Purali River

The Hab and Purali rivers are significant watercourses located in the region of Southern Baluchistan, serving as important arteries within the area's network of internal communication. Both streams drain into the Arabian Sea.

## The Hab River
The Hab river is situated in a region where the drainage generally trends south and west. It is characterized as a considerable stream and serves as the boundary to the west of Karachi. The river is associated with the Hab valley, a long and narrow line located between the hill ranges of the Mor and the Kirthar. These ranges separate the plains of Las Bela from Sind and strike nearly north and south, decreasing in height as they approach the sea. This geography allows for a route that skirts the coast between the city of Karachi and Bela.

## The Purali River
The Purali river, known in ancient times as the *Arabus*, drains the low-lying flats of Las Bela. It is one of two rivers flowing from the north—the other being the Kud—that unite to provide a plentiful water supply to the region. The Purali is central to the geography of Las Bela, a small independent state located south of Kalat and ruled by the Jam (or Cham), a protected chief under the British Raj.

The plains of Las Bela, which occupy approximately 6,000 square miles on the north-east corner of the Arabian Sea, are described as hot, malarial, alluvial, and highly fertile due to extensive irrigation. The Purali delta is currently growing with measurable strides over plains that were once covered by the bay of Sonmiani.

The town of Bela, the capital of the state, is situated on a rocky site above the banks of the Purali. The state of Las Bela, covering an area of 6,357 square miles, has historically been a vital point of transit; from time immemorial, the great trading route between Persia and Sind has passed through this area.

## Regional Context
The Hab and Purali rivers are part of a broader hydrographic system in Southern Baluchistan. Other considerable streams in this network that drain into the Arabian Sea include the Dasht and the Hingol (anciently known as the *Tomerus*), both of which drain Makran. 

This system is distinct from other regional drainage patterns, such as the Mulla river, which carries floods from the Kalat highlands into the Gandava basin, or the Lora river (the river of Quetta and Pishin), which drains toward the west into deserts and swamps near Nushki.
