# Bambusa and Phyllostachys

Bambusa and Phyllostachys are genera of bamboo, which is the popular name for a tribe of grasses known as *Bambuseae*. These plants are characterized as large, often tree-like grasses with woody stems. The stems emerge from an underground root-stock and frequently grow in crowded, dense clumps. While the largest species of bamboo can reach heights of 120 ft., the slender stems are hollow and feature well-marked nodes or joints where the cavity is closed by a strong diaphragm.

## Botanical Characteristics
The bamboo plant possesses numerous branches, which are spiny in some species. Its leaf-blades are typically narrow, often short, and usually jointed at the base with a short stalk attaching them to a long sheath. The flowers differ from most other grasses in that they usually possess six stamens and three lodicules. The spikelets are variously arranged in panicles or racemes and are usually many-flowered.

Blooming patterns vary by species; some bloom annually, while others do so only at intervals of many years, often blooming simultaneously over large areas. For example, *Bambusa arundinacea*, one of the largest species, has been observed blooming simultaneously on the west coast of India at intervals of thirty-two years. Following the ripening of the seed, the leafless flowering culms always die down.

## Genera and Species
Many bamboo species are native to Japan and China, including those in the genera *Bambusa*, *Phyllostachys*, and *Arundinaria*. Some species of *Arundinaria* are also native to the Himalayas.

Notable species include:
*   ***Bambusa***: *B. vulgaris* grows between 20 to 50 ft. high. *B. palmata* is characterized by leaves three inches broad and a foot or more long. Other species include *B. tesselata* and *B. quadrangularis*, the latter of which is remarkable for having square stems.
*   ***Phyllostachys***: *P. mitis* can reach 60 ft. in height in its native China and Japan. *P. nigra* is a handsome species named for its black stem.
*   ***Arundinaria***: *A. nitida* is described as remarkably hardy and the most attractive and daintiest of its genus. *A. simoni* has reached 18 ft. in height in the Kew bamboo garden and frequently fruits and flowers in Britain. *A. japonica* is a tall plant often grown in gardens under the name *Bambusa metake*.

## Cultivation and Propagation
Several bamboo species are hardy under cultivation in the British Isles. They are described as "hungry plants" that flourish in rich loam that is not too stiff, provided they receive generous treatment and are well mulched for the first two years. To thrive, they require shelter from winds and consistent watering during their growing period. When transplanting these plants, it is necessary to disturb the roots as little as possible.

Propagation can be achieved through division, cuttings, or seed. However, because fruit occurs rarely, propagation by seed is seldom applicable.

## Uses and Products
In Oriental countries, the products and parts of the bamboo are used in nearly endless ways. The soft, succulent shoots are harvested as they begin to spring and served at the table similarly to asparagus. To extend their fitness for consumption, they are earthed over, providing a continuous supply throughout the year, though the supply is most abundant in autumn. These shoots may also be candied, preserved in sugar, salted, or prepared as pickles to be eaten with rice.

As the plant ages, a fluid is secreted in the hollow joints. This leads to the development of a siliceous concretion known as *tabascheer* or *tabaxir*. While this substance possesses peculiar optical properties and was once highly valued in the East for medicinal qualities, the bamboo plant is entirely inert as a medicinal agent and has not been included in the European materia medica.

## Fruit Characteristics
The fruit of *Bambusa* and *Arundinaria* resembles the grain typical of grasses. This differs from other genera; for instance, the fruit in *Dendrocalamus* is a nut, and in *Melocanna*, it is rarely fleshy and resembles an apple in appearance and size.
