# The Vision of Life in the Qur'an: Purpose, Afterlife, and Moral Order

The Qur'an presents itself as a clear message and a sufficient exposition for mankind, revealed in Arabic to ensure that people may understand its contents. Its primary purpose is to serve as a warning to humanity and to establish the knowledge that Allah is One. Through the use of various similitudes and parables, the text seeks to provide admonition, encouraging men of understanding to reflect and take heed.

## Purpose and Guidance
The text is described as a "plain Reading" and a "Scripture" that makes things clear, containing no crookedness. This clarity is intended to guide individuals so that they may guard against evil. The Qur'an utilizes "the most beautiful of stories" and narratives—such as the account of Yusuf (Joseph)—to convey its messages. For example, the narrative of Yusuf includes a vision of eleven stars, the sun, and the moon prostrating to him, a vision his father warned him not to share with his brothers to prevent them from concocting a plot against him.

## Moral Order and Human Nature
The Qur'an outlines a moral order where human actions are observed and judged. It teaches that the Lord encompasses mankind and that certain experiences, such as specific visions or the "Cursed Tree," are appointed as trials or ordeals for humanity. 

The text also addresses the nature of temptation and enmity, identifying Satan (Shaitan) as an open foe to man. This theme of obedience and rebellion is further illustrated in the account of the creation of Adam; while the angels bowed down to Adam as commanded, Iblis refused, questioning why he should make obeisance to a being created from clay or dust.

## The Life of the World and the Hereafter
The Qur'an presents a stark contrast between the temporary nature of earthly existence and the permanence of the afterlife. It notes that those who disbelieve may be left to eat, enjoy the good things of this life, and be beguiled by false hope, but asserts that they will eventually come to know the truth.

The text describes a system of divine justice where those who rejected prophets in the past were met with a doom or chastisement that came upon them unexpectedly. While Allah may allow some to taste humiliation or disgrace in the life of the world, the Qur'an emphasizes that the punishment of the Hereafter is significantly greater. Consequently, those who disbelieve are described as eventually wishing ardently that they had been Muslims and bowed to Allah's will.
