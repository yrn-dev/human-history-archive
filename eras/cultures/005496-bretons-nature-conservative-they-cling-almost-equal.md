# The Bretons

The Bretons are characterized by a conservative nature, maintaining a strong attachment to their local customs and religious superstitions. This conservatism is evident in the region's religious history; in some areas, paganism was not nominally abolished until the 17th century. Consequently, popular Christianity in Brittany has assimilated more from earlier creeds than perhaps any other district in Europe, with continued beliefs in the influence of fairies and witchcraft.

## Culture and Folklore
Brittany possesses a flourishing folk-literature, characterized by a preserved taste for marvelous legends and a large collection of folk-songs and folk-tales. The region's linguistic landscape includes the continued use of the Celtic language in lower Brittany, where four distinct dialects are clearly marked.

The traditional costume of the Bretons is peculiar in both color and cut, though styles vary by district. Men's waistcoats and coats, as well as women's clothing, frequently utilize bright blue, violet, and red. Detailed illustrations of these styles are documented in Bouet's *Breiz-izel, ou vie des Breions de l'Armorique* (1844).

## Music and Instruments
The biniou (or bignou) is a traditional and popular instrument used by Breton peasants in Morbihan and Cornouailles. While some erroneously derive the name from the phrase *bigno, se renfler beaucoup*, Breton dictionaries indicate it is the plural form of *benvek*, meaning tool or instrument. The term is also used in the phrase *Sac'h ar biniou*, referring to a bag used by weavers for spindles and tools.

The biniou is almost always paired with the bombard, a survival of the medieval chalemie, hautbois, or musette. Together, these instruments are played by traditional musicians known as *sonneurs*, who perform ancient Breton melodies and *rondes* at wedding feasts, threshing dances, *pardons*, and other festivals.

## Geography and Administration
The duchy was historically organized into nine bishoprics. Upper Brittany contained the bishoprics of St Malo, Nantes, Dol, Rennes, and St Brieuc. Lower Brittany comprised the bishoprics of Vannes, Quimper, Tréguier, and St Pol de Léon.

## Notable Figures
Several individuals associated with the name or region have contributed to literature and the arts:

*   **Julien Auguste Pélage Brizeux (1803–1858):** A French poet born in Lorient, Morbihan, to a family of Irish origin. He was a student of Breton archaeology and philology, collecting materials for a dictionary of Breton place-names. His literary work includes *Les Bretons* (1845), which drew inspiration from the legends and folklore of Brittany, and *Telen-Aroor* (1844), written in the Breton dialect.
*   **Manuel Bretón de los Herreros (1796–1873):** A Spanish dramatist born in Quel. Though not a resident of Brittany, he was a prolific writer of approximately 360 original plays. Originally a liberal, he became conservative with age, leading to his dismissal from the national library in 1840 after he ridiculed the National Guard in the play *La Ponchada*.
