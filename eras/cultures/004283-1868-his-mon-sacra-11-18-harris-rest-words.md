## Literary and Religious Publications
In 1868, Ceriani published a work titled *Mon. Sacra* (v. 11-18), which included the Greek text of the book known as "The Rest of the Words of Baruch." This text is also referred to in the Abyssinian Church as "The Rest of the Words of Jeremiah," which is its Greek name (*ta paraleipomena Hieremiou prophetou*). The work has been preserved in several languages, including Slavonic, Armenian, Ethiopic, and Greek.

The content of "The Rest of the Words of Baruch" begins with an account regarding the removal of the Temple's sacred vessels prior to its capture by the Chaldees. The narrative describes Baruch remaining in Jerusalem while Jeremiah accompanies the Exiles to Babylon. After a period of 66 years in exile, Jeremiah leads the sons of Israel back to Jerusalem, though he refuses entry to those who had brought heathen wives. The text concludes with a vision of Jeremiah that is described as Christian. Scholars, such as Kohler in the *Jewish Quarterly Review* (1893), have noted that while the book was originally written by a person of the Israelite people, it was later revised by a Christian.
