# Median Longitudinal Section of a Capsule in Funaria hygrometrica

## Structural Components of the Capsule
The median longitudinal section of the capsule identifies several distinct anatomical features:

*   **Seta and Apophysis:** The capsule is supported by a long seta. At the base of the capsule, the seta gradually widens into a region known as the apophysis. The peripheral cells of the seta are thick-walled, and it contains a central strand of elongated conducting cells. The epidermis of the apophysis contains functional stomata, similar to those found in higher plants.
*   **Columella and Archesporium:** Extending from the base to the apex of the capsule is a definite sterile columella. The archesporium is derived from the outermost layer of cells of the endothecium.
*   **Spore-Sac and Air-Space:** An air-space exists between the wall of the capsule and the spore-sac.
*   **Dehiscence Mechanisms:** The apex of the capsule features an operculum, which is a distinct lid usually detached with the assistance of the annulus. Below this is the peristome, consisting of one or two series of teeth. The peristome serves to narrow the opening of the capsule, ensuring that spores are shed gradually during dry weather.

## Development of the Sporogonium
The sporogonium of *Funaria hygrometrica* begins its growth via a two-sided apical cell. Initially, the structure is of uniform thickness. Over time, the lower portion develops into the foot—which is embedded in the end of the stem—and the long seta. Simultaneously, the upper region increases in diameter to form the capsule.

During this growth, the archegonial wall is eventually broken through. The basal portion of this wall remains as a tubular sheath around the lower end of the seta, while the larger upper part, terminated by the neck, is carried up on the capsule as the calyptra.

## Comparative Anatomy in Bryales and Other Groups
While *Funaria hygrometrica* represents a typical construction for most Bryales, there are notable exceptions and variations within the archive's records:

*   **Archidium:** Unlike *Funaria*, *Archidium* has a small foot and practically no seta. It lacks a distinction between a sterile columella and an archesporium; instead, a variable number of cells become spore-mother-cells while others nourish the spores. Its capsule ruptures the archegonial wall irregularly rather than using an operculum.
*   **Polytrichaceae:** In this group, an additional air space separates the spore-sac from the columella.
*   **Andreaea:** In the genus *Andreaea*, the sporogonium has no seta and is instead raised on a pseudopodium. The capsule does not open via an operculum but through four or six longitudinal slits. Additionally, the dome-shaped archesporium extends over the summit of the columella, and the air-space found in *Funaria* is absent.
*   **Cleistocarpous Forms:** Some forms, such as *Phascum* and *Ephemerum*, are cleistocarpous, meaning the capsule does not open to liberate spores. In *Ephemerum*, the columella is absorbed during spore development.

## General Characteristics of Funaria hygrometrica
*Funaria hygrometrica* is a common moss found on the soil of paths and at the foot of walls, typically growing in crowded tufts. The plants consist of short leafy shoots attached to the soil by branched rhizoids composed of rows of elongated cells. The leaves are simple and generally one layer of cells thick, except for the midrib. The stem contains a central strand of narrow cells that functions as water-conducting tissue. Sexual organs develop at the apices, with antheridial groups usually terminating the main axis and archegonia borne on lateral branches.
