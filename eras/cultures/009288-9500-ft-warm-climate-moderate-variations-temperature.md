# The Valle Climatic Zone of Bolivia

The *valle* is one of several distinct climatic zones identified by the natives of Bolivia. This specific zone is characterized by its elevation, which encompasses the deep valleys ranging from 5,000 to 9,500 feet.

## Climate and Characteristics
The *valle* zone is sub-tropical in both its character and its productions. It is defined by a warm climate that experiences moderate variations in temperature and an absence of cold weather. Because of these stable conditions, the region is sometimes described as a region of perpetual summer. 

In general, the *valle* region is considered healthy and agreeable. It is characterized by a moderate temperature in the shade and a plentiful amount of rainfall. These conditions support varied and abundant products.

## Comparison with Adjacent Zones
The *valle* zone exists as part of a vertical arrangement of climatic zones in Bolivia, where temperature variations are primarily driven by prevailing winds, mountain barriers, and elevation. It is situated between the *yungas* and the *cabezera de valle*:

*   **The Yungas:** Located below the *valle* zone, the *yungas* comprise the mountain valleys and lowlands up to 5,000 feet. This region is tropical and exceedingly humid, with no known winter and an official mean temperature of 70 deg. F. Unlike the healthy *valle* region, the *yungas* are prone to fevers and malaria, and the ground is covered in decaying vegetation.
*   **The Cabezera de Valle:** Located above the *valle* zone, this region includes the heads of the deep valleys at elevations between 9,500 and 11,000 feet. Its climate is temperate and divided into regular seasons, which is favorable for the production of vegetables and cereals.

## Broader Bolivian Climatic Context
Bolivia lies entirely within the torrid zone, meaning it possesses every gradation of temperature, ranging from the tropical lowlands to the Arctic cold found at the snow-capped peaks. While the *valle* and *cabezera de valle* are agreeable, higher elevations present harsher conditions. The *puna* (11,000 to 12,500 feet) is cold and dry, with a mean temperature of 54 deg. F., limiting production to barley and potatoes. Above this lies the *puna brava*, extending from 12,500 feet to the snow limit of approximately 17,500 feet, which is a bleak territory inhabited only by miners and shepherds.

The distribution of these zones is modified by rainfall. In the north and east of the Cordillera Oriental, rain falls throughout the year, though November through March is the rainy season. Conversely, the plateaus on the west side of the Cordillera have limited rainfall in the north, which decreases toward the south until the land becomes absolutely barren.
