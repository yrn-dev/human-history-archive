# The Politico-Religious Revolution of 1870

In 1870, a remarkable politico-religious revolution occurred among the Ba-Luba, a Bantu negroid race located in Central Africa. The Ba-Luba are distributed across eight degrees of longitude, stretching from the Kasai in the west to Lakes Bangweulu, Mweru, and Tanganyika in the east.

## Origins and Context
The Ba-Luba are recognized as one of the most cultivated peoples of Central Africa. In the eastern regions, where they maintain the greatest racial purity, they established the states of Uguha, Urua, and Katanga. In the west, the Ba-Luba—referred to as the Ba-Shilange—intermixed to some extent with the aborigines known as the Ba-Kete. This process involved the partial dispossession of the Ba-Kete, who were divided into northern and southern portions.

The westward movement of the Ba-Luba occurred in relatively recent history, specifically at the beginning of the 19th century or the end of the 18th century. Following this migration, a chief named Kalamba Mukenge founded a large state.

## The Revolution of 1870
The politico-religious revolution of 1870 resulted in the establishment of a secret society termed the *Bena Riamba*. This society was centered around a cult of hemp-smoking. As a consequence of this revolution, members of the *Bena Riamba* abandoned their previous practices of fetish worship.

The society adopted a form of communism, the central idea of which was the blood-brotherhood of all its members. While this movement was significant, the practice of hemp-smoking became less common as one moved toward the east.

## Related Regional Powers
The Ba-Luba are connected to the founders of the Lunda empire, which is now divided between Angola and the Belgian Congo. This empire was ruled by a monarch who held the title of Muata Yanvo (or Jamvo).
