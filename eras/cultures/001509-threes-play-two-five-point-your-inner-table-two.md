# Backgammon

Backgammon is a game played by two persons using a board divided into four "tables." Each of these tables is marked with six differently colored "points." The inner and outer tables are separated by a projecting bar. The game is played with fifteen white and fifteen black men, which are arranged in a specific set. The equipment required includes two dice-boxes (one for each player) and a pair of dice. The dice sides are numbered one through six; the number one is referred to as an "ace," two as a "deuce," and three as a "trey." Historically, the four was called "quatre," the five "cinque," and the six "six."

## Gameplay and Movement
Players move their men from point to point based on the numbers thrown by the dice, alternating turns. The direction of movement differs by color: white moves from the inner table of black to black's outer table, then to white's own outer table, and finally to white's inner table. Black moves in the opposite direction.

A player may move any of their men the number of points indicated by the dice, provided the destination point is not "blocked" by two or more of the adversary's men. A player may use the whole throw for one man or move two separate men. If doublets are thrown (such as two sixes), the player makes four moves of that number.

Movement is compulsory unless blocked. If a player can move the whole throw by changing the order of the dice (for example, moving a five first to enable a subsequent move of six), they must do so. Any part of a throw that cannot be moved has no effect.

## Opening Moves and Strategy
At the start of the game, players throw one or two dice to determine who plays first; the higher thrower starts and may either use those numbers or throw again using both dice. Certain throws are considered more advantageous than others:

*   **Aces:** Considered the best of all throws; the recommended move is two on the bar-point and two on the five-point.
*   **Threes:** The recommended play is two on the five-point of the inner table and two on the four-point of the adversary's inner table, or making the bar-point.
*   **Deuces:** The recommended move is two on the four-point of the inner table and two on the trey-point of the opponent's inner table.
*   **Other Combinations:** An "ace, trey" is used to make the five-point in the inner table, while an "ace, six" is used to make the bar-point.

Strategic priorities include making points that obstruct the opponent and choosing positions for "blots" (single men) where they are least likely to be hit. The most valuable points to secure are the five-point in both one's own and the adversary's inner table, followed by one's own bar-point and the four-point in one's own inner table.

## Advanced Tactics and Bearing Off
When carrying men home, the most efficient method is to move the most distant man to the adversary's bar-point, then to the six-point of the outer table, and finally to the six-point of the inner table. Players are advised to avoid placing too many men on the deuce or trey-point of their own tables, as these men become out of play.

The game proceeds until a player moves all their men into their inner table (home), at which point they begin to "bear" them off the board by removing a man from a point corresponding to the dice throw. If a player is hit on a blot after they have begun bearing off, they must re-enter the man on the adversary's inner table and bring it home before they can continue bearing off.

## Winning and Scoring
The winner is the first player to remove all their men from the board. The value of the win depends on the progress of the adversary:
*   **Single Game (Hit):** Won if the adversary has already begun bearing their own men.
*   **Double Game (Gammon):** Won if the adversary has not yet borne a single man.
*   **Triple Game (Backgammon):** Won if the adversary has not borne a man and either has a man remaining in the winner's inner table or has a man "up."

In a series of games, the winner of a "hit" takes the first throw of the next game. If a "gammon" is won, a single die is thrown by each player to determine the first move.
