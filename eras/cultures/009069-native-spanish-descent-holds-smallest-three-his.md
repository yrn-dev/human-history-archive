# Bolas

Bolas (the plural of the Spanish word *bola*, meaning ball) are weapons used for war and the chase by South American Indians. These weapons consist of balls made of stone that are attached to the ends of a rope made of braided or twisted hemp or hide.

## Types and Usage
According to descriptions by Charles Darwin in his *Voyage of the Beagle*, there are two primary kinds of bolas:

*   **The Simple Type:** Used primarily for catching ostriches, this version consists of two round stones covered in leather. These stones are joined by a thin, plaited thong measuring approximately 8 feet in length.
*   **The Three-Ball Type:** This version differs from the simple type by having three balls united by thongs to a common center. When using this weapon, the Gaucho (a native of Spanish descent) holds the smallest of the three balls in his hand and whirls the remaining two around his head. Once he takes aim, he sends them revolving through the air like chain shot. Upon striking an object, the balls wind around it, cross each other, and become firmly hitched.

## Distribution and Theory
Bolas have been utilized for centuries within the forest regions of the Rio Grande and the South American pampas. F. Ratzel, in *History of Mankind*, suggests that the bolas are a form of lasso. Additionally, a similar weapon is employed by the Eskimos for the purpose of killing birds.

The archive also notes the existence of *bolas perdidas* (lost bolas), which are characterized as stones attached to a very short thong, or in some instances, having no thong at all.
