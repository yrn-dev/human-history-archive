# Phasianus

The genus *Phasianus* consists of splendid birds that are restricted in their wild state to northern Asia. These birds are part of a broader ornithological landscape in northern Asia that is closely allied to that of Europe, characterized by a development of groups suited to temperate and cold regions and an absence of tropical forms.

## Regional Distribution and Related Genera
While *Phasianus* is found in northern Asia, other magnificently clad birds of a similar nature are confined to specific districts in the mountains of north eastern Asia, specifically the eared pheasants (*Crossoptilon*) and the gold pheasants (*Thaumalea*). 

In the Indian region, the *Phasianidae* family is highly characteristic; however, this classification in that region is exclusive of true *Phasianus*. The Indian region is noted as one of the richest and most varied bird assemblages in the world, surpassed only by tropical America.

## Specific Species and Observations
Within the broader group of phasianids, various species have been documented across Asia:
*   ***Phasianus Reevesii* (or *veneratus*):** Known to the Chinese of Tung-lin, near Peking, as *Djeu-ky* (meaning "hen-arrow").
*   ***Crossoptilon auritum*:** Referred to by the name *Ma-ky*. This bird is nearly as large as a turkey.
*   ***Lophophorus impeyanus* (Múnál):** A glorious bird described by Ælian as having a variegated, ornate crest resembling a crown of flowers. Its tail feathers are broad and carried in a train like those of a peacock, with colors ranging from golden to emerald or azure.

Additional discoveries of the genus *Crossoptilon* include a species found by Captain Prjevalsky in Alashan (identified as the Egrigaia) and another discovered by Abbé Armand David at the Koko Nor.

## Fossil Record
The history of *Phasianus* extends into the prehistoric record. Remains of *Phasianus* have been discovered in Greece at Pikermi, dating to the Pliocene epoch. These remains were found alongside other birds, including *Gallus* and a large *Grus*.
