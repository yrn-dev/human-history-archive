# The Flora of the Rainless Region of South-Western Asia

## Geographic Extent and Continuity
The flora of the rainless region of south-western Asia is continuous with the desert flora found in northern and eastern Africa. This botanical zone extends from the coast of Senegal to the 75th meridian East. Geographically, it reaches from the great African desert to the border of the rainless tract located along the southern parts of the Punjab and the Indus. 

This continuous region encompasses several key areas, including the peninsula of Arabia, the shores of the Persian Gulf, south Persia, and the regions of Baluchistan and Afghanistan. Its westernmost limit is found in the Cape Verde Islands, and the flora is partially represented in Abyssinia.

## Characteristics of the Desert Tracts
The tracts that form an almost unbroken desert—stretching from Arabia through Baluchistan and south Persia to Sind—are characterized by extreme heat and dryness. These areas exhibit a considerable uniformity in their types of life, which closely approach the types found in neighboring hot and dry regions of Africa.

In the south-western part of Arabia, known as Arabia Felix, the landscape includes mountainous regions with summits rising to 7,000 or 6,000 feet. In these higher elevations, rainfall is sufficient to support more luxuriant vegetation. The flora of the valleys in Arabia Felix resembles that of the less elevated parts of Baluchistan and Afghanistan, as well as similarly situated areas of southern Persia, sharing characteristics with the hotter Mediterranean region.

## Vegetation and Plant Species
In the rainless regions of south-western Asia, aromatic shrubs are abundant, while trees are rare. The few tree species present are almost restricted to *Celtis*, *Dodonaea*, *Pistacia*, poplars, and the date palm. The dry hills are covered by prickly forms of *Astragalus* and *Statice*. During the spring, there is an abundance of herbaceous vegetation and many bulbous plants. The genera of these plants are, and in some cases the species are, identical with those of the Syrian region; some of these forms extend as far as the Himalaya.

Regarding cultivated plants, Arabia grows crops that are largely the same as those found in northern India, including barley, wheat, indigo, cotton, lemons, and the common *Sorghum*, as well as dates. Coffee is also cultivated, though it is restricted to the slopes of the western hills.

## Comparison with Other Asian Regions
The rainless region of south-western Asia is distinct from other botanical zones in Asia:

*   **Central Asia:** The area comprising Mongolia, Turkestan, western Manchuria, and the region between the southern border of Siberia and the temperate alpine zone of the Himalaya and north China is also an almost rainless region. However, its plants and animals have a special character suited to intense summer heat and extreme winter severity, making them more closely allied to the adjacent northern Siberian tract than to other bordering regions.
*   **Humid Regions:** In contrast to the arid south-western tracts, the region comprising southern China, the Indo-Chinese peninsula, India, and the western part of the Malay Archipelago is characterized by high temperatures and heavy periodical summer rains, showing a different similarity in plants and animals throughout its extent.
*   **South-Eastern Asia:** The indigenous plant species of south-eastern Asia (from the 65th to the 105th meridian), estimated by Sir J.D. Hooker at 12,000 to 15,000 species, are dominated by tropical plants. Principal orders in this region include Orchidaceae, Leguminosae, Rubiaceae, Compositae, Gramineae, Euphorbiaceae, Acanthaceae, Labiatae, and Cyperaceae.
