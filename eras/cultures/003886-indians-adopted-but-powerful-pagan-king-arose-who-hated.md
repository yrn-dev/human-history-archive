# The Legend of King Abenner and Josaphat

## The Christian Influence in India
According to Greek narrative, the people of India were converted by St. Thomas. Following the emergence of the eremitic life in Egypt, this lifestyle was adopted by many Indians. However, this period of religious development was interrupted by the rise of a powerful pagan king named Abenner. King Abenner harbored a deep hatred for Christians and actively persecuted them, directing particular hostility toward the ascetics.

## The Birth and Prophecy of Josaphat
For a long duration, King Abenner remained childless. Eventually, a son was born to him who was considered matchless in beauty and greatly desired; the boy was named Josaphat. In his joy, the king summoned astrologers to predict the destiny of his child. While the astrologers foretold a future of prosperity and glory exceeding that of all previous predecessors, one sage—the most learned of the group—provided a specific qualification. He intimated that the glory predicted for the boy would not occur within his father's kingdom, but rather in another kingdom that was infinitely more exalted. Furthermore, the sage predicted that the child would adopt the very faith that his father persecuted.

## The Seclusion of the Prince
As Josaphat grew, he exhibited a devout and thoughtful nature. This behavior, combined with the memory of the sage's prediction, troubled King Abenner. To prevent his son from discovering the Christian faith or contemplating a future life, the king took extreme measures to isolate him. 

Abenner selected a secluded city and commissioned the construction of a splendid palace for the boy. Josaphat was to live there attended only by servants and tutors who were in the flower of health and youth. The king imposed strict rules to ensure the boy's environment remained entirely pleasant:
* No strangers were permitted access to the palace.
* The boy was to be kept ignorant of all human sorrows, specifically excluding any knowledge of death, old age, disease, or poverty.
* He was strictly forbidden from hearing any word regarding Christ or the Christian religion.

The king's objective was to remove any inducement for the prince to think about a life beyond the present one.
