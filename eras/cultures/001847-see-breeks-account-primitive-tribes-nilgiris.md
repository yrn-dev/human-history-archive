# Primitive Tribes of the Nilgiri Hills

The Nilgiri Hills of India are inhabited by several distinct groups, including the Badagas, the Kotas, and the Kurumbas. These groups vary significantly in their social structures, economic activities, and origins.

## The Badagas
The Badagas, whose name literally means "a Telugu man," are an agricultural people and are the wealthiest and most numerous of the hill tribes. According to the census of 1901, their population was 34,178. While some authorities suggest they are not a jungle or aboriginal race, they are likely Dravidian by descent. In terms of religion, they are Hindus of the Saiva sect.

It is believed that the Badagas migrated to the Nilgiris from Mysore around A.D. 1600, following the collapse of the kingdom of Vijayanagar. Their language is described as a corrupt form of Kanarese. Economically, they pay a tribute in grain and other goods to the Todas.

## The Kotas
The Kotas (also referred to as Kotar, Koter, Kohatur, or Gauhatur) are an aboriginal tribe of the Nilgiri hills. Physically, they are described as tall, well-made people with good features and a dull copper color, though some are among the fairest of the hill tribes. In 1901, their population was recorded as 1,267.

The Kotas are divided into *keris* (streets) rather than castes, and marriage must occur outside of one's own *keri*. They live in seven large villages, which average between thirty and sixty huts. Their economy is based on agriculture and herding; notably, they are the only hill tribe to practice industrial arts, excelling as tanners, smiths, carpenters, and basket-makers. Like the Badagas, the Kotas pay a tribute to the Todas, for whom they perform menial work. Their language is a rude and old dialect of Kanarese. Their religious practice involves the worship of ideal gods who are not represented by images.

## The Kurumbas
The Kurumbas are aboriginal tribes of southern India. There are two distinct types: those living in the plains, who are considered civilized and speak Kanarese, and those living on the Nilgiri plateau. The plateau-dwelling Kurumbas are described as mere savages.

The Nilgiri plateau Kurumbas are a small population, numbering 4,083 according to the 1901 census. They are characterized physically by pot-bellies, large mouths, thick lips, projecting jaws, prominent teeth, and sickly appearances, with scanty beards and wild matted hair. They reside in mountain glens or forests in villages called *mottas*, which typically consist of groups of four or five huts. They speak the Kurumba dialect.

## Other Nilgiri Groups
The Todas are also mentioned as a group inhabiting the Nilgiri Hills. They are noted for retaining primitive habits, including the weaving of *daris* and *satranjis*—flat surface texture floor coverings woven in cotton with blue and white or blue and red stripes. This method of making ground coverings is considered older in origin than cut pile carpets. The Todas also receive tributes from both the Badagas and the Kotas.
