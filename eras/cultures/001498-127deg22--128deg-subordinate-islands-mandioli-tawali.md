# Bachian

Bachian, also known by the Dutch name *Batjan*, is one of the Molucca Islands located in the Molucca Sea. It is situated within the residency of Ternate in the Dutch East Indies, specifically at the coordinates 0deg13'-0deg55' S. and 127deg22'-128deg E. The island has an area of 914 sq. m. and is accompanied by several subordinate islands, including Tawali and Mandioli. Geographically, Bachian lies to the west of the southern peninsula of Jilolo (also known as Halmahera).

## Physical Geography and Natural Resources
The island is irregular in form and consists of two distinct mountainous regions connected by a low isthmus; it is noted that a slight subsidence would be sufficient to submerge this connecting land. The geology of Bachian is partly of volcanic formation, a fact indicated by the presence of hot springs. However, the southern portion of the island contains ancient rocks that are non-volcanic. The highest point on the island is the mountain of Labua, which reaches an elevation of 6,950 ft.

The island's natural resources include discovered minerals and coal. Much of the land is richly wooded, producing an abundance of cocoa-nuts, sago, and cloves, the latter of which are indigenous to the area. Bachian is also noted for its richness in insects and birds. Furthermore, it is significant as the most eastern point on the globe inhabited by the *Quadrumana*, a black ape also found in Celebes.

## Population and Settlement
The interior of Bachian is uninhabited. The coastal populations are not indigenous and are composed of a diverse mix of people, including:
* Malays
* The Sirani (Christian descendants of the Portuguese)
* Immigrants from Celebes
* Galela men from northern Halmahera
* Arabs and Chinese
* A Papuan element

The total population of the island is approximately 13,000 inhabitants. The primary village is called Bachian, though the inhabitants refer to it as Amasing; this village is located on the west side of the isthmus.

## Governance and Economy
Historically, Bachian was the most important island in a group governed by a sultan. This changed in 1889, when governance shifted to a committee of chiefs operating under the control of a Dutch *controleur*.

Economic exploitation of the island was attempted by a Batjan company starting in 1882. However, these efforts were unsuccessful due to a lack of laborers and a deficient understanding of the capabilities and nature of the soil.
