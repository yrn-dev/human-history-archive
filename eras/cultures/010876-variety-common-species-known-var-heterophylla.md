# Fraxinus and Related Botanical Varieties

The genus *Fraxinus*, commonly known as ash, encompasses a variety of species and cultivars distributed across Europe, Asia, and the Americas. These trees are distinguished by various morphological characteristics, including leaf structure, branch orientation, and stem texture.

## Varieties of the Common Ash
Within the common species of ash, several distinct varieties exist. The variety known as *var. heterophylla* is characterized by having simple leaves and occurs wild in the woods of England and Europe. Another variety, *pendula*, is noted for its weeping and pendulous branches. To create a pleasing aesthetic effect, the *pendula* variety is sometimes grafted onto the tall stem of the common ash. This weeping variety is said to have been first observed in Cambridgeshire, specifically at Gamlingay. Other variations include *crispa*, which possesses curled leaves, and *verrucosa*, which is identified by its warty branches and stems.

## Global Species of Fraxinus
Beyond the common ash and its varieties, the genus includes several other species native to different regions:
*   **Europe:** *F. ornus*, known as the manna ash, is a handsome tree native to south Europe featuring greenish-white flowers. Southern Europe is also home to *Fraxinus parvifolia*, a small-leaved ash.
*   **Asia:** *F. floribunda* is a large tree native to the Himalayas, characterized by white flowers in terminal panicles.
*   **America:** Several species are native to the Americas, including the white ash (*Fraxinus americana*), the red ash (*F. pubescens*), and the black ash (*F. sambucifolia*).

## Other Temperate Tree Genera
The archive contains records of other temperate forest trees, such as those in the genus *Fagus* (beech). This genus is widely spread across temperate regions and contains approximately 15 species in addition to the native beech. *Fagus americana* is widely distributed in the forests of eastern North America and is distinguished from *F. sylvatica* by its lighter green, more sharply-toothed leaves and paler bark. In Japan, *F. sylvatica var. Sieboldi* is one of the most abundant deciduous-leaved forest trees. 

Southern Chile, Patagonia, and the shores of the Straits of Magellan also host beech forests. These consist largely of two species: *Fagus betuloides*, which is evergreen, and *F. antarctica*, which has deciduous leaves. Other species of *Fagus* are found in New Zealand and Australia.
