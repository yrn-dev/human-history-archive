# The Hot and Dry Desert Tracts of Asia

The extremely dry and hot tracts of Asia form an almost unbroken desert region that extends from Arabia, through south Persia and Baluchistan, and into Sind. This geographical area is characterized by a considerable uniformity in its types of life, which closely resemble the life forms found in the neighboring hot and dry regions of Africa.

## Zoological and Botanical Classifications
The zoological provinces of Asia correspond very closely to its botanical divisions. The extreme south-west part of the continent—which includes Arabia, Palestine, and southern Persia, and reaches as far as Baluchistan and Sind—constitutes a separate zoological district. According to the primary divisions of the earth established by Dr. Sclater, this area belongs to the Ethiopian region, which also extends over Africa south of the Atlas.

The flora and fauna of these regions are adapted to the specific climatic conditions. In the hot and dry countries of western Asia and Arabia, the horse is produced in the highest perfection.

## Related Arid Regions of Central Asia
Beyond the south-western desert tract, other areas of Asia exhibit extreme dryness and heat. Central Asia—comprising Mongolia, Turkestan, western Manchuria, and the area between the southern border of Siberia and the margin of the temperate alpine zone of north China and the Himalaya—is an almost rainless region. This area is marked by intense summer heat and winters of extreme severity.

The vegetation in the dry region of central Asia is notable for a high relative number of Polygonaceae, including frequent leafless forms, and Chenopodiaceae, with *Salicornia* and other salt plants being common. Peculiar forms of Leguminosae also prevail. These plant types, along with others from the colder desert tracts of Afghanistan and Persia or the drier regions of southern Siberia, extend into Tibet. In Tibet, the hot, nearly vertical sun and extreme drought create a summer climate similar to that of the plains of central Asia.

## Regional Variations in Dry Climates
Other specific zones within Asia exhibit distinct "dry" characteristics:

*   **Burma:** The "dry zone of Burma" consists of undulating lowlands between the western fringe of the Southern Shan States and the Arakan Yomas. It stretches along both sides of the Irrawaddy from Thayetmyo to the north of Mandalay, encompassing districts such as Mandalay, Sagaing, Shwebo, Meiktila, Magwe, Pakôkku, Minbu, Kyauksè, Yamèthin, and Myingyan.
*   **Bokhara:** In the lowlands of Bokhara, a very hot summer is followed by a short, cold winter where the Oxus may freeze for a fortnight and frosts may reach -20 deg. Fahr. The lowlands are also subject to terrible sand-storms from the west that can kill cotton trees.
*   **Bellary:** In south India, the district of Bellary is characterized by extreme dryness because air passes over a great extent of heated plains. It has a smaller rainfall than any other district in south India. The area consists primarily of an extensive plateau between the Western and Eastern Ghats.

## General Principles of Dry Phases
The relationship between geography and climate suggests that periods of high mountains and deep seas result in a larger dry land surface for the world. High mountains precipitate moisture from the atmosphere and hold it as glaciers and snow, which restricts surface evaporation from smaller oceans. 

Because the sun's heat passes more freely through dry air than moist air, a greater amount of heat reaches land surfaces during these dry phases. Consequently, dry phases in earth's history are characterized by hot days; however, they also result in cold nights because heat is radiated away as abundantly as it arrived.
