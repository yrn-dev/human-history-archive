# The Rainless Region from Africa to the Indus

## Geographic Extent and Flora
The rainless region of south-western Asia is characterized by a continuity of flora that links it with the desert flora of northern and eastern Africa. This biological zone extends from the coast of Senegal to the 75th meridian East, stretching from the great African desert to the border of the rainless tract along the Indus and the southern portions of the Punjab. This vast area encompasses the peninsula of Arabia, the shores of the Persian Gulf, south Persia, Afghanistan, and Baluchistan. Its westernmost limit reaches the Cape Verde Islands, and it is partially represented in Abyssinia.

## Regional Characteristics and Climate
The tracts from Arabia, through Baluchistan and south Persia to Sind, are extremely hot and dry, forming an almost unbroken desert. These regions exhibit a considerable uniformity in their types of life, which closely resemble those found in the neighboring hot and dry regions of Africa. 

In contrast, other adjacent zones present different climatic profiles. The region comprising southern China, the Indo-Chinese peninsula, India, and the western Malay Archipelago is defined by high temperatures and heavy periodical summer rains. Further north, the area between the southern border of Siberia and the margins of north China and the temperate alpine zone of the Himalaya—including Mongolia, Turkestan, and central Asia—is almost rainless. This northern region experiences summers of intense heat and winters of extreme severity, with flora and fauna more closely allied to the northern Siberian tract.

## The Indus Borderlands and Sind
The borderlands along the Indus river feature diverse terrains. In the District of Dera Ismail Khan, the country to the east of the river's present bed includes a wide tract known as the *Kachi*, beyond which lies a sparsely cultivated, barren, almost desert plain inhabited by nomadic herdsmen. To the west of the Indus, the characteristics resemble those of Dera Ghazi Khan.

The District of Dera Ghazi Khan consists of a long, narrow strip of country sloping from western hills to the Indus. While the eastern portion benefits from Indus floods, a barren tract exists between the hills and the river. This area is subject to scanty rainfall and extreme temperatures. Further south, in the Sind province of Bombay, the native state of Khairpur consists of a great alluvial plain that is rich and fertile near irrigation canals and the Indus; however, the remaining area is a continuous series of sand-hill ridges covered in stunted brushwood where cultivation is impossible. These desert tracts in Khairpur contain pits of *natron*.

## Persian and Afghan Plateaus
The portion of Asia west of British India (excluding Syria and Arabia) forms an extensive plateau. Its eastern flank follows a line of hills a short distance from the Indus between the Himalaya and the river's mouth. This high region is occupied by Baluchistan, Afghanistan, Armenia, Asia Minor, and Iran (Persia).

In the Persian province of Kerman, the landscape is divided between mountainous and desert regions. The true desert of Kerman is located mainly in the north and north-east, where it merges into the great "Lut" desert stretching into Khorasan. These southern deserts are sandier, drier, and less saline than the *kavir* of central Persia. Despite the almost rainless summer conditions, large tracts are kept under cultivation via springs and canals fed by snow on the mountain ranges, such as the Kuh Hazar and Jamal Bariz.

## Demographics and Culture of the Region
The population and linguistic landscape vary significantly across this tract. West of the Sutlej river, the population is described as wholly Mahommedan, with the exception of small areas in Syria and Asia Minor where Christians predominate. 

Linguistically, the dialects west of the Indus approach Persian. This language meets Arabic and Turki along the Caspian, the Turkoman desert, and west of the Tigris. Throughout this region, the characters used for writing are common to Arabic, Turkish, and Persian, and are written from right to left. In the borderlands of the Indus, such as Dera Ismail Khan and Dera Ghazi Khan, the population is chiefly composed of Baluch and Pathan Mahommedans.
