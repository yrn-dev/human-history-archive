# Mahommedans

The term Mahommedan refers to followers of the Mahommedan law and creed, a group associated with the Moslem world. Their history, religious divisions, and chronological systems are documented across various archival records.

## Sects and Geography
The Mahommedan faith is divided into different sects with distinct doctrinal adherence and geographic distributions. In the region of Badakshan proper, the population consists of Arabs, Turks, and Tajiks. These groups speak the Turki and Persian languages and adhere to the Sunnite sect, which professes the orthodox doctrines of the Mahommedan law. In contrast, the mountainous districts of the same region are inhabited by Tajiks who speak various distinct dialects and profess the Shi'ite creed.

## Chronology and the Hegira
The Moslem world utilizes a specific era for dating, which begins with the Hegira (also spelled Hijra). This event is defined as the flight from Mecca, through the desert, to Medina (also spelt Măkka and Mădina). 

The Moslem year, denoted as A.H. (the Moslem reckoning), is based on a lunar calendar consisting of twelve months totaling 354 days. Because this is eleven days shorter than the year used in Western chronology, the A.H. reckoning gains one year on A.D. approximately every 33 years. For example, A.D. 1920 corresponds to A.H. 1338 until September 15, at which point A.H. 1339 begins. It is noted that A.D. 20,526 and A.H. 20,526 will be partly coincident.
