# The Kikuyu-Kamba Group of British East Africa

The Kikuyu-Kamba group is a linguistic and ethnic classification located in British East Africa, situated to the east of the Rift Valley. This group primarily consists of the Wakikuyu and Wakamba tribes, who are representatives of the Bantu division of the negro races.

## Linguistic Composition
The group is defined by its speech patterns, which include the special dialects of Kikuyu and Ukambani. In addition to these primary dialects, the group encompasses all scattered fragments of Bantu speech found on the upper Tana river (referred to as Dhaicho) and on Mount Kenya.

## Geography and Topography
The region occupied by this group is characterized by high-altitude uplands and volcanic activity. The land rises from the coast in a series of steps, eventually reaching a broad zone of high ground. The uplands in this area are largely composed of lava, with general altitudes varying between 5,000 and 8,000 feet.

The landscape is a mixture of forest and cultivated ground, with the latter being especially prevalent in Kikuyu. This zone contains some of the highest elevations in British East Africa, including:
*   **Mount Kenya**: A volcanic pile reaching 17,007 feet.
*   **Sattima**: Reaching 13,214 feet.
*   **Nandarua**: Approximately 12,900 feet.
*   **The Sattima (Settima) range**: Also known as the Aberdare Mountains, which maintain a general elevation of fully 10,000 feet.

To the west, the region is bounded by the eastern wall of the rift-valley. This transition is marked by a line of cliffs, most notably the Kikuyu escarpment, which stands at 8,000 feet and is located just south of 1° S.

## Geology
The geological makeup of the area is complex. While the coast plain consists of recent rocks and the foot plateau is composed of sedimentary rocks from the Trias to Jurassic periods, the region extending to the borders of Kikuyu is an ancient plateau. This area is composed of ancient crystalline rocks. Over time, immense quantities of volcanic rocks from the post-Jurassic to Recent periods have accumulated here, creating the volcanic plateau of Central East Africa.

## Population and Settlement
The inhabitants of the Kikuyu-Kamba region are diverse. The Bantu population is represented chiefly by the Wakikuyu and Wakamba. In addition to these indigenous groups, the Kikuyu uplands are a primary location for the white population, who are mostly agriculturists. There are also numbers of Indian settlers residing in these same districts.

The region serves as a borderland between Hamitic and Negro peoples. Neighboring the eastern rift-valley uplands are the Masai, a Hamitic race of cattle-rearers who speak a Nilotic language. A specific branch of the Masai, known as the Wakuafi, has transitioned from nomadic life to become settled agriculturists.
