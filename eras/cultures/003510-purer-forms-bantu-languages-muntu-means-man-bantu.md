# Bantu Languages

The term Bantu (literally *Ba-ntu*) is the most widely spread and archaic designation for "people," "mankind," or "men" within this group of languages. While the term has faced criticism, it remains the simplest and most consecrated designation for this section of Negro languages.

## Linguistic Structure and Morphology
The leading feature of Bantu tongues is the governing of an unchangeable root by prefixes. The syllable *-ntu* does not stand alone in modern usage but originally signified "person" or "object," and is occasionally used as a relative pronoun meaning "he who," "that," or "that which." By combining this root with different prefixes, various meanings are created. In the purer forms of these languages, *muntu* means "a man," *bantu* means "men," *kintu* means "a thing," *bintu* means "things," and *kantu* means "a little thing."

The Bantu verb also consists of a practically unchangeable root, which serves as the second person singular of the imperative. This root is modified by prefixed and suffixed particles, which are often reduced prepositions, adverbs, or worn-down verbs acting as auxiliaries. While the building of moods and tenses likely occurred independently across principal groups based on a common fundamental system, some features are shared. For example, the preterite tense in many languages uses a form like *-ile* or *-ili* (connected to the root for "to be" or "exist"), as seen in the Zulu word *tanda* (love) becoming *tandile* (have loved).

In most Bantu verbs, the terminal vowel is almost invariably *a*. Departures from this rule are rare and typically result from borrowed words from English, Portuguese, or Arabic. However, changing this terminal vowel can alter the tense—such as changing *a* to *e* for the subjunctive or to *i* for a negative sense—or transform a verb into a noun. In Luganda, the root *senyua* (to pardon) becomes *ki-senyuo* (a pardon).

## Gender and Concord
Bantu languages do not recognize sexual gender in their concord or pronouns. Gender is instead indicated through specific prefixes or suffixes. A feminine prefix may be *na* or *nya* (connected to the root for "mother"), or a suffix such as *kazi* or *ka* (indicating "female" or "wife"). A male prefix, often identical to the word for "father," is used for males. The first and second prefixes are usually restricted to humanity and invariably indicate living beings.

## Geographical Distribution and Classification
The domain of Bantu speech is broadly divided into four great sections:
1. The languages of the East Coast and Great Lakes, extending down to the Zambezi basin.
2. The South-Central group, including Bechuana-Zulu.
3. The South-West group, ranging from the Angola-Congo coast and Damaraland to the southern Belgian Congo.
4. The Western group, encompassing Cameroon and Central and Northern Congo languages.

Within these regions, various groups are identified. The *Bangala-Bobangi-Liboko* group comprises commercial languages of the Upper Congo, extending as far north as 3deg 30' N. The *Bateke* (*Batio*) group includes dialects west of the Sanga river and east of the Atlantic coast-lands. In the Great Lakes region, the *Tanganyika* languages are spoken near Lake Albert Edward and the Lukuga outlet, while the *Manyema* (*Baenya*) group includes corrupt dialects between the main stream of the Luapula-Congo and the western watershed of Tanganyika.

Notably, the Congo between Stanley Falls and the Rubi river lies outside the Bantu field; languages such as *Wamanga* and *Bondonga* are not Bantu.

## Origins and Affinities
The creation of the pronominal-prefix type of Bantu language predates the exodus from the Bantu mother-land. The exact birthplace and the specific group of dialects from which it sprang remain a puzzle. There are some affinities with other languages; for instance, Fula shows resemblance in grammatical construction (though it uses suffixes instead of prefixes) and shares similarities in the numerals 3 and 4. Distinct affinities in the numerals 2, 3, 4, and sometimes 5 exist between Bantu and languages of the Gold Coast, lower Niger, Benue river, and N. Togoland. However, there is absolutely no resemblance in word-roots between Bantu and the prefix-using languages of the north Guinea coast, such as the Timne.

## Archaic Forms and Dialects
The most archaic dialects currently existing are found in Uganda, Unyoro, Mount Elgon, Ruwenzori, the north coast of Tanganyika, and the Bemba country. Other archaic types include the Subiya speech of the western Zambezi, Ci-bodzo of the Zambezi delta, and Oci-herero of Damaraland. While Zulu-Kaffir was once considered the most archaic type, it has been deposed by the languages of the Great Lakes, as it exhibits recent deviations in phonetics and vocabulary.

The homogeneity of the Bantu family is striking compared to the linguistic confusion found in Africa north of the Bantu borderland. This is illustrated by the word for "fowl," which appears as *enkoko* in Luganda, *kuku* in Ki-Swahili, *inkuku* in Zulu, and *nkuku* on the Zambezi.
