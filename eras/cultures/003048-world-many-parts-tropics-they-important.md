# The Tropics: Flora, Fauna, and Regional Characteristics

The tropical regions of the world are characterized by diverse biological assemblages and critical agricultural productions. These areas, spanning the Old World and the New World, support a wide array of plant and animal life adapted to hot, damp, or arid conditions, and serve as vital sources of food and industrial materials for their inhabitants.

## Tropical Flora and Agriculture

In many tropical regions, certain plants hold a level of importance for local inhabitants comparable to that of grain plants in cooler climates. A primary example is the genus *Musa*, which contains approximately 40 species distributed throughout the Old World tropics and introduced into the New World. These plants thrive in hot, damp climates, with cultivation limits reaching Florida (south of 29° lat.), Egypt, the Canary Islands, and south Japan in the north, and south Brazil and Natal in the south.

The genus *Musa* includes several varieties:
*   ***Musa paradisiaca***: The source of most generally used fruits. Its sub-species *sapientum* provides the fruits known in England as bananas (eaten raw), while the species itself produces plantains, which require cooking.
*   ***Musa acuminata***: Used as fruit in the Malay Archipelago.
*   ***Musa Fehi***: Used as fruit in Tahiti.
*   ***Musa Cavendishii***: Known as the Chinese banana, found in cooler countries, featuring a fragrant flesh and thinner rind.
*   ***Musa corniculata***: Found in the Malay Archipelago and Cochin China; a single fruit from this species can provide a meal for three men.

In the Indian region, agriculture combines harvests from both temperate and tropical zones. Cultivated plants include rice, maize, various millets (*Sorghum, Penicillaria, Panicum, Eleusine*), coffee, tea, sugar, indigo, cotton, and plantains. Palms such as *Cocos, Phoenix,* and *Borassus* provide toddy and cocoa-nuts. In southern India and similar Asian islands, the climate does not permit the proper cultivation of barley or wheat.

In the tropical and sub-tropical zones of Bolivia, indigenous plants include cassava (*Manihot utilissima* and *M. aipi*), sweet potato, pineapple, guava, pawpaw (*Carica papaya*), peanuts, and chirimoya (*Anona cherimolia*). The region also produces rubber-producing trees, dyewoods, and hardwoods such as mahogany, rosewood, cedar, and jacaranda.

## Specialized Tropical Adaptations

Certain plants have evolved to survive the hot, dry regions of tropical America. The Cactaceae family, which belongs almost entirely to the New World, features species like *Opuntia* and *Cereus*. These plants withstand aridity through thick skins and a paucity of evaporating pores (stomata), allowing their fleshy stems to store water. In Spanish America, *Opuntias* are planted around houses to serve as impenetrable fences, and their succulent fruits are used as cooling drinks during fevers.

## Tropical Fauna

The distribution of animals in the tropics varies by region and species:

**Avian and Terrestrial Life**: The bird population of the Indian region is one of the world's richest, surpassed only by that of tropical America. It features peculiar genera such as *Buceros* and *Pajo*, and the family *Eurylaemidae* is entirely confined to this part of Asia. In Africa, the fauna south of the Sahara is characterized by a gradual disappearance of specifically tropical forms as one moves toward the southern extremity.

**Marine Life**: Pelagic fishes, including Teleosteans and the largest Selachians (such as sword-fishes, scombrids, and sharks), are most numerous between the tropics. These rapid swimmers often pass between oceans and occasionally wander far beyond their habitual tropical range.

**Livestock**: In tropical climates, the buffalo is an invaluable milk-yielder, particularly amid jungles and swamps.

## Regional Geography and Infrastructure

The tropical regions are divided into distinct biological and political zones:
*   **The Indian Region**: Spanning from the 65th to the 105th meridian, this area includes India and the Malayan peninsula. Sir J.D. Hooker estimated the indigenous plant species here at 12,000 to 15,000, with Orchidaceae being universally present in large proportions.
*   **The African Region**: This encompasses Africa south of the Sahara. In the north-east, the fauna penetrates the isthmus of Suez into Syria.
*   **The Neotropical Region**: This refers to Tropical America.
*   **The Tropical Pacific Region**: This includes New Guinea, Australia (excluding the south-eastern portion), and islands east of Wallace's line up to the Sandwich group.

In tropical Africa, industrial and political development is focused on establishing communication from South Africa to the Mediterranean. Proposed railway routes involve connections between the Congo, Lake Albert, Lake Tanganyika, and Lake Victoria Nyanza, utilizing locations such as Stanleyville, Elizabethville, and Tabora. These industrial developments are considered central to future world politics.
