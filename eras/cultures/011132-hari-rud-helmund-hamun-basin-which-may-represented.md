# Hari Rud and the Helmund Hamun Basin

The Hari Rud and the Helmund hamun basin are significant geographical features of Central Asia, playing a critical role in the region's hydrography, political boundaries, and the development of transportation networks.

## The Hari Rud River
The Hari Rud, also known as the river of Herat, serves as a primary geographical marker in the north-west of Afghanistan. It defines the western boundary of the district of Badghis—a region known as the "home of the winds"—which lies between the Hari Rud and the Murghab rivers and extends north to the edge of the Sarakhs desert.

Politically, the Hari Rud has been central to the demarcation of international borders. The Russo-Afghan Boundary Commission of 1884-1886 established a boundary between the Hari Rud and the Oxus. This boundary is described as almost entirely artificial in its construction. It leaves the Hari Rud at Zulfikar, located approximately 70 miles south of Sarakhs, and reaches its most southerly point where it crosses the Kushk, about 60 miles north of Herat.

In the region of Khorasan, the Hari Rud is a recipient of the Kashaf-rud (or Tortoise River), which flows east from the Hazar Masjed range. The junction of these two rivers forms the Tejen, which flows toward the Caspian but disappears into the desert before reaching it.

## The Helmund Hamun Basin
The Helmund hamuns are characterized as typical relics of an earlier geological period, occurring when land areas recently upheaved from the sea were spread at low levels with alternating inundations of fresh and salt water. These plateau hamuns, similar to those found in Persia and south-west Baluchistan, are subject to seasonal changes in form and extent. Many are impregnated with saline deposits originating from the underlying strata.

The basin is part of a broader central section of depression in the continent where drainage is lost in swamps or hamuns, with an average level likely not exceeding 2,000 feet above sea level.

## Geographic Significance and Connectivity
The valley of the Hari Rud is of great significance due to its role as a natural corridor. When dividing the continent into northern, central, and southern sections, the narrow valley of the Hari Rud to the west of Herat provides the only direct connection between the northern and southern sections that does not involve formidable barriers of altitude or mountain passes.

The only significant orographic obstacle between the Caspian and Karachi is the divide separating the valley of the Hari Rud from the Helmund hamun basin. This divide is represented by an altitude of approximately 4,000 feet above sea level. Because of this unique geography, the area is considered highly significant for the development of Asiatic railways.

## Regional Boundaries and Access
The boundary between Afghanistan and Baluchistan involves several salt swamps and water-divides. Starting from Nushki, the boundary cuts across the Lora hamun and runs through the desert south of the Gaud-i-Zirreh salt swamps. This boundary passes 50 miles south of the Helmund, effectively shutting off the Helmund valley and the approach to Seistan from Baluchistan, except during seasons of flood when the area between the Helmund and the Gaud-i-Zirreh provides the only approach from the east.

However, a connected desert route remains open in ordinary seasons between Nushki and Seistan. This route has been developed with the digging of wells and the establishment of serais at intervals. This "new Helmund desert route" has facilitated trade between Quetta and Seistan, supported by the extension of the railway from Quetta to Nushki and the acquisition of administration rights for the Nushki district by Great Britain.
