# Aridity and Extreme Heat in Global Regions

The interaction of extreme heat, dryness, and sandy soil creates distinct environmental conditions across various global regions, influencing geography, climate, and biological adaptation. These conditions range from the sultry deserts of Asia to the arid plateaus of South America and Africa.

## Regional Climates of Asia

In the presidency containing Sind, there are great varieties of climate. Upper Sind is characterized by extreme heat and dryness combined with the aridity of sandy soil, which makes the region resemble the sultry deserts of Africa. In this area, thermometers have been known to register 130 deg. in the shade. Lower Sind is also hot; at Hyderabad, the mean maximum temperature during the six hottest months is 98 deg. F. in the shade, and the waters of the Indus reach blood heat.

Other regions in the area exhibit varying degrees of heat. While the heat in Gujarat and Cutch is less than in Sind, it remains very great. In contrast, the Konkan is hot and moist, with monsoon rains sometimes approaching 300 in. The southern Mahratta country and the table-land of the Deccan above the Ghats generally have an agreeable climate, except during the hot months. In detached heights such as Singarh and Mahabaleshwar, Europeans can go out at all hours with impunity. Bombay Island is typically cooled by sea breezes, though it becomes oppressively hot in October and May.

In the District of Dera Ghazi Khan, which covers 5,306 sq. m., the country is high and arid below the western hills, often featuring sandy undulations. This district is subject to very scanty rainfall and great extremes of temperature.

## Aridity in South India and Brazil

The district of Bellary in south India is characterized by extreme dryness. This is caused by air passing over a great extent of heated plains. Consequently, Bellary has a smaller rainfall than any other district in south India, with average daily thermometer variations between 67 deg. and 83 deg. F. The region is prone to famines resulting from series of bad seasons, with memorable occurrences in 1751, 1793, 1803, 1833, 1854, 1866, 1877, and 1896.

In Brazil, the Atlantic state of Bahia contains a great part of arid, barren *chapada* (plateau). The interior is hot and dry, while the coast is hot and humid. Within Bahia, there are two plateaus—one gneissose and one limestone—that are arid, barren, and inhospitable, except where ridges capture moisture from sea clouds. A third plateau is covered with compact sandy soil and loses its arid character as it nears the coast. In the interior of Bahia, the cattle-raising industry was nearly extinguished by increasing aridity and devastating droughts, exacerbated by the practice of annually burning the *campos* to improve grass.

## Tropical and Desert Variations

In British East Africa, climate and vegetation are arranged in zones parallel to the coast. The coast is hot and dry due to the dryness of the soil and constant breezes from the Indian Ocean. Moving inland, the outer plateaus and plains become more arid. In contrast, the highlands (above 5,000 ft.) are very healthy, with average temperatures between 66° F. in the cool season and 73° F. in the hot season.

Other examples of extreme dryness are found in the *Wadys* of Arabia, which exhibit an alternation between absolute dryness and extreme temporary violence and fullness.

## Atmospheric and Global Perspectives

The perception of heat is often influenced by humidity. In certain regions, when the *sherki* (south wind) blows during June, July, and August, temperatures can reach 122deg F. by two o'clock. However, because of a constant breeze (usually from the N.W.) and the extreme dryness of the atmosphere, this heat is felt much less than a lower temperature would be in a more humid atmosphere.

On a broader scale, the climatic history of the world is marked by slow fluctuations between heat and cold rather than a simple cooling process. There have been periods of great dryness and great wetness throughout the earth. Living species constantly adapt to these changing conditions, though there is no finality in this adaptation.

## Application in Botany

Sandy soil and specific temperature controls are utilized in the cultivation of *Cordyline* (often called *Dracaenas*). These plants can be increased by cuttings planted in sandy soil at night temperatures of 65 deg. to 70 deg. Established plants thrive in fibry peat made porous by sand, requiring a day temperature of 75 deg. in summer and 65 deg. in winter.
