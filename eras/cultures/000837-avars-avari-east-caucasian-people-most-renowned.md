# AVARS

The term "Avars" (or "Avari") refers to two distinct groups in historical records: an East Caucasian people inhabiting the Caucasus region and a nomadic empire that once existed on the Danube.

## The East Caucasian Avars
The Avars are an East Caucasian people and are the most renowned of the Lesghian tribes. They inhabit central Daghestan. Among the Lesghian tribes, the Avars are unique in that they possess a written language, for which they utilize Arabic characters. 

Regarding their origins, Komarov asserts that they are a tribe of more recent origin. According to this view, their name is derived from Lowland Turki, where it means "refugee" or "vagrant."

## The Avar Empire of the Danube
A separate group of Avars, described as a Hunnish or mixed people, migrated from the east into Hungary approximately one hundred years after the era of Attila's Huns. This group established an empire on the Danube. During the seventh and eighth centuries, the Avars conducted raids to the west and south from the Hungarian plain.

The Avar empire expanded to include the Slovenes (also known as Corutanes), a Slavonic people who had settled the district around 590 and were part of the kingdom of Samo. While the Franks claimed some authority over the Slovenes, such claims were not considered serious as long as the Avars maintained possession of the land.

## Conflict with the Franks and Decline
The Avars were characterized as pagans, practicing a religion similar to the primitive religion of the Greeks, where gods were viewed as human-like "school prefects" of more powerful beings.

The decline of the Avar empire was precipitated by the Franks. In 791, Charlemagne, having established authority over the Bavarians (*Bajuvarii*), crossed the river Enns to campaign against the Avars. Following this initial attack and subsequent campaigns by his lieutenants, the Avars were finally subdued in 805. Their lands were then incorporated into the Frankish empire. To defend the eastern border of his empire during this struggle, Charlemagne established a mark known as the East Mark, which was ruled by a series of margraves from 799 to 907.

## Migrations and Related Groups
The movement of the Avars toward the west was precipitated by pressure from the Turks in Asia. The conquering Turks followed the Avars' footsteps between 560 and 580. 

In the region of Hungary, the Avars were eventually massacred or assimilated by the Magyars (modern Hungarians), a Turko-Finnish people who settled the area around 900. The Magyars followed a similar pattern of assimilation that had previously occurred with the Huns and mixed Scythians.
