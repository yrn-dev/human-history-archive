# Santalum and Bamboo of India

## Santalum (Sandalwood)
*Santalum*, commonly known as sandalwood, is a tree native to southern India. Within the context of India's economically useful timber, it is noted alongside other species such as teak, sal, babool, toon, and sissoo. Specifically, *Santalum album* is identified as a native of Malaya and Malabar. In the Hebrew Scriptures, the wood known as algum or almug is generally identified with this species.

## Bamboo: Botanical Characteristics
Bamboo is the popular name for a tribe of grasses known as *Bambuseae*. These plants are often tree-like with woody stems that spring from an underground root-stock, frequently forming dense clumps. The largest species can reach heights of 120 feet. The stems are slender and hollow, featuring well-marked nodes or joints where the cavity is closed by a strong diaphragm. 

The leaves are narrow, often short, and usually jointed at the base with a short stalk attached to a long sheath. Branches are numerous and may be spiny in some species. The flowers differ from most grasses by typically possessing six stamens and three lodicules; the spikelets are arranged in panicles or racemes and are usually many-flowered. While some species bloom annually, others bloom at intervals of many years across large areas. For example, on the west coast of India, the simultaneous blooming of *Bambusa arundinacea*, one of the largest species, has been observed every thirty-two years. Following the ripening of the seed, the leafless flowering culms die.

## Distribution and Environmental Context
Many sorts of bamboo are found in all parts of India. In the lieutenant-governorship of Bengal—specifically in the river valleys of Bengal proper, Behar, and Orissa—the "feathery bamboo" is one of the vegetable products that abounds in the fertile land. In the forests of the Indian peninsula and the west coasts of Burma, bamboo is found growing mixed with other trees, including teak.

The prevalence of bamboo is such that it has influenced local nomenclature; the town of Belgaum was anciently named Venugrama, a name said to be derived from the bamboos characteristic of the surrounding neighborhood.

## Utility and Applications
The stem of the bamboo is applied to a vast variety of uses, serving as a primary material for construction, transport, and domestic tools.

### Construction and Shipping
Bamboo is used extensively as a timber wood. Entire houses may be constructed from it:
* **Structural Elements:** Complete sections of the stem serve as columns or posts. Split bamboo is used for rafters, floors, and laths or planks.
* **Walls and Roofing:** Interwoven lattice-work is used for the sides of rooms to allow for air and light. Roofs may be made solely of bamboo.
* **Shipping:** Bamboo is used in all types of shipping. Strong plants are selected for the masts of moderate-sized boats, while larger vessels may use masts formed by joining several bamboos together.

### Domestic and Specialized Tools
The versatility of the plant extends to numerous household and professional items:
* **Containers:** Large joints are used as water buckets, while smaller ones serve as bottles. Among the Dyaks of Borneo, they are used as cooking vessels. Other uses include oil-cans, water-stoups, and pawn-boxes.
* **Equipment:** Bamboo is used for scaffolding, ladders, landing-jetties, irrigation scoops and wheels, oars, yards, and fishing apparatus.
* **Miscellaneous:** It is used to create hats, helmets, bows, quivers, pipe-sticks, dinner-trays, mats, paper, and musical instruments.

## Food and Consumption
The grains of the bamboo are available for food. In China, there is a proverb suggesting that bamboo produces seed more abundantly when rice crops fail, indicating its role as a food source during times of dearth. In India, Hindus consume bamboo grains mixed with honey as a delicacy; equal parts of the two are placed in a hollow joint, coated in clay, and roasted over a fire. Additionally, the fleshly fruit of *Melocanna*, a plant native to India (and sometimes cultivated in Mauritius), is baked and eaten.
