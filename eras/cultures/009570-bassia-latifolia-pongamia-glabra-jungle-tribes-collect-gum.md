# Bassia latifolia, Pongamia glabra, and the Collection of Gum

## Botanical Resources of the Bombay Presidency
The Bombay Presidency contains two primary categories of forests: those located in the alluvial plains and those found in the hills. The hill forests are distributed over a wide area, extending from 14 to 23 degrees North latitude, with the majority situated within the Western Ghats or the Sahyadri hills. The alluvial forests are located in Sind, extending over 550 square miles on or near the banks of the Indus.

Within these forests, several trees are considered valuable specifically for their berries, nuts, or fruits, regardless of the quality of their timber. Among these species are *Bassia latifolia* and *Pongamia glabra*, as well as the jack (*Artocarpus integrifolia*), mango (*Mangifera indica*), *Aegle Marmelos*, *Zizypkus Jujuba*, *Terminalia Chebula*, and *Calophyllum Inophyllum*.

## Gum Collection and Forest Products
In the Bombay Presidency, jungle tribes collect gum from various types of trees. Additionally, in the region of Sind, the Forest Department generates a small amount of revenue from lac. 

Beyond the Bombay Presidency, gum is a significant resource in other global regions. In Kordofan, the chief wealth of the population consists of oxen, ostrich feathers, camels, and gum obtained from grey acacias. The grey gum acacia, known as *hashob*, is the primary constituent of the low scrub in the northern part of the country, while red gum acacias, known as *talh*, are abundant in the south. In this region, the Gowama is the most important village tribe, as they own most of the country that produces gum.

In French Indo-China, specifically in the town of Luang-Prabang, gum benjamin is one of the exports. This product, along with other jungle products such as wax, sticklac, cutch, and hill rice, is imported from surrounding districts by the hill people, known as the Kha.

## Other Resins and Odoriferous Materials
In Arabia, gum-mastich is one of the substances that grow there, along with cinnamon, cassia, myrrh, and frankincense. The Arabians collect frankincense by burning storax to produce smoke, a method used to drive away small, multicolored winged serpents that guard the trees in great numbers.

In India, the consumption of gum resins, odoriferous resins, seeds, fruits, flowers, dried leaves, roots, and woods is enormous for both religious and social purposes. Gum is used as a component in the production of incense sticks and pastils known as *aggar-ki-buti* ("wood aloes light") or *ud-buti* ("benzoin-light"). These are composed of a mixture including sugar-candy or gum, mastic, sandalwood, benzoin, wood aloes, rock lichen, patchouli, rose-malloes, and *talispat* (the leaf of *Flacourtia Cataphracta*).
