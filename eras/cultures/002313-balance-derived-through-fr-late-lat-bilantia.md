# BALANCE

## Etymology and Definition
The term "balance" is derived from the Late Latin *bilantia*, which referred to an apparatus for weighing. This root is further composed of *bi*, meaning two, and *lanx*, meaning a dish or scale. Originally, the term was used to describe a weighing machine or an ordinary beam balance featuring two scale pans. Over time, the definition has expanded to include various other apparatuses used for the comparison and measurement of forces and weights, whether or not they are qualified by an adjective.

## Types of Balancing Apparatus
Beyond the standard beam balance, the archive identifies several specialized instruments:
*   **Spring Balances:** Used for measuring weight.
*   **Torsion Balances:** These devices measure or compare forces by observing their twisting moment on a wire. Torsion balances are employed particularly in experiments involving electrostatics, magnetism, and gravitation.

## Conceptual Applications of Balance
The term "balance" also carries a connotation of equalization or equality. This concept is applied across several technical fields:

**Bookkeeping**
In the context of accounting, a balance refers to the specific amount required to equalize credit and debit accounts.

**Horology**
In the study of timekeeping, a "balance wheel" is a device used in clocks or watches to equalize the relaxing of the spring. Similarly, a "fusee"—a coned spindle used in the wheel train of spring clocks and watches—is employed to equalize the action of the mainspring.

**Engineering**
The "balancing of engines" is the technical art of minimizing total vibrations while an engine is running. This process generally involves the introduction of masses that induce vibrations which oppose the vibrations produced by the essential parts of the engine.
