# Hydrography and Geography of Southern Baluchistan

The region of Southern Baluchistan is characterized by a complex hydrographic system and a diverse landscape. Located south and south-west of the dividing railway, this area consists of a great block of territory measuring roughly 300 miles by 300 miles. It is primarily the home of the Baluch and the Brahui, though it contains representatives of half the great races of Asia and nearly every conceivable phase of climate.

## Drainage and River Systems
The drainage of Southern Baluchistan generally trends toward the south and west. Water from this region typically follows one of two paths: it either flows into the Arabian Sea or disappears into central inland swamps, such as those of Mashkel and Lora, which are prominent features of south-west Asia.

Several considerable streams serve as important arteries for internal communication by draining into the Arabian Sea:
* **The Hab River:** This river forms the boundary to the west of Karachi.
* **The Purali:** Known anciently as the *Arabus*, this river drains the low-lying flats of Las Bela.
* **The Hingol:** Known anciently as the *Tomerus*, it drains the Makran region.
* **The Dasht:** Also a drainage stream for Makran.

An exception to this general southward and westward trend is the Mulla river. The Mulla carries floods from the Kalat highlands into the Gandava basin, serving as one of the most important ancient highways from the Indus plains to Kandahar.

## The Kalat Plateau and Highlands
The fortress of Kalat is situated approximately 6,800 feet above sea level on the western edge of a cultivated plain. It acts as a "hub" from which the Mulla, the Bolan, and the southern Lora affluents radiate. The region is divided into distinct highland areas:
* **Jalawan:** These are rugged bands of elevated highlands where drainage gathers into channels that cut deep gorges in the hills before passing eastwards into the plains of Sind.
* **Sarawan:** To the north of Kalat, the Sarawan ridges consist of long sweeping lines that enclose narrow fertile valleys, extending south-west toward the edge of the Kharan desert.

Sixty miles south of Kalat, beyond the sources of the Mulla, begins a separate hydrographic system encompassing all of south-western Baluchistan. To the west of this system lies the Kharan desert, characterized by intermittent river channels.

## Las Bela and the Coastal Region
Las Bela is a small independent state located south of Kalat, ruled by the Jam (or Cham) as a protected chief under the British Raj. Its capital, Bela, is situated on a rocky site above the banks of the Purali river. The state covers an area of 6,357 square miles, with a population of 56,109 as of 1901, of whom 54,040 were Mussulmans.

The geography of Las Bela consists of hot, malarial, and low-lying alluvial plains occupying about 6,000 square miles on the north-east corner of the Arabian Sea. These plains are highly fertile and irrigated by the Purali and Kud rivers, which unite to provide water. Historically, the bay of Sonmiani covered most of these plains, though the Purali delta is currently growing.

## Regional Boundaries and Trade Routes
Las Bela is bordered by Sind to the east and Makran to the west. The Mor and Kirthar hill ranges part the plains of Las Bela from Sind; these ranges strike nearly north and south and diminish in height as they approach the sea. This geography allows for a coastal route between Bela and Karachi. To the west, the ridges form a parallel formation that curves from south to west, creating the coast barrier of Makran.

From time immemorial, a great trading route between Persia and Sind has passed through Las Bela. This route, which was one of the greatest trade routes during the medieval days of Arab ascendancy in Sind, traverses barren ridges and the Kolwah valley, providing an open road with an easy gradient to Kej and Persia.
