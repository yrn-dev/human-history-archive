# Erzgebirge (Ore Mountains)

The Erzgebirge, known in Czech as *Rudo Hori*, is a mountain range that forms the north-west frontier of Bohemia. It is characterized by an average altitude of 2,600 ft., reaching its highest point at the Keilberg, which stands at 4,080 ft. Due to the presence of numerous mining villages, easy passes traversed by good roads, and a great number of cultivated areas, the range often possesses the appearance of a hilly undulating plain.

## Geography and Connectivity
The Erzgebirge is part of a broader network of highlands. To the west, it combines with the Fichtelgebirge through the Elstergebirge; the Fichtelgebirge, in turn, connects to the Bohmerwald via the plateau of Waldsassen. To the east, the range is separated from the Elbsandsteingebirge by the Nollendorf pass. This pass serves as an ancient military route to Saxony and was utilized by Napoleon I following the battle of Dresden in 1813.

To the south of the range lies the area described as the "Thermopylae of Bohemia," which was the site of the battle of Arbesau and the battle of Kulm. The village of Kulm, located at the foot of the Erzgebirge approximately 3 miles north-east of Teplitz, was the scene of a battle on August 29 and 30, 1813, where Austrian, Prussian, and Russian forces defeated the French. Further east, the Elbe river exits Bohemia into Saxony at the region's lowest point, with an altitude of 367 ft.

Several villages are situated very close to the summits. One such village, Gottesgab, has a population of approximately 1,500 and sits at an altitude of 3,345 ft., making it the highest place in central Germany and Bohemia.

## Geology and Mineral Resources
The Erzgebirge is noted for its mineral wealth. Tin is extracted at Graupen, which is the only location in Austria where this metal is found. Additionally, the range contains a half-metre coal seam within the Keuper geological formation.

The region has also been significant for scientific discovery. Crystals suitable for goniometric measurement of the mineral enstatite (a magnesium metasilicate) were discovered in a meteorite that fell at Breitenbach in the Erzgebirge.

## Industry and Commerce
The Erzgebirge region supports various industrial activities. A notable home-industry in this area is the production of lace, with its primary center located at Weipert. 

More broadly, the mountains of Bohemia provide the necessary minerals, fuel, and silica required for the glass industry, which was introduced from Venice in the 13th century. The metallurgic industries of the region are generally concentrated around the mines, favored by the abundance of iron and coal.
