# Aye-Aye

The aye-aye, scientifically known as *Chiromys* (or *Daubentonia*) *madagascariensis*, is described as the most aberrant and remarkable of all the Malagasy lemurs. The term "aye-aye" is of uncertain signification and may perhaps be only an exclamation.

## Physical Characteristics
The animal bears a superficial resemblance to a dusky-coloured cat with long hair and unusually large eyes. Its physical structure is characterized by a broad, rounded head and a short face. It possesses large, naked eyes and large hands. The fingers are long and thin with pointed claws; the third finger is particularly notable for its extreme slenderness.

The aye-aye's foot is similar to those of other lemurs, featuring a large opposable great toe with a flat nail, though the remaining toes possess pointed, compressed claws. The animal has a long, bushy tail and a general dark brown coloration. Its outer fur is long and rather loose, complemented by a woolly under-coat. In terms of anatomy, it has two teats located in the inguinal position.

## Discovery and History
The aye-aye was discovered in 1780 by Pierre Sonnerat. The specimen brought to Paris by Sonnerat remained the only known example of the species until 1860. Following this, many more specimens were obtained. One individual lived for several years within the gardens of the Zoological Society of London.

## Habitat and Behavior
The aye-aye is completely nocturnal in its habits. It lives primarily in bamboo forests, existing either alone or in pairs. The animal constructs large, ball-like nests made of dried leaves; these nests are lodged in the fork of the branches of large trees and feature an opening on one side.

## Diet and Feeding Methods
Observations of captive specimens indicate that the aye-aye feeds principally on juices, with a preference for sugar-cane. To access these juices, the animal uses its strong incisor teeth to tear open the hard, woody circumference of the cane stalk. 

Additionally, the aye-aye is said to devour certain species of caterpillars that bore into wood. To obtain this prey, the animal first uses its teeth to cut down upon the burrows of the caterpillars and then uses the claw of its attenuated middle finger to pick them out of their retreat.
