# Norwegian Popular Stories (Norske Folkeeventyr)

*Norwegian Popular Stories*, known in Norwegian as *Norske Folkeeventyr*, is a seminal work of folklore collection produced by the collaboration of Peter Christen Asbjornsen and Jorgen Engebretsen Moe. Upon its initial release, the work was recognized throughout Europe as a significant contribution to both literature and comparative mythology.

## The Collectors
The work was the result of a lifelong partnership between Peter Christen Asbjornsen (1812–1885) and Jorgen Engebretsen Moe (1813–1882). Asbjornsen, born in Christiania on January 15, 1812, came from an ancient family of the Gudbrandsdal. He began collecting and recording legends and fairy stories in 1832 at the age of twenty, later expanding his collection by traveling on foot across Norway. Professionally, Asbjornsen was a zoologist who conducted investigating voyages along the Norwegian coasts, with a particular focus on the Hardanger fjord.

Jorgen Engebretsen Moe was born on April 22, 1813, at Mo i Hole parsonage in Sigdal Ringerike. He first met Asbjornsen at the age of fourteen, beginning a friendship that lasted until their deaths. Moe had independently begun searching for relics of national folklore, a fact Asbjornsen discovered in 1834. Following this discovery, the two friends decided to work in concert, comparing their results and combining their efforts. Moe, who left Christiania University in 1839 to study theology and work as a tutor in Christiania, utilized his holidays to collect stories while wandering through remote mountain districts.

## Publication and Reception
The first installment of *Norwegian Popular Stories (Norske Folkeeventyr)* was published between 1842 and 1843. The success of the first volume was followed by the publication of a second volume in 1844 and a subsequent new collection in 1871. The work gained international attention, and in 1859, Sir George Dasent translated many of the *Folkeeventyr* into English.

While the *Folkeeventyr* was a joint effort, Asbjornsen also published a separate collection of Norwegian fairy tales, titled *huldreeventyr og folkesagn*, in 1845 without the assistance of Moe.
