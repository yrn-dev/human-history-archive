# Geography and Natural History of the Bay of Bengal and the China Sea

The southern boundaries of Asia are defined by a highly irregular coastline featuring the Arabian Sea, the Bay of Bengal, and the China Sea. These waters reach approximately to the northern tropic at the mouths of the Ganges, the Indus, and the Canton river. The region is characterized by significant peninsulas, including Hindostan, Arabia, and Cambodia, which descend to about 10 deg. N., while the Malay peninsula extends to within a degree and a half of the equator.

## The Bay of Bengal and Bengal Region
The Bay of Bengal serves as the southeastern boundary for Orissa, which contains the rich deltas of the Mahanadi and neighboring rivers. The lieutenant-governorship of Bengal is divided into sub-provinces, including Behar, Orissa, Bengal proper, and the mountainous region of Chota Nagpur. Bengal proper extends from the sea-board to the Himalayas, with its southern portion formed by the delta of the Ganges and its northern portion consisting of the Ganges valley.

The Ganges valley is noted as one of the most fertile and densely populated tracts in the world. It produces a vast array of goods, including jute, cotton, wheat, indigo, turmeric, pepper, ginger, betel-nut, quinine, and oil-seeds. The region also produces timber from the *sal* tree, bamboo, and palm. In the Darjeeling district of Bengal, the town of Kurseong—located 4,860 ft. above sea-level—serves as a center for the tea trade and a sanatorium.

Geologically, the districts near the sea are composed of alluvial formations; it is reported that no substance as coarse as gravel is found within 400 m. of the river mouths or throughout the delta.

## The China Sea and Eastern Asia
The China Sea is bounded by the continent of Asia to the west and eastern islands to the east. Its coastline is broken by the recession of the Yellow Sea and the Gulfs of Siam and Tongking, as well as the projections of the Korean and Kamchatka peninsulas. 

Nearby islands include Formosa, situated under the northern tropic near the Chinese coast, which features a high mountain range reaching nearly 13,000 ft. and plains occupied by immigrant Chinese on its western side. The Philippine Islands, located between Borneo and southern China (between 5 deg. and 20 deg. N.), have a highest point of 10,250 ft. and fertile land suited for agriculture. Further north, the islands of Japan lie between the 30th and 45th parallels and are traversed by volcanic mountains reaching approximately 13,000 ft.

## Climate and Rainfall
The region is heavily influenced by periodical winds known as monsoons. In the Bay of Bengal, the south-west monsoon is strongest from the south and south-east, followed by north-east winds after October, which eventually transition to northerly and north-westerly winds. The China Sea is fully exposed to both monsoons.

Rainfall varies drastically across these regions:
* **Bengal:** The eastern part of the Ganges delta averages 75 to 80 in., while Calcutta stands at about 64 in. The Khasi hills to the east of Bengal experience extreme rainfall, with a ten-year average of over 550 in.
* **China and Southeast Asia:** At Canton, near the northern tropic, rainfall reaches 78 in. In the Malay peninsula, rainfall increases on coasts not sheltered by Sumatra, while the Burmese and Tenasserim coasts register falls of more than 200 in.
* **Other areas:** Singapore receives nearly 100 in., and Batavia in Java receives about 78 in.

## Flora and Fauna
The tropical climate of southern Asia supports a wide variety of plant and animal life. The genus *Musa* (bananas and plantains) is likely native to India and southern Asia. Specific varieties include *M. acuminata* in the Malay Archipelago and *M. corniculata* in the Malay Archipelago and Cochin China. In southern China, tropical fruits such as the mango, banana, pineapple, pomelo, and lichee grow abundantly.

The marine life of the Indian and China seas is diverse. The *Serranus* genus of perches is very numerous in Asia. Other notable species include:
* **Mango fish:** A delicacy inhabiting the seas from Siam to the Bay of Bengal.
* **Sciaenidae:** Found from the Bay of Bengal to China.
* **Stromateidae (pomfrets):** Extending to the Pacific and China.
* **Other species:** Mackerels, gobies, blennies, mullets, sword fishes (*Xiphidae*), and scabbard fishes (*Trichuridae*) are distributed throughout the seas of south Asia.
