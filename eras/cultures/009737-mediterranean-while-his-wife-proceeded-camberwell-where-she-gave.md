# Jerome Bonaparte

## Early Life and Military Career
Jerome was born on November 15, 1784, in Ajaccio. During the early years of the French Revolution, he shared the fortunes of his family and received an education at Juilly. In 1800, he was called to join his brother, who was then serving as the First Consul of France. Jerome was known for an affectionate but impetuous nature; while serving in the Consular Guard, he was wounded during a duel with the younger brother of General Davout.

Following this period, Jerome was transferred to the navy and cruised in the West Indies. During this assignment, a British cruiser blockaded him, prompting Jerome to leave his ship and travel through the United States.

## Marriage to Elizabeth Patterson
While in Baltimore, Jerome fell in love with Miss Elizabeth Patterson and married her, despite the fact that she was a minor. This action was viewed by Napoleon as a disregard for the laws of France and for discipline, which caused the emperor great annoyance. 

When Jerome brought his wife to Europe in 1805, Napoleon ordered that she be excluded from his states. Jerome attempted to change his brother's mind during an interview held at Alexandria, but these efforts were unsuccessful. In May 1805, Jerome was given command of a small squadron in the Mediterranean. During this time, his wife went to Camberwell, where she gave birth to a son.

## Naval and Military Assignments
In November 1805, Jerome sailed as part of a squadron commanded by Admiral Willaumez with the intent to ravage the West Indies, though a storm scattered the fleet. Jerome eventually reached France safely in August 1806 after damaging British commerce in the North Atlantic.

Upon his return, Napoleon named him a prince of France and placed him in command of a division of South Germans for the 1806 campaign. Following the events at Jena, Jerome accepted the surrender of several Prussian towns.

## Kingdom of Westphalia
The marriage between Jerome and Elizabeth Patterson was annulled by an imperial decree. Subsequently, the emperor united Jerome with Princess Catherine of Wurttemberg. Under the terms of the treaty of Tilsit, signed July 7, 1807, Jerome was raised to the throne of the new kingdom of Westphalia.

While reigning in Westphalia, Jerome was frequently rebuked by the emperor. He was noted for his fondness for luxury, his numerous *amours*, and for falling deeply into debt. However, the kingdom saw some benefits due to its connection with France: the *Code Napoleon* was introduced, feudalism was abolished, education received an impulse in its higher departments, and repressive laws against the Israelites were removed.
