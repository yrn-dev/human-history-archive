# Sackpfeife and Dudelsack of Germany

The *Sackpfeife* or *Dudelsack* refers to the bag-pipe of Germany, a complex reed instrument of great antiquity. It serves as a developmental link between the primitive organ and the syrinx by utilizing a reservoir for wind supply, which allows the performer to regulate sound-producing pressure using their arm.

## General Construction
A bag-pipe consists of an air-tight leather bag featuring three to five apertures. Each aperture contains a short tube or fixed stock that serves as an air-chamber to protect the reeds and as a socket for the pipes. There are three types of pipes:
1. **Blow-pipe**: A simple valved insufflation tube used by the performer to fill the bag.
2. **Chaunter (Chanter)**: The melody-pipe, which always utilizes a double-reed. Depending on the variety, it may have a cylindrical or conical bore, lateral holes, a bell, and occasionally keys.
3. **Drones**: Jointed pipes with a cylindrical bore that typically end in a bell. These pipes lack lateral holes and produce a single fixed note.

## Varieties of the German Bag-pipe
Michael Praetorius described and illustrated the *Sackpfeife* in five distinct sizes:

*   **Grosser Bock**: A double-bass bag-pipe described as formidable in appearance. It features a single, very long cylindrical drone and a chaunter; both terminate in a curved ram's horn, which gives the instrument its name. The chaunter possesses a thumb-hole at the back, a vent-hole in front, and seven finger-holes. Its drone is tuned to G, one octave below the chaunter.
*   **Bock**: Similar in construction to the *Grosser Bock*, but pitched a fourth higher in C.
*   **Schaeferpfeife**: This variety has two drones tuned to F and B flat. Praetorius noted that the upper notes of its chaunter suffered from faulty intonation because it lacked the thumb-hole found in all other varieties.
*   **Huemmelchen**: This instrument features two drones tuned to C and F.
*   **Dudey**: The smallest of the family, the treble sackpfeife. It has three drones tuned to E flat, B flat, and E flat. The compass of its chaunter ranges from F or E flat to C or D.

## The Magdeburg Bag-pipe
Praetorius also documented a specific kind of *sackpfeife* found in Magdeburg. This instrument was pitched a third lower than the *schaeferpfeife* and was somewhat larger. It featured a conical bore and lacked keys. Unlike the other varieties, it had two chaunters mounted in a single stock. Each chaunter had one thumb-hole at the back and three holes in front. This configuration allowed the performer to play simple two-part melodies: the left-hand chaunter sounded G, A, B, C, D, while the right-hand chaunter sounded D, E, F, G, A. Its drones were tuned to G and D.

## Comparative Instruments
The German *Sackpfeife* exists alongside other regional variations of the bag-pipe. The French *musette* is similar in pitch to the *huemmelchen* but is inflated using bellows rather than a blow-pipe. In contrast, the *Calabrian bag-pipe* uses a goatskin bag with the hair left on and is inflated by a blow-pipe. The Calabrian variety features two drones and two chaunters in one stock; the right-hand pipe includes a key enclosed in a perforated box to cover the fourth hole. Both the drones and chaunters of the Calabrian instrument use double reeds.
