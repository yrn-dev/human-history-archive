# N.N.W.-S.S.E. Geological Orientation

The designation N.N.W.-S.S.E. refers to a specific directional strike found within the geological formations of the Balkan Peninsula.

## Geological Context of the Balkan Peninsula
The Balkan Peninsula is geologically divided into four distinct areas. A central region, roughly triangular in shape, features its apex in Servia and its base resting upon the Aegean Sea. This central area is primarily characterized by faulting rather than folding and consists largely of schistose and crystalline rocks, with a core formed by the mountain masses of Rila, Perin, Belasitza, and Rhodope. In this core, Mesozoic and Palaeozoic beds are absent, and the earliest sedimentary deposits are Tertiary beds lying flat upon crystalline rocks.

Bordering this central region are two belts of folded beds. To the north lies the chain of the Balkans, and to the west are the mountain ranges of the Ionian and Adriatic coasts. Beyond the Balkans is the great Rumanian depression, which is occupied chiefly by Tertiary and Cretaceous undisturbed strata.

## The Western Folded Belt
The strike of the folds in the western folded belt is oriented N.W.-S.E. or N.N.W.-S.S.E. While there are many local irregularities, this general direction is maintained as far as the southern extremity of Greece. At this southern point, the folds exhibit a tendency to curve toward Crete.

The composition of this chain varies by region:
*   **Northern Region:** The chain is formed in part by the Jura and Trias, and Carboniferous beds are present. The involvement of Sarmatian beds indicates that the folding process was not completed until Pliocene times.
*   **Southern Region:** Older beds disappear in the south, and the chain is formed primarily of Cretaceous beds, although Jurassic and Eocene rocks are likely present.
