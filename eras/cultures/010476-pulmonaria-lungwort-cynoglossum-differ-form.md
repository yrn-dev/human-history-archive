# Pulmonaria and Cynoglossum

*Pulmonaria* (commonly known as lungwort) and *Cynoglossum* (known as hounds-tongue) are genera of plants belonging to the Boraginaceae. This order of plants is categorized within the sympetalous section of dicotyledons and is a member of the Tubiflorae series.

## Botanical Characteristics
Plants within the Boraginaceae order are generally perennial or annual herbs characterized by rough hairs. While most are herbs, some genera, such as *Ehretia* and *Cordia*, are arborescent or shrubby and are found in sub-tropical or tropical regions. 

The leaves of these plants are usually narrow, entire, and alternate. In certain genera, including *Pulmonaria* and *Cynoglossum*, there is a distinct difference in form between the stem-leaves and the radical leaves; the radical leaves are generally broader and may sometimes be heart-shaped.

## Floral and Fruit Structure
A defining feature of the Boraginaceae is the dorsiventral, or one-sided, inflorescence. This is seen in species of *Myosotis* (forget-me-not), where the cyme begins as a close coil and uncoils as the flowers open. The flowers often undergo a color change during this process, transitioning from red in the bud to blue upon expansion, a trait observed in *Symphytum*, *Echium*, and *Myosotis*.

The form of the corolla varies across the order:

In some instances, the corolla is bent (as in *Lycopsis*) or oblique (as in *Echium*). The throat of the flower is frequently closed by scale-like outgrowths known as the corona. These scales protect honey and pollen from undesirable visitors or wet conditions and may indicate the position of honey through a difference in color, such as the yellow eye found in the forget-me-not.

The fruit typically consists of four one-seeded nutlets, though some may remain undeveloped. The characteristics of these nutlets vary by genus; for example, they are polished in *Myosotis*, stone-hard in *Lithospermum*, and covered with bristles in *Cynoglossum*.

## Distribution and Usage
The Boraginaceae order contains approximately 1,200 species across 85 genera. Its primary center of distribution is the Mediterranean region, extending into Asia and central Europe, though it becomes less frequent as it moves north. A secondary, smaller center is located on the Pacific side of North America. The order is less developed in the south temperate zone.

While the order is considered to be of little economic value, certain genera have historical applications. *Pulmonaria* and borage were formerly utilized in medicine. Additionally, the roots of some plants in this order, such as *Alkanna tinctoria* (alkanet), are used to produce brown or purple dyes.

## Common Names and Related Species
In the European context, *Pulmonaria officinalis* is known as Virgin Mary's honeysuckle. This is distinct from other plants that share the "honeysuckle" name, such as the meadow clover (*Trifolium pratense*) or the French honeysuckle (*Hedysarum coronarium*).

Other representatives of the Boraginaceae found in Britain include *Myosotis*, comfrey (*Symphytum*), and bugloss (*Echium*). Borage (*Borago officinalis*) is also present, typically occurring in waste ground as a garden escape.
