# Lake Bangweulu and the Luapula River

Lake Bangweulu is a shallow lake located in British Central Africa, situated between 10° 38' and 11° 31' S. and cut by 30° E. It is positioned within a central basin on an extensive plateau at an altitude of approximately 3,700 to 3,760 feet above sea level.

## Physical Geography and Composition
The lake is formed by the head streams of the Congo. While the term "Bangweulu" is sometimes used to describe the entire depression, it properly refers only to the area of clear water. The lake's banks are clearly defined only on its western and south-west sides. The main body of open water extends approximately 60 miles from north to south and 40 miles from east to west.

The water surface is diminished by several islands and long, narrow sandbanks. The sandbanks nearly separate the main body of water from Chifunawuli, the western pan of the lake. Notable islands include:
* **Kirui (Chiru):** The largest island, located on the east side near the swamp.
* **Kisi (Chishi):** A small island in a central position just south of 11° S.
* **Mbawali:** An island measuring 20 miles long by 3 miles broad, located south of Kisi.

The surrounding land slopes gently into the depression from the north, east, and south. Numerous streams drain into this area, transforming much of it into a morass of papyrus and reeds. This swamp extends south of the lake to 12° 10' S.

## Hydrology and Drainage
The hydrology of the region is characterized by the interaction of several water bodies. The Chambezi, the most remote head stream of the Congo, flows into the east side of the swamp. The Chambezi does not enter the lake itself but mingles its waters with those of the Luapula within the swamp.

The Luapula River exits Lake Bangweulu at its southernmost point. At this outflow, the river is about a mile wide, though it quickly narrows to approximately 300 yards. From its exit, the Luapula flows south through the swamp for some 50 miles before turning west and then north. The river eventually issues from the north end of Lake Mweru.

Near the Luapula's outflow lies Lake Kampolombo, which measures 20 miles long and 8 miles broad at its southern end. While a sandy track and a forest-clad tongue of land called Kapata separate Lake Kampolombo from the Luapula and Bangweulu, various channels connect the river to the lake.

The swamp is undergoing gradual solidification due to the deposition of large quantities of alluvium from other streams and the flood waters of the Chambezi. Additionally, it is believed that the Luapula is very slowly draining Lake Bangweulu.

## Regional Context and Exploration
Lake Bangweulu is part of a territory of about 250,000 square miles that includes other significant water bodies such as Lake Nyasa, Lake Tanganyika, and Lake Mweru. The region is characterized by high plateaus, with an average altitude of 3,500 feet.

The lake was first heard of in 1798 by the Portuguese traveller Francisco de Lacerda. It was first reached in 1868 by David Livingstone, who died six years later in the swamps to the south. Subsequent exploration included a partial survey by French traveller Victor Giraud in 1883 and the first circumnavigation by Poulett Weatherley in 1896.

## Local Languages
The region between the Chambezi river and Lake Bangweulu to the north, and the Luangwa river to the east and south, is home to the Bisa (or Wisa) family of languages, which includes the dialects of Ilala and Lubisa. Some languages along the course of the Upper Luapula river may also belong to this family.
