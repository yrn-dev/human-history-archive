# Franz Bopp and the Study of Sanskrit

Franz Bopp (1791–1867) was a German philologist whose work is credited with the beginning of the science of comparative grammar. Born in Mainz on September 14, 1791, Bopp was educated at the Lyceum in Aschaffenburg, Bavaria. His interest in the literature and languages of the East was sparked by the lectures of Karl J. Windischmann, as well as the influence of G.F. Creuzer, J.J. Gorres, the brothers Schlegel, and specifically Friedrich Schlegel's 1808 work, *Uber die Sprache und Weisheit der Indier*.

## Early Studies and Initial Publications
In 1812, the Bavarian government funded Bopp's studies in Paris, where he studied Sanskrit for four years. During this time, he associated with scholars such as S. de Sacy, A.L. Chezy, L.M. Langles, and Alexander Hamilton. Bopp utilized the Imperial library's collection of Sanskrit manuscripts, many of which had been brought from India by Father Pons in the early 18th century, as well as books from the Serampore and Calcutta presses.

His first major work, published in 1816 in Frankfort-On-Main, was titled *Uber das Conjugationssystem der Sanskritsprache in Vergleichung mit jenem der griechischen, lateinischen, persischen und germanischen Sprache*. In this text, Bopp sought to trace the common origin of the grammatical structures of these languages, rather than simply proving their common parentage, which had already been established.

## Contributions to the Mahabharata and Sanskrit Texts
Following a period in London, where he instructed Wilhelm von Humboldt in Sanskrit and befriended H.T. Colebrooke and Sir Charles Wilkins, Bopp produced several critical editions of Sanskrit texts. In 1819, he published a Latin translation and critical edition of the story of *Nala and Damayanti*, described as the most beautiful episode of the *Mahabharata*. 

Bopp continued to edit episodes of the *Mahabharata* over the next decade, including:
* *Indralokagamanam* and three other episodes (Berlin, 1824).
* *Diluvium* and three other episodes (Berlin, 1829).
* A new edition of *Nala* (Berlin, 1832).

These works, alongside A.W. Schlegel's 1823 edition of the *Bhagavadgita*, served as essential tools for students beginning to read Sanskrit texts.

## Transition to Grammatical Investigations
Bopp's focus shifted away from the editing of literary texts following the publication of the complete *Mahabharata* in Calcutta. At that point, he discontinued editing Sanskrit texts and confined his professional activities exclusively to grammatical investigations.

In 1821, on the recommendation of Humboldt, Bopp was appointed to the chair of comparative grammar and Sanskrit at Berlin, and he joined the Royal Prussian Academy in 1822. His subsequent grammatical works included:
* *Ausfuhrliches Lehrgebaude der Sanskrita-Sprache* (1827), with a Latin edition completed in 1832 and a shorter grammar in 1834.
* A Sanskrit and Latin glossary (1830), which later incorporated cognate languages in editions published in 1847 and 1867.

## The Comparative Grammar
Bopp's primary achievement was his *Comparative Grammar* (*Vergleichende Grammatik des Sanskrit, Zend, Griechischen, Lateinischen, Litthauischen, Altslavischen, Gothischen, und Deutschen*), published in six parts between 1833 and 1852. This work was preceded by a series of monographs in the *Transactions of the Berlin Academy* from 1824 to 1831. 

The *Comparative Grammar* had three main objectives:
1. To describe the original grammatical structure of the languages through intercomparison.
2. To trace their phonetic laws.
3. To investigate the origin of their grammatical forms.

The work evolved over time; for instance, Old-Slavonian was included from the second part onwards, and a revised German edition (1856–1861) added Old-Armenian. The text was translated into English by E.B. Eastwick in 1845 and into French by Professor Michel Breal in 1866.

## Legacy and Death
Bopp's meticulous research into linguistic phenomena provided insights into the practices, tenets, and domestic usages of ancient Indo-European nations. In 1866, to mark the fiftieth anniversary of Windischmann's preface to his first work, the *Die Bopp-Stiftung* fund was established in Berlin to promote the study of comparative grammar and Sanskrit.

Franz Bopp died on October 23, 1867. Despite the global celebration of his academic achievements, he died a poor man.
