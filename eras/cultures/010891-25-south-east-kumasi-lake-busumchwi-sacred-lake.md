# Lake Busumchwi

## Overview and Location
Lake Busumchwi is a sacred lake of the Ashanti. It is situated approximately 25 miles south-east of Kumasi. The lake is nearly circular in shape and reaches a maximum diameter of 6 miles.

## Geography and Topography
The lake is surrounded by hills clad in forest, which reach heights of some 800 feet. The broader region of the country generally maintains an elevation below 2,000 feet, although the land rises toward the north.

The area is traversed by several river systems. The northern frontier is formed by the Black Volta and the lower Volta. In the eastern portion of the country, various tributaries of the Volta run generally in a northerly direction. The central parts of the region contain the upper courses of the Ofin and several tributaries of the Prah. Further to the west are the Bia and Tano rivers, which flow into the Assini lagoon. With the exception of the Volta, the rivers in Ashanti are navigable only by canoes.

## Climate and Environment
The water supply in the region is abundant and good. The climate is characterized by a rainy season that lasts from October until the end of May, during which violent and frequent storms occur. In Kumasi, the mean annual rainfall is 40 inches and the mean temperature is 76 degrees F. While the climate is considered less unhealthy than that of the coast towns of West Africa, it is noted as being unsuited for the prolonged residence of Europeans.
