# Mediterranean Shores in Winter

The climatic conditions of the northern Mediterranean shores during the winter season are significantly influenced by the presence of nearby mountain ranges and the resulting movement of cold air.

## Atmospheric Dynamics and the Bora
On the northern shores of the Mediterranean, there is a consistent northern tendency in the winds during winter. This phenomenon occurs because cold air from the mountains slides down toward the sea, where the barometric pressure is lower. When a cyclone forms over the Mediterranean, the currents in its north-western area draw air from cold northern regions; during the passage of such a cyclone, the bora prevails.

The bora is an Italian term used to describe a violent, cold wind blowing from the north and northeast. It is particularly common in the Adriatic region, with a high prevalence on the Dalmatian and Istrian coasts. A wind of a precisely similar character, known as the mistral, prevails along the French Mediterranean littoral and in Provence. Additionally, the bora occurs at Novorossiysk on the Black Sea.

## Regional Climatic Variations
The interaction between mountain ranges and the sea creates distinct local contrasts in temperature and weather patterns across the region.

### The Balkan Peninsula
The Balkan Peninsula is a predominantly mountainous region bounded by the Mediterranean to the south, the Adriatic and Ionian Seas to the west, and the Black Sea and Sea of Marmora to the east. Its climate is characterized by rapid transitions of temperature and extreme cold in winter.

In Bulgaria, the severity of the winter is attributed in part to the general configuration of the Balkan Peninsula and the extent of its mountain ranges. In districts extending from the Danube to the Balkans, the winter cold is intense due to exposure to the bitter north wind. In these areas, the temperature has been known to drop to 24° below zero, and the river frequently freezes over despite its rapidity and volume.

The influence of the sea provides some moderation in the eastern districts. While the proximity of the sea tempers the extremes of heat and cold, the coastline remains exposed to violent northeast winds. Consequently, the sea is occasionally frozen at Varna.

### Other Regional Effects
The broader atmospheric patterns of the continent also play a role in these seasonal shifts. Winter cold causes an accumulation of air over cold areas, contributing to an increase in barometric pressure over Asia during the winter months, which is the opposite of the effect produced by summer heat.
