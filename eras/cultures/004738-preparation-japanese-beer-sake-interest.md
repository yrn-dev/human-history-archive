# Saké

Saké is an intoxicating beverage of ancient origin produced in Japan. It is described as a kind of beer brewed from rice. Until the opening of intercourse with the Occident, it was the only kind of intoxicating liquor known in Japan.

## Origins and History
The process of preparing saké is said to have been taught by Sukuna. Sukuna arrived in Japan from a foreign country, which was probably China, during the period when the Kami, Okuni-nushi, was establishing order within the Japanese islands.

## Production Process
The preparation of saké involves a specific three-stage process that is noted for the use of a single micro-organism—a mould—to perform the combined functions of fermentation and saccharification. This mould replaces the yeast used in European breweries and the diastase found in malted grain.

### Stage One: Preparation of Koji
The first stage of production is the creation of *Koji*. This is achieved by treating steamed rice with a culture of *Aspergillus oryzae*. The role of this micro-organism is to convert the starch in the rice into sugar.

### Stage Two: Creation of Moto
In the second stage, the *Koji* is added to a vat containing a thin paste of fresh-boiled starch. This mixture is converted into *moto*. Once this is established, fermentation begins and continues for a period of 30 to 40 days.

### Stage Three: Final Fermentation and Finishing
The third stage involves adding water, more *Koji*, and additional rice to the *moto*. This triggers a secondary fermentation that lasts between 8 and 10 days. Following this period, the liquid is filtered and heated before being run into casks. At this final point, the beverage is known as saké.
