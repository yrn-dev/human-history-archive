# Gold Coast and Hinterland

The Gold Coast and its hinterland constitute a region in West Africa. According to archival population estimates, the area is inhabited by a population of 2,700,000 people, who are described as being chiefly black.

## Geography and Administration
The region includes the Gold Coast colony and its hinterland. To the north lies the British Protectorate known as the Northern Territories of the Gold Coast. A significant portion of the hinterland is comprised of Ashanti, a British possession. Ashanti is an irregular oblong territory with a triangular projection to the south known as the country of the Adansi. It covers an area of 23,000 square miles and had an estimated population of 500,000 in 1907.

Ashanti is bounded to the west by the French Ivory Coast colony and to the east by the river Volta, which serves as the border with the German colony of Togoland. Its southern frontier is the same as the northern frontier of the Gold Coast colony.

## Economy and Trade
The economy of the region is based on the export of vegetable products, minerals, and the collection of taxes.

### Agriculture
The vegetable products of the hinterland are similar to those found on the Gold Coast. Key commercial products include:
*   **Rubber:** The rubber tree (*Funtumia elastica*) is the most important commercial vegetable product.
*   **Cocoa:** Since 1905, many cocoa plantations have been established, particularly in the eastern districts.
*   **Kola:** The nut of the kola tree is in great demand.
*   **Tobacco:** This is cultivated within the northern regions.
*   **Gum Copal:** This substance is exported from the region.

### Mining and Infrastructure
Gold is found in the western districts of the country. Between 1895 and 1901, several companies were formed to operate the mines. In 1902 and subsequent years, the majority of gold exported from the Gold Coast originated from the Obuassi mines. In 1905, the gold output from Ashanti was 68,259 oz., with a value of L254,790.

To support this industry and general trade, a railway was completed in 1903 running from Sekondi to Kumasi, passing through the auriferous (gold-bearing) region.

### Trade and Revenue
Kumasi serves as the distributing center for the hinterland and the whole of Ashanti. While some trade from Ashanti was previously diverted to the French port of Assini due to wars between England and the Ashanti, measures were taken to improve trade between Cape Coast and Kumasi following the suppression of a revolt in 1900.

Ashanti conducts considerable trade with its neighbors to the north (the Niger countries) and its French and German neighbors. Trade moving southward through British territory is included in the figures for the Gold Coast. The revenue and expenditure of Ashanti are also included in those of the Gold Coast. Primary sources of revenue include:
*   Caravan taxes
*   Liquor licences
*   Rents from government land
*   Contributions from gold-mining companies
