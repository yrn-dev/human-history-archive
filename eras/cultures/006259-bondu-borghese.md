# BONDU AND BORGHESE

## Bondu
Bondu is a French protectorate located in West Africa and is dependent on the colony of Senegal. Geographically, it is situated between 13 and 15 degrees North and 12 and 13 degrees West, lying between the upper course of the Gambia and the Faleme river. The terrain consists of an elevated plateau with central and southern hills that are generally unproductive and covered in stunted wood. In contrast, the lower regions are fertile and characterized by various valuable fruit-trees, including the tamarind and the baobab. The region is traversed by *marigots*, which are torrents that flow rapidly during the rainy season but remain empty during the dry season.

The population of Bondu is primarily Fula, although Mandingos largely control the trade. The laws and religion of the country are Mahommedan, though the precepts of the faith are not observed with great rigor.

### European Exploration and Political History
Mungo Park became the first European traveler to visit Bondu in 1795, during which time he faced many exactions from the reigning prince. At that time, the royal residence was located at Fatteconda. By 1818, when Major W. Gray, a British officer attempting to solve the Niger problem, visited the country, the royal residence had been moved to Bulibani, a small town of approximately 3,000 people enclosed by a strong clay wall. In 1818, René Auguste Caillié also traveled to Bondu to deliver supplies to a British expedition, though he was forced to return to France due to fever.

In August 1845, the king of Bondu signed a treaty recognizing French sovereignty. Although the natives disregarded this treaty, Bondu came definitely under French control in 1858, after which the country experienced considerable prosperity.

## Borghese
The Borghese are a noble Italian family of Sienese origin, first mentioned in 1238. The family established itself in Rome through Marcantonio Borghese, the father of Camillo Borghese (1550–1620). Camillo was elected pope in 1605 under the name Paul V.

### Rise to Nobility and Titles
Pope Paul V expanded the family's status by creating his nephew a prince of Vivero on November 17, 1609. In 1610, Philip III of Spain conferred the title of prince of Sulmona upon him. The family's position among the higher Roman nobility was further solidified in 1614 through the marriage of the prince's son, Paolo, to Olimpia, the heiress of the Aldobrandini family.

In the 19th century, Camillo Filippo Ludovico, Prince Borghese (born 1775), married Pauline, the sister of Emperor Napoleon and widow of General Leclerc, in 1803. Pauline, born in Ajaccio in 1780, resided with the prince in Rome but later returned to Paris. In 1806, she received the title of duchess of Guastalla. Camillo Filippo Ludovico was also made duke of Guastalla in 1806 and served for several years as the governor of the Genoese and Piedmontese provinces. Following the fall of Napoleon, he lived in Florence until his death in 1832. Pauline died of cancer in 1825.

### Art and Architecture
The Borghese family is associated with several significant architectural and artistic landmarks in Rome:

*   **The Borghese Palace:** One of the city's most magnificent buildings, it once housed a splendid gallery of pictures. Most of these works were transferred to the Villa Borghese, located outside the Porto del Popolo, which is now known as Villa Umberto I and is owned by the Italian government.
*   **Villa Borghese:** This villa was planned by the architect Baccio d'Agnolo. It houses various artworks, including Madonnas by Lotto and a statue in a sitting posture with an open hand. While some vulgarly attribute this statue to Belisarius, others ascribe it to Augustus propitiating Nemesis.
*   **Patronage:** Cardinals Borghese were among the employers of the painter Domenichino, for whom they commissioned fresco works in Rome.
