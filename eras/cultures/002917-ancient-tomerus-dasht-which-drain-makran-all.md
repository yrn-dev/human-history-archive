# The Ancient Tomerus and the Geography of Makran

The ancient Tomerus, known in modern geography as the Hingol, is a considerable stream located within the great block of Southern Baluchistan. It serves as one of the primary arteries in the region's network of internal communication, draining the territory of Makran and flowing into the Arabian Sea.

## Hydrography of Southern Baluchistan
The drainage of Southern Baluchistan generally trends toward the west and south. While some waters flow into the central swamps of Mashkel and Lora, several significant streams discharge into the Arabian Sea. In addition to the Hingol (Tomerus), these include the Dasht, which also drains Makran, and the Purali—historically referred to as the Arabus—which drains the low-lying flats of Las Bela. Further west, the Hab river marks the boundary west of Karachi.

An exception to this southward drainage is the Mulla river. The Mulla carries floods from the Kalat highlands into the Gandava basin, serving as a vital ancient highway connecting the Indus plains to Kandahar. The fortress of Kalat is positioned as a central "hub" between the sources of the Mulla and the Bolan, situated approximately 6,800 feet above sea-level.

## The Region of Makran
Makran is identified as the country of the Ichthyophagi. In the context of Persian Baluchistan—a province of Persia with an area of approximately 60,000 square miles—Makran is one of twenty administrative districts. It is further subdivided into several subdistricts, including Dasht, Bahu, Bint, Geh, Kucheh, and Kasrkand.

The physical landscape of the broader region is characterized by arid, waterless plains, barren mountains, and deserts consisting of stony ground or moving sands. While some rivers in Persian Baluchistan flow into the sea, they are generally unimportant and remain dry for most of the year. Other rivers, such as the Bampur and Mashkid, flow away from the sea and disappear into depressions known as *hamuns*.

## Ethnography and Population
The population of Makran and the surrounding areas is diverse, consisting of various races and social classes:

*   **Persian Population:** An underlying Persian (Tajik) population is found throughout the country. In Makran, this group is often represented by agricultural bondsmen or slaves, such as the Nakibs, Dehkans (or Dehwars), and the Durzadas.
*   **Arab Influence:** Arab influence is strongly impressed upon the ethnography of the region. All Rind tribes claim Koraish extraction and Arab origin.
*   **Other Groups:** The Naushirwanis, a purely Persian race, inhabit the Mashkel and Kharan deserts. Between these desert dwellers and the fish-eating coastal populations are numerous tribes, some of whom are recognized in the works of Herodotus. In the province of Las Bela, the dominant tribe is the Lumris (or Lasis), who are considered recent arrivals that displaced earlier Brahui and Tajik occupants.

## Historical and Trade Connections
Makran and the surrounding Baluchistan region have historically served as links between the east and west. The Turanian town of Rhambakia, located on the borders of Makran, was once a center for the trade of "bdellium." 

In the 7th century A.D., the province of Gandava was Buddhist, and Las Bela was governed by a Buddhist priest; evidence of this era remains in the form of Buddhist caves excavated in conglomerate cliffs at Shahr-i-Rogan (also called Gondrani). Following the influx of Arabs, Bela (then known as Armel or Armabel) became an important trading link between Sind and Persia. Medieval Arab geographers, including Ibn Haukal, mapped various towns in the delta, such as Kambali and Yusli, which featured bazaars, citadels, and caravanserais. The site of Armabel is believed to be Karia Pir, which was destroyed in 710 during the march of Mahommed Kasim toward Sind.
