# Banda Islands

The Banda Islands are a group of islands located in the Banda Sea, south of Ceram, within the Dutch East Indies. They belong to the residency of Amboyna and possess a total land area of approximately 16 square miles.

## Geography and Geology
The archipelago consists of three chief islands and several lesser ones. The main islands include Great Banda (also known as Lontor), Banda Neira to the north of Lontor, and Gunong Api to the west of Banda Neira. Further west lies Wai (or Ai), with Run situated to its south-west. Other islands in the group include Pisang, located north of Gunong Api, and Suwangi, which lies to the north-west.

The islands exhibit a volcanic formation. Lontor is a sickle-shaped island which, together with Neira and Gunong Api, forms part of a crater's circle, an arrangement comparable to Santorin in the Aegean Sea. Gunong Api, meaning "Fire Mountain," is an active volcano reaching a height of 2,200 feet. Its earthquakes and eruptions have frequently caused destruction, most notably in 1852, when a huge sea wave caused the primary damage.

The region features a good harbour located in the space between Gunong Api, Neira, and Lontor. This harbour has entrances on either side, allowing vessels to enter regardless of the monsoon. A third channel exists between Neira and Gunong Api, though it is only navigable for small vessels.

## Settlements and Administration
The chief town is Banda, located on the island of Neira. It is described as a pleasant settlement and is commanded by two Dutch forts from the early 17th century: Belgica and Nassau. While the climate of the islands is generally hot, it is not considered unhealthy; however, the largest island, Lontor, was found to be too unhealthy to serve as the site for the principal settlement.

## Economy and Commerce
The primary articles of commerce for the Banda group are mace and nutmeg, the latter of which is indigenous to the islands. Nutmegs are grown under the shade of other trees, typically the canari, in accordance with natural conditions. Annual production amounts to nearly 1,500,000 lb of nutmegs and 350,000 lb of mace. Additionally, jatti (or jalti) wood is cultivated on the small island of Rosingen.

Historically, the Dutch cleared off the native population, and the plantations (known as *perken*) were worked by convicts and slaves until emancipation occurred in 1860. Following this, Chinese and Malay labourers were introduced. Originally, the conquerors of the natives held the plantations while the government maintained a monopoly on produce at a fixed rate; this monopoly was abolished in 1873.

## History
The Portuguese explorer Antonio D'Abreu discovered and annexed the Banda Islands in 1512. By the beginning of the 17th century, the Dutch expelled the Portuguese. In 1608, the British established a factory on Wai, which the Dutch demolished immediately after the English vessel departed. Shortly thereafter, the natives resigned Lontor and Banda Neira to the British, and in 1620, Wai and Run were added to British dominions. Despite existing treaties, the Dutch attacked and expelled the British.

In 1654, Cromwell compelled the Dutch to make satisfaction for the massacre of Amboyna and to restore Run. However, due to a lack of adequate support from home, English settlers lost the island when the Dutch retook it in 1664. The Dutch maintained undisturbed possession until 1796, when the British took the islands. They were restored to the Dutch via the treaty of Amiens in 1800, captured again, and finally restored by the treaty of Paris in 1814.
