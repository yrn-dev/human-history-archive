# Cordillera

The term "cordillera" is a Spanish word meaning a range or chain of mountains. It is derived from the Old Spanish *cordilla*, which is the diminutive of *cuerda*, meaning a cord or rope. While the term has been applied to the eastern mountain system of Australia, it is not used as a generic term for parallel ranges of mountains outside of the Americas.

## Geographic Distribution

The name was first applied to the Andes ranges of South America (*Las Cordilleras de los Andes*) and was subsequently used for the extension of this system into Mexico. In North America, the term "Cordilleras" refers to the parallel ranges of mountains that include the Sierra Nevada and the Rocky Mountains. The portion of the western continent crossed by these ranges is known as the "Cordilleran region." In British Columbia, the province comprises nearly the entire width of the Cordilleran belt of North America, consisting of the Coast Range on the south-west (Pacific) side and the Rocky Mountains proper on the north-east side, with an "Interior Plateau" and various subordinate ranges located between them.

## The Andean Cordilleras of Bolivia

In Bolivia, the Andean cordilleras cross the south-west corner of the country and project east toward the Brazilian highlands. These ranges are divided into two great parallel chains with spurs and flanking ranges to the east, forming the massif of the Andean system. This system consists of parallel ranges that enclose deep ravines and great elevated plateaus.

### The Cordillera Occidental
The western range, known as the Cordillera Occidental, serves as part of the boundary between Bolivia and the northern provinces of Chile. It follows the outline of the coast and acts as the western rampart for the Bolivian tableland (*alta-planicie*). This plateau extends 500 miles in length and approximately 80 miles in breadth, covering about 40,000 square miles. The plateau's average elevation near Lake Titicaca is about 12,650 feet, descending 1,000 feet toward its southern end. The northern region of the plateau is called the *puna*, while the southern part is known as the "desert of Lipez."

The Cordillera Occidental is characterized by volcanic activity and snow-clad peaks, including:
*   Huallatiri (21,654 ft.)
*   Sajama (21,047 ft.)
*   Parinacota (20,918 ft.)
*   Pomarape (20,505 ft.)
*   Licancaur (19,685 ft.)
*   San Pedro y Pablo (19,423 ft.)
*   Oyahua (19,226 ft.)
*   Lirima (19,128 ft.)

### The Cordillera Oriental and Eastern Ranges
The eastern rampart of the plateau consists of the eastern ranges. Unlike the western range, the great summits of the eastern range, such as Sorata and Illimani, are formed of granitic intrusions and Palaeozoic rocks rather than volcanic material. To the north-east of Lake Titicaca, the ridges intersect in a confused mass or knot known as the Nudo de Apolobamba, which contains some of the highest peaks in South America.

## Geology and Climate

### Geological Composition
The geological makeup of the Bolivian cordilleras varies by range. The Western Cordillera consists primarily of Cretaceous and Jurassic beds, along with ashes and lavas from volcanoes. The eastern ranges are composed of Palaeozoic rocks with other intrusions. The intervening plateau is covered by terrestrial and freshwater deposits, including gypsiferous marls and red sandstones. Palaeozoic beds in these regions have yielded fossils from the Carboniferous, Devonian, Ordovician, and Cambrian ages.

### Climatic Influence
The high mountain ranges govern the rainfall conditions of the region. The Cordillera extracts moisture from prevailing easterly winds, leaving the elevated plateaus to the west with limited rainfall in the north and absolute barrenness toward the south. In contrast, east of the Cordillera Oriental, rains fall throughout the year, with the rainy season typically occurring from November to March.

## Other Cordillera Systems

### Central America
In Costa Rica, two volcanic cordilleras traverse the interior, separated by the central plateau of Cartago and San Jose. These chains form a single watershed that is precipitous on the Pacific slope and more gradual toward the Atlantic. The more northerly range, known in one region as the Sierra de Tilaran, features volcanic cones such as Tenorio (6,800 ft.), Orosi (5,185 ft.), Miravalles (4,698 ft.), and Rincon de la Vieja (4,500 ft.).

In Honduras, the southern half of the country is traversed by a continuation of the main Nicaraguan Cordillera. This chain acts as a natural wall when viewed from the Pacific, featuring many volcanic peaks. The mountains in the southern region sometimes exceed 10,000 feet in altitude.

### Venezuela
In western Venezuela, the drainage basin of Lake Maracaibo is bounded on the south-east by the Cordillera de Merida and on the west by the Eastern Cordillera. The Eastern Cordillera is noted for heavy rainfall on its eastern slopes, which is said to exceed 86 inches per annum.
