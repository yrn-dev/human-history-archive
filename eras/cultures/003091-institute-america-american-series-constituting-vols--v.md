# Papers of the Archaeological Institute of America, American Series

The *Papers of the Archaeological Institute of America, American Series* is a scholarly publication consisting of volumes i. through v. These volumes contain extensive archaeological, ethnological, and historical research focusing on the Americas and other ancient civilizations.

## Contributions of Adolph Francis Alphonse Bandelier
A significant portion of the American Series is comprised of the work of Adolph Francis Alphonse Bandelier (born August 6, 1840, in Bern, Switzerland). Bandelier, an American archaeologist who emigrated to the United States as a youth, devoted himself after 1880 to ethnological and archaeological studies. His work in the American Series includes investigations into the prehistoric civilizations and history of Mexico, Arizona, and New Mexico.

Bandelier's contributions to the *Papers* include several specific studies regarding the ancient Mexicans:
*   "On the Art of War and Mode of Warfare of the Ancient Mexicans"
*   "On the Distribution and Tenure of Lands and the Customs with respect to Inheritance among the Ancient Mexicans"
*   "On the Social Organization and Mode of Government of the Ancient Mexicans"

These studies were originally featured in the *Annual Reports* of the Peabody Museum of American Archaeology and Ethnology at Harvard University between 1877 and 1879. Additionally, the American Series includes Bandelier's *Historical Introduction to Studies among the Sedentary Indians of New Mexico, and Report on the Ruins of the Pueblo of Pecos* (1881), his *Report of an Archaeological Tour in Mexico in 1881* (published in 1884), and his *Contributions to the History of the South-western Portion of the United States carried on mainly in the years from 1880 to 1885* (1890). The series also contains his *Final Report of Investigations among the Indians of the South-western United States*, published in two volumes between 1890 and 1892.

## Research on Assus
The *Papers of the Archaeological Institute of America* also document explorations conducted outside of the Americas. Volumes i. and ii. contain the work of Mr. J.T. Clarke, who conducted explorations at Assus (known by its Turkish name, Behram) between 1881 and 1883. 

The primary objective of these explorations was the clearing of the Doric temple of Athena, which was constructed around 470 B.C. This temple is noted for its sculptured architrave, which served as a replacement for the standard frieze and featured heraldic and mythological scenes, including the labours of Heracles. Clarke's research, published in the two-volume work *Assos* (1882 and 1898), included the creation of a scientific ground-plan and the study of well-preserved Hellenistic walls.
