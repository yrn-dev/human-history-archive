# Armina- and Related Etymologies

## The Term Armina-
In the study of Old Persian, the name of Armenia is recorded as *Armina-*. According to archival records, the origin of this specific name is considered uncertain, despite the fact that it has frequently been connected to other terms in linguistic studies.

## Comparative Philology and the "Aryan" Root
The term *Armina-* is discussed within the broader context of comparative philology, particularly the works of Max Muller. Research indicates that a related word was used as a national name across several regions, including Persia, Bactria, and India. This term appears in various forms: *ariya-* in Old Persian, *airya-* in Zend, and *arya-* in Sanskrit.

There are ongoing scholarly debates regarding the precise root of these terms. Max Muller asserted a connection to *ira*, a Sanskrit word for earth, though this remains far from certain. Spiegel noted that while the word can be linked to a root *ar-*, there are multiple roots of that form with different meanings, and no certain criterion exists to determine which one is the correct relation.

## Linguistic Connections and Distinctions
Efforts to establish a connection between these terms and *Erin* (Ireland), as attempted by Pictet, have been deemed impossible by Whitley Stokes. However, it is suggested that the word may share an origin with the *Ario-* found in Celtic and Germanic names, such as *Ariovistus*.

Within the Sanskrit language specifically, scholars distinguish between two probable words:
1. *Arya*, which is the origin of "Aryan" and the source of the usual term *arya*.
2. *Arya*, which appears in the *Rig Veda* as an epithet for deities.

Additionally, the term *aryas* may be interpreted as the genitive of *ari*, which is explained as meaning "pious, devoted, active."
