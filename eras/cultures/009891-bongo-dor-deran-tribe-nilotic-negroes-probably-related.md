# BONGO (DOR or DERAN)

The Bongo, also referred to as the Dor or Deran, are a tribe of Nilotic negroes. They inhabit the south-west portion of the Bahr-el-Ghazal province in the Anglo-Egyptian Sudan. It is believed that they are probably related to the Zandeh tribes of the Welle district.

## Population and History
According to G.A. Schweinfurth, who lived among the Bongo for two years, the tribe numbered at least 300,000 before the arrival of slave-raiders around 1850. Their population was greatly reduced by these slave-raiders and subsequently by the dervishes. The tribe was only able to begin recuperating after the Sudan government established effective control between 1904 and 1906. Historically, the Bongo lived in numerous small, peaceful, and independent communities; under the administration of the Sudan government, they have returned to managing their own affairs.

## Physical Characteristics
The Bongo are a race of medium height and tend to be thick-set. Their complexion is described as red-brown, resembling the soil of their residence, and they have black hair. Schweinfurth noted that their heads are nearly round, stating that, to his knowledge, no other African race possesses a higher cephalic index. In later life, Bongo women incline toward steatopygia. Schweinfurth observed that this deposit of fat, combined with the tail of bast worn by the women, gave them the appearance of "dancing baboons" while walking.

## Dress and Ornamentation
Historically, Bongo men wore only a loin-cloth and dozens of iron rings on their arms, which were arranged to function as a type of armour. Women wore a girdle with an attached tuft of grass. In more recent times, both sexes have largely adopted cotton cloths for their dress.

Tribal ornaments include plugs or nails passed through the lower lip. Women frequently wear a disk several inches in diameter in the lower lip, along with a bit of straw or a ring in the upper lip, a ring in the septum, and straws in the *alae* of the nostrils.

## Housing and Subsistence
The Bongo construct well-built huts, some of which reach heights of 24 feet. Unlike other Negroes of the upper Nile, the Bongo are not great cattle-breeders; instead, they devote their time to agriculture. Their primary cultivated crops include durra, sesame, tobacco, and sorghum.

Their diet is diverse, consisting of the fungi, tubers, and fruits abundant in their region. They consume almost every type of creature, including reptiles, insects, beasts, and birds, with the sole exception of the dog. They do not despise any flesh, whether it is fresh or putrid, and they drive vultures from carcasses.
