# Commerce and Demographics in Bukovina and Related Regions

## Economic Activity in Bukovina
In the duchy and crownland of Austria known as Bukovina, commerce is primarily managed by Armenians and Jews. Their commercial activities are largely restricted to the trade of raw products. These products specifically include wood, wool, cattle, and agricultural produce. 

The broader economy of Bukovina is heavily reliant on its fertile soil and natural resources. Agriculture has seen significant progress, with the primary products being barley, oats, rye, maize, wheat, hemp, flax, and potatoes. Additionally, cattle-rearing serves as a major source of revenue for the region. The government operates a salt mine at Kaczyka, which is the principal mineral source in the area. The region's chief industries consist of milling, distilling, and brewing.

## Geography and Environment of Bukovina
Bukovina is bounded by Russia and Rumania to the east and south, Galicia to the north, and Hungary and Transylvania to the west. It covers an area of 4,035 square miles. The landscape is characterized by the offshoots of the Carpathians, particularly in the southern regions, where the Giumaleu reaches an altitude of 6,100 feet. Key mountain passes include the Borgo Pass and the Radna Pass.

The region's hydrology is defined by the watershed of the Danube, with the exception of the Dniester river which runs along the northern border. Other major rivers include the Sereth—with its tributaries the Moldava, the Suczawa, and the Bistritza—and the Pruth. The name "Bukovina" is derived from the abundance of beech trees; woodland occupies 43.17% of the total area. The remaining land is divided into arable land (27.59%), meadows (12.68%), pastures (10.09%), and gardens (0.78%). The climate is described as healthy but severe, especially during winter, though it is generally milder than the climate of Galicia.

## Population and Society in Bukovina
As of 1900, Bukovina had a population of 729,921, resulting in a density of 181 inhabitants per square mile. The population is ethnically diverse:
* Ruthenians: Over 40%
* Rumanians: 35%
* Jews: 13%
* Other groups: Armenians, Russians, Hungarians, Poles, and Germans.

The official language used in the university, the law-courts, and the administration is German. Religiously, nearly 70% of the population are members of the Greek Orthodox Church under the jurisdiction of the metropolitan or archbishop of Czernowitz. Other religious affiliations include the Roman Catholic Church (11%), the Greek United Church (3.25%), and Protestants (2.5%).
