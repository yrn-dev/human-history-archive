# GOLD

## Mining and Geological Occurrences
In South Africa, the term "banket" is used in mining to describe beds of auriferous conglomerate. These are primarily found in the Witwatersrand gold-fields. The term "banket" was derived from a Dutch word for a sweetmeat resembling almond hard-bake, which in turn is related to the word "banquet" and the root "bank" or "bench," meaning a table-feast.

In Thrace, gold mines existed that were inherited by the ancestors of the father of Thucydides. Thucydides himself is said to have died in a small Thracian town called Skapte Hyle, which was located near these gold mines.

## Mythological and Ancient Accounts
In the natural history of the ancients, the griffin (also known as the griffon or gryphon) was an imaginary rapacious creature. It was described as having the fore part of an eagle—including a beak and wings—and the hinder part of a lion, with four legs. Some writers further described its tail as a serpent. 

The griffin was believed to inhabit Asiatic Scythia, a region where precious stones and gold were abundant. According to these accounts, the creatures watched over hidden treasures and gold mines; if strangers attempted to gather these materials, the griffins would leap upon them and tear them to pieces as a punishment for human greed and avarice. Herodotus (iii. 16) notes that the one-eyed Arimaspi were in a state of constant war with these creatures. Additionally, Sir John de Mandeville described the griffin in his *Travels* as being eight times larger than a lion.

## Coinage and Currency
Gold has been utilized in various forms of coinage across different eras and regions:

*   **Early English Coinage:** Anonymous gold coins resembling Frankish trientes in standard (21 grains) and type were fairly common, though they passed out of use early. Larger gold coins known as *solidi* are considered very rare.
*   **The Mancus:** During the reign of Offa, a new gold coin called the *mancus* was introduced from Mahommedan countries. This coin resembled the Roman solidus in standard, weighing approximately 70 grains. The oldest existing specimen of the *mancus* features a faithfully copied Arabic inscription.

## Gold in Ancient Society and Luxury
Gold was frequently used as a symbol of wealth, status, and reward in antiquity. In the records of King Darius, the monarch promised a reward to whichever of his three bodyguards could provide the most truthful and wise oration on a specific set of questions. The promised rewards included:
*   Drinking from cups of gold.
*   Sleeping upon gold.
*   A chariot featuring bridles of gold.
*   A chain of gold to be worn around the neck.

In other instances, gold served as a form of diplomatic or personal gift. The Athenian ambassador Timagoras was condemned and executed in part for receiving presents from a king, which included gold and silver.

## Awards and Honors
In more modern contexts, gold has been used for commemorative honors. For example, the American sculptor Cyrus Edwin Dallin was awarded a gold medal at the St Louis Exposition in 1904.
