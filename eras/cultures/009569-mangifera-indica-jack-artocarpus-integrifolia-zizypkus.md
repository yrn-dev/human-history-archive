# Mangifera indica, Artocarpus integrifolia, and Zizypkus

## Overview of Botanical Distribution
In the Bombay Presidency, forests are categorized into two primary classes: those located in the alluvial plains and those found in the hills. The hill forests are distributed over a wide area, extending from 14 to 23 degrees North latitude, with the majority situated within the Western Ghats or the Sahyadri hills. The alluvial forests are located in Sind, positioned on or near the banks of the Indus, covering an area of 550 square miles.

## Fruit-Bearing Trees of the Bombay Presidency
While the forests of the region contain various principal timber trees—including teak, *Tamarindus indica*, *Acacia arabica*, and two varieties of blackwood (*Dalbergia Sisu* and *Dalbergia latifolia*)—there is a specific group of trees valued primarily for their berries, nuts, or fruits, regardless of the quality of their timber.

Among these valuable fruit-bearing species are:
*   **Mango** (*Mangifera indica*)
*   **Jack** (*Artocarpus integrifolia*)
*   **Zizypkus** (*Zizypkus Jujuba*)

Other trees in this category include *Aegle Marmelos*, *Terminalia Chebula*, *Pongamia glabra*, *Bassia latifolia*, and *Calophyllum Inophyllum*.

## Related Flora and Forest Products
The botanical landscape of the presidency also includes various palms, specifically the areca catechu, date, palmyra, and cocoa-nut. Additionally, the forests provide non-timber resources; jungle tribes are known to collect gum from several tree varieties, and the Forest Department in Sind generates a small amount of revenue from lac.
