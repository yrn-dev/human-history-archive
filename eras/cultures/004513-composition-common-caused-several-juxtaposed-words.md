# Composition

In the context of the Human History Archive, the term "composition" appears across various disciplines, ranging from linguistics and music to geology, anatomy, and literary production.

## Linguistic Composition and Polysyntheticism
In certain languages, composition is a common process where juxtaposed words are combined and contracted. This results in words becoming partially fused with one another, a linguistic process identified as polysyntheticism. Examples of this process include the combination of the word for "cloud" (_odei_) and the word for "noise" (_ots_) to form the word for "thunder" (_odots_). Similarly, the words for "forehead" (_belar_) and "foot" (_oin_) combine to create _belaun_, meaning "knee" or the front of the foot.

## Literary and Musical Compositions
The term composition is frequently used to describe the creation of intellectual and artistic works.

### Literary Works
A "book" is defined as the common name for any literary production of some bulk. Specifically, it refers to a printed composition that forms a volume or a single organic literary work consisting of more than one volume. The term is also used to describe the internal sections or divisions of a comprehensive work. In the context of the Alexandrine library, compositions were put in order and corrected by skillful hands and then copied, creating what is known as a cyclic edition.

### Musical Works
In music, the term refers to the written works of composers. For example, the compositions of Wilhelm Friedermann Bach include instrumental works—most notably fantasias, polonaises, and fugues for clavier, as well as a sestet for horns, clarinet, and strings—and many church cantatas.

## Scientific and Physical Composition
The term is also applied to the makeup of biological and geological structures.

### Anatomy
In anatomy, the composition of the brachial plexus—formed by four or five of the lowest cervical nerves—varies significantly. This variation occurs not only between different species but also individually.

### Geology
In geology, the term describes the makeup of rocks. Greywacke (or grauwacke), a German term for a grey earthy rock, is described as highly composite. These gritty, impure rocks are characterized by a complex composition and are common among Cambrian and Silurian rocks. They consist of a variety of minerals, including plagioclase, orthoclase, quartz, iron oxides, and graphitic carbonaceous matters, along with fragments of quartzite, gneiss, slate, chert, and felsite. Other minerals present may include augite, hornblende, garnet, apatite, epidote, tourmaline, and chlorite. The cementing material for these composite deposits may be calcareous, argillaceous, or siliceous.
