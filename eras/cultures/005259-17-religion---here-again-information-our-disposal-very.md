# Religion

The study of religion serves as a window into the genius, character, and soul of history, providing insight into the manners, disposition, and laws of various nations. This is exemplified by the approach of Ulysses in Homer's *Odyssey*, who sought to inform himself exactly of the customs of the people in the cities he visited.

## Heathen Angli Beliefs
Information regarding the religion of the heathen Angli is limited, but evidence suggests the worship of several specific deities. These include Ti (Tig), Woden, Thunor, and the goddess Frigg; these figures are the sources of the names for Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, and Friday. Ti is likely the same deity referred to by early Roman writers as Mars, while Thunor was the thunder-god. Most royal families claimed descent from Woden, and Seaxneat, the ancestor of the East Saxon dynasty, was likely a god as well.

While there is no clear evidence of anthropomorphic representations of these gods, the archive notes the existence of shrines in sacred enclosures where sacrifices were performed. The priesthood consisted of persons specially set apart who were forbidden from riding—except on mares—or bearing arms. References to sacred stones, springs, groves, and trees are more common in the records than mentions of the gods themselves. Additionally, the records mention magic, charms, valkyries, and witches. For example, certain Runic letters were believed to possess the power to loosen bonds. It is probable that their beliefs regarding a future life and the spirit world mirrored those found in Scandinavian religion.

## Early Christianity and Jesus
Jesus, the Christ of Christianity, was born in Judea during the reign of Augustus Cæsar and appeared as a prophet in the reign of Tiberius Cæsar. He preached in the manner of preceding Jewish prophets and was approximately thirty years of age. 

The primary direct sources for the life and teachings of Jesus are the four Gospels. While critics suggest the Gospel of St. Mark is the most trustworthy account of his actual words and personality, and the Gospel of St. John is viewed as being colored by Hellenic theology, all four agree on a definite personality. The first three gospels (Matthew, Mark, and Luke) are thought by some to be derived from earlier documents. Despite the presence of incredible or miraculous additions, these accounts convey a conviction of reality similar to early accounts of Buddha.

## The Christian Community in the Roman Empire
The religion arising from Jesus eventually became the official religion of the Roman Empire. During its growth, the community faced periods of severe persecution. This included the systematic destruction of Christian churches, the hunting of copies of Holy Writ, and the imprisonment of bishops and presbyters. 

The severity of these persecutions is a subject of historical debate. While some estimates, such as those by Gibbon, suggest approximately two thousand victims, others note that the Christian community had become a considerable part of the population, and some officials charged with enforcing the edicts were themselves of the proscribed faith. Galerius, who controlled the eastern provinces and was a vigorous persecutor, eventually granted toleration on his deathbed in 371, recognizing the futility of attacking such a large community.

An imperial edict regarding the "deluded Christians" described them as having renounced the ceremonies of their fathers in favor of "extravagant laws and opinions." The edict eventually permitted them to freely profess private opinions and assemble in conventicles without fear, provided they respected established laws and government. In return, the empire hoped the Christians would offer prayers to their deity for the safety and prosperity of the republic and the emperors.

## Other Religious Traditions
In India, Brahminism and Buddhism prospered side by side. The coexistence of these two religions occurred amidst various shifts in political power, including the empire of the Guptas and the Kushans, though these political changes had little impact on the religion or ordinary way of life of the Indian people.

In the context of Jewish tradition, records from Josephus mention the constitution of the first high priest, which required the approbation of the people and an excellent character for virtuous actions. Additionally, Josephus's copies of the Pentateuch included foretellings by Moses regarding the building, burning, and rebuilding of the temple upon the future disobedience of the Bani Israel.
