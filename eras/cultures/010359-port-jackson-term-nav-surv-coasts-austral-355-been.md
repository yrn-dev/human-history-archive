# Port Jackson Term

The "Port Jackson term" is a designation used by Captain King in 1827 to refer to the word "boomerang." This term was derived from the native name used by a single tribe located in New South Wales.

## The Boomerang
The boomerang is a missile weapon utilized by the Australian aborigines and other peoples. It is sickle-shaped and constructed from wood. The physical proportions of the weapon are generally modeled such that the thickness is approximately 1/6th of the breadth, while the breadth is 1/12th of the length. The total length of these weapons varies from 6 inches to between 3 and 4 feet.

There are two primary types of boomerangs:
*   **The return boomerang:** Usually weighing some 8 oz. and measuring 2 to 3 feet in length. In Australia, these are always curved at an angle of 90 degrees or more. They feature a skew where the arms are twisted 2 or 3 degrees from the plane running through the center of the weapon. The cross section is asymmetrical, with a convex upper side and a lower side that is flat or nearly flat. This specific type must be thrown with the right hand.
*   **The non-return or war boomerang:** This type is similar to the return boomerang but possesses a skew in the opposite direction.

Contrary to some beliefs, the boomerang is not onomatopoeic, as it whistles in the air rather than making a "boom" sound. It has also been erroneously connected with the spear-thrower, known as the *womera*.

## Global Distribution and Similar Instruments
Both the return and non-return types of boomerangs are found in most parts of Australia. Similar instruments exist in other regions:
*   **Africa:** General Pitt-Rivers indicated that the return form was used in ancient Egypt. A weapon closely resembling the boomerang survives in North-East Africa, which has evolved into allied metal forms such as throwing knives.
*   **Asia:** Among the Dravidians of South India, there is a boomerang-shaped instrument made of steel or ivory that can be made to return.
*   **North America:** The Hopis (Moquis) of Arizona utilize a non-return form of the weapon.

Despite these similarities, it remains uncertain if the instruments found in India and Egypt have a real resemblance to the Australian return boomerang.

## Geography of Port Jackson
Port Jackson is located in Australia. Within this area is Darling Harbour, on the western shore of which sits the town of Balmain in Cumberland county. Balmain is a suburban town situated 2 miles by water from Sydney. As of 1901, Balmain had a population of 30,881 and served as a home for many of Sydney's working classes. 

Established as a municipality in 1860, Balmain is a center for industry, housing important docks and some of the largest factories. Its industrial establishments include:
*   Iron foundries and saw-mills.
*   Chemicals, glass, and soap works.
*   Shipbuilding yards.
*   A cocoanut-oil factory, which operates in connection with the soap-manufacture at Port Sunlight, England.
