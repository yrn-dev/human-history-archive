# British Central Africa: Physical Features and Geography

## Territorial Overview
British Central Africa comprises a territory of approximately 250,000 square miles. The region's geography is defined by a complex system of basins; more than two-thirds of the country is situated within the Zambezi basin, specifically within the subordinate basins of the rivers Luengwe-Kafukwe and Luangwa, as well as Lake Nyasa. Other portions of the territory drain into Lake Tanganyika, the river Congo, and the salt lake Chilwa. 

The southern border of the territory is marked by the north bank of the Zambezi river, extending from the confluence of the Luangwa at Zumbo to the confluence of the Kafukwe. East of Zumbo, the territory is separated from the Zambezi by Portuguese possessions.

## Lakes and Water Bodies
The territory includes several significant lakes and wetlands:
*   **Lake Nyasa:** Two-thirds of this lake are within the territory's limits.
*   **Lake Tanganyika:** The territory includes the southern end of this lake.
*   **Lake Mweru:** More than half of this lake is contained within the borders.
*   **Lake Bangweulu:** The territory encompasses the entirety of this lake.
*   **Lake Chilwa:** A salt lake (referred to by the Bantu word *Kilwa*, though sometimes wrongly styled Shirwa) located on the borders of the Portuguese province of Moçambique. It currently has no outlet, though it likely emptied into the Lujenda river and the Indian Ocean in past ages.
*   **Other Water Bodies:** The region includes Lake Malombe on the upper Shiré, the Great Mweru Swamp (located between Mweru and Tanganyika), and Moir's Lake, a small mountain tarn or possible crater lake situated between the Luapula and Luangwa rivers.

## River Systems
The river systems of British Central Africa are extensive, though their nomenclature can be complex. The Luangwa (also known as Loangwa) is an independent affluent of the Zambezi. In contrast, the Luanga (or Lunga) is a tributary of the Luengwe-Kafukwe, which is frequently referred to as the Kafue.

Other primary rivers include:
*   **The Shiré:** Nearly the whole course of this river is within the territory.
*   **The Chambezi:** The territory includes the whole of this river, which is identified as the most remote of the headwaters of the river Congo.
*   **The Luapula:** Also known as the upper Congo, the territory includes the right or east bank of this river from its exit from Lake Bangweulu to its issue from the north end of Lake Mweru.

## Lake Bangweulu and the Congo Headwaters
Lake Bangweulu is a shallow lake formed by the head streams of the Congo, situated at an elevation of about 3,700 feet above sea level. It is located between 10° 38' and 11° 31' S. and is cut by 30° E. The lake is characterized by a central basin in an extensive plateau, with land sloping gently toward the depression from the north, east, and south. Much of the area is a morass of papyrus and reeds.

The lake's open water extends approximately 60 miles north to south and 40 miles east to west. It contains several islands, including the largest, Kirui (Chiru) on the east side, as well as Mbawali (20 miles long by 3 broad) and the centrally located Kisi (Chishi). Chifunawuli, the western pan of the lake, is almost separated from the main body of water by long, narrow sandbanks.

The Luapula river leaves Bangweulu at its southernmost point, measuring about a mile wide at the outflow before narrowing to roughly 300 yards. Near this outflow lies Lake Kampolombo (20 miles long and 8 broad), separated from the Luapula by a forest-clad tongue of land called Kapata and from Bangweulu by a sandy track. The Chambezi river flows into the swamp on the east side of Bangweulu, where its waters mingle with those of the Luapula without entering the lake itself.

## Linguistic Groups of the Region
The territory is home to various linguistic groups:
*   **Bisa or Wisa:** This family includes dialects such as *Lubisa* and *Ilala*, found between the Luangwa river to the east and south, and Lake Bangweulu and the Chambezi river to the north.
*   **Bemba or Emba:** This group occupies the area between the Upper Chambezi river, Lake Mweru, and the south and south-west coasts of Tanganyika. It includes the *Ki-lungu* and *Ki-mambwe* modifications.
*   **Tumbuka:** This section, including the *Henga* and *A-tonga* peoples, occupies the region between the Upper Luangwa river and the western shores of Lake Nyasa.
*   **North Nyasa or Nkonde:** This group comprises dialects of the north and north-west coasts of Lake Nyasa, such as *Iki-nyikiusa* and *Ici-wandia*, as well as *Ishi-nyi[chi]a* of the Nyasa-Tanganyika plateau.
