# De Brazi

## Etymology and Early Cartography
The term *De Brazi* appears in historical cartographic records as a designation for a specific geographic area. In a map dating from 1351, the western group of islands in the Western Ocean—now identified as the Azores—was named the Brazil Island (*De Brazi*). During this period, the word "Brazil" was employed as a general term for any red dye-stuff.

## The Azores and Early Exploration
The Azores are described by Arabian geographers, including Ibn-al-Wardi in the 14th century and Edrisi in the 12th century, who noted nine islands in the Western Ocean located north of the Canaries. These writers characterized the islands as having been populous with cities of some magnitude, though they noted that the inhabitants had been significantly reduced by intestine warfare. 

While it has been conjectured that the Genoese were the discoverers, there is insufficient evidence to confirm this. Other accounts mention a "Flemish discovery" by van der Berg, who was reportedly driven to the islands in 1432, an event that drew interest from the court of Lisbon. Following this, the navigator Gonzalo Velho Cabral was sent to pursue the discovery. An alternative version suggests that Prince Henry the Navigator of Portugal possessed a map of the islands and dispatched Cabral based on confidence in that map's accuracy.

## Brazil in South America
In the context of South America, Brazil is noted for its diverse natural resources and political history.

### Natural Resources and Biology
Brazil is a habitat for various species of macaws, including the red-and-yellow species (*A. chloroptera*). The region is also a source of vegetable dyestuffs; specifically, the plant *Bixa orellana* grows in Brazil and South America. The pulpy mass surrounding its seeds, known as annatto, is used to dye cotton and silk an orange colour in an alkaline or soap bath, which turns red when acids are applied. Geologically, Brazil contains a series of gypsiferous marls and red sandstones, similar to the *formacion petrolifera* found in Argentina.

### Political History and Statehood
Brazil's political trajectory differed from the Spanish colonies in America. In 1807, the Portuguese monarchy fled to Brazil after French armies under Napoleon occupied Portugal, effectively making Portugal a dependency of Brazil. In 1822, Brazil declared itself a separate Empire under Pedro I, a son of the Portuguese King. This monarchical system lasted until 1889, when the Emperor was shipped back to Europe and the United States of Brazil became a republic.

## Orders and Honors
Several orders of merit have been associated with Brazil:
* **The Order of the Tower and Sword:** Founded in Brazil in 1808 by the regent (later King John VI of Portugal) as a revival of the old Order of the Sword. It was reconstituted in 1832 as a general order of military and civil merit consisting of five classes.
* **Abolished Orders:** Brazil has abolished several orders, including the *Order of the Southern Cross* (1822), *Dom Pedro I* (1826), and *the Rose* (1829). It also abolished Brazilian branches of Portuguese orders, specifically the orders of *Christ*, *St James*, and *St Benedict of Aviz*.
