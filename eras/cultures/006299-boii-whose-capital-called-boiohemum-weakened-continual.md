# Boii

The Boii were a Celtic people whose original homeland was Gallia Transalpina. Their name may perhaps be translated as "the terrible." Belonging almost certainly to the Early Iron Age, the Boii are characterized as having been a race of large men, a conclusion drawn from the size of the handles of the long iron swords they likely used to deliver cutting blows. Evidence from tombs at La Tene, Hallstatt, and other locations suggests that they were fairly civilized.

## Migrations and Settlements
At an early date, the Boii split into two primary groups: one migrated into Germany and the other into Italy. The group entering Italy crossed the Pennine Alps via the Great St Bernard into the Po valley. After finding the area occupied, they drove out the Umbrians and Etruscans, establishing themselves as far as the Apennines in the region of modern Romagna. According to Cato, this population comprised as many as 112 different tribes.

While some Boii migrated, others remained in their original homeland. During the Second Punic War, a Boian prince named Magalus offered to guide Hannibal into Italy after the crossing of the Pyrenees.

Other movements of the Boii included:
* **The Danube and Bohemia:** Most authorities assume a Boii settlement existed on the Danube from a very early period in part of modern Bohemia, an area known as *Boiohemum* ("land of the Boii").
* **Noricum and Pannonia:** Around 60 B.C., some Boii migrated to these regions.
* **Gaul:** Approximately 32,000 Boii joined a Helvetian expedition into Gaul, where they shared in a defeat near Bibracte.
* **The Black Forest:** The Boii penetrated the Black Forest, leaving traces of their name in Bohemia.

## Conflict with Rome
The Boii were known to the Romans at least by the time of Plautus, who referred to them contemptuously in the *Captivi*. Their relationship with Rome was marked by several wars:
* In 283, the Boii were defeated alongside the Etruscans at the Vadimonian lake.
* In 224, following the battle of Telamon in Etruria, they were forced to submit.

Despite this submission, the Boii maintained a hatred for Rome. Irritated by the establishment of the Roman colonies of Placentia and Cremona, they provided valuable assistance to Hannibal during the Second Punic War (218). They continued their struggle against Rome from 201 to 191, at which point they were finally subdued by P. Cornelius Scipio Nasica and lost nearly half of their territory. Following this, Strabo records that they were driven back across the Alps to settle on the land of the Taurisci on the Danube, adjoining Raetia and Vindelicia.

## Decline and Legacy in Bohemia
In the region of Bohemia, the Boii established a capital called Boiohemum. According to ancient traditions accepted by modern historians of Bohemia, the tribe was weakened by continual warfare with neighboring tribes. They were eventually subdued around 12 B.C. by the Marcomanni, a Teutonic tribe. 

The Boii who settled in Noricum were later mingled with the Lombards and became known as the Boiovarii or Boio Arii (Bavaria), with "var" appearing in some German dialects to mean descendants or remains.

Following the Boii, the Marcomanni were expelled by other Teutonic tribes. Eventually, Slavic tribes conquered Bohemia, with the Cechs becoming the most important among them. Archaeological research indicates Slavic inhabitants were present in Bohemia as early as the beginning of the Christian era, though the Cechs are believed to have become the masters of the country in the 5th century.
