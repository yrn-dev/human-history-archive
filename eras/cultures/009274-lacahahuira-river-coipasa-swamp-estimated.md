# Lacahahuira River and the Coipasa Swamp

## Overview of the Central Plateau Drainage
The Lacahahuira river and the Cienegas de Coipasa are part of the drainage system of the great central plateau, also known as the *alta-planicie*. This region is recognized as one of the most elevated lacustrine basins in the world. While the basin once drained toward the east, it currently possesses no surface outlet. Instead, the drainage of this extensive district is entirely absorbed by evaporation and the dry soil of the desert.

## The Hydrographic Sequence
The water system of the *alta-planicie* begins with Lake Titicaca, which is situated near the northern end of the plateau at an elevation of 12,644 feet above sea level. Lake Titicaca receives water from several short streams from the surrounding heights. Its waters discharge through the Desaguadero, a sluggish river that flows south for 184 miles. As the Desaguadero flows, its depth gradually diminishes until it reaches Lake Pampa Aullaguas, which is also referred to as Lake Poopo. The Desaguadero is navigable for small craft and is fed by two or three small tributaries originating from the west.

## The Lacahahuira and Coipasa
Lake Pampa Aullaguas receives water from two small streams. The lake has a small outlet known as the Lacahahuira. The Lacahahuira river flows west for a distance of 60 miles, eventually emptying into the Cienegas de Coipasa, which are salt-swamps.

## Related Regional Drainage
In the extreme south of this region, the Rio Grande de Lipez is also absorbed by the desert and evaporation in a manner similar to the drainage of the central plateau.
