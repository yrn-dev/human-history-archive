# Kej Tributaries and the Sources of the Hingol

The Kej tributaries and the sources of the Hingol river are located within the southern region of Baluchistan. This area is characterized by a complex network of internal communication and diverse ethnographic compositions, situated between the interior deserts and the coastal regions.

## Geography and Hydrography
The Hingol, known in ancient times as the Tomerus, is one of the considerable streams of Southern Baluchistan that drains the Makran region into the Arabian Sea. The drainage of this great block of Southern Baluchistan generally trends toward the west and south, flowing either into the Arabian Sea or into the central swamps of Mashkel and Lora.

The Kej tributaries are associated with narrow valleys. In the context of Arabic medieval geography, the term "Kej" (along with "Kach," "Kir," or "Katz") is used as a prefix to denote valleys or low-level flats, specifically to distinguish these areas from hills or mountains. This region was part of the ancient Kaiani kingdom.

## Ethnic Composition and Tribal Groups
The areas enclosed within the narrow valleys of the Kej tributaries and around the sources of the Hingol are inhabited by "tribes innumerable." These populations are described as remnants of races that can be traced through the records of recent immigration or recognized in the works of Herodotus.

These groups are situated geographically between the fish-eating populations of the coast and the Naushirwanis of the Mashkel and Kharan deserts. The Naushirwanis are described as a purely Persian race who entered Baluchistan during historic times, though the specific date is not known. They are considered identical to the Tahukani or Tahuki found in Perso-Baluchistan.

## Broader Regional Ethnography
The wider region of Baluchistan contains several other distinct groups:

*   **Nomadic Tribes:** The central desert contains scattered nomads known as Rekis (desert people). The most numerous of these are the Mohamadani, who are likely of Arab origin and occupy the central mountain region.
*   **Persian Population:** An underlying Persian population, referred to as Tajik, is scattered throughout the country. This group is often represented by agricultural bondsmen or slaves, such as the Nakibs. Other examples include the Dehkans (or Dehwars) and the Durzadas, who are found throughout Makran.
*   **Arab Influence:** Arab influence is strongly impressed upon the ethnography of the region. All Rind tribes, including the Bozdars and the Marri and Bugti sections, claim extraction from Koraish and an Arab origin. The Arabs occupied southern Baluchistan and Seistan from an early date and later spread through the Sind valley until the 12th century.
