# Tornaria

## Overview and Classification
The *Tornaria* is a larval form associated with the Enteropneusta, a group of organisms that exhibits a comparatively uniform plan of external form and composition. Within the Enteropneusta, development occurs via two distinct schemes: direct and indirect. While direct development—characterized by a gradual succession of stages without striking metamorphosis—appears limited to the family *Balanoglossidae*, the families *Ptychoderidae* and *Spengelidae* contain species that likely follow an indirect course of development. The *Tornaria* larva is the culmination of this indirect developmental process, eventually undergoing a metamorphosis to reach the adult form.

## Physical Characteristics
The *Tornaria* larva is characterized by being transparent and pelagic. Its physical structure is distinct and differs significantly from that of the adult organism. A primary feature of the *Tornaria* is a complicated ciliated seam known as the longitudinal ciliated band. This band is frequently drawn out into lappets and convoluted bays. Additionally, the larva possesses a dorsal water-pore that communicates with the anterior body-cavity.

## Biological Affinities and Development
Due to its external form, the *Tornaria* larva bears a certain similarity to the larvae of Echinoderms, such as sea-cucumbers, star-fishes, and sea-urchins; indeed, it was described as such when first discovered. The possession of the dorsal water-pore and the larva's general form suggest the possibility of a remote affinity between the Enteropneusta and the Echinoderms. 

In contrast to the indirect development of the *Tornaria*, direct development in this group involves the three divisions of the coelom arising as pouches that are constricted off from the primitive gut, or archenteron. This specific process resembles the development of the mesoblastic somites found in *Amphioxus*. While direct development provides insight into the special plan of organization for the Enteropneusta, the indirect development represented by the *Tornaria* provides a potential clue regarding their derivation.
