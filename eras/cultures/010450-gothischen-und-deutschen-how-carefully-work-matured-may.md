# Gothischen, und Deutschen

The terms "Gothischen" (Gothic) and "Deutschen" (German) appear across various scholarly disciplines within the archive, most notably in the fields of comparative linguistics, philology, history, and law.

## Comparative Linguistics and Grammar
A significant contribution to the study of these languages was made by Bopp, who was appointed to the chair of comparative grammar and Sanskrit at Berlin in 1821. His primary work, the *Vergleichende Grammatik des Sanskrit, Zend, Griechischen, Lateinischen, Litthauischen, Altslavischen, Gothischen, und Deutschen* (Comparative Grammar of Sanskrit, Zend, Greek, Latin, Lithuanian, Old-Slavonic, Gothic, and German), was published in six parts between 1833 and 1852. 

The development of this work was preceded by a series of monographs printed in the *Transactions of the Berlin Academy* from 1824 to 1831, titled *Vergleichende Zergliederung des Sanskrits und der mit ihm verwandten Sprachen*. Bopp's objective in this comparative study was threefold:
1. To describe the original grammatical structure of the languages based on their intercomparison.
2. To trace their phonetic laws.
3. To investigate the origin of their grammatical forms.

Bopp's research utilized the best available sources and incorporated new findings. The work was translated into English by E.B. Eastwick in 1845 and into French by Professor Michel Breal in 1866. A revised second German edition (1856–1861) also included Old-Armenian.

## Economics and Banking
In the modern era, the German banking system has been the subject of several analyses. These include Dr. W. Scharling's *Bank Politik* (1900) and his history of the Reichsbank (1876–1900). Other comparative and specific studies include Dr. Adolf Weber's comparison of German and English banking (*Depositenbanken und Spekulationsbanken*, 1902), Siegfried Buff's work on *Das Kontokurrentgeschaft im deutschen Bankwerbe* (1904), and Dr. Riesser's investigation into the development of large German banks (*Zur Entwicklungsgeschichte der deutschen Grossbanken*, 1905).
