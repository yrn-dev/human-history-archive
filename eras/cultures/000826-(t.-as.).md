## Ornithology: Kestrel Species
In the study of birds, the abbreviation "T." is used to categorize various species of kestrels across different global regions.

### African and Island Species
Several species are identified in Africa, including *T. rupicola*, which is common in the Cape, as well as *T. rupicoloides* and *T. alopex*, both of which are found in the interior. Specific islands within the Ethiopian region host peculiar species, such as *T. newtoni* in Madagascar, *T. punctatus* in Mauritius, and *T. gracilis* in the Seychelles. Additionally, *T. neglectus* is the designated kestrel of the Cape Verde Islands.

### American and Australasian Species
The *T. sparverius* is known as the "sparrow-hawk" in the United States and Canada. While some ornithologists, such as Bowdler Sharpe, have recognized up to six species based on locality, others, like R. Ridgway, recognize only three: *T. sparverius*, *T. leucophrys*, and *T. sparverioides*. 

Ridgway further identifies five geographical races of *T. sparverius*:
*   The typical *T. sparverius* (North American continent, excluding the Gulf of Mexico coast).
*   *T. australis* (South American continent, excluding the Caribbean and North Atlantic coasts).
*   *T. isabellinus* (Continental America from Florida to French Guiana).
*   *T. dominicensis* (Lesser Antilles up to St Thomas).
*   *T. cinnamominus* (Western Brazil and Chile).

Regarding other classifications, *T. leucophrys* is associated with Cuba and Haiti, while *T. sparverioides* is considered peculiar to Cuba, though this validity was contested by Dr Gundlach in 1876. Later findings in 1881 suggested that *T. australis* and *T. cinnamominus* are inseparable, and that Ridgway's *T. leucophrys* should be renamed *T. dominicensis*.

In other regions, *T. moluccensis* is spread throughout the Malay Archipelago, and *T. cenchroides* inhabits Australia and Tasmania. No kestrels are found in New Zealand, although the *Hieracidea* (or *Harpe*) *novae-zelandiae* is a similar form.
