# Bantu Language Groups of West and Central Africa

The distribution of Bantu languages in West and Central Africa is characterized by several distinct groups and dialect clusters, extending from the coast-lands of the Cameroon region southwards toward the Loango and Sete Kama coast.

## Northern and Coastal Groups
In the coast-lands north of the Cameroon delta (Ambas Bay) and on the west slopes of the Cameroon Mountains, the *Isubu-Bakwiri* group is located. Nearby, on the island of Fernando Po, are the Bantu dialects of *Bani*, *Bateti*, and *Ediya*, which are distantly allied to other groups.

Further east, the *Barondo-Bakundu* group begins at the Rio del Rey, situated on the extremity of the Bantu field near the estuary of the Cross river. This group encompasses various dialects, including *Bas[=a]*, *Barombi*, *Abo*, *Nkosi*, and *Bonken*, which are described as much-debased. These are spoken on the Cameroon river (Magombe), the eastern slopes of the Cameroon mountains, and extending to the Nyong and Sanaga rivers.

## Semi-Bantu and Transition Zones
To the north-east and east of the *Barondo-Bakundu* group, certain languages are classified as "semi-Bantu." These include *Bafut*, *Bayon*, *Mbudikum*, *Nki*, *Bati*, and *Mbe*. These languages show affinities with the *Fan* and with speech forms found in the basin of the Central Benue river.

## Southern Extensions
Turning southwards from the north-westernmost limit of the Bantu field, the archive identifies the *Aduma* and *Mpongwe-Orungu* languages of French Gabun. This region also includes the tongues of the Fernan Vaz promontory and the Lower Ogowe.

Continuing further south, these languages transition into the *Kakongo* dialects of the Sete Kama and Loango coast. This group includes *Ka-kongo* (*Kabinda*), *Ba-kamba*, *Ba-vili*, *Ma-yombe*, *Ba-nyanga*, and *Ba-kama*.
