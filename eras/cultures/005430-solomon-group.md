# Solomon Group

The term "Solomon Group" appears in the archive's records in two distinct contexts: as a geographical designation for a collection of islands in the Pacific Ocean and in relation to the historical and religious accounts of the figure Solomon.

## Geographical Solomon Group
The Solomon Group refers to a collection of islands in the Western Pacific. In the context of colonial administration, the Southern Solomon islands were included in a high commission created by order in 1877. This commission provided jurisdiction over various islands not already under the control of other colonial governments or civilized powers, leading to the establishment of protectorates over these islands by 1900.

Additionally, the archive notes that the island known as Ongtong Java is part of the Solomon Islands. Scientific research has been conducted on the region's geology; specifically, H. B. Guppy published a work in 1885 titled "The Recent Calcareous Formations of the Solomon Group" in the *Transactions of the Royal Society of Edinburgh*.

## The Hosts of Solomon
According to sacred texts, Solomon commanded diverse and organized hosts. These armies consisted of humankind, jinns, and birds, all of whom were marshalled and kept in ranks or battle order. 

An account describes these hosts reaching a valley of ants. Upon their arrival, an ant warned other ants to enter their dwellings to avoid being crushed by Solomon and his hosts, who would have done so without knowing it. Upon hearing the ant's speech, Solomon smiled and prayed to his Lord to be enabled to be grateful for the favors bestowed upon him and his parents, to perform righteousness pleasing to God, and to be included among the ranks of His righteous servants.

## Reign and Administration
Solomon succeeded his father, David, to the throne. His early reign involved the removal of enemies, including the murder of his brother—who had sought the throne—and the execution of Joab by Benaiah within the Tabernacle. Solomon also replaced a hostile chief priest with his own adherent, Zadok.

Solomon's administration was characterized by significant wealth and infrastructure projects. He maintained an alliance with Hiram, King of Sidon, which facilitated trade and the building of shipping on the Red Sea, resulting in the accumulation of great wealth in Jerusalem. To support his building projects, Solomon utilized gang labor, organizing porters throughout the land and sending relays of men to Lebanon to cut cedarwood under Hiram.

## Architectural Works and Religious Shifts
Solomon is noted for several major construction projects:
*   **The Temple:** Solomon built a temple of cedar-sheathed stone to house the Ark of the Covenant, which had previously been kept in a large tent. Inside the temple's inner chamber, the ark was placed between two gilded olivewood winged figures. He also created twelve brazen oxen and cherubims, following patterns left by David.
*   **The Royal Palace:** He constructed a costly and splendid palace for himself.
*   **City Fortifications:** Solomon fortified the city of Jerusalem and built other great cities.

Under Solomon, the religious practice of the Israelites shifted; sacrifices, which had previously been offered at various high places, were henceforth restricted to the altar before the ark in the temple.

## Later Life and Succession
Solomon's reign lasted eighty years, and he lived to the age of ninety-four. He is described as having been superior to other kings in wisdom, riches, and happiness. However, records indicate that in his later years, he was deluded by women and transgressed the law. This period of adversity is linked to the rise of Ader (or Hadad) and Jeroboam.

Jeroboam, an ambitious young man, attempted to persuade the people to forsake Solomon. When Solomon discovered this treachery and sought to kill him, Jeroboam fled to Shishak, the king of Egypt, where he remained until Solomon's death. Following Solomon's passing, he was buried in Jerusalem. His son, Rehoboam, succeeded him, but the people subsequently forsook Rehoboam and ordained Jeroboam as king over the ten tribes.
