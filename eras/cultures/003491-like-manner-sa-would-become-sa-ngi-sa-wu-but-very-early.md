# Bantu Languages: Pronouns and Linguistic Structure

The Bantu languages exhibit a complex system of pronouns, verbal modifications, and noun classifications. These linguistic elements are characterized by specific root forms and evolutionary contractions over time.

## Pronouns and Negation
Pronouns in Bantu languages are generally traceable to a set of general forms. The first person ("I, me, my") is associated with forms such as *ngi*, *mi*, and *ngu*. The second person ("thou, thee, thy") is linked to *gwe*, *ku*, and *-ko*. Third-person singular forms ("he or she, him, her, his") include *a-*, *ya-*, and *wa-* in the nominative, as well as *ngu-* (which evolves into *yu-*, *ye-*, *wu-*, *hu-*, or *u-*), *-mu* in the accusative, and *-ka* or *-kwe* for possessives. In the Western Bantu sphere, *ndi* is also used for nominative and possessive forms.

Plural pronouns include *isu*, *swi-*, *tu-*, and *ti-* for "we, us, our" (with *-tu-* for accusative and *-itu* for possessive). For "ye, you, your," forms include *inu*, *mu-*, *nyu-*, *nyi-*, and *-ni* (with *-nu* or *-mu-* for accusative and *-inu* for possessive). The third-person plural ("they, them, their") uses *babo* and *ba-*, with *-ba-* for the accusative and *-babo* for the possessive.

Negation is often expressed through particles attached to pronouns. For example, *ka-ngi* means "not I" (as in *ka-ngi tanda*, meaning "not I love"), *ka-ku* or *ka-wu* means "not thou," *ka-a* means "not he, she," *ka-tu* means "not we," *ka-nu* means "not ye," and *ka-ba* means "not they." Similarly, the particle *sa* can become *sa-ngi* or *sa-wu*. 

Historically, these forms underwent contraction: *ka-ngi* or *sa-ngi* became *kai* and *sai*, and eventually *ki* and *si*. *Ka-ku* or *ka-wu* contracted into *ku*, while *kaa* or *saa* remained *ka* or *sa*. In some modern languages, negative particles like *ti* or *si* are used independently of pronouns. In other instances, negation is expressed via stress, an adverb, or a suffix traceable to the roots *-pe* or *-ko*.

## Verbal Forms and Roots
The Bantu verb system includes specific modifications for mood and negation. In several languages, a verb used in a negative sense changes its terminal *-a* to *-i*. The subjunctive mood is frequently formed by changing the terminal *-a* to *-e*; for instance, the verb *tanda* (love) becomes *tande* (may love).

The verbal particle *li* or *di* means "to be." Other forms include *-ira* or *-era*, and occasionally *[vs]a*. An example of this is *aka* (to be on fire), which becomes *a[vs]a* (to set on fire). While *tanda* is a common root used to demonstrate these variations and is found in Zulu, it does not exhibit all these variations within Zulu itself. In other languages where all such variations exist, the root *tanda* may be replaced by *londa* or *randa*.

## Noun Classes
Bantu languages utilize a system of noun classes, often identified by specific prefixes:
* **Class 1:** *Umu-* (Ngu-mu-)
* **Class 2:** *Aba* (Mba-ba or Nga-ba)
* **Class 3:** *Umu-* (Ngu-mu-)
* **Class 4:** *Imi-* (Ngi-mi-)
* **Class 5:** *Idi* (Ndi-di-)
* **Class 6:** *Ama-* (Nga-ma-)
* **Class 7:** *Iki-* (Nki-ki-)
* **Class 8:** *Ibi-* (Mbi-bi-)
* **Class 9:** *I-n-* or *I-ni-* (?Ngi-ni-)
* **Class 10:** *Iti-*, *Izi-*, *Iti-n-*, *Izi-n-* (?Ngi-ti-)
* **Class 11:** *Ulu* (Ndu-du-)
* **Class 12:** *Utu* (?Ntu-tu-), often serving a diminutive sense.
* **Class 13:** *Aka* (?Nka-ka-), usually diminutive and sometimes honorific.
* **Class 14:** *Ubu-* (?Mbu-bu-), generally used for abstract nouns and sometimes in a plural sense.
* **Class 15:** *Uku* (?Nku-ku-), used as an infinitive with verbs or with nouns indicating body functions; it is identical to the preposition "to."
* **Class 16:** *Apa* (Mpa-pa-), a locative used to indicate place or position, identical to the adverb "here."

## First Person Variations
The first-person pronoun *mi* is possibly a softening of *ngi* or *ni*. In certain dialects, *ngi* evolves into *nji*, *ndi*, *ni*, or *mbi*. In north-eastern Congo and some coast Cameroon languages, the word *mbi* or *mba* is used for "I" or "me," which appears to be borrowed from the Sudanian Mundu tongues. Possessive pronouns for the first person are derived from the forms *-ami* and *-angi* (including variations such as *-am*, *-angu*, *-anji*, and *-ambi*).
