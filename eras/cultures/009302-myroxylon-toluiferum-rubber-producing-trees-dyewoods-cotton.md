# Myroxylon toluiferum and Related Botanical Productions

## Myroxylon toluiferum and Balsam of Tolu
*Myroxylon toluiferum* is a tree species native to Venezuela and New Granada. It is the source of Balsam of Tolu, a substance classified as a balsam because it is a resin or oleo-resin containing cinnamic acid, benzoic acid, or both. 

Balsam of Tolu is brown in color and is thicker than the balsam of Peru. When kept over time, it attains a considerable degree of solidity. In terms of its application, it is utilized in perfumery and serves as a constituent in lozenges and cough syrups. Additionally, it is used as incense in America.

## Comparative Balsams
*Myroxylon toluiferum* is closely related to *Myroxylon Pereirae*, the lofty leguminous tree that produces balsam of Peru. While *Myroxylon toluiferum* is found over a wide area of equatorial America, *Myroxylon Pereirae* grows within a limited area in San Salvador, Central America, and has been introduced into Ceylon. 

Balsam of Peru is a fragrant, viscid oleo-resin of a deep brown or black color. While it is used in perfumery and is listed in pharmacopeias, it possesses no special medicinal virtues. In 1571, Pope Pius V granted a faculty to the bishops of the West Indies allowing the substitution of balsam of Peru for the balsam of the East during certain preparations.

## Rubber-Producing Plants
Rubber is produced by a variety of trees and vines across different global regions:
*   **Bolivia:** Rubber-producing trees are indigenous to the sub-tropical and tropical zones.
*   **Africa:** In the districts bordering Victoria Nyanza, two varieties of valuable rubber-vine—*Landolphia florida* and *Landolphia Kirkii*—are found in the forests and near the coast. In Ashanti, lianas (monkey-ropes) yield one kind of commercial rubber. In Dahomey, plantations of rubber vines and trees have been established, and rubber is a primary export. In the coast regions and shores of Victoria Nyanza, the collection of rubber is a significant employer of people.
*   **Asia:** In the province of Assam, rubber is brought in by hill tribes and is also grown in government plantations.

## Dyewoods and Natural Colorants
Dyewoods are used to produce various colors for textiles such as silk, wool, and cotton. 
*   **Logwood:** Derived from the heart-wood of *Haematoxylon campechianum* in Central America, logwood is a primary natural dyewood. It contains haematoxylin, which is changed into haematein through grinding and oxidation. It is used to dye dark blues and black; specifically, it produces dark blue or black with aluminium and chromium mordants, dark purple with tin, and black with iron.
*   **Other Dyewoods:** Other mentioned dyewoods include peachwood, sapanwood, and limawood, which contain a coloring matter closely allied to haematein. Fustic and other yellow dyewoods are used to provide the yellow portion of compound shades.

## Cotton Production
Cotton is cultivated in several diverse geographic regions for different purposes:
*   **Africa:** In the grassy uplands of the interior of Dahomey, inhabitants grow cotton of a fairly good quality for their own use. In the Kikuyu escarpment, cotton is found among the flora, and it is cultivated in the coast region and by the shores of Victoria Nyanza.
*   **Asia:** Cotton is grown in large quantities along the slopes of the Assam range.
*   **Bolivia:** Cotton is an indigenous plant of the tropical and sub-tropical zones.
