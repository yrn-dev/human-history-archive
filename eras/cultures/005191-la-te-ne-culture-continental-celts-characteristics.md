# La-Tène Culture of the Continental Celts

The La-Tène culture represents a significant artistic and civilization phase associated with the continental Celts. This culture served as a direct connection and influence for the native Late Celtic fashion developed by tribes in Britain.

## Artistic Characteristics
The art of the La-Tène culture is characterized by a flamboyant and fan-like style. This native Late Celtic fashion did not emerge in isolation but was descended from far-off Mediterranean antecedents. In Britain, the artistic expressions of the tribes were more directly connected to this continental La-Tène tradition.

## Related Celtic Populations and Settlements
The continental Celts, particularly those in Gaul, shared various cultural and linguistic ties with other Celtic-speaking peoples. In Britain, the arrival of Celtic civilization and dialects is attributed to two invasions: the Goidels during the later part of the Bronze Age, and the Brythons and Belgae during the Iron Age. By the time of Julius Caesar, most inhabitants of Britain—excluding perhaps some tribes in the far north—had adopted Celtic speech and customs.

The settlement patterns of these peoples varied significantly:
* **Fortifications:** Many dwelt in hill forts constructed with walls of rude stone or earth.
* **Domestic Dwellings:** Other populations lived in villages consisting of round huts sunk into the ground (similar to those found in parts of northern Gaul), subterranean chambered houses, or pile-dwellings located within marshes.
* **Urbanization:** In the south of Britain, town life began to emerge with the development of market centres and the use of improved house types.

## Political and Social Structure
Politically, the Celtic tribes were generally divided into separate entities that were often at war with one another. Each tribe was governed by its own princes. 

In the southern regions of Britain, a monetary system was established. This included the use of gold coinage as well as a currency consisting of iron bars or ingots. These iron ingots, as attested by surviving examples and the accounts of Caesar, typically weighed 1-1/3 lb, though some weighed two-thirds of a pound or 2-2/3 lb.

## Religious Practices
The priesthood of the Druids was the chief feature of Celtic religion in both Gaul and Britain. The Druids were characterized by the following:
* **Knowledge and Influence:** They taught a secret lore and wielded great influence over the people.
* **Rituals:** They practiced magical arts and "barbarous rites of human sacrifice."
* **Political Role:** Despite their social influence, they ordinarily took no part in politics in their capacity as Druids.
