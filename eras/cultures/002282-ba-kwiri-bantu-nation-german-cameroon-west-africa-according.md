# BA-KWIRI

The Ba-Kwiri are a Bantu nation located in West Africa, specifically within the region of German Cameroon. According to their own traditions, the Ba-Kwiri are migrants who arrived from the eastward. Their name translates to the "Brushmen."

## Social Structure and Characteristics
The Ba-Kwiri are organized into approximately sixty separate clans. They are described as an intelligent and lively people, characterized as daring hunters and brave fighters. They possess a strong development of domestic affections and are noted for their superiority in elocution, music, and songs compared to many other negro races.

Physically, the Ba-Kwiri exhibit a significant disparity between the sexes in terms of complexion and size; the women are generally much lighter in color and shorter than the men.

## Law and Religion
Within their own community, the Ba-Kwiri are open-handed and generous. However, they adhere to a merciless application of the law of blood for blood, a practice that is maintained even in instances of accidental homicide.

Their religious beliefs consist of a blend of magic, witchcraft, and ancestor-worship. They believe in the existence of both evil and good spirits, with a particular fear reserved for the spirits of the seas and the forests.

## Communication and Language
The Ba-Kwiri utilize a specialized horn language unique to their nation. Additionally, they share a "curious drum language" with their neighbors, the Dualla. This system of drum-tapping is used to convey news from one clan to another. The use of this language is restricted; women and slaves are not permitted to master it. Those who are initiated, however, are required to repeat the messages to ensure they are passed along.

Linguistically, the Ba-Kwiri are categorized within the Isubu-Bakwiri group. This group is located on the west slopes of the Cameroon Mountains and in the coast-lands north of the Cameroon delta (Ambas Bay). As a Bantu language, it belongs to the Western group, which encompasses the languages of Cameroon and the Central and Northern Congo.
