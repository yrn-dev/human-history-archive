# Flora and Environment of the Victoria Nyanza Border Districts

The districts bordering Victoria Nyanza are characterized by a typically tropical environment. In these regions, the flora closely resembles that of Uganda.

## Climate and Precipitation
The region bordering Victoria Nyanza is defined by its tropical climate, with annual rainfall exceeding 60 inches. Due to these conditions, the area is considered quite unsuitable for Europeans. 

Within the broader protectorate, the climate and vegetation are arranged in zones parallel to the coast. While the coast region is hot and receives approximately 35 inches of rain per year, the inland highlands—defined as districts over 5,000 feet in elevation—are healthier and cooler. In these highlands, the average temperature ranges from 66° F in the cool season to 73° F in the hot season, with rainfall between 40 and 50 inches. Above 7,000 feet, the climate becomes distinctly colder, and frosts occur.

The general weather patterns for the protectorate include a hottest period from December to April and a coolest period from July to September. Rainfall occurs in two primary seasons: the "greater rains" from March to June and the "smaller rains" in November and December. However, this rainfall is not as regular as is typical for tropical countries, and the region occasionally experiences severe droughts.

## Regional Flora
The vegetation of the protectorate varies significantly by geography:

*   **Coast Regions:** The characteristic trees are the coco-nut palm and the mangrove. Rubber-vines, specifically the varieties *Landolphia Kirkii* and *Landolphia florida*, are found in the forests and near the coast.
*   **Escarpments:** The Mau escarpment features vast forests of junipers and olives. On the Kikuyu escarpment, bamboo, fig, and cotton are found.
*   **Forests and Mountains:** Some regions contain dense forests of great trees with lowest branches reaching 50 feet from the ground. Ebony is found within the scrub-jungle. The higher mountains preserve distinct species that are surviving remnants of flora from a cooler period.

## Fauna of the Region
While fauna is not abundant overall, large mammals are numerous on the drier steppes. These include the giraffe, zebra, buffalo, rhinoceros, elephant, hippopotamus, lion, various antelopes, and numerous monkeys. The rhinoceros is noted as being particularly dangerous and abundant in some districts. 

In the waters of Victoria Nyanza and other large rivers, crocodiles are common. Other wildlife includes:
*   **Birds:** The larger varieties include the secretary-bird, bustard, stork, and ostrich. Other birds include the hornbill, weaver, wild pigeon, various spur fowl, and guinea fowl. Thousands of flamingoes, pelicans, and cranes are found by the banks of rivers and lakes.
*   **Other Animals:** The puff-adder is the most dangerous of the somewhat rare snakes. Insects such as mosquitoes, scorpions, and centipedes are less common than in most tropical countries, though bees are exceedingly numerous in certain districts.

## Geography and Administration
The Victoria Nyanza is adjoined by the provinces of Kisumu and Naivasha. These two provinces originally formed the eastern province of Uganda but were transferred to the East Africa protectorate on April 1, 1902. Naivasha is located in the rift-valley close to Lake Naivasha at an elevation of 6,230 feet.

The region is also a site of diverse human habitation. The Kavirondo dwell by the north-east shores of Victoria Nyanza. Additionally, evidence suggests that a Hottentot-Bushman group once extended as far north as the district between Victoria Nyanza and Kilimanjaro before being driven south by Bantu tribes.
