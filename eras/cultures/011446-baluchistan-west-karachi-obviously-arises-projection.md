# Baluchistan

Baluchistan is a sparsely populated region that covers a larger area than any Indian province with the exception of Bengal, Madras, and Burma. The territory is divided into two distinct parts by a railway line, its surrounding plains, and the dominant hills encircling Quetta.

## Geography and Regional Divisions

The northern section of Baluchistan is situated between the plains of the Indus and Afghanistan. This area is characterized by long ridges of picturesque but rough highlands, including the central ranges of the Suliman system, which served as the prehistoric home of the Pathan highlander. The climate here is clear and bracing, with no great extremes of temperature, and the vegetation is often alpine. This northern district averages 150 miles in breadth, though it expands to over 200 miles along the railway line and narrows to less than 100 miles on the line of the Gomal.

South of the railway lies a square block of territory measuring approximately 300 miles by 300 miles. This region is primarily the home of the Baluch and the Brahui, though it contains representatives of half the great races of Asia and a wide variety of climates. This southern block includes the Kalat plateau, known as Jalawan, where drainage channels cut deep gorges through the hills to reach the plains of Sind.

## Hydrography and Drainage

The drainage systems of Baluchistan vary significantly by region:

*   **Northern Drainage:** Water generally flows north toward the Gomal, passing through uplands west of the Suliman Range, or gathers in lateral valleys and bursts eastward through the limestone axis of the hills toward the Indus.
*   **Southern Drainage:** In Southern Baluchistan, drainage generally trends south and west. Major streams flowing into the Arabian Sea include the Hab river (which forms the boundary west of Karachi), the Purali (ancient *Arabus*), the Hingol (ancient *Tomerus*), and the Dasht.
*   **The Kalat System:** The fortress of Kalat, situated 6,800 feet above sea level, serves as a "hub" for the Mulla, the Bolan, and the southern Lora affluents. The Mulla carries floods from the Kalat highlands into the Gandava basin, forming an ancient highway from the Indus plains to Kandahar. The Lora drains the Pishin valley to the north; these systems unite in Shorawak before disappearing into swamps and the desert west of Nushki.
*   **Western Regions:** To the west of the Mulla sources lies the Kharan desert, characterized by intermittent river channels.

## Climate and Rainfall

The rainfall patterns of the region are heavily influenced by geography. The cessation of rains on the southern border of Baluchistan, west of Karachi, is caused by the projection of the south-east coast of Arabia. This landmass limits the length of the coastline exposed to the south-west monsoon and restricts the breadth of the monsoon air current. 

In contrast, the rainfall in Sind and along the Indus is small and irregular because there are no obstacles to stop the vapour-bearing winds, which instead carry uncondensed rain to the Punjab, where it falls on the western Himalaya and Afghanistan.

## Political and Administrative Status

Baluchistan is divided into British and independent sectors:

*   **British Baluchistan:** This is a distinctive province leased from Kalat and brought under the civil administration of British India. It is an irregularly shaped block of upland territory that includes the Toba plateau, the upper Lora tributaries, the basins of the Nari and Bolan as far as Sibi, and the Thal Chotiali valley. Its climate is described as dry, bracing, and generally favorable for Europeans, though severe temperature extremes can lead to severe forms of fever.
*   **Independent Baluchistan:** Areas to the north and south of the British province remain independent but under British political control, managed primarily by military officers. The level of independence typically increases with the distance from Quetta.
*   **Las Bela:** Located south of Kalat, Las Bela is a small independent state ruled by the Jam, a protected chief under the British Raj. It consists of fertile, highly irrigated, but malarial alluvial plains.

## Trade and Boundaries

Baluchistan serves as an intervening state and buffer system between British India and Afghanistan. The boundary with Afghanistan is precisely demarcated, following the Gomal river and extending through the Kundar stream and the western edge of the Toba plateau near New Chaman.

Historically, the region has been vital for east-west connectivity. A great trading route between Sind and Persia passes through Las Bela, traversing barren ridges and the Kolwah valley. This route was one of the most significant trade paths during the medieval days of Arab ascendancy in Sind. Recent surveys of Makran and Baluchistan have further clarified these ancient connections and led to the reopening of old trade routes between India and Seistan.
