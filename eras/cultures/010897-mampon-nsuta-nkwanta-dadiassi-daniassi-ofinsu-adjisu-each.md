# Mampon, Nsuta, Nkwanta, Dadiassi, Daniassi, Ofinsu and Adjisu

## Tribal Classification
Mampon, Nsuta, Nkwanta, Dadiassi, Daniassi, Ofinsu, and Adjisu are identified as tribes among the Ashanti. They are part of a larger group of tribes, of which approximately a dozen are distinguished, including others such as the Kumasi, Kokofu, Juabin, Adansi, and Bekwai.

## Political Organization and Governance
Each of these tribes possesses its own king. However, from the beginning of the 18th century, the king of Kumasi was recognized as the king paramount, also referred to as the king of Ashanti. The symbol of authority for this paramount king is the "golden stool."

The government of the Ashanti was historically a combination of military aristocracy and monarchy. For the purposes of war, the confederate tribes were originally organized into six great clans or divisions, a structure that eventually became the primary social fabric of the state. The King's Council was composed of the chiefs of these clans along with a small number of sub-chiefs who held hereditary rights. In many instances, the council of chiefs exercised more power than the king, unless the king possessed an exceptionally strong character. Each chief maintained his own court, characterized by a profuse display of barbaric pomp.

## Social and Land Structure
Land within these tribes is held in common. Any lands that remain unallotted are designated as "stool lands," which are attached to the office of the king or the head chief. Additionally, polygamy is practiced by those who have the financial means to afford it.

## Origins and National Character
Tradition suggests that the Ashanti originated from bands of fugitives during the 16th or 17th century. These groups were driven before Moslem tribes that were migrating southward from the Senegal and Niger countries. The Ashanti established themselves in a region of impenetrable forest, where they defended themselves with a valour that became a defining part of their national character, eventually elevating them to a powerful and conquering nation.

The Ashanti are described as being of the pure negro type and are believed to be of the same race and language as the Fanti people located closer to the coast. The separation between the Fanti and the Ashanti is attributed to a famine; the Fanti moved south and lived on herbs (*fan*), while the Ashanti subsisted on Indian corn (*san*), leading to the respective names Fanti and Santi.

## Military History and Mampon
The tribe of Mampon is specifically noted in the context of conflict with British forces. Following the transference of the Elmina fort from the Dutch to the British on April 2, 1872, and the captivity of several Europeans, the Ashanti invaded the British protectorate on January 22, 1873. After defeating the Fanti and inciting disputes at Elmina, the Ashanti force encamped at Mampon, located near Cape Coast, which caused significant alarm among the local inhabitants.
