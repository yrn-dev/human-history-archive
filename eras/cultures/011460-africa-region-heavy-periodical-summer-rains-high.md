# Africa

## Regional Climates and Weather Patterns
The African continent exhibits a wide variety of climatic conditions, ranging from extreme aridity to heavy tropical rainfall. In the coast regions, the climate is characterized as very hot and moist. These areas experience four distinct seasons: the "little rains" from September 15 to December 1, the harmattan (a long dry season) from December 1 to March 15, the season of the great rains from March 15 to July 15, and a short dry season from July 15 to September 15. During the harmattan, the atmosphere lacks moisture, causing vegetation to droop, skin to parch and peel, and woodwork to warp and crack; temperatures typically drop by about 10 degrees during this period. Near the sea, the average temperature is approximately 80° F, often tempered by a sea-breeze that can reach the force of a stiff gale at sundown during the summer.

In the interior of these regions, the seasonal cycle is simplified into two periods: a rainy season from June to October and a dry season from November to May. Rainfall in the interior is more scanty and decreases further in the northern regions.

## British East Africa
In British East Africa, climate and vegetation are arranged in zones parallel to the coast. The coast region is tropical and hot, though generally healthier than other tropical coast lands due to dry soil and constant breezes from the Indian Ocean. Annual rainfall on the coast is approximately 35 inches. Moving inland, the outer plateaus and plains are more arid.

The highlands, defined as districts over 5,000 feet high, are considered very healthy, with fever being almost unknown. The average temperature in these highlands is 73° F in the hot season and 66° F in the cool season. At elevations exceeding 7,000 feet, the climate becomes distinctly colder and frosts occur. Rainfall in the highlands ranges between 40 and 50 inches.

The region bordering Victoria Nyanza is typically tropical, with annual rainfall exceeding 60 inches, rendering it unsuitable for Europeans. Across the protectorate, the hottest period occurs from December to April, while the coolest is from July to September. Rainfall consists of "greater rains" (March to June) and "smaller rains" (November to December), though severe droughts occasionally occur.

## Geography and Environmental History
The African landscape has undergone significant changes over the last hundred centuries. The Sahara is a wind desert rather than a dried-up sea, though it was once rich in life and fertile. As the Sahara became increasingly sandy and dry, it created a barrier that separated the Caucasians from the primitive Negro population located in the central forest region of Africa.

Regarding the Atlantic islands, Madeira is located approximately 400 miles (or 4,000 stadia) from the coast of Africa. It possesses a temperate climate where the thermometer rarely drops below 60° F and occasionally reaches 90° F. Due to its mountainous terrain and varied elevation, Madeira produces both temperate and tropical products.

## Flora and Fauna
The biological characteristics of Africa's hot and dry regions show considerable uniformity and closely approach the types of life found in the dry tracts of Arabia, south Persia, Baluchistan, and Sind.

In the aquatic realm, certain species of fish are shared across continents. The *Silurus* (a member of the *Siluridae* family) is found in Europe, southern Asia, and Africa. Additionally, "walking or climbing fishes" are peculiar to Africa and south-eastern Asia. These fish are biologically organized to breathe while out of the water, an adaptation that allows them to survive in regions characterized by periodical rain and drought.

## The Nile and Solar Influence
Observations regarding the upper parts of Libya indicate that the air is clear and the country is warm due to a lack of cold winds. It is suggested that the Sun, in its course, draws water and drives it to the upper parts of the country, where winds scatter it as rain. This process makes the South and South-west winds the most rainy. While most rivers flow in large volumes during the winter due to the mingling of rain water, the Nile is unique; it flows in less than its proper volume during the winter and increases in summer.
