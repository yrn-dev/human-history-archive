# Boraginaceae

Boraginaceae is an order of plants categorized as members of the series Tubiflorae and the sympetalous section of dicotyledons. The order consists of approximately 1,200 species across 85 genera.

## Distribution and Habitat
The order is widely distributed across tropical and temperate regions. Its primary center of concentration is the Mediterranean region, from which it extends into Asia and central Europe, though it becomes less frequent as it moves northwards. A smaller center of distribution is located on the Pacific side of North America. In the south temperate zone, the order is less developed.

## Physical Characteristics
Plants within this order are typically perennial or annual herbs characterized by rough hairs. While most are herbs, some tropical or sub-tropical genera, such as *Ehretia* and *Cordia*, are arborescent or shrubby. 

The leaves are generally narrow and entire, usually appearing in an alternate arrangement. In certain genera, such as *Cynoglossum* and *Pulmonaria* (lungwort), the radical leaves differ from the stem-leaves, often being heart-shaped or broader.

A defining feature of the order is the dorsiventral (one-sided) inflorescence. This is seen in species of *Myosotis* (forget-me-not), where the cyme begins as a close coil and uncoils as the flowers open. This process is often accompanied by a change in flower color; for instance, in *Symphytum*, *Echium*, and *Myosotis*, the buds are red and turn blue as they expand.

## Floral Structure
The flowers are generally regular, though there are departures from this in a few allied genera and *Echium*, where the corolla is oblique, or in *Lycopsis*, where it is bent. The form of the corolla varies by genus:
* **Rotate:** Borage (*Borago officinalis*)
* **Tubular:** Comfrey (*Symphytum*)
* **Funnel-shaped:** Hounds-tongue (*Cynoglossum*)
* **Salver-shaped:** Alkanet (*Anchusa*)

The throat of the flower is frequently closed by scale-like outgrowths from the corolla, which form a structure known as the corona.

## Economic and Medicinal Use
The order is considered to be of little economic value. Historically, several genera were utilized in medicine, specifically *Pulmonaria* and borage. Additionally, the roots of certain plants, such as *Alkanna tinctoria* (alkanet), are used to produce brown or purple dyes.

In terms of horticulture, the cherry-pie or heliotrope (*Heliotropium peruvianum*) is a well-known garden plant. In Britain, the order is represented by *Myosotis*, comfrey, bugloss (*Echium*), and hounds-tongue, while borage is found in waste ground as a garden escape.
