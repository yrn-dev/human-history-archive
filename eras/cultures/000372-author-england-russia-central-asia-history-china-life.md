# Demetrius Charles Boulger

## Biography and Works
Demetrius Charles Boulger is an author known for several historical and geographical texts. His published works include *England and Russia in Central Asia*, *History of China*, *Life of Gordon*, *India in the 19th Century*, and *History of Belgium*, as well as *Belgian Life in Town and Country*.

## Central Asia and the Mongol Influence
The history of Central Asia is characterized by the movements of various tribes, including the Huns, Mongols, and Turks. These groups were occasionally united into states and recruited armies from various neighboring military races. Following the Christian era, Central Asia experienced at least seven major invasions and conquests. These included the early invasions of Europe by Bulgarians, Huns, and Avars; the conquests of Timur; the Turkish conquest of eastern Europe and Asia Minor; and the Mogul conquest of India. 

The Mongols specifically achieved a period of world ascendancy, which included the temporary subjection of Russia—penetrating as far west as Silesia—and the conquest of China under Kublai. However, by the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, Mongol energy decayed, leading to a state of extreme political impotence. This recession may have been caused by infections of a malarial type, unrecorded pestilences, or changes in climate. Some authorities also suggest that the spread of Buddhist teaching from China had a pacifying effect on these peoples.

## Russian Expansion in Asia
By the sixteenth century, the Turkish, Tartar, and Mongol peoples were no longer expanding but were instead being pushed back by China in the east and Christian Russia in the west. Throughout the seventeenth century, Cossacks spread eastward from European Russia, settling in areas with suitable agricultural conditions. These Cossacks—a mixture of fugitives from the Tartars and various Russian outcasts, including criminals, rebellious serfs, and religious secretaries—acted as a barrier between the Tartars to the east and the feudal agriculture of Hungary and Poland to the west.

Russia's expansion into Asia occurred by land rather than by sea. Moving along the line of least resistance, Russia conquered Siberia and Central Asia with little difficulty, as the region consisted of semi-civilized tribes or weak principalities. To the north-east, Russia expanded until reaching the Pacific. Because these possessions are continuous with European Russia, they have been more thoroughly russified than British possessions in Asia were anglicized.

## History of China
China's history has been marked by periods of prosperity and foreign subjugation. Following a pestilence at the end of the second century A.D. that overthrew the Han dynasty, China experienced a period of Hunnish conquests and division. It was reunited under the Suy dynasty before the end of the sixth century, followed by the Tang dynasty (616-906), a period of great prosperity.

China was later conquered by the Mongols, but threw off this "Mongol yoke" in 1360. The native Ming dynasty flourished until 1644, at which point the Manchus—another Mongol people—reconquered China. The Manchus remained the masters of China until 1912.

China was also impacted by the "Black Death," a virulent pestilence that originated in Central Asia or South Russia. According to Chinese records, thirteen million people perished in China. This caused social disorganization and the neglect of river embankments, resulting in devastating floods in crowded agricultural lands.

## Mongol Governance and Legacy
The administrations established by the descendants of Jengis Khan in China, Russia, and Central Asia were primarily taxing governments designed to collect revenue for the ruler's central establishment, such as the imperial city at Pekin or the Golden Horde in South Russia. These rulers generally left the life and ideas of the subjugated people alone. Consequently, after centuries of Mongol submergence, a thoroughly Chinese China, a Shiite Persia, and a Christian Moscow and Kieff were able to rise again.

In contrast, the Mogul emperor Akbar is noted for creating a "new India" through consolidation and organization. His work provided the ruling classes and princes of India with a sense of common interest. This administrative foundation was later continued by the British, and the British monarch adopted the Mogul title *Kaisar-i-Hind*.
