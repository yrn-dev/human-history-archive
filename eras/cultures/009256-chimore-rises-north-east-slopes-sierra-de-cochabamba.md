# Chimore

## Overview and Geography
Chimore is a location situated on the north-east slopes of the Sierra de Cochabamba in Bolivia. It is positioned a little south of the 17th parallel and sits at an elevation of 925 feet above sea level.

## Hydrology and the Mamore River
Chimore serves as the starting point for the upper section of the Mamore River. From Chimore, the river follows a serpentine course toward the north, primarily situated between the 65th and 66th meridians. The Mamore has a total length of approximately 600 miles. 

A significant portion of the river—specifically three-fourths of its length from Chimore to the rapids located near its mouth—crosses a level plain and is navigable. The Mamore eventually unites with the Beni river at latitude 10 deg. 20' S. to form the Madeira, one of the major tributaries of the Amazon.

## Regional River Systems
The river system involving Chimore is part of the Amazon drainage basin, which is one of three distinct regions in Bolivia, alongside the La Plata and the Central Plateau. This basin covers more than one-half of the republic. 

The Mamore is joined by several other tributaries that rise on the north-east flanks of the Andes, including the Secure, Chapare, Manique (also known as Apere), and the Yacuma. The Yacuma drains a region of swamps and lakes north of the Sierra Chamaya. 

Additionally, the Guapay (or Grande) is a major Bolivian tributary of the Mamore. The Guapay rises in the Cordillera Oriental east of Lake Pampa Aullaguas and flows east to the north extremity of the Sierra de Misiones. After curving around the south-east extremity of the Sierra de Cochabamba and skirting the Llanos de Chiquitos, the Guapay unites with the Mamore at the Junta de los Rios (located at 15 deg. 20' S. lat. and 64 deg. 40' W. long.). While the Guapay is longer and larger than the Mamore above their confluence, its tortuous course of over 700 miles is described as not navigable.
