# Essential Doctrines in History

The study of essential doctrines encompasses the core teachings of religious systems, philosophical theories, and political principles that have shaped human civilization. These doctrines often emerge as responses to existing beliefs or as revolutionary challenges to established institutions.

## Buddhist Doctrines
Buddhism, practiced across India and east and central Asia, is based on a system expounded by the Buddha. The main features of this system were first detailed in a discourse delivered to the five men he initially sought to win over to his faith. This report is preserved in the sacred books within the *suttas* (containing the doctrine) and the *Vinaya* (the rules of the society or order he founded).

While the Buddha rejected the ritualistic magic and sacrifices of the brahmin schools, the soul-theory and asceticism of the Jains, the animistic superstitions of the general population, and the pantheistic speculations found in the pre-Buddhistic *Upanishads*, he retained a belief in transmigration. This doctrine posits that after the death of the body, the soul transmigrates into other bodies, which may be those of gods, beasts, or men. 

In India, this belief was integrated with the ethical conception of *Karma*. According to this doctrine, a person's physical advantages or social position in life are the direct result of their actions in a previous birth. For example, a person born blind is explained to have had a "lust of the eye" in a previous birth, while someone with unusual hearing powers is seen as having loved to listen to the preaching of the law in a previous existence.

## Greek Philosophical and Mystery Doctrines
In ancient Greece, the sophist Protagoras taught a doctrine regarding the cognitive process and truth, famously stating, "Man is the measure of all things; both of that which exists, and of that which does not exist." This theory suggested that knowledge consists of sensation and that the sensations of each individual serve as the canon and measure of truth. While Aristotle dismissed this as a trivial claim—arguing it merely means that perception is the measure of all things—the doctrine highlighted the relative nature of cognition. Plato further represented Protagoras as viewing the distinction between truth and falsehood as resolvable into the distinction between good and evil.

Separately, the Grecian mysteries contained "concealed treasures of doctrine." Some scholars, such as Warburton, maintained that the interior doctrine of these mysteries taught the existence of one Supreme Divinity and the Euemeristic creed, which held that pagan gods were originally mere men.

## Doctrines of World Religions
From 600 B.C. onward, new world religions emerged as "religions of the heart and of the universal sky," moving away from the altar and temple cults dedicated to finite gods.

*   **Christianity:** The main teaching of Jesus was the doctrine of the Kingdom of Heaven. This is described as a revolutionary and uncompromising demand for a complete cleansing of the human race, both internally and externally. This doctrine challenged the established habits and institutions of the time.
*   **Islam:** Islam introduced the fundamental doctrine of the need for a universal devotion of all men to one Will. Its founder, Muhammad, preached a broad idea of human brotherhood under God and emphatically insisted that he himself was merely a man, though he asserted that he was God's especial prophet.

## Political and Moral Doctrines in Japan
In the history of the Japanese people, specific doctrines regarding national development emerged during the period of interaction with the West. Hashimoto Sanae advocated for several progressive principles to the baron Matsudaira Yoshinaga. These included the promotion of foreign trade to enrich the nation and develop independence, the study of foreign sciences and arts, the fostering of industries, the purchase of firearms and ships, and the sending of publicists and students to Western countries for instruction. Additionally, Sanae established the principle that probity is essential for success in commerce.

## The Role of Sacred Texts in Doctrine
The preservation and dissemination of doctrine are often tied to the use of letters and sacred books. In the case of the "Christian Barbarians," the introduction of letters was essential for a religion whose doctrines were contained in a sacred book. The translation of Scriptures into native tongues facilitated conversion and encouraged the clergy to study original texts, the sacred liturgy, and ecclesiastical traditions found in the writings of the fathers.
