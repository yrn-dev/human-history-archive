# Bantam Residency

The Bantam residency is the westernmost residency of the island of Java in the Dutch East Indies. Its geographical boundaries are defined by the Java Sea to the north, the Strait of Sunda to the west, the Indian Ocean to the south, and the residencies of Preanger and Batavia to the east. The residency includes several smaller coastal islands as well as Dwars-in-den-weg ("right-in-the-way") Island and Princes Island in the Sunda Strait.

## Geography and Topography
The coast of Bantam is frequently marshy and low-lying. The residency is divided into two primary geographical portions. The northern portion is generally flat and represents the most fertile area of the residency, though it contains a central hilly group featuring two inactive volcanoes, Pulosari and Karang. The north-western corner is marked by the isolated Gede Mountain. In the center of the residency, there are various cold and warm sulphur springs and geysers. On a ridge of the Karang Mountain lies the large crater-lake Dano.

The southern portion of the residency is covered by the Kendang Mountains (the word "Kendang" being Malay for "range"), which extend into the Preanger. The rivers within the residency are only navigable at their mouths.

## Population and Administration
As of 1897, Bantam had a population of 709,339. This population included 1,959 Chinese, 302 Europeans, and 89 Arabs and other Asiatic foreigners. The native population consists of Javanese in the northern Serang division and Sundanese elsewhere.

Administratively, Bantam is divided into five native territorial divisions or regencies: Lebak, Charingin, Pandeglang, Anjer, and Serang. The capital of the residency is Serang, which is located 2 1/2 miles from Bantam Bay. Other principal towns include Rangkas Betug, Menes, Pandeglang, and Chilegon.

## Agriculture and Economy
The principal products of cultivation in Bantam are rice, coffee, and pulse (*kachang*). In 1835, the government drained a large part of the crater-lake Dano to facilitate rice cultivation. Historically, under government culture, indigo, sugar, and especially pepper were also grown in large quantities.

The residency's economic prosperity has been impacted by several disasters. In 1879, a cattle plague occurred, which was followed by a fever epidemic that resulted in the deaths of 50,000 people. Additionally, the eruption of Krakatoa in 1883 ruined the former coasting and fishing trade, as the resulting inundation destroyed the seaport towns of Anjer and Charingin along with a large stretch of the coastline.

Due to these factors, there is a considerable emigration of natives from the residency, except during the rice season.
