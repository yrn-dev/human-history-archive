# Family Spirillaceae

The Spirillaceae is a family of bacteria belonging to the order Haplobacterinae. The members of this family are characterized by vegetative cells that are cylindrical in shape but curved to a varying degree spirally. Within this family, cell divisions occur perpendicular to the long axis of the organism.

## Classification and Genera
The family Spirillaceae includes several distinct genera categorized by the degree of their curvature and their method of motility:

*   ***Vibrio* (Mueller-Loeffler):** These organisms are comma-shaped and motile. Their motility is described as monotrichous.
*   ***Spirillum* (Ehrenb.):** These exhibit a more pronounced curvature, forming open spirals. They are motile and characterized as lophotrichous.
*   ***Spirochaete* (Ehrenb.):** These are spirally coiled with numerous close turns. While they are motile, this movement appears to be the result of flexile movements, as no cilia are found in these organisms.

## General Characteristics of Schizomycetes
As members of the Schizomycetes (bacteria), the Spirillaceae are minute vegetable organisms that lack chlorophyll. They are defined by the absence of an ordinary nucleus and the absence of ordinary sexual reproduction. They multiply through a process of repeated bipartitions.

The vegetative body of the order Haplobacterinae, to which this family belongs, is unicellular and may be spheroidal, cylindrical, or spirally twisted. These cells may exist in isolation or be connected in other growth series or filamentous arrangements.

## Morphology and Structure
The cells of the Schizomycetes vary in size; some unicellular forms are less than 1 micron in diameter, while others can reach thicknesses of 4, 5, 7, or 8 microns. The length of the cell can range from the size of its diameter to many times that measurement.

Every bacterial cell is enclosed by a definite cell-membrane. This membrane consists of a proteid substance and is considered a modification of the general protoplasm. Unlike the cell walls of higher plants, it typically does not react to cellulose, nor does it contain chitin as seen in fungi. However, in certain cases, such as *B. tuberculosis*, analysis reveals a large amount of cellulose. In some instances, the cell wall may swell into a gelatinous mass; when this sheath is regular and dense, it is referred to as a "capsule."

## Growth and Division
The growth of these organisms is marked by the elongation of the cell, followed by division into two equal halves across the long axis. This is achieved through the formation of a septum. Depending on whether the septum splits immediately or remains intact, the result is either isolated cells that mirror the parent cell or the formation of filaments.

In the case of curved and spiral forms, the sinuosity determines the classification: slight sinuosity results in the *Vibrio* form, while a well-marked, pronounced spiral winding results in forms such as *Spirillum* and *Spirochaete*. These terms may refer to individual cells or to filaments composed of several cells.

Rapid division allows for the production of hundreds of thousands of cells within a few hours. Under specific conditions of temperature and medium, these cells may cloud a fluid, form films on the surface, or create deposits. When dense swarms of vegetative cells become fixed in a matrix of their own swollen cell-walls, they enter a resting state known as a zoogloea.
