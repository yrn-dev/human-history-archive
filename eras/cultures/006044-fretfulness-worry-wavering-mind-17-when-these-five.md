# Fretfulness, Worry, and Wavering of Mind

In the study of human psychology, ethics, and historical behavior, the concepts of fretfulness, worry, and the wavering of the mind appear across various cultural and philosophical frameworks. These states are often characterized as obstacles to peace, resolution, or political stability.

## Philosophical and Spiritual Perspectives on Mental Hindrances

In certain philosophical traditions, fretfulness and worry are categorized as specific mental impediments. Specifically, "fretfulness and worry" and the "wavering of mind" are listed as the fourth and fifth of the Five Hindrances. Other hindrances in this group include torpor of mind, the corruption arising from a wish to injure, and hankering after worldly advantages.

The removal of these five hindrances is viewed as a transformative process. When an individual cuts these away from within, they are described as feeling secure and free, likened to being rid of disease, released from jail, or freed from debt. This realization triggers a progression of internal states: gladness springs up, which leads to joy. This joy puts the entire frame at ease, filling the individual with a sense of peace that stays the heart.

Further spiritual practice emphasizes the importance of a mind that does not waver. In the context of Right Conduct, the cultivation of "love without measure" (Pali: *Metta*) is encouraged. This involves maintaining a heart of love that is unstinted and unmixed with opposing interests toward all beings in the world. Practitioners are urged to maintain this mindfulness while standing, walking, sitting, or lying down, ensuring the mind does not waver and that no evil speech is uttered.

## Rectitude and Resolution

In the code of the samurai, the opposite of a wavering mind is found in the concept of Rectitude. One definition of Rectitude is the "power of resolution," described as the ability to decide upon a specific course of conduct based on reason without wavering. This includes the resolve to strike or die when it is right to do so. Rectitude is viewed as the "bone" that provides firmness and stature to the human frame; without it, neither learning nor talent can make a person a samurai.

Similarly, the philosopher Mencius describes Righteousness (or Rectitude) as a straight and narrow path that a man should follow to regain a lost paradise. Mencius lamented those who lose their mind and do not know how to seek it again, comparing it to the way people seek lost dogs or fowls.

## Historical Instances of Wavering and Determination

Historical records illustrate how wavering or steadfastness influenced political and military outcomes. During the conflict in Sicily, the prospects of Nikias became so hopeless and those of Syracuse so triumphant that "the waverers" decided to declare themselves. Consequently, most Greek cities in Sicily—excluding Naxos, Katana, and the neutral Agrigentum—resolved to aid the winning cause. Later, the city of Agrigentum was also understood to be in a political crisis and wavering.

In contrast, determination is noted in the behavior of the Czechs during their journey to the Pacific. Described as finely disciplined and possessing an air of determination, they were not worried by the future of their cross-country journey. This discipline was driven by an anxiety to accomplish their goals, leading them to march irresistibly.

In the early history of Islam, the relationship between Muhammad and Abu Bekr is highlighted as a study in support and steadfastness. While Muhammad is described as having wavered at times, Abu Bekr is noted as the "conscience and its will" of primitive Islam who sustained him. Abu Bekr is characterized by a devotion that never wavered and a faith described as "that which moves mountains."

## Societal and General Trends

On a broader historical scale, the evolution of social, economic, and political ideas often exhibits a pattern of fluctuation. Human actions are observed to waver between the new and the traditional, with a general tendency to gravitate toward the traditional. While a new order in human affairs may begin to shape itself, the details and formulae of this transition often remain fluctuating and vague.
