# The Prefix Tu- and Diminutives in Bantu Languages

In the study of Bantu languages, the prefix *Tu-* (or *Utu-*) serves a specific grammatical and semantic function, primarily associated with the expression of size and number. Within the classification of prefixes common to members of the Bantu language family, *Tu-* is identified as the plural prefix corresponding to the singular prefix *Fi-* or *Vi-*.

## Diminutive Function and Classification
The prefix *Tu-* is categorized as Prefix No. 12 in the archival records of Bantu linguistic structures. It is frequently used in a diminutive sense, meaning it is employed to denote a smaller version of an object or entity. This diminutive quality is shared by other prefixes in the family; for example, Prefix No. 13 (*Aka*) is also usually diminutive, though it can occasionally serve an honorific purpose.

The singular counterpart to *Tu-*, the prefix *Fi-* or *Vi-* (Prefix No. 17), likewise possesses a diminutive sense. This singular prefix is found predominantly in north-western Bantu tongues, specifically within Cameroon and the northern half of the Congo basin. Its reach extends as far east as the Manyema language on the Upper Congo, near Tanganyika, where it appears in the form *I-*. Because *Fi-/Vi-* is used exclusively in the singular, it cannot be traced to derivation from any of the other sixteen common prefixes.

## Contrast with Augmentative Prefixes
The diminutive use of *Tu-* and *Vi-* stands in contrast to prefixes used for augmentative purposes. Prefix No. 18 (*Ogu-*) and its plural counterpart, Prefix No. 19 (*Aga-*), are used to indicate a larger or augmented sense. The use of these augmentative prefixes appears to be limited to certain branches of the Unyoro language and the Masaba and Luganda dialects. Unlike some other particles, these are considered regular prefixes because they are provided with the concord *-gu-* and *-ga-*.

## Linguistic Context and Related Prefixes
The Bantu prefix system includes a variety of other classes with distinct meanings:
* **Abstract Nouns:** Prefix No. 14 (*Ubu-*) is generally used to indicate abstract nouns, though it is sometimes used in a plural sense.
* **Locatives:** Prefix No. 16 (*Apa*) is used as a locative to indicate position or place and is identical to the adverb "here."
* **Infinitives:** Prefix No. 15 (*Uku*) is identical to the preposition "to" and is used as an infinitive with verbs, as well as with certain nouns related to body functions.
* **Prepositions:** The prefix *Mu-* (Prefix No. 20) functions as a preposition meaning "into" or "in," often combined with the suffix *-ni*. Unlike the diminutive prefixes, *Mu-* does not seem to have a complete concord and lacks a pronominal accusative.
