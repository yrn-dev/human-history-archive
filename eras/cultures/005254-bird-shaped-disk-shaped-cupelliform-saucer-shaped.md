# Bird-Shaped, Disk-Shaped, and Cupelliform Ornaments

In the study of historical ornaments, specifically brooches found in heathen cemeteries, scholars have identified eight leading types of design. These ornaments were worn by both sexes. Among these classifications are bird-shaped (Type 6), disk-shaped (Type 7), and cupelliform or saucer-shaped (Type 8) brooches.

## Bird-Shaped and Disk-Shaped Brooches
Bird-shaped brooches (Type 6) are considered to be of continental origin. Similarly, disk-shaped brooches (Type 7) are likely of continental origin in part, though this type exhibits significant variation. These specimens range from magnificent gold pieces studded with gems to rude, almost plain disks made of bronze.

## Cupelliform and Saucer-Shaped Brooches
The cupelliform or saucer-shaped brooch (Type 8) is believed to be peculiar to England. These ornaments are found primarily in the southern Midlands and are usually discovered in pairs. The interiors of these brooches are gilt and are sometimes set with brilliants or furnished with detachable plates.

## Other Brooch Types and Origins
The other five types of brooches include circular or ring-shaped (Type 1), cruciform (Type 2), square-headed (Type 3), radiated (Type 4), and S-shaped (Type 5). Type 5, like the bird-shaped variety, appears to be of continental origin, and this is probably also true for Type 4. 

The remaining types were likely brought over by the Anglo-Saxons during the invasion. Types 1 and 3 are widespread outside of England. Type 2 is common in Scandinavian countries but is rarely found south of the Elbe; in England, it occurs mainly in the northern counties. Types 2 and 3 vary in size from 2½ to 7 inches or more, with larger specimens being highly ornamental and gilt, while smaller ones are plain. Historically, some brooches reached values as high as six mancusas, which was equivalent to the value of six oxen.
