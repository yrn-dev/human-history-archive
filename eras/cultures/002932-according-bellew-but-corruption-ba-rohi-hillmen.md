# Hillmen and Related Ethnic Groups

The term "hillmen" appears in historical records across various regions, often describing specific social classes, ethnic groups, or military recruits. These populations are frequently characterized by their geographic isolation in mountainous or upland areas and their distinct roles in political or military conflicts.

## Hillmen in Ancient Greece and Macedonia
In ancient Greece, hillmen were sometimes associated with social grievances and the rise of political leaders known as tyrants. In Athens, for example, the tyrant Peisistratus—who held power between 560 and 527 B.C. with two intervals of exile—received support from poverty-stricken Athenian hillmen. 

In Macedonia, the presence of hillmen is noted during the era of Alexander. Records indicate that a revolt of the hillmen in the north occurred, which required Alexander's immediate attention.

## Hillmen in Southeast Asia
In Burma, experiments were conducted to recruit hillmen for military service. Specifically, the Kachin hillmen were tested for enlistment. However, these efforts were contrasted with the general Burmese character, which was described as being so averse to control and discipline in petty matters that it proved impossible to find suitable men for enlistment even within the civil police.

## Other Regional Populations and Ethnic Groups
Beyond those specifically labeled as hillmen, various other distinct races and tribes are recorded in the archives:

### Baluchistan and the Persian Influence
The Baluchistan region contains a variety of nomadic and settled peoples:
*   **Rekis:** Known as "desert people," these scattered nomadic tribes include the Mohamadani, who are likely of Arab origin and occupy the central mountain region.
*   **Naushirwanis:** A purely Persian race that entered Baluchistan within historic times. They are considered identical to the Tahukani or Tahuki found in Perso-Baluchistan and are described as a fine, manly race.
*   **Tajik:** An underlying Persian population found throughout the country, often serving as agricultural bondsmen or slaves (such as the Nakibs). This group includes the Dehkans (or Dehwars) and the Durzadas.
*   **Rind Tribes:** These tribes claim extraction from Koraish and Arab origin.

### The Province of Pashai
Located ten days' journey south of Badashan, the Province of Pashai is inhabited by people with a brown complexion and a peculiar language. Described as crafty and "pestilent," they are noted for being adepts in diabolic arts and sorceries. Their diet consists of rice and flesh, and the men are known to wear brooches and earrings made of silver and gold set with pearls and stones.
