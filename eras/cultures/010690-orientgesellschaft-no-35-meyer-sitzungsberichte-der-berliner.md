# Orientgesellschaft and Related Academic Records

The *Mitteilungen der deutschen Orientgesellschaft* and the *Sitzungsberichte der Berliner Akademie* are academic publications that have served as primary venues for documenting archaeological discoveries, linguistic analyses, and historical research concerning the ancient Near East and the Roman Empire.

## Contributions to Indo-European Linguistics
Research published in the *Mitteilungen der deutschen Orientgesellschaft* (specifically No. 35) and the *Sitzungsberichte der Berliner Akademie* (1908) has provided significant evidence regarding the separation of the Aryan languages from other Indo-European groups. German excavators at Boghaz Keui—a site identified as Pteria in Cappadocia—discovered treaties dating to the beginning of the 14th century B.C. between the king of the Hittites and the king of Mitanni. 

These treaties are of particular importance to historians and linguists because they appear almost certainly to contain the names of Indra, Varuna, and Mitra, which are deities belonging to early Aryan mythology. These findings, discussed by H. Winckler and E. Meyer, represent a more favorable stage of investigation into identifying unknown languages within cuneiform inscriptions.

## Babylonian and Assyrian Research
The *Mitteilungen der deutschen Orientgesellschaft* has been a recurring source for the study of ancient Mesopotamia. Publications from the society between 1899 and 1906, as well as specific entries in 1899, are cited as authorities on the ruins and history of Babylon. Additionally, Nos. 21 and 22 of the *Mitteilungen* are referenced in conjunction with the work of L. W. King regarding early Babylonian chronicles.

These academic records contribute to a broader understanding of the region, including the observation that the Assyrian language is practically identical to the Babylonian language, and that the Assyrians and Babylonians are the same people, despite some foreign admixtures.

## The Sitzungsberichte der Berliner Akademie
The *Sitzungsberichte der Berliner Akademie* serves as a repository for diverse scholarly inquiries beyond the study of the Orient. Its records include:

*   **Roman Imperial Administration:** The publications include research by O. Hirschfeld (1901) regarding the *Illustres*, the highest magistrates of the later Roman Empire. This title, which became official during the time of Valentinian I, was granted to commanders-in-chief of the army, chief praefecti or ministers, and consuls. Those holding the title of *Illustres* possessed the privilege of being tried in criminal cases only by the emperor or his deputy.
*   **Indo-European Languages:** As noted previously, the 1908 records include work by E. Sieg and W. Siegling on Tocharish, the language of the Indoskythen. Preliminary accounts from 1909 suggest that Tocharish is an Indo-European language that may be more akin to Western groups than to the Aryan group.
