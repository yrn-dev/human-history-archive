# The Boomerang

The boomerang is a weapon characterized by a peculiar flight pattern that is primarily determined by its skew. There are two main types of boomerangs: the return type and the non-return type.

## Mechanics of the Return Boomerang
To achieve a return flight, the boomerang is held vertically with the concave side facing forward. It is thrown in a plane that is parallel to the surface of the ground, with the thrower imparting as much rotation as possible. 

The flight sequence typically proceeds as follows:
* **Initial Flight:** The weapon travels in a straight line for 30 yards or more, maintaining a nearly vertical rotation.
* **The Curve:** The boomerang then inclines to the left, lying over on its flat side and rising into the air.
* **The Return:** After describing a circle with a diameter of 50 yards or more, the weapon returns to the thrower.

Some observers note that the boomerang can return after striking an object, and it is possible for the weapon to strike the ground without affecting its ability to return.

## Performance and Variations
The capabilities of the boomerang vary based on the skill of the thrower and the type of weapon used. Australian natives are capable of throws where the weapon travels 100 yards or more before the leftward curve begins. In such instances, the boomerang may rise as high as 150 feet in the air and circle five times before returning to the thrower.

In contrast, the non-return type of boomerang is normally thrown in the same manner as the return type, which allows it to travel an immense distance. However, it can be made to return in a nearly straight line if it is thrown at an angle of 45 degrees.

## Comparative Records
While accurate measurements of throws by Australian natives are unavailable, an English throw has been recorded at 180 yards. For comparison, the same thrower achieved a distance of 70 yards when throwing a cricket ball.
