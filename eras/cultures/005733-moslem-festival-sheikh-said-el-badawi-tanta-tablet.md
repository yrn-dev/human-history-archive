# Moslem Festival of the Sheikh Said el Badawi at Tanta

## Overview and Historical Parallels
The Moslem festival of the sheikh Said el Badawi, held at Tanta, is noted for its scale and character. In historical terms, it is described as the modern equivalent of the ancient festival of Bubastis. The festival of Bubastis, as recorded by Herodotus, was a riotous affair that attracted thousands of attendees from all regions of Egypt.

## Context of Islamic Festivals
The term "Bairam" is a Perso-Turkish word meaning "festival," which is used in Turkish to describe the two primary festivals of Islam. These are categorized as the "Lesser Festival" and the "Greater Festival."

The Lesser Festival, known as *Kutshuk Bairam* or *Sheker Bairam* in Turkish, and *'Id al-Fitr* or *Al-'id as-saghir* in Arabic, occurs immediately after the fasting-month of Ramadan. It takes place during the first three days of the tenth month, Shawwal. This festival is characterized by significant outward signs of rejoicing, including the wearing of new clothes, the exchange of presents, private visits, official receptions, and the visiting of the graves of relatives.

The Greater Festival, referred to as *Qurban Bairam* in Turkish and *Al-'id al-kabir* or *'Id al-adha* in Arabic, occurs on the tenth and subsequent two or three days of the final month, *Dhu-l-hijja*. This festival commemorates the ransom of Ishmael with a ram. During this time, pilgrims in the valley of Mina slay a cow, he-goat, ram, or camel. Throughout the Moslem world, those who can afford it sacrifice a legal animal, either consuming the meat or providing it to the poor. While celebrated similarly to the Lesser Festival, it is generally done with less ardour. Both of these primary festivals follow a lunar calendar, moving through the solar year every thirty-two years.

## The Role of the Sheikh in Islamic Tradition
The festival at Tanta is associated with a sheikh. In the history of Dervish Fraternities, the relationship between devotees was traditionally that of a pupil and a revered sheikh, who served as a *pir* (teacher), *ustadh*, or *murshid* (guide). Pupils lived with the sheikh, shared his religious practices, and received instruction. 

Following the death of a master, the sayings and memory of his life were often passed down through generations. While continuous corporations to perpetuate a sheikh's name were slow to form, some fraternities eventually traced their origins to specific saints. For example, the Qadirite order descends from 'Abd al-Qadir al-Jilani, who died in 1166. In Egypt, all such fraternities are under the rule of a direct descendant of Abu Bekr.

## Note on the Tablet of Canopus
The Tablet of Canopus is a historical record that provides information regarding the ancient festivals of Bubastis, the ancient precursor to the Tanta celebrations. The tablet indicates that there were two distinct festivals for Bubastis: a great festival and a lesser festival. It is suggested that the lesser festival may have been held at Memphis, specifically in the quarter known as Ankhto, which contained a temple dedicated to the goddess.
