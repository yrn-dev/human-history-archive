# Sumatra

## Geography and Climate
Sumatra is located in a region of Asia where it serves as a geographical barrier to the south-west monsoon. Its position affects the rainfall patterns of the surrounding areas; specifically, the quantity of rainfall increases considerably on those parts of the Malay peninsula coast that are not sheltered from the south-west by Sumatra.

In the broader region, rainfall varies significantly. For example, at Singapore, the yearly fall is nearly 100 inches, and at Batavia in Java, it is approximately 78 inches. Further east, on the Burmese and Tenasserim coasts, registered rainfall exceeds 200 inches and is never less than 75 or 80 inches.

## Flora and Agriculture
Sumatra is one of the regions where the durian (*Durio zibethinus*) is cultivated. The durian tree, a member of the natural order Bombaceae, can reach heights of 70 to 80 feet and possesses yellowish-green flowers and oblong, tapering leaves. 

The fruit of the durian is spherical, measuring 6 to 8 inches in diameter, and is characterized by a hard external husk covered in sharp, pyramidal thorny points. Inside, the fruit contains five oval cells filled with a smooth, glutinous, cream-colored pulp and one to five seeds the size of chestnuts. While the pulp and roasted seeds are edible, the fruit is known for a most offensive smell, which has been compared to putrid animal matter or rotten onions. The taste of the pulp has been described as a rich, butter-like custard flavored with almonds, with notes reminiscent of brown sherry, onion-sauce, and cream-cheese.
