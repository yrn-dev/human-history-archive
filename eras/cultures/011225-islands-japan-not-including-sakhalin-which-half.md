# The Islands of Japan

The islands of Japan are located between the 30th and 45th parallels. Geologically, it has been proven that these islands were once part of the Asiatic continent, a fact supported by the discovery of Tertiary mammals in gravel, including various unnamed varieties of horse, ox, deer, bison, rhinoceros, bear, and whale, as well as *elephas primigenius*, *elephas Namadicus*, and *stegodon Clifti*.

## Geography and Physical Features
The Japanese archipelago is traversed by a line of volcanic mountains, some of which remain active. The highest point in the group reaches approximately 13,000 feet above sea level. The country is characterized as being fertile, well-watered, and generally well-cultivated.

The islands maintain six primary routes of communication with the Asian continent:
*   **North:** One route runs from the northwest point of Yezo across the narrow Soya strait to Saghalien and into the Amur region of Manchuria; a secondary route leads from the northeast point of Yezo through the Kurile chain to Kamchatka.
*   **Southwest:** One route extends from the northwest of Kyushu via the islands of Tsushima and Iki to southeast Korea; another runs from the south of the Izumo promontory.
*   **South:** One route leads from the southwest of Kyushu via the Goto Islands to southeastern China; the final route runs from the south of Kyushu through the Philippines, Formosa, and the Ryukyu Islands toward Polynesia and Malaysia.

## Early History and European Contact
The various avenues of communication with the continent likely provided access for immigrants from Polynesia, Malaysia, China, and Siberia. In the 16th century, the Portuguese discovered Japan in either 1542 or 1543. Three Portuguese travelers, sailing from Spain to Macao via junk, were driven off course and landed at Tanegashima, a small island south of Kyushu. 

The Japanese received the strangers hospitably and showed great interest in their arquebuses, which were the first firearms seen in Japan. Because the parties could not speak the same language, a Chinese crew member used ideographs traced in the sand to explain the situation. Following this, local feudatory armorers began manufacturing arquebuses. Consequently, Kyushu became the primary center for European intercourse during the latter half of the 16th century.

## Territorial Relations and Disputes
### Sakhalin and the Kuriles
The island of Sakhalin has been a point of contention between Japan and Russia. While the southern half of Sakhalin originally belonged to Japan, it passed into Russian possession through an arrangement that the Japanese nation strongly resented. In 1875, Japan agreed to withdraw entirely from Sakhalin on the condition that Russia withdrew from the Kurile Islands.

The Kurile Islands were discovered in 1634 by Martin de Vries, a Dutch navigator. While the islands of Shikotan, Etorofu, and Kunashiri are believed to have belonged to Japan from a remote date, Russians began encroaching on the northern Kuriles in the early 18th century after conquering Kamchatka, seeking fur-bearing animals. 

### The Ryukyu Islands
The Ryukyu Islands were subdued by Satsuma and included in the Satsuma fief for two centuries. In 1876, Japan extended its system of local government to Ryukyu and pensioned its ruler. This prompted a protest from China, which claimed Ryukyu had always been a tributary. While Ryukyu had sent gift-bearing envoys to Peking—a custom Japan also followed—China had not attempted to save the islands when they were subdued by Satsuma. Following a suggestion for a peaceful compromise by General Grant in 1880, a conference was held in Peking. Although it was initially agreed to divide the islands between Japan (north) and China (south), China drew back at the moment of signing. Japan subsequently retained the islands.

## Culture and Society
The Japanese people are noted for their ancient civilization and remarkable artistic faculty. In the modern era, they have integrated Western methods and demonstrated a capacity for progress in commerce and war, establishing themselves as a great modern world-force among Eastern races.
