# Division of the Coelom (Body-Cavity)

In certain biological configurations, the coelom—also referred to as the body-cavity or perivisceral cavity—is organized into three distinct portions. This tri-regional differentiation of the body-cavity corresponds with the external configuration of the body.

## Regional Divisions and Structure
The coelom is divided into three portions that are completely separated from one another by septa. These three divisions are:
1. The proboscis-coelom, which is the first body-cavity.
2. The collar-coelom, which is the second body-cavity.
3. The truncal coelom, which is the third body-cavity.

## Communication with the Exterior
The first two divisions of the coelom communicate with the exterior through a pair of ciliated pore-canals. These canals are located at the posterior end of their respective segments.

### The Proboscis-Pores
The pore-canals of the proboscis-coelom exhibit high variability. In many instances, only one pore is present, located on the left side. In other cases, the pore-canals may unite to open via a common median orifice. There are also instances where the communication between the pore-canals and the proboscis-coelom appears to be occluded, or where the pore-canals are entirely vestigial.

### The Collar-Pores
Unlike the proboscis-pores, the collar-pores are characterized by their constancy. This stability is attributed to their adaptation for a specific function: the inhalation of water, which serves to render the collar turgid during progression.

### The Truncal Coelom
While the first two cavities have established pore-canals, the truncal coelom is generally not described as having them. However, there are reasons to suppose that it was provided with pore-canals at one time. Vestiges of these structures have been described for only one genus, *Spengelia*, where they are situated near the anterior end of the truncal coelom.

## Related Cellular Linings
The body-cavity is lined by a very thin continuous sheet of cells. These cells develop from the mesothelium (a portion of the mesoderm) and are termed mesothelial cells. This lining is found in the peritoneal, pericardial, and pleural cavities. Some sources include these mesothelial cells under the broader category of endothelial cells, which also line lymph spaces, lymph vessels, and blood vessels.
