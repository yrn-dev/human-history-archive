# Uzbegs

The Uzbegs are the dominant race in the region of Bokhara. They are characterized as fanatical Moslem Sunnites who maintain an old social division based on clans or tribes. According to archive records, the Uzbegs scorn work and hold a despise for their Iranian subjects.

## Regional Demographics and Population
The total population of the Bokhara region is estimated at 1,250,000. While the Uzbegs are the dominant race, the population is diverse. The Sarts, who make up the majority of the inhabitants in the towns, are a mixture of Iranians and Turks. Iranian Tajiks compose the great bulk of the general population and differ very little from the Sarts. 

Other groups present in the region include:
*   Nomads and semi-nomads, who account for nearly 20% and 15% of the population respectively, including the nomad Kirghiz and nomad Turkomans (both of Turkish origin).
*   Afghans, Persians, Arabs, and Armenians.
*   A colony of Hindus from Shikarpur, who handle much of the trade.
*   Jews.

## Geography and Climate of Bokhara
The climate in Bokhara is described as extreme, varying significantly by elevation:

*   **Lowlands:** These areas experience very hot summers and short, cold winters. Frosts can reach -20 deg. Fahr., and the Oxus may freeze for a period of two weeks. The lowlands are also subject to terrible sand-storms from the west that can kill cotton trees and exhaust people. Malaria is widely prevalent here and can become malignant following a wet spring.
*   **Highlands:** This region experiences hot, dry summers followed by four months of winter.
*   **High Altitudes:** In regions above 8,000 ft., there is a significant development of glaciers and snowfields. The passes in these areas are buried under snow, and the short summer season is rainy.

## Religious Context: Sunnite Islam
The Uzbegs adhere to Sunnite Islam. Within the broader context of the Moslem community, Sunnites hold specific beliefs regarding leadership (the Imam). They believe that the Imam—the head of the community and lieutenant of the Prophet—is appointed by men. The basis for this appointment is obedience to the general usage of Moslem peoples from the earliest times, rather than tradition from Mahomet, the Koran, or analogy. 

Furthermore, Sunnites regard the Imam as liable to err and believe he should be obeyed even if he personally sins, provided that he maintains the ordinances of Islam, as effective leadership is considered the essential point.
