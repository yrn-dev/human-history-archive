# Quinoa and Coca

In the agricultural landscape of Bolivia, quinoa and coca are identified as perhaps the two indigenous products that receive the most attention. Both plants are deeply integrated into the local economy and the lives of the native population, though they serve very different purposes.

## Quinoa
Quinoa (*Chenopodium quinoa*) is a plant characteristic of the puna region. It is grown in large quantities and serves as a staple article of food among the natives.

## Coca
Coca is regarded as one of the most important plants in the country and is highly esteemed by the natives, who masticate the leaf. Beyond its local use, coca is an article of export utilized for medicinal purposes.

The cultivation of coca is concentrated in the departments of La Paz and Cochabamba, with particular prevalence in the province of Yungas. The plant is grown on the eastern slopes of the Andes at altitudes ranging from 5,000 to 6,000 feet. These specific locations provide a uniform temperature where frosts are unknown, creating ideal conditions for the plant's growth.

## Related Indigenous Flora of Bolivia
Quinoa and coca exist alongside a diverse array of other indigenous vegetable life, which varies according to the country's diverse climatic zones.

### High Altitude and Temperate Regions
In the puna region, quinoa is found alongside the potato, oca (*Oxalis tuberosa*), and various coarse grasses. In the temperate and warm regions, Indian corn was cultivated long before the arrival of Europeans.

### Sub-tropical and Tropical Zones
The indigenous plants of the tropical and sub-tropical zones include:
*   **Food Crops:** Cassava (*Manihot utilissima* and *M. aipi*), sweet potato, peanuts, and pineapple.
*   **Fruits:** Guava, chirimoya (*Anona cherimolia*), and pawpaw (*Carica papaya*).
*   **Other Natural Products:** Cotton, vanilla, sarsaparilla, *ipecacuanha* (*Cephaelis*), false jalap (*Mirabilis jalapa*), copaiba, and tolu (*Myroxylon toluiferum*).
*   **Timber:** Rubber-producing trees, dyewoods, and hardwoods such as mahogany, rosewood, cedar, walnut, jacaranda, colo, and quebracho.

Additionally, the eastern Andes produce quina, also known as calisaya, at altitudes between 3,000 and 9,000 feet. The bark of the calisaya tree is a significant item in Bolivia's foreign trade, although natural sources are diminishing due to destructive collection methods.

## Comparison with Exotic Products
The indigenous products of Bolivia are grown alongside various exotic introductions. Sugar-cane was introduced early on; while it is grown in eastern Bolivia, it is heavily taxed and primarily used for the manufacture of rum. Other exotics include rice in tropical districts, as well as wheat, rye, oats, beans, and pease. Coffee of the best flavor is grown in warmer districts of the eastern Andes, while superior grade cacao is grown in the department of Beni and the warm Andean valleys of Cochabamba and La Paz.
