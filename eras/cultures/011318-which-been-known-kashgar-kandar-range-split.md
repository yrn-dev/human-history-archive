# Sarikol Range

The Sarikol range is a meridional mountain system located on the western edge of the Kashgar plains. It serves as the defining political boundary between China and Russia. Historically, the range was known as Taurus by the ancients and as Bolor during medieval times.

## Geological Structure and Topography
The Sarikol range is characterized by two parallel chains. The western chain serves as the water-divide of the Pamirs. The eastern chain, which has been referred to as the Kandar or Kashgar range, is split at intervals by lateral gorges. These gorges facilitate the passage of the main drainage originating from the eastern slopes of the Pamirs.

In the context of the Western Kuen-lun, the Sarikol range and the Muztagh (or Kashgar) range together form a double border-ridge that separates the lowlands of East Turkestan from the Pamir highlands. This ridge system has its western foot situated on the Pamirs at elevations between 10,500 and 13,000 feet above sea level, while its eastern foot descends into the Tarim basin at 4,000 to 4,500 feet. The summits of this system reach extreme heights, such as the Muztagh-ata, which rises to 25,780 feet, extending well above the limits of perpetual snow.

## Regional Geography and Extensions
The double border-ridge of the Sarikol continues east of the meridian of Yarkent (77° E) as a series of twin ranges running from the west-north-west to the east-south-east. These extensions constitute the Kuen-lun proper. The northerly range includes the Khotan, Keriya, and Kilian (or Kiliang) Mountains, while the southerly range comprises the Ullugh-tagh, Sughet, and Raskem (or Raskan) Mountains.

Although these ranges decrease in altitude as they move east, they still reach elevations of 19,000 feet, with some peaks ascending an additional 2,000 to 2,500 feet. The region is characterized by rugged terrain and high-altitude passes; for example, the Sughet-davan in the higher range is 17,825 feet, while the Kyzyl-davan and Sanju-davan in the lower range are 16,900 feet and 16,325 feet, respectively.

## Political and Strategic Context
The Sarikol range intervenes as a rugged line between Chinese Turkestan and the Russian Pamirs. While the range defines the boundary, the actual dividing line has been described as indefinite. 

The surrounding region of central Asia is bounded by the Khingan range to the east, the northern face of the Tibetan plateau to the south, and the Yablonoi Mountains and Tian-shan to the north and north-west. This area forms the Gobi desert. Within this region, the plains around Yarkand and Kashgar sit at elevations between 4,000 and 6,000 feet.

## Historical Routes and Exploration
The area around Kashgar has been a point of significance for travelers and explorers. The Mirza's exploration from Caubul to Kashgar in 1869 followed a route through the Wakhán Valley, the Pamir Steppe, and various stations including Sirikul, Táshkurgán, and Yangi-Hissar before arriving at Kashgar on February 3, 1869. 

Historical analysis of Marco Polo's itinerary suggests he traveled from Badakhshan to Eastern Turkestan, visiting Kashgar before proceeding to Yarkund. This route is considered natural given the positions of the cities. In the 19th century, Kashgar served as the capital (from 1865 to 1877) of Ya’kúb Kúshbegi, a soldier of fortune and Tajik of Shighnan who took the title of Atalik Gházi after the removal of Chinese rule.
