# Wuthering Heights

## Literary Significance and Intensity
The novel *Wuthering Heights*, written by Emily Brontë, is described as a monument of intensity. It is characterized as a work that is passionate, unforgettable, and haunting in its grey melancholy and grimness. Within the context of English literature, the novel is regarded as a "thing apart," standing alone in its achievement.

## Independence from Tradition
The intensity of *Wuthering Heights* is noted for its complete independence. It owes nothing to the achievement of earlier writers, nor does it owe anything to tradition.

## The Brontë Legacy
Emily Brontë is recognized as a great poet and novelist, securing a certain place for all time among women writers. Her poetic achievements include "Last Lines" and "Old Stoic," which are considered among the finest poetry produced by any woman in English literature. 

The broader Brontë tradition provides a vital context for the family's work. While the novels of Anne Brontë are constantly reprinted, they survive primarily through the vitality of this tradition. Charlotte Brontë, though undistinguished as a maker of verse, produced four novels containing passages of great magnificence and pure poetry, specifically in *Villette*. Both *Villette* and *Jane Eyre* are noted for their rough individuality, independence, and intensity, ensuring they will continue to command attention regardless of the future of English fiction.
