# Krishna

The name Krishna appears in the archive's records in two distinct contexts: as a central figure of religious worship and literature in India, and as a native surveyor involved in the geographical exploration of Asia.

## Religious Figure and Worship
Krishna, known as "the Dark One," is an incarnation of Vishnu and serves as one of the most popular objects of worship throughout northern India. Originally, Krishna is believed to have been a deified hero of the Kshatriya caste. His role evolved across different texts; in the older framework of the *Mahabharata*, he is depicted as a great chieftain and an ally to the Pandava brothers. In the *Bhagavad-gita*, an interpolated episode, he is identified as Vishnu and the revealer of the doctrine of *bhakti*, or religious devotion.

Later popular developments of the cult, as seen in the *Vishnu Purana*, present Krishna as the son of a king who was saved from a slaughter of the innocents. In these accounts, he was raised by a cowherd and performed miraculous feats during his childhood while sporting with milkmaids. These events are set near Muttra on the right bank of the Jumna, an area still considered holy ground. Another significant location associated with his later life is Dwarka, located at the westernmost point of the Kathiawar peninsula.

Krishna is known by several names, including *Gopal* (the cowherd), *Gopinath* (the lord of the milkmaids), and *Mathuranath* (the lord of Muttra). While his legitimate consort was Rukmini, the daughter of the king of Berar, he is always associated with Radha in his temples.

## Sects and Preachers
Two prominent founders of sects and preachers of Krishna-worship emerged toward the end of the 15th century: Vallabha and Chaitanya. 
*   **Vallabha:** His followers, the Vallabhacharyas, are primarily found in Gujarat and Rajputana. Their high priests, known as *gosains* or maharajas, receive semi-divine honors. In 1862, a lawsuit in the high court at Bombay exposed licentious practices within this sect.
*   **Chaitanya:** He served as the Vaishnav reformer of Bengal and resided at Nadiya.

Additionally, Swami Narayan was an influential 19th-century preacher in Gujarat, where his followers remained numerous and wealthy.

## Influence on Literature
Krishna's history has heavily influenced Oriya literature, which dates back to the 16th century. The translation of the *Bhagavata Purana* into Oriya during the first half of the 16th century continues to exert a great influence on the masses. In the 17th century, Dina Krsna Das wrote *Rasa Kallola* ("The Waves of Sentiment"), a popular but occasionally indecent work focusing on the early life of Krishna in which every verse begins with the letter "k." 

The broader scope of Oriya poetry is described as an artificial production by *pandits* who utilized Sanskrit rhetoric. Much of this literary tradition is overshadowed by the great temple of Jagannath (a name for Krishna) located at Puri in Orissa.

## Geographical Exploration
In the field of scientific geographical exploration, Krishna was a native surveyor attached to the Indian survey. Between 1879 and 1882, he undertook a remarkable journey that extended from Lhasa northwards through Tsaidam to Sachu (or Saitu) in Mongolia. He also traveled through eastern Tibet to the town of Darchendo (or Tachienlu), situated on the borders of China on the high road between Peking and Lhasa. Although he was unable to reach India via Upper Assam, he returned to the vicinity of Lhasa and eventually crossed the Himalayas using a more westerly route.

In the context of modern map construction and the definition of the highland plateau stretching from the Himalaya to Chinese Turkestan, Krishna provided specific longitude values. For Sachu, Krishna's value is listed as 94 deg. 23', and for Darchendo, the value is 102 deg. 15".
