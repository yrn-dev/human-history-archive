# German and French Influence and Relations

The interactions between German and French entities across history manifest in diverse fields, including military conflict, diplomatic relations, infrastructure development, literature, and athletics.

## Military Conflict and Strategic Movements
During a period of crisis, the French government is described as having acted faultlessly, assenting to peace proposals and abstaining from provocative actions. France delayed its mobilization and held covering troops back from its frontier despite the gathering of German forces. War ensued after Germany demanded that France break her treaty and abandon Russia, with the German military objective being to "take and break France forthwith."

In subsequent military operations, the British Army aligned with French forces. While the 7th German Army was repulsed by a French Corps near D’Einville, it appeared the Germans abandoned an enveloping movement on the Anglo-French left flank, opting instead for a direct attack on the Allied line.

## Infrastructure and Economic Interests
In Asia Minor, German and French interests competed through the construction of railways. The *Gesellschaft der anatolischen Eisenbahnen*, a German company, operated lines from Haidar Pasha to Angora and from Eski-shehr to Bulgurli. The Germans maintained a virtual monopoly over the central plateau. Conversely, a French company operated the railway line from Smyrna to Afium-Kara-hissar, including a branch to Soma. Additionally, a line from Mersina to Tarsus and Adana was under primarily French control.

## Literature and Intellectual Influence
The relationship between French and German influences is evident in the evolution of Dutch literature during the late 18th century. During a general revival of intellectual force in Holland, German lyricism and the romantic movement in Germany replaced the influence previously held by French classicism.

In the realm of English literature, the ballade—considered the noblest artificial form of verse in English—shows a relation to the earliest French forms. Modern English ballades were largely modeled on the 15th-century lines of Henri de Croi.

Furthermore, scholarly works have frequently crossed these linguistic boundaries. The *History of the United States* by Bancroft was translated into both French and German, with competent judges considering the latest volumes as important as the earlier ones. In the sciences, certain papers written in French were presented to the Berlin Academy and later collected in volumes of *Chymische Schriften* between 1761 and 1767.

## Athletics and Competition
In the field of international fencing, French and English teams encountered one another in the final of an international match at the Olympic games in Athens in 1906. This followed the English team's victory over the Germans. The match between the English and French teams, described as the first time an English team faced a French team of the first rank on even terms, resulted in a tie. Upon fighting off the tie, the French were awarded the first prize and the English the second.
