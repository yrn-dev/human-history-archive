# South Africa

## Population and Demographics
The native population of South Africa is estimated at 5,211,329. This population is composed of several distinct racial and ethnic groups. The Bantu, who make up the majority of the native population, are dark-skinned negroids. Within the Bantu stock, there are various tribal groups, including the Damara, Bechuana, Zulu, and Kaffirs, which are further divided into smaller groups.

The aboriginal races of the region are the Hottentots and the Bushmen. Both of these groups have been rapidly diminishing in number; it is expected that within the twentieth century, they will become extinct in British South Africa.

## Mixed Races and Migration
Over the last two hundred years, other populations have been introduced to South Africa from various regions, including British India, Madagascar, Ceylon, Mozambique, and Java. These immigrants include Indians and Malays. Through intermarriage with one another and with the native populations, a hybrid population has emerged, generally categorized as the Mixed Races. The skin colors of this group vary from dark brown to yellow.

## Exploration and Literature
South Africa has been the subject of various travel and research accounts. Notable works include David Livingstone's *Missionary Travels and Researches in South Africa*, published in London in 1857. Other related explorations of the broader southern and central African regions have been documented by authors such as Major A. St H. Gibbons, who wrote *Africa South to North through Marotseland* (1904).
