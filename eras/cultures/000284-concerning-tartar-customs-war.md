# Concerning the Tartar Customs of War

The Tartars, including the Mongols, were a nomadic people of the steppes whose society and military capabilities were deeply intertwined with their environment and lifestyle. Originally a tribe subject to the Kin in the twelfth century, they evolved into a fighting force of exceptional quality through an insurrection against the Kin, during which they assimilated Chinese military science.

## Military Organization and Tactics
The Tartar military was characterized by a high degree of organization and speed. Their troops were organized using a decimal division. A distinctive feature of their battlefield coordination was the use of the Nakkára, a big drum; the boom of this drum served as the signal for action. In some accounts, the entire host of Tartars would play and sing in chorus while waiting for this signal to begin the battle.

The bow was the characteristic weapon of the Tartars, leading Armenian historians to refer to them as "The Archers." To protect themselves, they utilized light yet impenetrable armor made of "cuirbouly," which was leather softened by boiling to take a required form and then hardened.

## Logistics and Sustenance
The Tartars' nomadic nature provided the foundation for their military mobility. They lived in tents and moved seasonally, drifting northward to cool mountains and valleys for summer pasture and southward to warm plains for winter pasture. Their diet consisted primarily of meat and products derived from mare's milk. 

One such product was Kumiz, a drink made from mare's milk. This was prepared by pouring the milk into deep wooden casks or skins and stirring it; the portion that rose to the top was considered the best. They also consumed mare's milk cheese and sour milk. Additionally, the archive mentions the use of "kurút," a type of Tartar curd, as part of their provisions.

## Customs of Vengeance and Execution
The Tartars practiced severe forms of punishment for treason and enmity. According to accounts from Hayton and Friar Ricold, traitors were sometimes executed in ways that served as examples to others. In one instance, a traitor was cut in two, and portions of his flesh were placed in the food eaten by the Emperor Abaka and his barons. In another account, a Tartar baron guilty of treason was boiled alive by Tartar women, and his body was cut into mince-meat to be eaten by the entire army. Other reports suggest that when they captured those in bitter enmity with them, they would eat the victims in vengeance and suck their blood.

## Cultural Degeneration and Influence
While the "genuine Tartars" maintained these specific institutions, later records indicate a period of degeneration. Those Tartars who settled in Cathay abandoned their own institutions in favor of the practices of local idolaters, while those who settled in the Levant adopted the customs of the Saracens.
