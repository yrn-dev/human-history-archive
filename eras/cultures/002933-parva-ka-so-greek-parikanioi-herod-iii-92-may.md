# Parva-ka

The term "Parva-ka" (associated with the Greek *Parikanioi* as referenced in Herodotus iii. 92) appears in historical contexts relating to the ethnography and geography of the ancient world, specifically within the regions of Baluchistan and the broader Persian influence.

## Ethnography of Baluchistan
In the Baluchistan region, the landscape is divided between desert plains and mountain regions. The central desert, known in Arabic medieval geography as the Kir, Kej, Katz, or Kash Kaian, was part of the ancient Kaiani kingdom. In this terminology, the prefixes *Kej* or *Kach* are used to denote valleys or low-level flats, distinguishing them from hills or mountains.

The region is inhabited by various groups:
*   **Mohamadani:** The most numerous of the nomad tribes found in the Baluchistan half of the desert, likely of Arab origin, who occupy the central mountain region.
*   **Naushirwanis:** A purely Persian race residing in the Mashkel and Kharan deserts. They are described as a fine, manly race and are considered identical to the Tahuki or Tahukani found in Perso-Baluchistan.
*   **Tajik:** An underlying Persian population scattered throughout the country. They often serve as agricultural bondsmen or slaves, such as the Dehwars, Dehkans, and the Durzadas (referred to by Herodotus as *Derusiaei*). In the Makran region, these slaves are known as Nakibs.
*   **Rind Tribes:** Groups that claim extraction from the Koraish and Arab origin.

Between the coast's fish-eating populations and the Naushirwanis of the Mashkel and Kharan deserts, there are numerous tribes located near the sources of the Hingol or within the narrow valleys of the Kej and Rakshan tributaries. These groups are identified as remnants of races found in the works of Herodotus or as results of recent immigration.

## Other Historical References to "Parva"
The Latin term *parva* (meaning small) appears in several other distinct historical and geographical contexts within the archive:

### Egypt and North Africa
In Lower Egypt, the town of Damanhur (ancient Timenhor) was known by the Greek name Hermopolis Parva. While its ancient name suggests the worship of Horus (Apollo), the Greek designation indicates Thoth as the local deity.

In North Africa, historical records distinguish between two locations: Leptis Magna and Leptis Parva. The latter is situated in the region called Emporia.

### Roman and Punic History
In the context of the Punic wars, the city of Toledo is referred to as *Urbs Parva*. By the sixth century, it became known as *Urbs Regia*.

## Linguistic Notes
The term *parva* also appears in Latin descriptions of architecture. Isidore of Seville describes a *tugurium* (hut) as a *parva casula* (small cottage), which rural populations called a *capanna* because it could only accommodate one person.
