# Serbs

## Origins and Settlement
The Serbs emerged as one of two groups of tribes, alongside the Croats, who began establishing permanent settlements in Illyria during the 6th century. These two groups spoke a single language and were so closely related that the origin of the distinction between them is obscure. Between 600 and 650, the main body of these immigrants occupied Illyria, with the Serbs settling in the eastern half of the region.

As the Serbs and Croats rapidly absorbed most of the Latinized Illyrians, Illyria became entirely Serbo-Croatian in population, language, and culture. However, this process was not universal; certain Roman provincials known as Mavrovlachi preserved their nationality and language in the interior mountains, and Illyrian tribes in the mountains of Albania remained unconquered and were never Slavonized. Additionally, wealthy coastal city-states maintained their independence and Italian character.

## Culture and Religion
The settlement patterns of the early Slavs led to a marked difference in civilization and creed. While the Croats in the west came under the influence of Italy and Roman Catholicism, the Serbs in the east were influenced by Byzantium and the Greek Church. This distinction has historically tended to keep the Illyrian Slavs politically disunited.

In Dalmatian public life, a distinction exists between the Roman Catholic "Croats," who prefer the Latin alphabet, and the Orthodox "Serbs," who use the Cyrillic alphabet. The Serbs are characterized by a conservative spirit, pride, indolence, and superstition, as well as a devotion to national traditions, poetry, and music. In terms of physical appearance, some Serbs, such as the Shumadian peasantry, are blond with blue or grey eyes; others, like the Montenegrins, are olive-skinned with dark hair and eyes. The Montenegrins are noted for their strength, courage, and stature.

## History and Political Status
In the Middle Ages, the Servian Empire included a vilayet whose northern districts are still referred to by Serbs as Old Servia (*Stara Srbiya*). A significant event in Balkan history and legend is the battle of Kossovo in 1389, fought in the plain of Kossovo (the "Field of Blackbirds"), a valley west of Prishtina watered by the Sibnitza, a tributary of the Servian Ibar. This battle resulted in the destruction of the power of Servia by the Turks.

In other historical contexts, Serb insurgents under Stratemirovi['c] defied Magyar power in the banat, receiving the support of Austrian officers with the permission of the minister of war. Within the framework of certain imperial constitutions, the Serbs, along with other Slav races such as the Croatians, Czechs, Poles, and Slovenes, were placed in a position of political inferiority.

## Political Representation and International Relations
Records of parliamentary representation show varying numbers of Serb delegates. In one instance, the Southern Slav Club included 37 Serbs and 20 Croats. In another parliamentary table, Serbs were represented by 2 members.

In the projected Covenant of the League of Nations, the Serb-Croat-Slovene State was listed as one of the original members of the league, alongside other powers such as the United States, the British Empire, France, and Japan.
