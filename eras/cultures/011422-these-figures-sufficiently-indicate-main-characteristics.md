# Characteristics of Asia's Air Temperatures

The air temperatures of Asia are defined by extreme seasonal variations and the influence of geography, elevation, and surface composition. In the northern portions of the continent, winters are characterized by extreme severity and long durations. Even as far south as the circle of 35 deg. N. lat., minimum temperatures frequently drop almost as low as zero degrees Fahrenheit. Conversely, summers in the northern latitudes are hot, although they are short; the maximum summer heat in these areas is only slightly less than the heat observed in tropical countries located further south.

## Geographic and Topographic Influences

The proximity of the ocean provides a moderating effect on temperatures, which is felt significantly along the eastern and southern parts of Asia, particularly where the land consists of peninsulas or islands. In contrast, the central part of Asia, as well as the table-lands of Persia and Afghanistan, feature great elevations above sea level that tend to exaggerate the cold of winter.

The sterility of the surface in these same regions, caused by small amounts of rainfall, acts to increase summer heat. Because the soil is dry and lacks vegetation or moisture, solar heat accumulates greatly on the surface during the summer. This heat cannot be released upwards through evaporation, nor can it be conveyed downwards as occurs in the ocean. A similar process occurs in the winter in a negative direction; long, clear nights lead to a prolonged loss of heat by radiation. This effect is further intensified in areas where the air has little vapour or where the surface is covered with snow.

## Solar Heat and Tropical Modifications

The high summer temperatures in areas north of the tropic of Cancer are explained by the increased length of the day in higher latitudes. This increased duration more than compensates for the loss of heat resulting from the smaller mid-day altitude of the sun. For example, on June 21st, the difference in the heating power of the sun's rays at noon between latitude 20 deg. and latitude 45 deg. is only approximately 2%. However, the accumulated heat received during the day is about 11% greater in the higher latitude, where the day lasts 15-1/2 hours compared to 13-1/4 hours in the lower latitude.

In the tropical region, the periodical summer rains serve as a very important modifying cause. These rains tend to greatly arrest the increase of summer heat in the areas where they occur, giving the climate peculiar characteristics.

## Subsurface Temperatures in Siberia

The slow diffusion of heat within the earth's solid crust is illustrated by the frozen soil in the vicinity of Yakutsk in Siberia. In this region, the earth remains permanently frozen to a depth of more than 380 ft., where temperatures remain 5 or 6 deg. Fahr. below the freezing point of water. At a depth of 50 ft., the temperature is approximately 15 Fahr. below the freezing point. Summer heat only manages to thaw the surface to a depth of about 3 ft. Despite these extreme soil conditions, the land is capable of producing crops of other grain and wheat from fifteen to forty fold.
