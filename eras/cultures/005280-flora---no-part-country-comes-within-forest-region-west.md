# Flora

The study of global flora reveals a diverse array of vegetation determined primarily by climatic conditions, altitude, and soil fertility. From the dense tropical forests of Asia and Africa to the specialized alpine zones of the Carpathians and the Andes, plant life varies significantly across different geographical regions.

## Asian and Indian Flora
The general physiognomy of Indian flora is mainly determined by humidity. A strong contrast exists between the impenetrable shady forests of the west coast of the Indian peninsula, Ceylon, eastern Bengal, and the Malay peninsula, and the more loosely-timbered districts found in the north-western Himalaya and central India. The Tarai jungles at the foot of the eastern Himalaya are characterized by luxuriant growth and dense vegetation. In contrast, areas cut off from rain are nearly complete deserts, while highly cultivated lowlands feature planted wood.

In south-eastern Asia—including the Malayan peninsula, islands, and India—Sir J.D. Hooker estimated the number of indigenous plant species to be between 12,000 and 15,000. The dominant orders include Leguminosae, Rubiaceae, Orchidaceae, Compositae, Gramineae, Euphorbiaceae, Acanthaceae, Cyperaceae, and Labiatae. While tropical plants dominate the region's character, there is a heterogeneous mix of temperate and alpine plants. The perennially humid regions of the Malayan peninsula and the western archipelago are covered in dense forests, often made difficult to traverse by the thorny cane of the genus *Calamus*.

In the Sikhota-alin mountains, the flora exhibits a combination of subarctic and warm-climate species, where cedar-pine and larch are joined by wild vines.

## African Flora
In British Central Africa, no part of the country falls within the forest region of West Africa; instead, the landscape is primarily savannah or park-like. While the region is well-covered with vegetation, actual forests of lofty West African type are few, limited mostly to the Shiré Highlands, Angoniland, and Nyika plateaus, as well as some valleys near the south end of Tanganyika. Tropical luxuriance is found on the slopes of Mounts Chiradzulu and Mlanje. The upper plateaus of Mount Mlanje feature the "Mlanje cedar" (*Widdringtonia whytei*), a yew-like conifer related to the cypress and the most northern form of a group otherwise confined to the Cape Colony in South Africa. Lower-lying plains are covered in coarse grass that can reach 10 feet in height.

In regions bordering Victoria Nyanza, the flora resembles that of Uganda. Coastal regions are characterized by the coco-nut palm and mangrove, while ebony grows in scrub-jungle. The Kikuyu escarpment features bamboo, fig, and cotton, while the Mau escarpment contains vast forests of junipers and olives.

## American Flora
In Bolivia, the flora represents every climatic zone due to diversity in altitude. The Amazon basin contains luxuriant tropical forests, while the lofty Cordilleras feature scanty Arctic vegetation. The puna region is characterized by coarse grasses, quinoa (*Chenopodium quinoa*), oca (*Oxalis tuberosa*), and the potato. In the sub-tropical and tropical zones, indigenous plants include cassava, pineapple, guava, peanuts, and various hardwoods such as mahogany, rosewood, and cedar. Coca is cultivated on the eastern slopes of the Andes at altitudes of 5,000 to 6,000 feet, and calisaya trees are found between 3,000 and 9,000 feet.

In Louisiana, heavy rainfall and fertile soil produce luxuriant subtropical vegetation. The river swamp regions are covered with cypress trees and Spanish moss. Alluvial regions feature various oaks, tupelo gum, and black walnut. Higher, drier soils support long-leaf and loblolly yellow pine, as well as red maple and elm. The state is noted for flowers such as magnolias, camellias, hibiscus, and various aquatic plants like water hyacinths and water lilies.

## European and Mountain Flora
The Carpathians act as a protective wall, creating a milder climate to the south. The mountains feature four distinct vegetation zones based on altitude:
1. Beech (up to 4,000 ft.)
2. Scottish fir (up to 5,000 ft.)
3. Pine (which becomes dwarfed and disappears at 6,000 ft.)
4. Lichen and moss-covered or bare rock.

The High Tatra and Transylvanian Mountains possess flora similar to the middle region of the Alps. Transylvania is also noted for its sea-shore flora found in marshes, ponds, and salt-impregnated lakes.

## Arid Regions
The flora of the rainless region of south-western Asia is continuous with the desert flora of northern and eastern Africa. This region extends from the coast of Senegal to the 75th meridian East, encompassing the peninsula of Arabia, Afghanistan, Baluchistan, south Persia, and the shores of the Persian Gulf. It reaches its eastern limit along the southern parts of the Punjab and the border of the rainless tract along the Indus.
