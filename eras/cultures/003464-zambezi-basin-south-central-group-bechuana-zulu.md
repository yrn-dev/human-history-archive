# Bantu Languages of the Zambezi Basin and South-Central Africa

The Bantu languages are characterized by a striking homogeneity, which distinguishes them from the linguistic confusion found in Africa north of the Bantu borderland. Within the broad classification of Bantu speech, the regions encompassing the Zambezi basin and South-Central Africa contain several distinct groups and dialects, some of which are noted for their archaic forms.

## The Zambezi Basin and Associated Groups

The Zambezi basin is a primary geographical marker for several Bantu language groups. The Nyanja group is perhaps the most extensive in the Bantu field. It is associated with the shores of the southern half of Lake Nyasa, the valley of the Shire down to its junction with the Zambezi, and the lands on both banks of the Zambezi, including its delta. The Nyanja domain extends west to the Central Zambezi and the river Luangwa via the Senga language. South of the Central Zambezi, Nyanja dialects extend as far west as the Victoria Falls and eastwards over Mashonaland to the sea-coast. Principal dialects include:
* **Cinyanja** (Eastern Nyasaland)
* **Ci-peta** and **Ci-maravi** (South-West Nyasaland)
* **Ci-mananja** (Shire highlands)
* **Ci-mobo** and **Ci-machinjiri** (Shire valley)
* **Ci-sena** or **Ci-nyungwe** (Tete and Sena)
* **Ci-mazaro** (Lower Zambezi)
* **Ci-podzo** (Zambezi delta), which is described as a very distinct and archaic type of language.

Other languages in the Zambezi region include the Barotseland languages, most notably **Si-luyi**, and the related **Mabunda** of Western Barotseland. The **Subiya-Tonga-Ila** group includes the dialects of the Ba-ila (Mashukulumbwe) and the Ba-tetela. **Ci-subiya**, the dominant language of South-West Zambezia and areas between the Zambezi and the Chobe-Linyante river, is considered one of the most archaic Bantu languages.

Additionally, the **Bisa** or **Wisa** family includes dialects such as **Lubisa** and **Ilala**, located between the Luangwa river and Lake Bangweulu.

## The South-Central Group (Bechuana-Zulu)

The South-Central group is divided into several distinct linguistic clusters:

**The Bechuana Group**
This group encompasses all Bantu dialects of the Bechuanaland protectorate west of the Guai river, stretching between the Zambezi to the north and the Orange river to the south. It extends westward to the domain of the Oci-herero, interrupted by Bushmen and Hottentot populations. Key dialects include:
* **Se-suto**: The language of Basutoland.
* **Se-rolon** and **Se-mangwato**: Spoken in the Eastern Kalahri.
* **Se-kololo**: The court language of Barotseland.
* **Ci-venda** and **Se-pedi** (or Peli): Principal dialects of the Transvaal.

**The Zulu and Ronga Groups**
The **Ronga (Tonga)** languages are found in Portuguese South-East Africa, including Gazaland, the Lower Limpopo valley, and Delagoa Bay. These languages represent a mingling of Nyanja and Zulu influences, though Zulu predominates. The **Zulu-Kaffir** language, while preserving some characteristics of the hypothetical Bantu mother-tongue, exhibits marked deviations in phonetics and vocabulary. It was once considered the most archaic Bantu type before the languages of the Great Lakes were studied.

## Regional Migration and Intermixture

The distribution of these languages is linked to historical movements. It is suggested that the Zulu-Kaffir race migrated from countries north-east of Tanganyika, crossing the Zambezi to reach their current home. Later, intestine wars and conflicts led to a north-eastward return migration of Zulus from Matabeleland.

In terms of population, the Bantu compose the majority of the native population in these regions. While the indigenous type is the Negro, some Bantu races show signs of intermixture with Bushmen or Hottentots. In South Africa, the Bantu (including Zulu, Bechuana, Damara, and Kaffirs) exist alongside diminishing populations of aboriginal Bushmen and Hottentots, as well as "Mixed Races" resulting from the introduction of Indians and Malays from regions such as Java, Ceylon, and British India over the last two hundred years.

## Linguistic Characteristics

The Bantu languages of this region exhibit specific structural features. In various Bechuana dialects of Central South Africa, a specific concord takes the form of "n" at the end of words. In Zulu-Kaffir, the form *ngabantu* (or *ngumuntu*) is observed. 

The archive notes that the most archaic dialects currently existing include the Subiya speech of the western Zambezi, the Ci-bodzo of the Zambezi delta, and the Kibemba (or Kiwemba) of the plateau country between Lake Nyasa, Lake Tanganyika, and the river Luapula. Kibemba is noted for coming very close to the original form of the Bantu mother-language.
