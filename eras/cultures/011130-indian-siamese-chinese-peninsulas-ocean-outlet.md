# Indian, Siamese and Chinese Peninsulas

The southern section of the Asian continent is characterized by a combination of highly elevated table-lands, reaching altitudes of 15,000 to 16,000 feet, and the lowlands of the Indian, Siamese, and Chinese peninsulas. These lowland regions are distinguished by having an ocean outlet for their drainage.

## Geography and Hydrography
The region's climate and geography are heavily influenced by the south-west monsoon, which carries heated air charged with watery vapour from the Indian Ocean over southern Asia. When this air encounters continuous ranges of mountains along the coasts of India and the Burmo-Siamese peninsula, the wind current is interrupted. This forces the air to rise, causing it to rarefy and its temperature to fall, which results in the condensation of vapour and the release of large amounts of rain upon the land.

In the Bay of Bengal, the Andaman and Nicobar island groups are situated on a line connecting Cape Negrais (the south-western extremity of Burma) and the north end of Sumatra; these islands may owe their existence to volcanic agencies extending across the Indian Ocean. Additionally, the Maldives and Laccadives are coral island groups located near the Indian peninsula.

## Indo-China and the Burmo-Siamese Region
The area comprising Burma, Siam, Cambodia, and Annam is often termed Indo-China, as these nations lie between India and China in both position and civilization. 

*   **Burma:** A province of British India, Burma is divided into Upper and Lower Burma. It is bounded by the Bay of Bengal to the west and the Mekong river to the east. Its coastline extends approximately 1,200 miles from the estuary of the Pakchan in the south to Taknaf in the north. The province includes Burma proper, the Chin hills, and the Shan States.
*   **Siam:** The Siamese (or Thai) are described as a late invasion from southern China, arriving around the 13th century. They speak a monosyllabic language of the Chinese type but utilize an Indian alphabet.
*   **Cambodia and Annam:** The Khmers of Cambodia formed an ancient kingdom with strong Hindu influence, evidenced by ruins dating from A.D. 800 to 1000. In contrast, Annam and Tongking utilize Chinese institutions and script. Annam's history was marked by a struggle for sovereignty with the Chinese until its acquisition by the French. Cochin China was previously the seat of the kingdom of Champa, which possessed a hinduized Malay civilization before being absorbed by Annam.

## Ethnography and Cultural Influence
The populations of the regions between India and the Mongolian areas are primarily of Chinese and Mongolian types. This includes the Siamese, the Burmese, and the Tibetans. These groups generally use letters derived from India and practice Buddhism, which was adopted from the Indians. The Burmese are linguistically allied to Tibetans and likely entered Burma from the north-west.

The Malay race is a seafaring people distributed across the Malay Peninsula, Java, Sumatra, Borneo, the Philippines, and other islands. Their history is marked by three periods: a semi-barbarous stage, a period of strong Hindu influence (evidenced by Indian words, customs, and ruins), and a subsequent transition to Islam before the 16th century. While the Arabic alphabet is currently used on the mainland, Indian alphabets persist in Sumatra and Java.

## Political and Military History
The history of the region involves frequent conflict and territorial shifts:
*   **Burmese-Siamese Conflicts:** The Siamese revolted in 1771 and were not subdued by the Burmans, though the Burmans held the coast as far as Mergui. After failed Burmese invasions of Siam in 1785 and 1786, peace was concluded in 1793. The Siamese ceded the coast of Tenasserim, including the seaports of Tavoy and Mergui, to the Burmans.
*   **British Involvement:** Britain annexed the kingdom founded by Alompra (who had united Burma around 1750) in 1885. Earlier, in 1795, a dispute arose when 5,000 Burmese troops entered the British district of Chittagong in pursuit of fugitives; the matter was settled amicably through General Erskine.
*   **Regional Shifts:** The advance of the Siamese contributed to the reduction of the Cambodian kingdom and the division of the Mon-Annam speaking peoples.

## Mapping and Surveying
Extensive trigonometrical surveys have been conducted in British India since 1800. These include an *Indian Atlas* (begun in 1827) that encompasses the Malay Peninsula and Ceylon. In Siam, a regular survey was organized between 1881 and 1883 by J. McCarthy, a former official of the Indian survey, which assisted in determining the Franco-Siamese frontier in 1906.
