# Regional Climates and Health

The study of global climates reveals a wide array of atmospheric conditions, ranging from tropical zones and continental interiors to temperate islands and mountainous highlands. These variations significantly influence the health of inhabitants, the nature of local vegetation, and the general habitability of different regions.

## European Continental and Temperate Climates

In Central and Eastern Europe, climates are often characterized by significant local contrasts. Bohemia possesses a continental climate that is generally healthy, though it varies across the country. The center, including Prague, is the mildest region with a mean annual temperature of 48.5° F. Rainfall in Bohemia is concentrated in the summer, with mean annual totals ranging from 18 inches in the interior to 40 inches in the Riesengebirge and 60 to 70 inches in the Bohmerwald.

Bulgaria's climate is more severe than other European regions of similar latitude due to the configuration of the Balkan Peninsula and its mountain ranges. This results in extreme summer heat and winter cold. In districts between the Balkans and the Danube, winter temperatures can drop to 24° below zero, and the river frequently freezes. Conversely, the high tableland of Sofia is more equable, with a maximum temperature of 86° and a minimum of 2°. The eastern districts are moderated by the sea, though the Black Sea is known for storms and the coast is exposed to violent north-east winds. Eastern Rumelia is comparatively warm, with spring arriving six weeks earlier than in other Bulgarian regions. The mean temperature for Bulgaria is 52°, with annual rainfall between 25 and 59 inches. While generally healthy, particularly in the mountains, malarial fever occurs in the upland plain of Sofia and the valley of the Maritza due to poor drainage.

In the kingdom of Saxony, the district of Lössnitz is sheltered from north winds by vine-clad hills, creating a climate so warm and healthy it is called the "Saxon Nice." In Ireland, the Liffey valley is described as very mild and healthy, whereas the northern districts are very moist with frequent fogs due to the extent of the bogs.

## Mediterranean and Southern European Regions

The climates of the Mediterranean regions vary by geography and elevation. In Greece, ancient writers such as Aristotle, Hippocrates, and Herodotus viewed the climate as changeful with diverse local temperatures, which they believed stimulated the energies of the inhabitants. They considered the climate of Asia to be more genial and favorable to vegetable and animal life, though more enervating than that of Greece. There is evidence that ancient Greece was healthier than it is currently, attributed to better water supplies and more industrious cultivation.

In Spain, Old Castile is healthy but subject to severe heat and cold, with heavy rainfall in the north-west and early snow in the mountains. New Castile is more rigorous; despite a mean annual temperature of 59° F, the highlands face icy or scorching gales and the valleys experience oppressive summer heat. Rainfall in New Castile rarely exceeds 10 inches annually.

On the islands, such as Corsica, the climate ranges from warmth in the lowlands to extreme rigour in the mountains, with the intermediate region being the most temperate and healthy. At Ajaccio, the mean annual temperature is 63° F, with dominant winds blowing from the south-east and south-west.

## African and Tropical Climates

Tropical regions exhibit distinct zoning, often parallel to the coast. In British East Africa, the coast is hot but generally healthier than other tropical coastlands due to dry soil and constant breezes from the Indian Ocean; annual rainfall here is approximately 35 inches. Inland highlands above 5,000 feet are very healthy, with fever being almost unknown and average temperatures ranging from 66° F in the cool season to 73° F in the hot season. Above 7,000 feet, the climate becomes colder and frosts occur. Rainfall in these highlands is between 40 and 50 inches. In contrast, the region bordering Victoria Nyanza is typically tropical with rainfall exceeding 60 inches, making it unsuitable for Europeans. The hottest period in the protectorate is December to April, and the coolest is July to September. Rainfall occurs in "greater rains" (March to June) and "smaller rains" (November to December), though severe droughts occasionally occur.

In Egypt, the climate of Cairo is generally healthy with a mean temperature of about 68° F. While rain is rare, early morning fogs occur in winter, and the districts near the Nile become damp from September to November as floodwaters subside. The north wind and rising water help keep the summer air cool. Historically, Cairo had a heavy death rate due to insanitary conditions, but improvements in drainage and water supply since 1882 have addressed this.
