# Symphytum and Related Botanical Orders

## The Boraginaceae Order
The *Boraginaceae* is an order of plants categorized within the sympetalous section of dicotyledons and is a member of the *Tubiflorae* series. These plants are typically perennial or annual herbs characterized by rough hairs. While most are herbaceous, some genera, such as *Ehretia* and *Cordia*, are arborescent or shrubby and are found in sub-tropical or tropical regions.

In Britain, this order is represented by several genera, including comfrey (*Symphytum*), bugloss (*Echium*), *Myosotis*, and hounds-tongue (*Cynoglossum*). Additionally, borage (*Borago officinalis*) is found in waste ground as a garden escape.

## Characteristics of Symphytum and Allies
Plants within the *Boraginaceae* generally possess narrow, entire leaves that are alternate. In certain genera, such as *Cynoglossum* and *Pulmonaria* (lungwort), the radical leaves differ from the stem-leaves, often appearing broader or heart-shaped.

A defining feature of this order is the dorsiventral (one-sided) inflorescence, which is particularly evident in species of *Myosotis* (forget-me-not). The cyme begins as a closely coiled structure and uncoils as the flowers open. During this process, the flowers often undergo a color change, shifting from red in the bud to blue upon expansion; this is observed in *Symphytum*, *Echium*, and *Myosotis*.

The flowers are generally regular, though the corolla form varies by genus:
*   **Comfrey (*Symphytum*):** Tubular corolla.
*   **Borage:** Rotate corolla.
*   **Hounds-tongue:** Funnel-shaped corolla.
*   **Alkanet (*Anchusa*):** Salver-shaped corolla.

In some instances, the throat of the corolla is closed by scale-like outgrowths known as the corona. Deviations from the regular corolla occur in *Lycopsis*, where it is bent, and in *Echium* and allied genera, where it is oblique. Regarding the fruit of *Symphytum*, it is surrounded by a persistent calyx, and the style appears to arise from the enlarged base of the carpels.

## Other Dicotyledonous and Monocotyledonous Orders
The archive contains records of various other botanical groups with distinct reproductive and structural features.

### Caryophyllaceae
This order of dicotyledonous plants contains approximately 1,300 species across 60 genera, distributed mainly in arctic, alpine, and temperate regions. These plants are herbs, sometimes shrubby at the base, featuring swollen nodes and opposite, simple leaves. Their flowers are regular, possessing four or five petals and sepals, twice as many stamens, and a pistil of two to five carpels. The fruit is a capsule that opens via apical teeth to release small seeds containing a curved embryo and floury endosperm. The tribe *Sileneae* within this order includes *Dianthus* (pink) and *Silene* (bladder campion, catchfly). Some species, such as *Silene nutans* and *Lychnis vespertina*, are night-flowering and scented to attract night-flying moths.

### Berberidaceae
Represented by barberry (*Berberis vulgaris*), this order contains about 100 species in the Andes of South America (extending into Patagonia) and the north temperate zone. These shrubs have spiny-toothed leaves and pale yellow flowers in hanging racemes. The flowers are arranged in regular succession below a one-carpel ovary, with sepals, petals, and stamens in multiples of 2, 3, or 4. The resulting orange-red berries are edible.

### Monocotyledons and Grasses
The archive also notes the *Bromeliaceae*, a natural order of Monocotyledons found in sub-tropical and tropical America. These xerophytes, including the pine-apple and Spanish moss, often have a rosette of leaves that forms a basin to collect water.

Additionally, the grass family includes *Hordeum sativum* (barley) and the *Bambuseae* (bamboo). Bamboo is characterized by woody, hollow stems that can reach 120 feet in height, springing from underground root-stocks. Its flowers typically possess six stamens and three lodicules.

## Other Shrub and Succulent Genera
The *Rhamnus cathartica* (buckthorn) is a much-branched shrub of the *Rhamnaceae* order, native to Europe, north Asia, and north Africa. It features blackish bark and small green regular flowers. Its succulent black fruit has strong purgative properties.

In the cactus family, the genus *Melocactus* (melon-thistle) consists of about 90 species primarily in Mexico, Brazil, and the West Indies. These plants form succulent masses with furrows and stellated spines, topped by a cylindrical crown (cephalium) of reddish-brown bristles. Small rosy-pink flowers emerge from this crown, followed by red berries with an acid flavor.
