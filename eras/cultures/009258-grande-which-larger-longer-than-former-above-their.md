# Grande (River)

The Grande, also known as the Guapay, is a significant river located in Bolivia. It is a principal Bolivian tributary of the Mamore river and is considered the main stream of its system because it is both longer and larger than the Mamore above the point where the two rivers meet.

## Course and Geography
The Grande rises in the Cordillera Oriental, situated to the east of Lake Pampa Aullaguas. From its source, it flows in an easterly direction toward the north extremity of the Sierra de Misiones, where it eventually emerges upon the lowlands of Bolivia. 

The river's path is characterized by a magnificent curve that turns toward the north, passing around the south-east extremity of the Sierra de Cochabamba and skirting the Llanos de Chiquitos. After this, the river turns north-west to reach its confluence with the Mamore. This meeting point is known as Junta de los Rios, located at approximately 64 deg. 40' W. longitude and 15 deg. 20' S. latitude.

## Physical Characteristics and Navigation
The Grande follows a tortuous course that extends over 700 meters. Despite its size and length relative to the Mamore, the course of the Grande is described as not navigable.

## Tributaries
The river has several principal tributaries, including:
*   The Mizque
*   The Piray (also referred to as the Sara)
*   The Yapacani

The Yapacani rises on the east slopes of the Cordillera Real and flows east through Cochabamba toward the sierras of the same name, where it makes a great bend to the north.

## Regional Context
The Grande is part of the larger Amazon river-system in Bolivia, one of three distinct regional systems (the others being the La Plata and the Central Plateau). This system consists of rivers flowing into the Madeira—a great tributary of the Amazon—as well as small tributaries of the Purus and Acre in the north. Together, these waters form a drainage basin that covers more than one-half of the Bolivian republic. The Mamore and the Grande unite to form the Madeira at 10 deg. 20' S. latitude.
