# Haute-Loire

Haute-Loire is a department in central France. It is partially associated with the former province of Auvergne, specifically through the arrondissement of Brioude.

## Geography and Terrain
The region is characterized by its volcanic origins, containing numerous mountains of this type. The department of Haute-Loire serves as a northern boundary to the department of Lozère.

## Brioude
Brioude is a town located in the department of Haute-Loire and serves as the capital of an arrondissement. Situated on the left bank of the Allier river at an elevation of 1,467 feet above sea level, it is located 47 miles northwest of Le Puy and is positioned on the Paris-Lyon railway. As of 1906, the population of Brioude was 4,581.

The town is noted for having largely escaped modernization, retaining many of its old fountains and houses. Its layout consists of narrow, irregular streets, though it is surrounded by wide, tree-lined boulevards. The most significant building in the town is the church of St Julian, constructed during the 12th and 13th centuries. This church is a fine example of the Romanesque style of Auvergne, particularly in its mosaic exterior ornamentation and its choir, which features radiating chapels and an apse.

Administratively, Brioude is the seat of tribunals of commerce and first instance, as well as a sub-prefect.

## Economy and Industry
The plain surrounding Brioude is highly fertile, supporting a considerable grain trade and market-gardening in the outskirts of the town. The industrial activities of the area include:
* Antimony mining and founding
* Lace-making
* Saw-milling
* Brewing
