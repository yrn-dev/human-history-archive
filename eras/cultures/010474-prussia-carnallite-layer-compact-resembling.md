# Prussia

## Geological Deposits and Minerals
In the salt deposits located at Stassfurt in Prussia, a massive variety of the mineral boracite occurs in the form of nodules, known as stassfurtite. The characteristics of this mineral vary depending on the layer in which it is found. Stassfurtite originating from the carnallite layer is compact and resembles fine-grained marble, appearing in white or greenish colors. Conversely, the variety found in the kainite layer is yellowish or reddish in color and possesses a soft, earthy texture.

## Political and Military History
In 1740, Prussia was characterized as a thoroughly organized and compact small power. At that time, it maintained an army of 100,000 soldiers. Although it was viewed as one of Europe's minor armies—having only participated in the desultory Rhine campaign of 1733-35—the Prussian infantry was drilled to a level of perfection previously unattained. This training allowed a Prussian soldier to fire five shots for every three fired by an Austrian soldier, though the Prussian artillery and cavalry were less efficient.

Because the standing army theory had been developed to full effect without the interruption of wars, Prussian regiments were capable of taking the field immediately, whereas the Austrians required drafts to complete their forces. This advantage allowed Frederick to concentrate his army on the Oder and cross the frontier into Silesia on December 16, 1740, without a declaration of war. The Prussian army subsequently held all of Silesia and invested the strong places of Neisse, Brieg, and Glogau.

## Geography and Urban Centers
The Prussian province of West Prussia includes the town of Kulmsee. Situated on a lake, Kulmsee is located 14 miles by rail north of Thorn and sits at the junction of railways leading to Marienburg and Bromberg. As of 1900, the town had a population of 8,987. Its architectural landmarks include an Evangelical church and a fine Roman Catholic cathedral; the latter was constructed in the 13th century and underwent restoration in the 15th century. Until the year 1823, Kulmsee served as the seat of the bishops of Kulm.

## Ecclesiastical Appointments
The archive records the appointment of Marc Marie, Marquis de Bombelles, to a Prussian religious office. Bombelles, a French ecclesiastic and diplomatist who had previously served in the army during the Seven Years' War and as the ambassador of France to Portugal, withdrew to the monastery of Brunn in Austria in 1804. Following this, he became the bishop of Oberglogau in Prussia.
