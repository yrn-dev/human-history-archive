# TAICAN AND THE MOUNTAINS OF SALT

## Geography and Location
Taican is a fortified location reached after a twelve days' journey. It is characterized as a fine place that serves as the site of a great corn market. The region is noted for its specific geological features, particularly the mountains situated toward the south, which are composed entirely of salt.

## The Salt Mountains of Taican
The salt found in the mountains south of Taican is described as the best in the world. It is characterized by extreme hardness, to the extent that it can only be broken using iron picks. The abundance of this resource is such that it is claimed it could supply the entire world until the end of time. Due to the quality and quantity of the salt, people travel from surrounding countries—some from as far as a thirty days' journey—to obtain it.

## Regional Flora and Environment
In addition to the salt mountains, other mountains in the vicinity produce pistachioes and almonds, both of which are noted for being exceedingly cheap. 

Regarding the route to Taican, records indicate that the road from Balkh toward the city was described by some as barren and dreary. However, the presence of ruins of houses and aqueducts suggests that the land was peopled at one time, though it later became destitute of inhabitants and water. This state of decline is attributed to Mongol devastations.

## Related Regional Provinces
Taican is associated in historical records with the province of Casem.
