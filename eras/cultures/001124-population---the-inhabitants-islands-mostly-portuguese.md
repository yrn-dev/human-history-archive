# Population of Various Island and Coastal Territories

The demographic composition of various global islands and coastal settlements varies significantly based on colonial history, migration patterns, and indigenous presence. These populations range from descendants of European settlers to immigrant laborers and indigenous groups.

## Portuguese-Influenced Populations
In the Azores, the inhabitants of the islands are primarily of Portuguese origin, though there is a well-marked strain of Flemish and Moorish blood. This population is characterized by a low average of infant mortality and a high birth-rate. While a large proportion of the poorer classes—particularly older men and women—are totally illiterate, education is spreading more rapidly than in Portugal itself because children are often sent to state schools in the United States. In the islands of St Michael's and Fayal, there are considerable numbers of mulattoes, Negroes, and immigrants from Ireland, Scotland, and England. As of 1900, the total number of resident foreigners was 1,490. In the administrative district comprising Corvo, Flores, Pico, and Fayal, the capital is Horta, which had a population of 6,574 in 1900.

In Macao, a Portuguese settlement on the coast of China, the population in 1896 totaled 78,627. This included 74,568 Chinese, 3,898 Portuguese, and 161 individuals of other nationalities. More than three-fourths of the Portuguese inhabitants are natives of Macao, a group described as being inferior in physique to their European ancestors.

In the Molucca Islands, specifically on Bachian, none of the coastal dwellers are indigenous. The population of approximately 13,000 includes Malays with a Papuan element, immigrants from Celebes, Arabs, Chinese, and the Sirani, who are Christian descendants of the Portuguese.

## Pacific Archipelagoes
The Marianas (or Ladrones) in the north-western Pacific have a population of 2,500 (excluding Guam). These inhabitants are mostly descendants of Tagal immigrants from the Philippines. The native population, known to early Spanish colonists as Chamorros, has died out as a distinct people, although their descendants intermarried with natives of the Carolines and immigrant Tagals. At the time of Spanish occupation in 1668, the Chamorros were estimated to number between 40,000 and 60,000, but this number fell to only 1,800 in less than a century.

In the Bonin Islands, the population grew to about 4,500 following a period of doubtful ownership and fitful communication. Early settlers included a party in 1830 consisting of a Dane, two American citizens, and two British subjects, accompanied by Hawaiian natives. By 1853, Peel Island had 31 inhabitants: four Americans, four English, one Portuguese, and the remainder natives of the Ladrones and the Sandwich Islands. By 1875, the colony grew to 69 people, only five of whom were pure whites. These settlers used English as their language and viewed themselves as a British colony.

## The West Indies and Americas
The populations of the West Indies are highly diverse and largely composed of imported African negroes, who make up four-fifths of the population in Jamaica. The islands also contain some Chinese and a considerable number of imported East Indian coolies. The aboriginal races consist of Caribs and American Indians from the mainland. Intermixture with Portuguese and Spanish blood has resulted in many mixed types. The total European population in this group of colonies exceeds 80,000, with an additional 15,000 in Bermuda.

## Other Island Populations
The Falkland Islands, which serve as a valuable coaling-station with a wireless installation, had a population of only 2,000. These inhabitants were mostly Scottish shepherds, along with the residents of the capital, Port Stanley.

In Bombay, the earliest known inhabitants were the Kolis, a race of fishermen and husbandmen who occupied the island around the beginning of the Christian era. The area remained under Hindu rule until 1348, when it was captured by a Mahommedan force from Gujarat.
