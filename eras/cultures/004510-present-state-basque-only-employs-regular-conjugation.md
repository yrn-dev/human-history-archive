# Basque Conjugation

The Basque language possesses a structural character that is absolutely different from the surrounding Aryan languages. In its current state, the language employs its regular conjugation only exceptionally. Instead, it has developed an extensive system of conjugation—likely under the influence of neo-Latin—that combines a few auxiliary verbs with what may be termed participles, which are in fact declined nouns.

## Auxiliary Verbs and Periphrastic Forms
The Basque system of conjugation relies heavily on auxiliaries. The principal auxiliaries are *izan* ("to be") and *ukan* ("to have"). Other verbs frequently used in this manner include *edin* ("to can"), *eza* ("to be able"), *egin* ("to make"), *joan* ("to go"), and *eroan* ("to draw" or "to move"). 

Examples of this combined structure include the phrase *ikusten dut*, meaning "I have it in seeing" (translated as "I see it"), and *ikusiko dut*, meaning "I have it to be seen" (translated as "I will see it").

## Complexity and Structure
The conjugation in Basque is considered most complicated because it incorporates the subject pronouns as well as the direct and indirect complements. Due to this integration, a single transitive form can offer twenty-four different variations, such as "he gives it," "he gives it to you," or "he gives them to us."

The language does not appear to use different voices within the same radical; consequently, there are separate verbs for transitive and intransitive functions.

## Tense and Mood
Primitively, the language featured only two tenses: the present and the imperfect. These were distinguished in the following ways:
*   **Transitive Verbs:** Distinguished by the placement of the personal subject element. For example, *dakigu* means "we are knowing it" (where *gu* signifies "we"), while *ginaki* means "we were knowing it."
*   **Intransitive Verbs:** Distinguished by a nasalization of the radical. For example, *niz* means "I am," while *nintz* means "I was."

In modern times, a conjectural future has been developed by adding the suffix *ke*, as seen in *dakiket* ("I will, shall or probably can know it"). 

Regarding moods, no proper moods are known. However, conjunctive or subjunctive forms are created by adding a final *n*. An example of this is *dakusat* ("I am looking at it") becoming *dakusadan* ("if I see it").
