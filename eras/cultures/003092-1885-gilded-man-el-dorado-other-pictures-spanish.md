# The Gilded Man (El Dorado) and other Pictures of the Spanish Occupancy of America

*The Gilded Man (El Dorado) and other Pictures of the Spanish Occupancy of America* is a work authored by the American archaeologist Adolph Francis Alphonse Bandelier. Published in New York in 1893, the text is cited as a reference for those studying the *Descubrimiento*, one of the world's famous narratives of travel.

## Author Biography
Adolph Francis Alphonse Bandelier was born in Bern, Switzerland, on August 6, 1840. During his youth, he emigrated to the United States. Following 1880, Bandelier dedicated his career to ethnological and archaeological work. His early studies focused on Mexico (specifically Sonora), as well as Arizona and New Mexico. Through this work, he became a leading authority on the history of that region and, alongside his successors and F. H. Cushing, a primary authority on its prehistoric civilization.

In 1892, Bandelier shifted his focus to South America, conducting historical, archaeological, and ethnological investigations in Peru, Bolivia, and Ecuador. During his career, he worked for the American Museum of Natural History in New York, Henry Villard of New York, and was involved with the Hemenway Archaeological Expedition. Bandelier is noted for using his research to demonstrate the falsity of various historical myths, particularly regarding the Inca civilization of Peru.

## Other Works by Bandelier
In addition to *The Gilded Man*, Bandelier produced several other scholarly publications. In 1885, he wrote "The Romantic School of American Archaeologists" for the New York Historical Society. His contributions to the *Papers* of the Archaeological Institute of America (American Series, vols. i.-v.) include:

*   *Contributions to the History of the South-western Portion of the United States carried on mainly in the years from 1880 to 1885* (1890).
*   *Final Report of Investigations among the Indians of the South-western United States* (1890-1892, 2 vols.).
*   *Report of an Archaeological Tour in Mexico in 1881* (1884).
*   *Historical Introduction to Studies among the Sedentary Indians of New Mexico, and Report on the Ruins of the Pueblo of Pecos* (1881).

He also authored three studies for the Harvard University, Peabody Museum of American Archaeology and Ethnology *Annual Reports* (1877, 1878, 1879) focusing on the ancient Mexicans: one on the mode and art of war, one on the customs of inheritance and tenure and distribution of lands, and one on the mode of government and social organization.
