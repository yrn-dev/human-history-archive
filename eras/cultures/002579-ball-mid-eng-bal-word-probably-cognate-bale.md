# BALL

A ball is defined as any rounded body, specifically those possessing a smooth surface. The term is used to describe objects utilized for games or as missiles, as well as anatomical features such as the protuberance at the big toe or the root of the thumb. In anatomy, the term also applies to an enarthrosis, known as a "ball socket" joint, such as those found in the shoulder or hip.

## Etymology and Related Terms
In Middle English, the word appeared as *bal*. It is likely cognate with "bale" and is Teutonic in origin. Related terms include the Greek *palla* and the Latin *follis*. 

The adjective "bald" (Middle English *balled*) is commonly explained as meaning "round and smooth like a ball," though it may alternatively be connected to a stem *bal* meaning shining or white, a derivation suggested by the Greek *phalakros*.

## Historical Use in Games
Because rolling objects appeal to human infants as well as puppies and kittens, balls have likely been an essential feature of physical games since the earliest times. Evidence of ball games is found in the following historical contexts:

*   **Ancient Egypt:** Portrayals of ball games exist on Egyptian monuments.
*   **Primitive Societies:** Ball games are played among the least advanced savage tribes in the present day.
*   **Ancient Greece:** In the works of Homer, Nausicaa is depicted playing at ball with her maidens in the land of the Phaeacians. Additionally, Laodamas and Halios performed ball play accompanied by dancing before Odysseus and Alcinous. The Greeks referred to these games as *sphairai* and viewed them as useful subsidiaries to more violent athletic exercises, as they helped render the body graceful and supple; however, these games were generally reserved for girls and boys.
*   **Ancient Rome:** Ball games were considered an adjunct to the bath and were tailored to the health and age of the bathers. Specific areas called *sphaeristeria* were set aside for these activities within the *thermae* (baths).

## Greek Ball Games
While few regular rules for ball games remain, the *Onomastikon* of Pollux of Naucratis lists several Greek forms of play:
*   ***Aporraxis***: The act of putting the ball on the ground using an open hand.
*   ***Ourania***: A game where the ball is flung into the air to be caught by two or more players.
*   ***Phaininda***: A game of catch involving two or more players that utilizes feinting to test the skill and quickness of the participants.

## Maritime Signaling
Balls are used as visual signals for the marking of wrecks. When a wreck-marking vessel is employed in the open sea or near a harbor/estuary, it exhibits three balls on a yard 20 feet above the sea during the day. Two balls are placed vertically at one end and one ball at the other; the single ball is positioned on the side closest to the wreck. 

In rivers, harbors, or narrow waters under local jurisdiction, different rules may apply. A wreck-marking vessel may carry a cross-yard on a mast with two balls placed horizontally between 6 and 12 feet apart. If an open boat or barge is used instead of a vessel, a ball or flag may be shown during the daytime.
