# Probability of Being Hit in Backgammon

In the game of backgammon, playing effectively requires a comprehensive understanding of the chances associated with the throw of two dice and the ability to apply this knowledge to gameplay. There are thirty-six different possible throws that can be made with two dice. By analyzing the combinations of these throws that include specific numbers, a player can determine the positions where "blots" (single pieces) may be left with the lowest probability of being hit by an adversary.

## Calculating the Odds for an Ace
To determine the probability of being hit when a blot can only be captured by an ace, one must identify all possible throws containing an ace. An adversary can achieve this by throwing two aces or an ace in combination with any other number from two to six. Because each of these combinations (excluding the double aces) can be thrown in two different ways, there are eleven total ways in which an ace may be thrown.

When these eleven successful outcomes are deducted from the total of thirty-six possible throws, twenty-five remaining outcomes result in the blot not being hit. Consequently, the odds against being hit on an ace are 25 to 11, which is approximately 9 to 4.

## General Probability Trends
The probability of a blot being hit varies based on its position relative to the adversary's pieces:

*   **Single Die Reach:** If a blot is left within the reach of one die, the probability of it being hit decreases the nearer it is left to the adversary's man.
*   **Double Dice Reach:** There are long odds against being hit on a blot that can only be reached with double dice. For blots positioned at any number from 7 to 11, the chance of being hit decreases as the blot is placed farther away.

These probabilities assume the board is open for every possible throw. If an intervening point is held by adverse men, blocking part of the throw, the chance of being hit is reduced.

## Strategic Principles for Moving Men
Based on these probabilities, two primary principles guide the movement of men in backgammon:
1.  **Obstructing the Opponent:** Players should aim to make points where there is the best chance of obstructing the opponent's progress.
2.  **Positioning Blots:** When a player is forced to leave blots, they should choose the position where the blot is least likely to be hit.

## Key Points and Opening Moves
Certain points on the board are more valuable to secure than others. The most desirable points to secure are the five-point in the player's own inner table and the five-point in the adversary's inner table. Following these, the next best positions are the player's own bar-point and the four-point in their own inner table.

For certain throws at the start of a game, the following moves are recommended:
*   **Aces:** Considered the best of all throws; the player should move two on the five-point and two on the bar-point. This throw is frequently granted to inferior players as a way of providing odds.
*   **Ace and Trey:** Used to make the five-point in the player's inner table.
*   **Ace and Six:** Used to make the player's bar-point.
*   **Deuces:** The player should move two on the trey-point in the opponent's inner table and two on the four-point in their own inner table.
*   **Deuce and Four:** Used to make the four-point in the player's own table.
