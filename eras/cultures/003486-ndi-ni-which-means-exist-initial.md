# Ndi or Ni

In the study of Bantu languages, the roots *ndi* or *ni* (and their variants *li* or *di*) are identified as verbal particles meaning "to be" or "exist." These roots serve as fundamental components in the construction of verbs and the formation of specific tenses across various Bantu dialects.

## Role in Verb Formation and Tense
The Bantu verb is generally composed of a practically unchangeable root, which functions as the second person singular of the imperative, with various particles prefixed or suffixed to it. These particles may be reduced prepositions, adverbs, or worn-down verbs that have transitioned into auxiliaries.

The root *li* or *di* (*ndi* or *ni*), meaning "to be" or "exist," is specifically connected to the formation of the preterite tense. This method of forming the preterite appears to be shared by a significant number of widely-spread Bantu languages. For example, in Zulu, the verb *tanda* (meaning "love") becomes *tandile* (meaning "have loved" or "did love"). The suffix *-ile* or *-ili* used in this construction is referable back to the root *li* or *di* (*ndi* or *ni*). 

The initial *i* in the particle *-ile* can cause rare changes in the verbal root by affecting the penultimate or last syllable. An illustration of this occurs in many Bantu dialects where the root *pa* (meaning "to give") becomes *pele* in the preterite, originating from *pa-ile*. Similarly, the Zulu form *tandile* is a contraction of *tanda-ile*.

## Pronouns and Linguistic Variations
The root *ndi* also appears in the context of Bantu pronouns. In the Western Bantu sphere, *ndi* is used as a form for the nominative and possessive for "he or she, him, her, his, &c." 

Additionally, there are phonetic relationships between various first-person pronouns and these roots. The form *mi* is possibly a softening of *ngi* or *ni*. In some dialects, *ngi* becomes *ni*, *ndi*, *nji*, or *mbi*. In the north-eastern Congo and some coast Cameroon languages, the word *mbi* or *mba* is used for "I" or "me," which appears to be borrowed from the Sudanian Mundu tongues.

## Classification and Root Application
The root *ndi* appears within the classification system of Bantu nouns. Specifically, Class 5 is identified as *Idi* (*Ndi-di-*).

While certain roots like *tanda* are common in specific languages such as Zulu, they may not exhibit all possible variations within a single language. In other languages where a verb might exhibit all these variations, the root itself may change; for instance, *tanda* might be replaced by *londa* or *randa*.
