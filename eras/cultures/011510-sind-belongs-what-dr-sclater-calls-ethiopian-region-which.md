# Sind and the Ethiopian Region

## Zoogeographical Classification
Sind is located in the extreme south-west part of the Asian continent. According to the zoological primary divisions of the earth established by Dr. P.L. Sclater, Sind belongs to the Ethiopian region. This region extends over Africa, south of the Atlas mountains, and reaches into Asia to comprise a separate zoological district that includes Arabia, Palestine, southern Persia, and Baluchistan.

Dr. Sclater's system of dividing the world into great regions was based primarily on the distribution of birds, specifically the mass of arboreal birds known at the time as the order Insessores or Passeres (which includes the orders Cuculiformes, Coraciiformes, and Passeriformes). Sclater grouped the Ethiopian, Indian, Palaearctic, and Australian regions together as the "Old World" (*Palaeogaea*).

## Environmental and Biological Characteristics
Sind is part of an almost unbroken desert of extremely dry and hot tracts that stretch from Arabia through southern Persia and Baluchistan. This area is characterized by a considerable uniformity in types of life that closely resemble those of the neighboring hot and dry regions of Africa.

The vegetation in this south-western region consists largely of plants diffused across Sind, Baluchistan, and Africa. Some of these plants extend into the hotter portions of India, and several common Egyptian plants are found within the Indian peninsula.

## Fauna of the Region
While the Ethiopian fauna plays a subordinate role in Asia, it intrudes into the south-western corner, occupying the desert districts of Syria and Arabia. Certain characteristic Ethiopian species extend further into Sind and Persia, and even into western India. Examples of these Ethiopian types include:
*   **Mammals:** The hunting leopard and the lion, as well as various species of other desert-loving forms and jerboas.
*   **Birds:** The Ethiopian type is identified by the prevalence of stone chats and larks, and is further marked by the complete absence of many peculiar genera that are characteristic of the Indian region.

In contrast, the adjacent Indian region is one of the richest and most varied in the world for birds, featuring peculiar genera such as *Ceriornis*, *Pajo*, *Euplocamus*, *Lophophorus*, *Harpactes*, and *Buceros*.

## Historical Context of Sind
Historically, Sind was ruled by the Brahman, Chach, during a period when the province of Gandava was Buddhist and a Buddhist priest governed Las Bela in the 7th century A.D. In 710, Mahommed Kasim conducted a victorious march to Sind, during which the town of Armabel (possibly represented by the site of Karia Pir) was destroyed.

The region has seen various occupations and influences. There are traces of ancient Arab, possibly Himyaritic, occupation at Gondakeha on the Kud river. The area also contains Buddhist caves excavated in conglomerate cliffs at Shahr-i-Rogan (or Gondrani). Following the influx of Arabs into Makran, the town of Armel (Armabel) became an important link in the trading chain between Sind and Persia. Medieval Arab geographers, such as Ibn Haukal, mapped various towns in the delta, including Kambali and Yusli.
