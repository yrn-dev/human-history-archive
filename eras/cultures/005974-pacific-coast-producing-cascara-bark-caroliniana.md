# Pacific Coast and Associated Flora

The term "Pacific coast" refers to various geographical regions bordering the Pacific Ocean across different continents, characterized by diverse climates, topographical features, and botanical resources.

## North American Pacific Coast
In the United States, the Pacific coast region is located to the west of the Rocky Mountains. From a horticultural perspective, this region possesses a climate that is somewhat similar to that of western Europe. This area is a significant center for the production of hops; between 1900 and 1906, 80% of the average annual United States hop produce (which totaled 423,471 cwt.) was raised in the three states of the Pacific coast. The yield per acre in these states is notably higher than in New York; while New York yields approximately 5 or 6 cwt. per acre, Oregon produces 9 or 10 cwt., and California and Washington produce between 12 and 14 cwt.

In British Columbia, the region is essentially mountainous. The Coast Range is situated on the south-west or Pacific side, while the Rocky Mountains are on the north-east. Between these two systems lies the "Interior Plateau," an elevated tract of hilly country. The province is characterized by parallel mountain ranges and long narrow valleys, with Vancouver Island and the Queen Charlotte Islands serving as remnants of a mountain range that is now almost entirely submerged beneath the Pacific waters.

## Botanical Species of the Pacific Coast
The Pacific coast is home to specific varieties of buckthorns. *Rhamnus purshiana*, also known as *Cascara sagrada*, is found on the Pacific coast and is utilized for producing cascara bark. Another American buckthorn is *R. Caroliniana*, which is identified as the alder-buckthorn.

## South American Pacific Coast
Along the Pacific coast of South America, specifically in Peru, the coast is separated from the Amazon valley by three high, snow-covered ranges of the Andes. Communication between the Amazon region and Pacific coast cities and ports requires crossing these ranges via extremely difficult passes and trails. One feasible route connects the Pacific port of Payta with Puerto Limon on the Marañon, crossing the Andes at a low elevation of 6,600 ft.

In Chile, the province of Coquimbo extends from the Pacific inland to the Argentine frontier. The surface near the coast is broken by well-watered river valleys used for producing alfalfa and pasturing cattle for export.

## Other Pacific Coastal Regions
In the Maritime Province of Siberia, the coast is influenced by the cold current from the Sea of Okhotsk, which brings chilling fogs and icy water to the Pacific shore and the coasts of Sakhalin. The climate is harsh; for example, at Vladivostok, the harbour remains frozen for nearly three months of the year.

From a biological perspective, the distribution of shore-fishes allows for the division of the Pacific into East Pacific and West Pacific areas (the latter including the coasts of the Indian Ocean). The fish-fauna of the Indo-Pacific is richer in species and genera than that of the Atlantic.
