# BONGO (Boocercus eurycerus)

The bongo (_Boocercus eurycerus_) is a species of West African bushbuck and is the largest member of its group.

## Physical Characteristics
The male bongo is characterized by a deep chestnut color. Its body is marked with narrow white stripes, and it possesses a white crescent on the chest. Additionally, the male has two white spots located on the face below the eye. 

In the East African bongo (_B. e. Isaaei_), the hue of the body is richer and stronger than that of the West African variety. Regarding the presence of horns, there is currently no evidence to confirm whether females of the true bongo possess them, although it is considered probable. In contrast, horns are present in both sexes of the East African form. Due to this characteristic, Mr. Oldfield Thomas has designated the East African form as the type of the genus.

## Classification and Related Species
The bongo is categorized as a bushbuck. Other members of the bushbuck group include a medium-sized red antelope found in South Africa (_Tragelaphus scriptus_), which is marked with spots and white lines and possesses spirally twisted horns in males only. 

There are several allied species known as harnessed antelopes that are larger in size than the standard bushbuck. One such example is the situtunga (_T. spekei_), which has been separated into the genus _Limnotragus_ because its hoofs are elongated for walking on swampy ground.
