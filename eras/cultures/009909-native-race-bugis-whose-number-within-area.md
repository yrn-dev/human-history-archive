# Bugis

The Bugis (also referred to as the Bughis) are a people of Malayan stock who originally occupied the kingdom of Boni, located in the south-western peninsula of the island of Celebes. From this original district, they expanded across the entirety of Celebes and established settlements throughout the Malay Archipelago.

## Physical and Behavioral Characteristics
The Bugis are described as being of robust build and middle size. Their complexion is slightly lighter than that of the average Malay. In terms of disposition, they are characterized as brave, fierce, and haughty. They are noted for an active and enterprising nature, and it is said that they are more predisposed toward "running amuck" than any other Malayans.

## Culture and Society
The Bugis are an industrious and ingenious people. They maintain their own schools and build well-constructed towns. Their language is allied to that of the Macassars and is written using similar characters; this language was studied and its letters reproduced in type by Dr. B.F. Mathes of the Netherlands Bible Society.

In terms of religion, the Bugis have been Mahommedans since the 17th century. Prior to this, their original form of nature-worship was heavily influenced by Hinduism, and they continue to retain rites associated with the worship of Siva.

## Economy and Trade
The Bugis practice agriculture more extensively than neighboring tribes. They manufacture cotton-cloth, which is produced for both export and domestic use. Due to their love of the sea, the Bugis have achieved an almost complete monopoly of trade around Celebes. They engage in significant trade involving vegetable and mineral products from Boni, including camphor, nutmegs, pearls, tortoise-shell, and gold-dust.

## Governance and the State of Boni
Boni is a vassal state of the government of Celebes in the Dutch East Indies, situated on the Gulf of Boni in the south-west peninsula of Celebes. The state covers an area of 2,600 sq. m. and produces sugar-cane, cotton, coffee, tobacco, and rice, though none of these are important as exports. The state is highly esteemed for its breeds of horses and buffaloes. The chief town, Boni, is located 2-1/2 m. from the peninsula's east coast and 80 m. north-east of Macassar.

The Bugis population within the area of Boni is approximately 70,000. The political structure of Boni is a confederation composed of eight petty states. The king is elected from among the Bugis by the chiefs of these eight states and is generally elected for life. The king is restricted in his authority and cannot decide on any public measure without the consent of these chiefs. Within the individual states, the office of chief is hereditary in some instances; in others, any member of the privileged classes may aspire to the position. Consequently, it is not uncommon for a woman to govern the state.
