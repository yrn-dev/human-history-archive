# BOLL

## Botanical Definition
In botany, a boll is a term used to describe a fruit-pod, with particular application to the fruit-pod of the cotton plant. 

## Etymology and Linguistic Variants
The term "boll" originates from the Old English word *bolla*. This linguistic root is also represented in the word "bowl," referring to a round vessel used for liquids. The variant "bowl" is derived from "ball," which in turn comes from the French word *boule*.

## Non-Botanical Usage
Outside of botanical terminology, "boll" is utilized as a unit of measurement, primarily in the north of England and in Scotland. In these regions, it serves as a measure of both weight and capacity:
*   **Weight:** When used to measure flour, one boll is equal to 140 lb.
*   **Capacity:** When used to measure grain, one boll is equal to 16 pecks.

## General Botanical Context of Fruits and Pods
A boll is a specific type of fruit-pod. In a general botanical sense, a fruit is developed from a flower following the fertilization of the ovule. During this process, the ovary enlarges to form the pericarp, and the ovules develop into seeds containing the embryo-plant. While the term "fruit" strictly refers to the mature ovary and its interior seeds, it can often include other floral parts such as floral envelopes and bracts.

Certain fruits are categorized as legumes or pods. A legume is defined as a many-seeded, unilocular, dry monocarpellary fruit formed from a single carpel. These typically dehisce (split open) via the dorsal and ventral sutures, a characteristic found in plants such as the pea and bean. However, some plants within the Leguminosae order produce pods that do not dehisce, such as the tamarind or *Arachis* (the ground nut).
