# Munimenta Academica

*Munimenta Academica* is a work written by Henry Anstey. The publication of this text was carried out under the direction of the master of the rolls.

## University Booksellers and Stationers
Through *Munimenta Academica*, details are provided regarding the role of the "sworn" university bookseller or stationer in Oxford. An example of such a figure was John More of Oxford, who functioned as a stationer and bookseller. More's activities involved supplying pupils with their necessary books; however, he also apparently acted as a pawnbroker.

The necessity for these services was driven by the financial situation of the students. According to Anstey, most students could not afford to purchase books. Furthermore, the advantage of owning books was not as significant as might be assumed, because the instruction provided to students was almost entirely oral.

## The Monopoly of Sworn Stationers
The primary method for obtaining books was through purchase from the university sworn stationers. These stationers held a monopoly to a great extent, and a very large number of books were constantly changing hands through them.

Despite this monopoly, there were many booksellers in Oxford who were not sworn. The presence of these unauthorized sellers led to the passage of a statute in 1373. This statute recited that because of the non-sworn booksellers, the owners of books were being cheated, and books of great value were being sold and carried away from Oxford. Additionally, the sworn stationers were deprived of their lawful business. To combat this, it was enacted that no bookseller—with the exception of two sworn stationers or their deputies—could sell any book exceeding half a mark in value, whether the book was their own property or that of another, under penalty.
