# AXLE

## Definition and Mechanical Function
An axle is defined as the pin or spindle upon which a wheel turns. In the context of carriages, the term "axle-tree" refers to the bar on which the wheels are mounted. Strictly speaking, the axles are the thinner, rounded prolongations of the axle-tree on which the wheels actually rotate. To prevent wheels from slipping off, pins are passed through the ends of the axles; these are referred to as axle-pins or "linch-pins." The term "linch" is a corruption of the Old English word for axle, *lynis* (comparable to the German *Luense*), resulting from confusion with the word "link."

## Etymology and Linguistic Cognates
The English word "axle" derives from the Middle English *axel-tre*, which originates from the Old Norwegian *oxull-tre*. It is cognate with the Old English *aexe* or *eaxe*. Furthermore, the term is connected to several other languages, including the Sanskrit *aksha*, the Greek *axon*, and the Latin *axis*.

## Related Concept: Axis
The Latin word *axis* translates to "axle" and shares the same primary meaning. However, the term "axis" is used with various extensions beyond the mechanical pin. It can denote:
* **Physics and Systems:** An imaginary line about which a system of bodies or a single body rotates, or a line that serves as the center for a symmetrically disposed action or body.
* **Geometry and Crystallography:** A line used to assist in the orientation of a figure.
* **Anatomy:** A term applied, among other uses, to the second cervical vertebra.
* **Botany:** A term meaning the stem.
