# Balearic Islands

The Balearic Islands (Spanish: _Baleares_) are an archipelago located in the Mediterranean Sea off the east coast of Spain. The archipelago forms a province of Spain and is situated between 1° and 5° E. and between 38° 40' and 40° 5' N. As of 1900, the total area of the islands was 1,935 square miles with a population of 311,649.

## Geography and Composition
The archipelago consists of four large islands and eleven small islands, which are divided into two distinct groups:

**The Eastern Group**
This larger group corresponds to the ancient _Insulae Baleares_. It includes the two principal islands of Majorca (Spanish: _Mallorca_) and Minorca (Spanish: _Menorca_). Additionally, this group contains seven islets: Botafoch, Aire, Aucanada, Cabrera, Dragonera, Pinto, and El Rey.

**The Western Group**
This group corresponds to the ancient Pityusae, also known as the Pine Islands. It comprises two relatively large islands, Iviza (Spanish: _Ibiza_ or formerly _Ivica_) and Formentera. The western group also includes the islets of Espalmador, Pou, Conejera, and Ahorcados.

Of the eleven islets in the archipelago, all were inhabited as of 1900, though their combined population was only 171. Most of these islets are of little importance, with the exception of Cabrera. Cabrera is characterized by its numerous caverns and was previously utilized as a place of banishment. In 1808, Spanish captors landed a large body of Frenchmen on Cabrera, where they were left to almost perish from starvation.

## Etymology and Historical Names
The origin of the name _Baleares_ is a matter of conjecture. However, the names of the two largest islands, Majorca and Minorca, are derived from the Latin words _Major_ and _Minor_ via the Byzantine Greek forms _Maiorika_ and _Minorika_.

The western islands also have ancient designations. Iviza is the older Ebusus, a name likely of Carthaginian origin; it was also called Ebusus by the Greeks and Romans. Iviza is described as being hilly, with high tracts well covered in fir and pine. Formentera was known to the Greeks as Ophiusa and to the Romans as Colubraria.

## Administration
The administration of the Balearic Islands is identical to that of other Spanish provinces located on the mainland. The province is divided into five judicial districts, known as _partidos judiciales_. These districts are named after their primary towns: Palma, Iviza, Manacor, Port Mahon, and Inca.
