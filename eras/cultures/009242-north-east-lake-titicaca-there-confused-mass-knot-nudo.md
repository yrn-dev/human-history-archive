# Nudo de Apolobamba and the Bolivian Alta-planicie

The Nudo de Apolobamba is a confused mass or "knot" of lofty intersecting ridges located north-east of Lake Titicaca. This geological feature includes some of the highest peaks in South America and forms part of the broader Andean system.

## Physiography of the Andean Cordilleras
The Andean cordilleras cross the south-west corner of Bolivia and project east toward the Brazilian highlands, forming a great obtuse angle. Roughly two-fifths of Bolivia's total area is comprised within these cordilleras. The system is divided into two great parallel chains with flanking spurs and ranges to the east, creating a massif composed of parallel ranges that enclose deep ravines and great elevated plateaus.

Below the mountainous area of the Nudo de Apolobamba, the ranges open out to enclose extensive plateaus. The western range, known as the Cordillera Occidental, serves as part of the boundary between Bolivia and the northern provinces of Chile. This range follows the coast outline and acts as the western rampart for the great Bolivian tableland, or *alta-planicie*.

## The Bolivian Alta-planicie
The *alta-planicie* is a great lacustrine basin that once possessed an inland sea with an eastern outlet through the La Paz gorge. It extends from the Vilcanota knot in Peru in the north to the Serrania de Lipez on the Argentine frontier in the south. The plateau measures approximately 500 miles in length and 80 miles in breadth, covering about 40,000 square miles.

The plateau's average elevation is approximately 12,650 feet near Lake Titicaca, descending by about 1,000 feet toward its southern extremity. The region is divided into two primary areas:
* **The Puna:** The northern part of the plateau.
* **The Desert of Lipez:** The southern part, which is characterized as being part of the great Puna de Atacama.

The environment of the plateau is described as bleak and inhospitable in the north and barren and arid toward the south. It contains great saline depressions that fill with water during the rainy season and is broken by various peaks and ridges. The highest of these is the Cerro de Tahua, reaching 17,454 feet.

## Surrounding Peaks and Lakes
The *alta-planicie* is overlooked from the west by several snow-clad peaks, including:
* Huallatiri (21,654 ft.)
* Sajama (21,047 ft.)
* Parinacota (20,918 ft.)
* Pomarape (20,505 ft.)
* Licancaur (19,685 ft.)
* San Pedro y Pablo (19,423 ft.)
* Oyahua (19,226 ft.)
* Lirima (19,128 ft.)

The two principal lakes of this plateau region are Lake Pampa Aullaguas (also known as Poopo) and Lake Titicaca. Lake Titicaca is situated near the north end of the *alta-planicie* at an elevation of 12,644 feet above sea level. It is one of the world's most elevated lakes, with a maximum length of 138 miles and a maximum breadth of 69 miles. Its water surface is 1,969 square miles (excluding peninsulas and islands), and its greatest depth is 892 feet. The lake is shared by Peru and Bolivia and is navigated by steamers traveling between Bolivian ports and the Peruvian railway port of Puno.

## Hydrography and Drainage
The *alta-planicie* is one of the most elevated lacustrine basins in the world. While it once drained eastward, it currently has no surface outlet. The drainage system operates as follows:
1. Lake Titicaca receives water from short streams from neighboring heights and discharges it through the Desaguadero, a sluggish river.
2. The Desaguadero flows south for 184 miles, becoming gradually shallower until it reaches Lake Pampa Aullaguas.
3. Lake Pampa Aullaguas has a small outlet called the Lacahahuira, which flows west for 60 miles into the Cienegas de Coipasa (salt-swamps).

The drainage of this extensive district is primarily absorbed by evaporation and the dry soil of the desert. In the extreme south, the Rio Grande de Lipez is absorbed in a similar manner.

## Regional Inhabitants and Fauna
The country around Lake Titicaca and the neighboring Andean valleys was formerly inhabited by the Aymara (ancient Colla) tribe. The Aymaras possessed a considerable culture before being conquered by the Incas in the 13th and 14th centuries, as evidenced by the megalithic ruins of Tiahuanaco. For the Incas, the region around Lake Titicaca was considered a "holy land," and national legends attributed the origin of all Quichua (Inca) civilization to this area. Today, the Aymara form the chief ethnical element in Bolivia, numbering approximately half a million people including half-breeds.

Regarding the local fauna, the region is home to specific bird species. Notably, the flightless *Centropelma* is found on Lake Titicaca; this bird is categorized as an immigrant from North America.
