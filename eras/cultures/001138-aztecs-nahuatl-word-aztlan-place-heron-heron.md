# AZTECS

## Etymology and Identification
The term "Aztecs" is derived from the Nahuatl word *aztlan*, which translates to "place of the Heron." Consequently, the group is also referred to as the "Heron people." In historical and national contexts, the name has frequently been used as an equivalent to the collective national titles of Mexicans or Nahuatlecas.

## Origins and Migration
According to native tradition, the Aztecs originated from a country they named Aztlan. While this ancestral homeland is generally believed to have been located toward the north-west, the precise localization of Aztlan remains one of the most significant difficulties in the study of Mexican history. 

The timing of the exodus from Aztlan is similarly undetermined. Various authorities place the departure in either the 11th or 12th century. One specific Mexican manuscript provides a date equivalent to A.D. 1164.

## Expansion and Empire
Upon arriving in the tableland of Mexico, the Aztecs were one of the tribes occupying the region at the time of the arrival of the Spaniards in America. Over time, they increased their influence among the other local tribes. Through a union with the Toltecs, who had occupied the tableland prior to the Aztecs, they expanded their empire. This empire eventually covered an area ranging from 18,000 to 20,000 square leagues.
