# Climate

The study of climate across various global regions reveals a diverse range of temperatures, rainfall patterns, and health impacts, often dictated by altitude, proximity to oceans, and geographical configuration.

## Tropical and Subtropical Regions

In British East Africa, climate and vegetation are arranged in zones parallel to the coast. The coast region is hot and generally healthier than other tropical coast lands due to dry soil and constant breezes from the Indian Ocean, with annual rainfall of approximately 35 inches. Inland, the highlands (districts over 5,000 ft.) are very healthy, with fever being almost unknown; temperatures average 66° F. in the cool season and 73° F. in the hot season. Above 7,000 ft., the climate becomes colder and frosts occur. Conversely, the region bordering Victoria Nyanza is typically tropical with annual rainfall exceeding 60 inches, making it quite unsuitable for Europeans. The protectorate's hottest period is December to April, and the coolest is July to September. Rainfall occurs in "greater rains" (March to June) and "smaller rains" (November and December), though severe droughts occasionally occur.

In Costa Rica, the proximity of two oceans and varied surface configuration create striking climatic extremes. Three zones exist: tropical, temperate, and cold, which generally succeed one another as altitude increases. The tropical zone (coast and foothills) has a mean annual temperature between 72° F. and 82° F. The temperate zone, specifically the San Jose plateau at 3,000–5,000 ft., averages 68° F. Above 7,500 ft., frosts are frequent, though snow is rare. The wet season on the Pacific slope lasts from April to December, interrupted in June by a fortnight of dry weather called the *Veranillo de San Juan*. The Atlantic coast receives rain in any month via trade-winds. Normal rainfall is about 80 inches, but cloud-bursts can increase this to 150 inches or more.

In Ashanti, the climate is less unhealthy than that of West African coast towns, though it is unsuited for the prolonged residence of Europeans. The mean temperature at Kumasi is 76° F. with a mean annual rainfall of 40 inches. The rainy season runs from the end of May until October, characterized by frequent and violent storms.

In northern India, the climate generally allows the population to secure the necessities of life without severe labour. However, extremes of drought and heat render the land unsuitable for pasture. In Ceylon, the lower levels possess a climate similar to regions of the same latitude on the Malay peninsula.

## European Climates

Bulgaria's climate is severe compared to other European regions of the same latitude due to its mountain ranges and the configuration of the Balkan Peninsula. It experiences extreme heat in summer and cold in winter. In districts between the Balkans and the Danube, winter cold is intense, with temperatures falling to 24° below zero and the river frequently freezing. The high tableland of Sofia is generally covered in snow during winter, with a maximum temperature of 86° and a minimum of 2°. The eastern districts are moderated by the sea, though the Black Sea is known for storms and the sea at Varna occasionally freezes. The sheltered plain of Eastern Rumelia is comparatively warm, with spring beginning six weeks earlier than in other parts of the country. The mean temperature for Bulgaria is 52°, and annual rainfall ranges from 25 to 59 inches.

In Spain, the climate of Old Castile is healthy but subject to severe heat and cold; snow falls early and stays late in the mountains, with heavy rainfall in the north-west. New Castile is more rigorous, with a mean annual temperature of about 59° F., though summer heat in the valleys is oppressive and highlands experience icy or scorching gales. Rainfall in New Castile rarely exceeds 10 inches per year.

In other regions, such as those described in the archive's records of the British Isles, some areas are characterized as very moist due to the height of mountains and proximity to the sea, which makes them unsuitable for cereal growth but mild enough in winter for indigenous warm-climate trees like the arbutus to grow. Another recorded county exhibits a wet and cold climate with an average annual temperature of 48° F. and an average annual rainfall of 35.5 inches.

## Health and Environmental Impacts

Climate significantly influences regional health. In Bulgaria, the climate is generally healthy, particularly in the mountains, though malarial fever prevails in the valley of the Maritza, the low-lying Black Sea coast, and the upland plain of Sofia due to poor drainage. In Costa Rica, malaria is common on both coasts, and rheumatism is prevalent on the Atlantic seaboard. In Persia, the town of Bander Abbas is noted for having bad water and an insalubrious climate.

From a biological perspective, changes in climate can drive the "Modification of Species." A gradual shift toward warmth that sweeps away snow can make white fur a handicap and brown coloring an advantage, leading to the weeding out of white creatures in favor of brown ones over generations. If such a change occurs too quickly, it may exterminate a species; if gradual, the species may adapt.
