# AZORES

The Azores, also known as the Western Islands, are an archipelago located in the Atlantic Ocean and belong to the kingdom of Portugal. The islands are positioned between 36° 55' and 39° 55' N. latitude and between 25° and 31° 16' W. longitude, extending in an oblique line from the northwest to the southeast. They rise from depths exceeding 2-1/2 miles.

## Geography and Climate
The archipelago is divided into three widely separated groups:
*   **South-eastern group:** Comprises St. Michael's (Sao Miguel), St. Mary (Santa Maria), and Formigas.
*   **Central group:** Comprises Fayal (Faial), Pico, St. George (Sao Jorge), Terceira, and Graciosa.
*   **North-western group:** Comprises Flores and Corvo.

The Azores are the most distant of all Atlantic island groups from any continent. The nearest continental land is Cape da Roca on the Portuguese coast, located 830 miles east of St. Michael's. Cape Cantin on the African mainland is more than 900 miles away, and Cape Race in Newfoundland is more than 1,000 miles distant. Despite this isolation, the islands are generally regarded as part of Europe due to their European flora and climate. The total area of the archipelago is 922 square miles, and as of 1900, the population was 256,291.

## History
There is no evidence that the Romans or ancient Greeks knew of the Azores. However, the discovery of Carthaginian coins on Corvo suggests the islands may have been visited by Carthaginians. In the 12th and 14th centuries, Arabian geographers Edrisi and Ibn-al-Wardi described nine islands in the Western Ocean north of the Canaries. These writers claimed the islands were once populous and contained cities of some magnitude, though the population had been reduced by intestine warfare.

The islands appear distinctly on a map from 1351, where the groups were named the Goat Islands (*Cabreras*) in the south, the Wind or Dove Islands (*De Ventura sive de Columbis*) in the middle, and the Brazil Island (*De Brazi*) in the west—"Brazil" then referring to a red dye-stuff. A 1375 Catalan map identifies St. George as *San Zorze*, Flores as *Li Conigi*, and Corvo as *Corvi Marini*.

While some conjecture the discoverers were Genoese, evidence is insufficient. Regarding the "Flemish discovery," it is noted that van der Berg was driven to the islands in 1432, which sparked interest in Lisbon. Subsequently, the navigator Gonzalo Velho Cabral was sent to prosecute the discovery. An alternative account suggests Prince Henry the Navigator sent Cabral based on the accuracy of a map in his possession.

## Natural History
The Azores are classified within the Mediterranean province of fauna and flora. The avifauna is the result of colonization occurring over a long period. Insular birds in the Azores tend to differ from their continental counterparts, typically possessing stronger bills and legs and darker plumage. 

A notable example is the bullfinch of the western islands (*Pyrrhula murina*), which has a sober mouse-colored breast instead of the ruddy breast found in *P. vulgaris*. Additionally, the chaffinch *F. tintillon* is found in the Azores, Madeira, and the Canaries, and is closely allied to the Mauritanian *F. spodogenia*. The name "Azores" is derived from the Portuguese *Acor*, meaning a hawk, referring to the buzzards or hawks that were numerous on the islands.

## Economy and Infrastructure
The port of Ponta Delgado is described as one of the most frequented and important coaling stations. Horta, located on the south-east coast of Fayal, serves as the capital of an administrative district that includes Corvo, Flores, Pico, and Fayal. As of 1900, Horta had a population of 6,574. 

Horta is a seaport featuring a harbor 2 miles long and nearly 1 mile broad, providing anchorage in 5 to 20 fathoms of water, though it is dangerous during south-easterly and south-westerly winds. The town is defended by a wall and two castles, though these are now obsolete. Horta is a center for the transatlantic cable system and serves as the headquarters for fisheries targeting mullet, bonito, tunny, and whale. Its primary exports include grain, wine, fruit, and sperm-oil. Between 1904 and 1897, the port accommodated approximately 140 vessels annually, totaling 220,000 tons, primarily of Portuguese or British nationality.
