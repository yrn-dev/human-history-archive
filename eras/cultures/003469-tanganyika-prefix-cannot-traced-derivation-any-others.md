# Tanganyika

Tanganyika is a region in Central Africa characterized by a diverse array of Bantu languages and a history of human habitation involving various Negro types. Its geography is defined by the Tanganyika basin and the surrounding plateau, which serve as critical points of linguistic and racial intersection.

## Linguistic Classifications
The languages of the Tanganyika region are part of the Bantu family, a group of tongues defined by the governing of unchangeable roots by prefixes. Within this family, the Tanganyika languages (Group 12) include dialects such as *Ki-rega*, *Kabwari*, and *Kiguha*. These are primarily spoken in regions to the north, east, and west-north-west of Tanganyika, extending from the Lukuga outlet in the south to the vicinity of Lake Albert Edward in the north. To the west, this group is bounded by the Manyema genus and the Congo Forest.

On the east coast of Tanganyika, languages such as *Ki-rundi* and *Kigeye* are spoken. While these are often included in the Tanganyika group for convenience, they appear more closely connected to the Uganda-Unyoro group (Group 1). Specifically, *Ki-rundi*—spoken in the Burundi country at the north end of Tanganyika—and other eastern languages down to Ufipa are closely allied with the Unyoro sub-section.

Other linguistic groups in the vicinity include:
* **The Manyema (Baenya) Group:** Located between the main stream of the Luapula-Congo and the western watershed of Tanganyika.
* **The Bemba or Emba Dialects:** Occupying the ground between the south coasts and south-west of Tanganyika, Lake Mweru, and the Upper Chambezi river. This includes locally modified dialects such as *Ki-lungu* and *Ki-mambwe* found in South and South-East Tanganyika.
* **The North Nyasa or Nkonde Group:** This includes *Ishi-nyi[chi]a* of the Nyasa-Tanganyika plateau.

## Archaic Bantu Dialects
The regions surrounding Tanganyika are noted for preserving some of the most archaic forms of Bantu speech. The north coast of Tanganyika, as well as the Bemba country to its south-west, are cited as areas where the most archaic dialects are spoken today. In the plateau country between the south end of Lake Tanganyika and the north end of Lake Nyasa, the Bantu dialects are of an archaic type. One such dialect, *Kibemba* (or *Kiwemba*), is considered by some to be very close to the original form of the Bantu mother-language.

## Human Population and Prehistory
The indigenous population of the Tanganyika region is of the Negro type. While the current population belongs linguistically to the Bantu family, there is evidence of earlier inhabitants. Indications of the former presence of people akin to the Bushman-Hottentot type of negro have been found as far north as the Tanganyika basin. 

Archaeological evidence supports this; rounded stones with a hole through the center—similar to those used by Bushmen in the south for weighting digging-sticks—have been discovered at the south end of Lake Tanganyika. Some Bantu races in the area exhibit signs of Bushman or Hottentot intermixture.

## Regional Connections and Migrations
The Tanganyika region has served as a corridor for migration and linguistic influence. The languages of North Eastern Rhodesia and northern Nyasaland are connected to the Bantu languages of Uganda through dialects spoken to the north and west of Tanganyika. 

Furthermore, there is evidence of a relationship between the Zulu-Kaffir race and the regions north-east of Tanganyika. It appears that the Zulu-Kaffir race may have migrated directly from these countries, crossing the Zambezi to reach their home in the south.
