# Naushirwanis of the Kharan Desert and Mashkel

The Naushirwanis are a people of purely Persian race residing in the Mashkel and Kharan deserts of Baluchistan. They are described as a fine, manly race who, in various respects, are superior to their modern compatriots in Iran. They passed into Baluchistan within historic times, though the precise date of their arrival remains uncertain. The Naushirwanis appear to be identical to the Tahukani (or Tahuki) found in Perso-Baluchistan.

## Geography and Environment
The Naushirwanis inhabit the southern regions of the central desert, specifically the Kharan and Mashkel deserts. This central desert area is known in Arabic medieval geography as the Katz, Kir, Kej, or Kash Kaian, and was part of the ancient Kaiani kingdom. In this terminology, the prefixes "Kach" or "Kej" are used to denote valleys or low-level flats, distinguishing them from hills or mountains.

The hydrographic system of this region is characterized by intermittent river channels. To the west of the fortress of Kalat lies the Kharan desert, while the drainage of Southern Baluchistan generally trends south and west toward the Arabian Sea or into central swamps, such as those found in Mashkel and Lora.

## Ethnic Context and Neighboring Populations
The Naushirwanis are distinct in origin from other groups in the region. To their north, the central mountain region is occupied by the Mohamadani nomads, who are likely of Arab origin and are the most numerous of the "Rekis" (desert people). 

Between the Naushirwanis of the Mashkel and Kharan deserts and the fish-eating population of the coast, there are numerous tribes located around the sources of the Hingol or enclosed in the narrow valleys of the Kej and Rakshan tributaries. These groups are described as remnants of races that can be traced through records of recent immigration or recognized in the works of Herodotus.

Additionally, an underlying Persian (Tajik) population is scattered throughout the country. This population is often represented by agricultural bondsmen or slaves, such as the Nakibs, which include the Durzadas (referred to by Herodotus as *Derusiaei*) and the Dehkans or Dehwars.

## Political History and Administration
The Naushirwanis have been involved in the complex political landscape of Baluchistan. A notable figure in their history was Azad Khan, a great Naushirwani chief. In 1883, a reconciliation was reached between Azad Khan and the khan of Kalat through an expedition to Kharan led by Sandeman. This event contributed to the final consolidation of the Kalat administration.

Following these developments, British Baluchistan was incorporated into British India via a resolution on November 1, 1887. The region was subsequently divided into two districts: Thai Chotiali and Quetta-Pishin.
