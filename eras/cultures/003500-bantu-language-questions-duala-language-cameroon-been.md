# Bantu Languages of Cameroon

The Bantu languages of Cameroon form part of a larger Western group of Bantu speech, which encompasses the languages of the Northern and Central Congo as well as those of Cameroon. This linguistic family is characterized by a striking homogeneity and a distinctive structural feature: the governing of an unchangeable root by prefixes.

## The Duala Group and Related Dialects
The Duala group consists of languages spoken within the delta and estuary of the Cameroon river. This group is noted for being of a "much purer Bantu type." In contrast, the forms of speech known as Fan or Pangwe—which occupy the hinterland of the Duala Cameroon, as well as the interior of French Gabun and German Cameroon—are described as being so corrupt that they are only just recognizable as Bantu. These Fan forms of speech extend as far north and east as the Mbam, Sanaga, and Sanga rivers.

Other Bantu dialects in the region include:
* **The Isubu-Bakwiri group:** Located on the west slopes of the Cameroon Mountains and the coast-lands north of the Cameroon delta (Ambas Bay).
* **The Barondo-Bakundu group:** This group begins in the north at the Rio del Rey near the Cross river estuary. It may include the Bas[=a], Barombi, Abo, Nkosi, and Bonken dialects, which are spoken on the Cameroon river (Magombe), the Nyong and Sanaga rivers, and the eastern slopes of the Cameroon mountains. These are described as "much-debased dialects."
* **Semi-Bantu languages:** To the north-east of the Barondo-Bakundu group are languages such as Bafut, Bayon, Mbudikum, Nki, Bati, and Mbe. These are classified as "semi-Bantu" and show affinities with the Fan forms of speech and languages in the basin of the Central Benue river.
* **Fernando Po dialects:** These include the Bani, Bateti, and Ediya dialects.

## Linguistic Characteristics and Vocabulary
The term "Bantu" (literally *Ba-ntu*) is an archaic term for "people," "mankind," or "men." The root *-ntu* originally meant "person" or "object" and can function as a relative pronoun. In purer Bantu languages, the meaning of the root changes based on the prefix: *muntu* means "a man," *bantu* means "men," *kintu* means "a thing," *bintu* means "things," and *kantu* means "a little thing."

A comparative example of the homogeneity of the Bantu family is found in the word for "fowl." In some Cameroon languages, the word is *ngok*, *ngoko*, or *lokoko*. This exists alongside the form *kuba*, which is also found in the Gold Coast and Lower Niger regions.

In some coast Cameroon languages, the word for "I" or "me" is *mbi* or *mba*. This specific form appears to be borrowed from the Sudanian Mundu tongues.

## Cultural and Communication Systems
Among the Bantu nations of German Cameroon, such as the Ba-Kwiri and their neighbors, the Duala, there exists a "curious drum language." This system allows news to be conveyed from clan to clan via drum-tapping. The use of this language is restricted; women and slaves are not permitted to master it, while initiated members are required to repeat the messages to pass them along. Additionally, the Ba-Kwiri possess a horn language unique to their people.
