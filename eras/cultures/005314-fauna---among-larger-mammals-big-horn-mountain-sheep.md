# Fauna

The study of fauna across various regions and geological epochs reveals a diverse array of mammals, birds, and reptiles, ranging from the specialized species of high-altitude steppes to the prehistoric creatures of the Eocene.

## Regional Mammals and Wildlife

### North American and Gulf Regions
In certain provinces, larger mammals include the Rocky Mountain goat (_Mazama montana_), the grizzly bear, moose, woodland caribou, coyote, and both white-tailed and black-tailed (or mule) deer, as well as the big-horn or mountain sheep (_Ovis canadensis_). These specific animals are found only on the mainland. Other species, such as the puma, lynx, wolf, black bear, wapiti, and Columbian or coast deer, are common to both the islands and the mainland. Marine mammals in these areas are characterized by the harbour-seal, sea-otter, fur-seal, and sea-lion.

In the Gulf states, the native fauna is similar across the region. While some feral fauna has declined, deer, wolves, and black bears persist. Pumas (panthers) and lynxes (wild cats) are occasionally seen in swamps. Common smaller mammals include opossums, squirrels, and raccoons.

### Northern Asia and the Pamir
The mammals of northern Asia largely mirror those of Europe, though the Palaearctic fauna is enriched by additional species adapted to temperate and cold regions. This includes numerous Carnivora such as foxes, wolves, weasels, and bears. Rodents are well represented by mice, squirrels, hares, and characteristic forms such as pikas (tailless hares, _Lagomys_) and marmots (_Arctomys_). Insectivora include shrews, moles, and hedgehogs. 

The Ungulata order is represented by various sheep, with ten to twelve wild species of _Ovis_ found in Asian mountain chains. Other peculiar forms include the yellow sheep (_Gazella gutturosa_) and the saiga (_Saiga tatarica_). The musk deer (_Moschus_) and various forms of the genus _Cervus_ are also characteristic of the region. Monkeys (Quadrumana) are nearly unrepresented, with only one species in Japan and one or two in Tibet and northern China.

In the high-altitude Pamir region, wild sheep of great size are found. One such species, _Ovis Poli_ (also known as the _Rass_), possesses massive horns. One recorded pair of horns measured 4 feet 8 inches in length on the curve, with a base of 14¼ inches and tips 3 feet 9 inches apart. These horns are used by Kirghiz hunters for stirrup-iron substitutes and horse shoes, and by shepherds to create bowls or enclosures for cattle. In this cold, lofty region, wolves are numerous and prey upon these sheep.

### Other Global Regions
In the drier steppes of other regions, large mammals are very numerous and include the giraffe, zebra, hippopotamus, lion, buffalo, rhinoceros, and camel (the latter confined to arid northern regions). The elephant is increasingly restricted to unfrequented districts. Rhinoceroses are noted as being particularly abundant and dangerous in some areas.

In the Carpathians, the fauna includes lynxes, wolves, and bears, along with birds of prey. This region is also distinguished by a mollusc fauna containing species found only in the Alpine region. In other mountainous areas, such as those where the Kerry breed of cattle is found, goats and sheep share the sweet pasturage of higher ridges.

## Avifauna

Bird populations vary significantly by geography. In some provinces, about 340 species occur, including Clark's crow (_Picicorvus columbianus_), Steller's jay, the American magpie, and the burrowing owl of the dry interior. Gallinaceous birds include the dusky grouse of the Rocky Mountains, the large blue grouse of the coast, and various "partridges" or ruffed grouse of the genus _Bonasa_. Ptarmigans are abundant in high mountains. Migratory _Anatidae_ include the mallard, teal, wood-duck, canvas-back, red-head, buffle-head, pin-tail, spoon-bill, and scaup-duck.

Northern Asia's ornithology is closely allied to Europe's, with nearly three-fourths of European species extending into Siberia and Japan. This region is the exclusive wild home of the genus _Phasianus_, as well as the eared pheasants (_Crossoptilon_) and gold pheasants (_Thaumalea_).

Other regions feature larger birds such as the secretary-bird, bustard, stork, and ostrich, while lakes and rivers host thousands of flamingoes, pelicans, and cranes. In the Gulf states, the avifauna includes hawks, eagles, owls, vultures, turkeys, geese, and "partridges" known elsewhere as "Bob White" or quail.

## Reptiles and Other Fauna

Reptiles vary by habitat. In the Gulf states, alligators are found in every bayou, and moccasins, rattlesnakes, horned toads, lizards, and turtles are abundant. In other tropical regions, crocodiles are common in Victoria Nyanza and large rivers, while the puff-adder is the most dangerous of the relatively rare snakes.

## Prehistoric Fauna of the Eocene

The Eocene period marked a significant transition in higher life forms. The Saurian reptiles that dominated the Mesozoic disappeared, replaced by placental mammals. Early Eocene mammals were generalized types that later diverged into specialized orders. 

Early carnivores included _Patriofelis_, _Provivera_, _Cynodon_, _Hyaenodon_, _Amblyctonus_, _Palaeonictis_, and _Arctocyon_. True cats did not arrive until later, though _Eusmilus_ appeared in the Upper Eocene of France. Primitive ungulates (Condylarths), such as _Phenacodus_, had five effective toes. The Amblypoda, including _Coryphodon_, were also prominent. Notable "elephantine" creatures of western America included _Loxophodon_, _Uintatherium_, _Tinoceras_, and _Dinoceras_—the latter featuring a pair of huge downward-projecting canine teeth and three pairs of protuberances or horns on its skull.
