# Herbaceous Vegetation of Rainy Regions

The herbaceous vegetation found in rainy regions varies significantly by geography, climate, and elevation, ranging from subtropical swamps and tropical mountains to temperate market gardens and high-altitude plateaus.

## Tropical and Subtropical Herbaceous Flora
In regions characterized by high temperatures, fertile soil, and heavy rainfall, such as Louisiana, the landscape is covered in luxuriant subtropical vegetation. This includes both arborescent and herbaceous forms. In these areas, flowering annuals are primarily aquatic; notable examples include water hyacinths, which can obstruct streams, as well as water lilies and a rich variety of irises that provide color to bayous and coast wastes.

In the hotter and wetter regions of eastern India, the truly tropical flora is continuous with that of the Malayan peninsula and islands. This vegetation extends along the lower ranges of the Himalaya, though it becomes less marked and is restricted to lower elevations as one moves westward, where winter cold increases and rainfall diminishes.

In other rainy regions, specific herbaceous families are prominent, including the Orchidaceae, Orontiaceae, Scitamineae, Gramineae, and Cyperaceae, along with ferns and other Cryptogams. Some of these forms, such as *Begonia*, *Osbeckia*, various Cyrtandraceae, Scitamineae, and certain epiphytical orchids, extend beyond the tropic and ascend into temperate zones on mountains.

## Mountainous and High-Altitude Vegetation
Vegetation in mountainous regions is heavily influenced by elevation and moisture. In the south-western part of Arabia (Arabia Felix), where summits reach 6,000 to 7,000 feet, rainfall is sufficient to support luxuriant vegetation. The spring season brings an abundance of herbaceous vegetation and many bulbous plants, some of which are identical to those in the Syrian region and extend to the Himalaya.

The higher, cooler, and less rainy ranges of the Himalaya exhibit a more uniform character that closely approaches European forms. At the greatest elevations, many alpine plants are found that are believed to be identical to species from the north Arctic regions. As the terrain reaches the Tibetan plateau, increased dryness causes the flora to assume features of the Siberian type, including many Siberian genera and species. A unique feature of Tibet is the presence of marine plant assemblages growing at elevations of 14,000 to 15,000 feet, particularly near salt lakes.

## Cultivated Herbaceous Plants and Crops
Human cultivation of herbaceous plants is widespread in rainy and fertile regions:

*   **India and Arabia:** Cultivated plants in Arabia are similar to those of northern India, including wheat, barley, common *Sorghum*, cotton, and indigo. In the Indian region, cultivation includes rice, maize, various millets (*Sorghum*, *Penicillaria*, *Panicum*, and *Eleusine*), pulses, peas, beans, mustard, rape, ginger, turmeric, pepper, capsicum, tobacco, *Sesamum*, poppy, *Crotolaria*, and *Cannabis*.
*   **West Africa:** In certain regions where the south experiences heavy rains and damp clay soil, indigenous plants include cotton and tobacco. On mountain slopes, grains such as wheat, durra, and *dukhn* (a staple millet) are grown, alongside sesame, cucumbers, water-melons, and onions.
*   **Europe:** In the London district, flower farms extensively cultivate herbaceous paeonies, Spanish and German irises, lilies of the valley, chrysanthemums, foxgloves, hollyhocks, wallflowers, and carnations. In Kent, sweet peas, pansies, and campanulas are also raised. In the Doubs department of France, the fertile plain produces cereals, hemp, and vegetables.
*   **South America and Tropical Regions:** The cassava plant (*Manihot utilissima* and *M. Aipi*) is a herbaceous or semi-shrubby perennial native to South America. It is characterized by slender stems (5 to 9 feet high) and large fleshy roots up to 3 feet long. It is extensively cultivated in the Malay Archipelago and west tropical Africa.

## Environmental Impacts on Vegetation
The health and appearance of herbaceous vegetation are often tied to seasonal rain cycles. In certain coastal regions, the "harmattan" or long dry season causes vegetation to dry up or droop due to a total lack of atmospheric moisture. Conversely, in the alluvial regions and coast swamps of Louisiana, the combination of fertile soil and heavy rain supports a profusion of flowers across both bottom lands and uplands, with the upland flora being the more diversified.
