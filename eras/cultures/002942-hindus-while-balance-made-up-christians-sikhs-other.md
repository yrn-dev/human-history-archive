# Hindus

## Distribution and Demographics
Hinduism is represented across various regions of Asia, with significant populations in India and Burma. In the province of Burma, Hindus constitute 2.76% of the total population, which according to the 1901 census equals 285,484 individuals. In Baluchistan, the population is overwhelmingly Mahommedan, with Hindus numbering 4,706 out of every 100,000 inhabitants. 

In other regions, such as Gujarat, Hinduism is the predominant religion, despite the influence of petty Mahommedan kingdoms. In the city of Bombay, Hindus form one of the large sections of the population, contributing to a variety of types alongside Mahommedans, Parsees, Jains, and Christians. In the Dogra country—the area around Jammu inhabited by the Dogra race of Hill Rajputs—the Hindu religion is represented alongside Sikh and Mahommedan faiths.

## Political and Social History
In the southwest of India, the Mahrattas, a Hindu people, rose against Islam and restored Brahminism as the ruling religion. For a period, they extended their power over the entire southern triangle of India. Similarly, in Rajputana, Brahminism replaced the rule of Islam, with powerful Rajput princes ruling at Jaipur and Bhurtpur. 

The social structure of Hindu society is characterized by a caste system. In the Dogra country, there are numerous castes, ranging from high-born Rajputs of the Maharaja's caste to low-born menials. In the Carnatic, a 12th-century Hindu reformation movement known as Lingayatism has been embraced by 45% of the population.

## Beliefs and Customs
Hindu beliefs include the concept of previous states of existence. This is reflected in the regard for dogs, which Hindus consider unclean. They believe every dog is animated by a malignant and wicked spirit that has been condemned to penance in that form due to crimes committed in a previous existence. Consequently, Hindus undergo various purifications if they accidentally come into contact with a dog.

The study of Hindu life has been documented by observers such as Jean Antoine Dubois, a French Catholic missionary. Dubois adopted native clothing and habits to better understand the "innermost life and character" of the population. His records on Hindu manners, customs, and ceremonies focus on three primary areas: a general view of society and the caste system, the four states of Brahminical life, and religion, including temples, feasts, and objects of worship.

## Cultural Influence and Interactions
Hindu modifications influenced the decorative art of India, which aimed at a symmetry of poise. This style eventually reached Japan, where it contrasted with the Japanese approach to decorative art and floral arrangement (ike-bana), which seeks a "balance of inequalities" or symmetry by suggestion.

In the context of religious interaction, the archive notes that during periods of Moslem rule, Hindus accepted the ruling faith in a smaller proportion than Europeans did under similar circumstances. Additionally, the Sikhs, a religious body that arose in the Punjab, assailed the Hindu Vedas along with the Moslem Koran, proclaiming the universal rule of one God.
