# Psychology

Psychology is a field of study that has evolved from philosophical inquiry into a distinct positive science, encompassing theoretical frameworks, academic instruction, and clinical application.

## Theoretical Developments in the 19th Century
In Great Britain during the 19th century, the field was significantly advanced by the work of Bain. He was the first in the region during this period to thoroughly apply physiology to the elucidation of mental states, sharing the conviction that no one is a psychologist unless they are also a physiologist (*psychologus nemo nisi physiologus*). Bain originated the theory of psycho-physical parallelism, which serves as a working basis for modern psychologists. 

Bain sought to clear psychology of metaphysics and introduced the natural history method of classification to psychical phenomena, giving the work a scientific character. While he advocated for the introspective method of investigation, he also recognized the utility of social, infant, and animal psychology. His work emphasized the importance of active experiences of effort and movement, including a theory of a central innervation sense. Professor Wm. James described Bain's work as the "last word" of psychology's earlier stage and the pioneer of its new stage.

## Academic and Philosophical Contexts
Psychology has historically been integrated into broader philosophical instruction. Lotze, for example, delivered annual lectures on psychology and logic as part of a traditional academic scheme. Following his death on July 1, 1881, his son published a small pamphlet on psychology that contained the final form of the subject as Lotze had treated it in his 1881 summer session lectures.

Additionally, psychological conceptions form the basis of Buddhist ethics. The *Dhamma Sangani*, a translation of one of the seven later works on *Abhidhamma* (an elaborate exposition of the doctrine set out in the *Nikayas*), is introduced under the title *Buddhist Psychology*. This manual for Buddhist students, estimated by translator Mrs. Caroline Rhys Davids to date from the 4th century B.C., provides a detailed account of these psychological conceptions.

## Clinical Application and Institutional Observation
In contemporary institutional settings, such as the MCC New York, psychology departments are utilized for clinical visits, risk assessments, and the observation of inmates. These applications include:

*   **Suicide Risk Assessment:** Psychology departments conduct formal, in-person assessments to determine if "suicide watch" is warranted. This process may involve evaluating "suicidal tendencies" noted in paperwork or identifying risk factors such as pre-trial status, the nature of charges, and the high-profile status of a case.
*   **Psychological Observation:** Inmates may be placed under psychological observation for specific periods. During this time, "Psychological Reconstruction" may be conducted by officials, such as the Assistant Director of the Reentry Services Division, to review the inmate's activities and visits.
*   **Housing Recommendations:** Psychologists provide directives regarding inmate housing. For instance, the Psychology Department may recommend that an inmate be housed with an "appropriate cellmate" based on previous incidents or clinical evaluations.
*   **Individualized Care:** The Bureau of Prisons (BOP) utilizes an individualized approach to care and custody, where psychologists, custody staff, and unit teams make care recommendations upon an individual's removal from psychological observation or suicide watch.
