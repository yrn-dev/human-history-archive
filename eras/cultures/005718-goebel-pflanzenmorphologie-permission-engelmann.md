# Goebel

The name Goebel is associated with several distinct individuals across the fields of botany, astronomy, political history, and exploration.

## Botanical and Biological Contributions
In the field of plant morphology, Goebel is cited as a source for the study of *Musci* (mosses). His work provides detailed structural analysis of the life history of mosses, specifically the example of *Funaria hygrometrica*. According to these records, the sexual generation of a moss is a leafy plant that develops from a filamentous protonema rather than directly from a spore. 

Detailed illustrations attributed to Goebel describe the components of the *Funaria hygrometrica* sporogonium, including the seta, the capsule, and the calyptra. Further anatomical details include the operculum, peristome, annulus, columella, and the archesporium, as well as the air-space located between the wall of the capsule and the spore-sac. Goebel's work also covers the germinating spores of *Funaria*, identifying the spore wall, vacuole, and rhizoid, and describes the protonema as a creeping filament with brown walls from which chlorophyll-containing cells arise. Additionally, Goebel is credited with illustrations of *Chiloscyphus polyanthos*, depicting the elongation of the seta and the "perianth" with perichaetial leaves. He is the author of the work *Organography of Plants* (Oxford, 1905).

## Scientific Studies on Bacteria
Engelmann, in studies alongside others such as Beyerinck, Lankester, and Ewart, made advances in the knowledge of chromogenic bacteria. This research focused on *Bacteriopurpurin*, a red colouring matter found in certain sulphur bacteria. This substance absorbs solar energy, allowing the organism to utilize that energy for life purposes. Engelmann demonstrated that these red-purple bacteria collect in bands—primarily in the ultra-red, and to a lesser extent in the green and orange—which correspond to the absorption spectrum of the extracted colouring matter. This research established a parallelism between the absorption of light by these bacteria and the chlorophyll of green plants, demonstrating that radiant energy trapped by colour-screens enables living cells to perform work, such as the reduction of highly oxidized compounds, and results in the release of oxygen.

## Astronomy and History
Karl Goebel is noted as an authority on the astronomer Johannes Kepler. In 1871, he published a work in Halle titled *Über Keplers astronomische Anschauungen*.
