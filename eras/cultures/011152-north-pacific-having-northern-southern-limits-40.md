# North Pacific

The North Pacific is a vast region characterized by diverse zoological zones, significant marine wildlife, and varied coastal geographies spanning from Asia to North America.

## Zoological and Climatic Zones
The North Pacific serves as a continuation of the equatorial zoological zone. This zone, which generally coincides with the tropics of Cancer and Capricorn, extends through the North Pacific along a line indicated by the tropic, eventually striking the coast of Mexico at the southern end of the Gulf of California. 

In terms of regional biodiversity, the Tropical Pacific region is noted for containing approximately 60 species. This is significantly fewer than other regions such as the Tropical American region, which is the richest with about 1,300 species, or the North American region, which contains about 400 species.

## Marine Fauna
The North Pacific is home to several species of sea-bears and sea-lions. These animals are gregarious and polygamous, with males typically being much larger than females. They are found in temperate regions of both hemispheres, though they are absent from the North Atlantic coasts. 

Notable species in the North Pacific include:
*   **The Northern Sea-Lion (_O. stelleri_)**: The largest of its genus, reaching approximately 10 feet in length.
*   **The Sea-Bear or Fur-Seal (_O. ursina_)**: This species is found in the North Pacific, and its skins are imported in immense numbers from the Pribiloff Islands. These skins, when dressed and stripped of longer outer hairs, are used in commerce as "sealskin."

## Asian Coastal Geography
China is situated between the North Pacific and the eastern flank of the Tibetan plateau. Its geographical limits are approximately 40 degrees North at its northern boundary and 20 degrees North at its southern boundary. The region is characterized as being well-wooded, populous, fertile, and highly cultivated, though the terrain is generally broken up by mountains of moderate elevation (excluding those on its western border). The climate features hot summers and cold winters, with the north experiencing decidedly severe winters.

## North American Pacific Coast
The Pacific coast of North America encompasses a wide range of territories and climatic conditions. In the United States, the region west of the Rocky Mountains is known as the Pacific coast region, where the climate is somewhat similar to that of western Europe.

### Canada and British Columbia
In Canada, the province of British Columbia and Vancouver Island were settled under the Hudson's Bay Company in 1821 and entered the Canadian confederation in 1871. The region is defined by several major river systems that flow toward the Pacific:
*   **The Fraser River**: Rises in the Rocky Mountains, flows north-west, then turns south to empty into the Strait of Georgia south of Vancouver.
*   **The Columbia River**: Rises in the Rocky Mountains, flows north for about 150 miles, then bends south through the Arrow Lakes into the United States, emptying into the Pacific at Astoria, Oregon.
*   **The Skeena River**: Flows south-west into the Pacific.
*   **The Stikine River**: Rises in British Columbia and crosses the coast strip of Alaska before entering the Pacific.

Infrastructure in this region includes the Pacific division of the Canadian Pacific railway, which runs approximately 500 miles across British Columbia to its terminus at Vancouver. On Vancouver Island, the Victoria & Sydney railway (16 miles) and the Esquimalt & Nanaimo railway (78 miles) connect southern ports with coal fields.

### Indigenous Populations
The Pacific coast and surrounding areas are home to various linguistic stocks. The Athapascan family is divided into Northern, Pacific, and Southern divisions. The Pacific division covers a strip of territory approximately 400 miles in length, extending from Oregon southwards into California.
