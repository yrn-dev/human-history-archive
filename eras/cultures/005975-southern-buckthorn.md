# Southern Buckthorn

## Identification and Nomenclature
The plant popularly known as "southern buckthorn" is botanically identified as *Bumelia lycioides* (also referred to as *Bumelia lanuginosa*). It is categorized among the buckthorns, a group of plants that includes various species of the genus *Rhamnus*.

## Related Species and Botanical Context
Southern buckthorn is distinct from other buckthorn varieties. In North America, other buckthorns include *Rhamnus purshiana* (also known as *Cascara sagrada*), which is found on the Pacific coast and produces cascara bark, and *Rhamnus caroliniana*, which is known as the alder-buckthorn.

The broader botanical group to which buckthorns belong is the natural order Rhamnaceae. This order also includes the genus *Zizyphus*, such as *Zizyphus lotus*, a bush native to southern Europe.

## Comparison with Other Buckthorns
While southern buckthorn is identified as *Bumelia lycioides*, other prominent species in the Rhamnaceae order exhibit different characteristics:

*   **Buckthorn (*Rhamnus cathartica*):** A much-branched shrub reaching 10 feet in height. It features blackish bark, spinous branchlets, and ovate, sharply-serrated leaves between 1 and 2 inches long. Its flowers are small and green, with male and female flowers appearing on separate plants. The fruit is a succulent, globose black berry containing four stones. This species is native to Europe, north Africa, and north Asia, and has become naturalized in parts of eastern North America.
*   **Black Alder (*Rhamnus frangula*):** An allied species common in England. It differs from *Rhamnus cathartica* by having bisexual flowers with parts in fives, non-serrated leaves, and an absence of spiny branchlets.
*   **Sea-buckthorn (*Hippophae rhamnoides*):** A willow-like shrub ranging from 1 to 8 feet in height. It is characterized by narrow leaves that are silvery on the underside and globose orange-yellow fruits measuring one-third of an inch in diameter. It is found on sandy seashores from Sussex to Kent and York, though it is not common.

## Utility of Related Rhamnaceae Species
Various species within the buckthorn family are utilized for their chemical and material properties. The fruit of *Rhamnus cathartica* possesses strong purgative properties, and its bark is used to produce a yellow dye. Similarly, the fruits of *Rhamnus frangula* are purgative and yield a green dye when they are unripe. The wood of *Rhamnus frangula* is soft and porous, known as black dogwood, and is used in the production of gunpowder.

Other species of *Rhamnus* are used specifically for dyes; *Rhamnus tinctoria* and *Rhamnus infectoria* are used for this purpose, with the former two yielding the commercial "China green." Additionally, *Rhamnus alaternus*, a species from the Mediterranean, is grown in shrubberies.
