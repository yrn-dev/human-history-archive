# Trou Madame

Trou Madame is a game that is similar in nature to the game known as Mississippi.

## Gameplay and Rules
The game of Trou Madame is characterized by specific variations in its rules when compared to Mississippi. In Trou Madame, a ball does not need to be played onto a cushion. Additionally, the scoring system regarding cups differs from that of Mississippi; if a ball falls into a cup during a game of Trou Madame, the value of that cup is scored by the opponent rather than by the striker.

## Relation to Mississippi
To understand the mechanics of Trou Madame, it is necessary to examine the game of Mississippi, from which it is derived. Mississippi is played using a bridge that is pierced with nine or more arches, depending on the size of the table used. These arches are numbered starting from 1. 

In a typical game of Mississippi, nine balls are used, although the black ball is sometimes omitted. Each player is allotted a round, and the primary objective is to send the balls through the arches. Under the rules of Mississippi, balls cannot be sent through the arches directly; they must first strike a cushion. If the black ball is utilized, it counts for double the value of the arch made. If a player sends a ball through an arch without first striking a cushion, the score is awarded to the adversary. However, if another ball is positioned in front of the bridge, the cue-ball may send it through provided the cue-ball has struck a cushion. In Mississippi, if a ball falls into a cup, the striker scores both the value of the arch and the value of the cup.
