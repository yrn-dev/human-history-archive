# Bantu Dialects of British East Africa

The Bantu languages of British East Africa are categorized into several distinct groups based on their geographical distribution and linguistic characteristics. These include groups located east of the Rift valley, in the highlands, and along the coast province.

## The Kikuyu-Kamba Group
Located east of the Rift valley in British East Africa, the Kikuyu-Kamba group consists of the special dialects of Kikuyu and Ukambani. This group also encompasses all scattered fragments of Bantu speech found on the upper Tana river (known as Dhaicho) and on Mount Kenya.

## The Kilimanjaro Group
The Kilimanjaro group, also referred to as the Chaga-Siha group, comprises the peculiar dialects spoken on Mount Meru, Mount Kilimanjaro, and Ugweno.

## The Coast Province Dialects
The Bantu dialects of the coast province of British East Africa are represented by the Pokomo-Nyika-Giriama-Taveita group. This group is situated between the frontier of German East Africa to the south and the Tana river to the north, including the Tana river area itself.

## Related Linguistic Studies and Bibliography
The study of Bantu and other African languages has been documented in various comparative grammars and dictionaries. Notable works include:
*   **Comparative Grammars:** Dr. W. I. Bleek authored *A Comparative Grammar of South African Languages* (1869), while Father J. Torrend produced the *Comparative Grammar of the South African Bantu Languages* (1894), which focused primarily on the languages of the Central Zambezi. Carl Meinhof contributed extensive information on the original structure of the Bantu mother-speech and languages in South-East and East Africa through his essays in *Mittheilungen des Seminaers fuer Orientalische Sprachen* and his 1906 work, *Grundzuege einer vergleichenden Grammatik der Bantusprachen*.
*   **Vocabularies and Dictionaries:** Sir H. H. Johnston provided illustrative vocabularies in *The Kilimanjaro Expedition* (1884), *British Central Africa* (1898), and *The Uganda Protectorate* (1902-1904). Dr. L. Krapf's *Swahili-English Dictionary* (1882) offers significant information on the Swahili language as spoken on the East African coast, including words that have since become obsolete. Additionally, Bishop Steere published various handbooks and vocabularies on the languages of the East African coast-lands, which are noted for preserving prefix forms that are currently passing out of use.
*   **Other Regional Studies:** George Grenfell's *George Grenfell and the Congo* (1908) classified the Congo, Fernandian, and Cameroon groups of Bantu speech. Other works include the *Dictionary of the Congo Language* by Rev. Holman Bentley and the *Introductory Handbook of the Yao Language* by Rev. Alexander Hetherwick.

## Other Bantu Dialects
Beyond East Africa, other Bantu groups are identified in different regions. In the coast-lands north of the Cameroon delta and on the west slopes of the Cameroon Mountains, the Isubu-Bakwiri group is found. The dialects of Fernando Po (including Bani, Bateti, and Ediya) are distantly allied to other groups. The Barondo-Bakundu group begins at the Rio del Rey near the Cross river estuary and may include dialects such as Bas[=a], Abo, Bonken, Nkosi, and Barombi, spoken on the Cameroon river (Magombe), the Sanaga and Nyong rivers, and the eastern slopes of the Cameroon mountains. 

Further east and north-east of the Barondo-Bakundu group are languages described as "semi-Bantu," such as Bafut, Bayon, Mbudikum, Nki, Bati, and Mbe, which show affinities with the Fan and speech forms in the Central Benue river basin. In French Gabun, the Mpongwe-Orungu and Aduma languages are present, along with tongues from the Fernan Vaz promontory and Lower Ogowe. These transition south into the Kakongo dialects of the Sete Kama and Loango coasts, including Ka-kongo (Kabinda), Ba-kamba, Ba-vili, Ma-yombe, Ba-nyanga, and Ba-kama.
