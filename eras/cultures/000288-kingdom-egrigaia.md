# KINGDOM OF EGRIGAIA

The Kingdom of Egrigaia was a province belonging to Tangut, situated eastward from Erguiul. Travelers reaching the province from Erguiul would ride for eight days to arrive in a region characterized by numerous villages and cities. The inhabitants of Egrigaia were subjects of the Great Kaan.

## Geography and Identification
Historical and linguistic analysis has sought to identify the precise location of Egrigaia. Some sources equate Egrigaia with Ning-hsia, noting that the Mongol text refers to it as Erigaia. This identification is supported by the fact that Eleuths of Alashan referred to Ning-hsia as Yargai, and Mongols have continued to call Ning-hsia "Irghai Khotun." 

Other research suggests that Egrigaia may be represented by the modern principality of Alashan. This theory is based on the identification of the chief town, Calachan, with Alashan. Calachan is mentioned by Rashiduddin as Kalaján among the cities of Tangut. The modern capital of Alashan, Dyn-yuan-yin, is located some distance west of the Hwang-Ho at approximately latitude 39°. 

Additionally, some scholars have linked Egrigaia to Ulahai (also appearing as Uiraca, Eyircai, or Arbaca), which was one of the circles of Tangut. Ulahai is described as a fortress in a pass of the Alashan Mountains. These mountains form a semicircle 500 li in extent with over forty narrow passes leading to the department of Ning-hsia; the most practicable of these, Ch’i-mu-K’ow, is no more than 80 feet wide.

## Administration and Society
The capital city of the kingdom was Calachan. The population was primarily composed of Idolaters, though the province also contained fine churches belonging to Nestorian Christians. 

The region was subject to the Mongol Empire. Historically, the broader Tangut area was the target of five separate invasions by Chinghiz Khan, occurring in 1205, 1207, 1209 (or 1210–1211), 1218, and 1226–1227. During the third invasion, Chinghiz took the town of Uiraca (referred to by some as Erica or Erlaca) and besieged the capital, then known as Chung-sing or Chung-hing (modern Ning-hsia). During the final invasion of 1226, Chinghiz proceeded to the Yellow River and invested Lingchau, south of Ning-hsia.

## Economy and Trade
Egrigaia was renowned for its textile production, specifically the manufacture of camlets made from camel's wool. These camlets were considered the finest in the world. The province was particularly noted for producing white camlets, made from the wool of white camels, which were regarded as the best of all. These stuffs were purchased by merchants in Calachan and transported across the world for sale. 

In addition to textiles, the department was famous during the time of Marco Polo for the cultivation of the safflower (*carthamus tinctorius*).
