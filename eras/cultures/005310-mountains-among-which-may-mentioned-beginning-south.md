# Mountains and Mountainous Regions

The archive's records detail various mountainous regions across the globe, ranging from the geological compositions of North American ranges to the historical and ethnic associations of highlands in Europe and Asia.

## The Rocky Mountains
The Rocky Mountains are primarily composed of palaeozoic sediments, with ages spanning from the Carboniferous to the Cambrian. Certain subordinate infolded areas of Cretaceous origin contain coal. Along the boundary of the United States, the range maintains an average height of 8,000 ft. However, the range reaches its culmination between the latitudes of 53° and 51°. In the Canadian Rockies, the highest peak is Mount Robson at 13,700 ft., though the highest peak in British Columbia is Mount Fairweather, located on the International Boundary, which reaches 15,287 ft. Other significant peaks in the Canadian Rockies include Columbia (12,740 ft.), Forbes (12,075 ft.), Assiniboine (11,860 ft.), Bryce (11,686 ft.), Temple (11,626 ft.), and Lyell (11,463 ft.).

Travel across the Rocky Mountains is facilitated by several passes. Listed from south to north, these include:
*   **South Kootenay or Boundary Pass**: 7,100 ft.
*   **Crow's Nest Pass**: 5,500 ft. (crosses great coal fields and is traversed by the southern branch of the Canadian Pacific railway).
*   **Kicking Horse or Wapta Pass**: 5,300 ft. (traversed by the main line of the Canadian Pacific railway).
*   **Athabasca Pass**: 6,025 ft.
*   **Yellow Head Pass**: 3,733 ft. (likely to be used by the Grand Trunk Pacific railway).
*   **Pine River Pass**: 2,850 ft.
*   **Peace River Pass**: 2,000 ft., through which the Peace river flows.

## Ecology and Agriculture of Mountainous Provinces
In provinces such as British Columbia, the mountainous character of the land is balanced by long valleys between interior mountain ranges, lower slopes, and river deltas, which provide good farming land. These areas are best suited for mixed farming, specifically the production of hay, potatoes, oats, and wheat. The country around Kamloops and the Yale district are noted as being especially suited for sheep and cattle raising. Additionally, the Okanagan valley is a center for fruit raising, producing strawberries, grapes, apricots, peaches, cherries, plums, and apples, as well as hops.

The fauna of these regions includes the Rocky Mountain goat (*Mazama montana*) and the big-horn or mountain sheep (*Ovis canadensis*), both found only on the mainland. Other mammals include the grizzly bear, moose, woodland caribou, coyote, and various deer (white-tailed, black-tailed or mule). In the higher mountain regions, ptarmigans abound, and the dusky grouse replaces the large blue grouse of the coast.

## European Mountainous Regions
### Greece and the Balkans
In the region of Mali, the Malians were surrounded by mountainous regions. They were harassed and overwhelmed in war by aggressive mountaineers from the neighboring region of Œta, known as Œtæans. The principal group among these were the Ænianes, an ancient Hellenic Amphiktyonic race. Their chief town, Hypata, was situated on the northern declivity of Mount Œta in the upper valley of the Spercheius. Other tribes in this extensive mountain range included the Kallians and Bomians (Ætolian tribes), whose abodes were described as high and cold.

In the region of Scythia, the Tauric race occupied a mountainous country that runs in the direction of the Pontus, extending to the "Rugged Chersonese" peninsula.

### Other European Highlands
In Corsica, mountains provide the source for rivers and torrents that bring large volumes of water to the island. The longest river, the Golo, rises in the mountains to the west of Corte in the pastoral region of Niolo, which is inhabited by a population of obscure origin. In Germany, the Bructeri, a non-Suevian tribe, dwelt in the Hartz Mountains on the borders of the Lippe.

## Asia Minor
In Asia Minor, the city of Iconium (modern Konia) is situated in a fertile plain. It is located 6 miles from the Pisidian mountains to the west, with further mountains to the north and south. The Pisidian mountains provide streams that create garden-like land to the south and south-west of the city. To the east, while the plain is a dead level for hundreds of miles, the view is interrupted by mountains described as "island-like."

## Botanical Observations
In certain rainy regions, various forms of trees and herbaceous vegetation ascend into temperate zones on the mountains. Examples include *Begonia*, *Osbeckia*, various Scitamineae, Cyrtandraceae, and a few epiphytical orchids.
