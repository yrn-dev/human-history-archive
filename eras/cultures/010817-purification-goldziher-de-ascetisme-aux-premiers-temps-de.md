## Sacrilege and Profanation
The archive contains records regarding the legal and religious penalties for the failure to maintain the purity of sacred objects and mysteries, termed as sacrilege. Such acts are viewed as crimes of "Leze-Majesté divine" because they are seen as direct attacks on the Divinity, the Sainte Vierge, and the Saints, intended to offend God.

Acts defined as exécrable sacrilege include:
* The profanation of the sacraments and the holy Eucharist.
* The misuse of holy Hosties, such as throwing them to the ground or employing them for vile and profane purposes.
* The profanation of baptismal fonts.
* The mockery or counterfeiting of sacred Mysteries during debaucheries.
* The striking, mutilating, or destroying of images consecrated to God, the Sainte Vierge, or the Saints.

The penalties for these acts have varied by jurisdiction and era. According to the Ancien Testament, the punishment for sacrilege was death by fire or lapidation. Roman Law prescribed punishments including the use of iron, fire, and wild beasts. In France, the penalty was arbitrary, depending on the circumstances and the status of the accused. However, for primary sacrilege attacking the Divinity, penalties included the cutting off of a hand, a fine (amende honorable), and death by fire.
