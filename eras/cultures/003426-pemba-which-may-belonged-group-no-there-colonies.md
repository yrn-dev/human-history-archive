# Pemba

## Geography and Settlement
Pemba is an island located off the East African coast. According to the traditions and legends of the Bantu peoples, they migrated down the east coast and took to the sea, occupying Pemba as well as the island of Zanzibar. This maritime expansion extended further to the Comoro archipelago and the west coast of Madagascar.

## Language and Dialects
The original local dialects of Pemba, along with those of Zanzibar, may have belonged to group No. 6. These original dialects were distinct from Swahili. Swahili is described as a somewhat archaic Bantu dialect, likely indigenous to the East African coast south of the Ruvu (Pangani) river. Through intermixture with Arabic, Swahili became the *lingua franca* of eastern Africa between the Zambezi and the White Nile, and it is now widely used as a commercial language across Central and Eastern Africa.

## Regional Linguistic Context
While Swahili is the language of Zanzibar and the opposite coast, the coast-lands between the Kilwa settlements to the south and the Ruvu or Pangani river to the north feature local languages and dialects that are more or less related to Swahili, though they remain independent languages. Examples of these include:
*   Shambala (located north of the Ruvu)
*   Bondei
*   Nguru
*   Zeguha
*   Ki-zaramo
*   Ki-mrima

Additionally, there are colonies of Swahili-speaking people located at Lamu, Malindi, and Mombasa, extending as far north as the Shebeli river in Somaliland, and as far south as Angoche along the coast of Portuguese and German East Africa.
