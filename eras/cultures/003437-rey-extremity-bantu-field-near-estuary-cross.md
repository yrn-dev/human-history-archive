# Rio del Rey and the Extremity of the Bantu Field

## Geography and Boundaries
The Rio del Rey is a creek located at the eastern end of the Calabar estuary. This geographical feature serves as the official boundary between (German) Cameroon and (British) Nigeria. The Calabar estuary itself is primarily formed by the Cross river, though it also receives water from other streams and the Calabar river. At its mouth, the estuary measures between 10 and 12 miles in breadth, a width it maintains for approximately 30 miles.

## Linguistic Classification
The Rio del Rey marks the northern starting point of the Barondo-Bakundu group, which is situated on the extremity of the Bantu field near the estuary of the Cross river. This linguistic group may include several other dialects described as "much-debased," specifically the Bas[=a], Barombi, Abo, Bonken, and Nkosi. These dialects are spoken on the Cameroon river (Magombe), the eastern slopes of the Cameroon mountains, and extending to the Nyong and Sanaga rivers.

To the north-east and east of the Barondo-Bakundu group are languages described as "semi-Bantu," including the Bafut, Bayon, Mbudikum, Nki, Bati, and Mbe. These semi-Bantu languages show affinities with the Fan and with forms of speech found in the basin of the Central Benue river.

## The Bantu Field and Related Dialects
The broader Bantu field in this region includes several other distinct groups:
* **The Duala Group:** Spoken on the delta and estuary of the Cameroon river, this group is characterized as being of a "much purer Bantu type."
* **The Fan (or Pangwe) Group:** These forms of speech occupy the hinterland of the "Duala" Cameroon and the interior of French Gabun and German Cameroon, extending as far north and east as the Mbam, Sanaga, and Sanga rivers. They are described as being so corrupt that they are only just recognizable as Bantu.
* **The Isubu-Bakwiri Group:** Located on the west slopes of the Cameroon Mountains and the coast-lands north of the Cameroon delta (Ambas Bay).
* **Fernando Po Dialects:** Including the Bani, Bateti, and Ediya, these are distantly allied to other Bantu groups.

## Linguistic Characteristics and Origins
The Bantu type of language is defined by a pronominal-prefix system. While the exact birthplace of the Bantu languages remains a puzzle, there are noted affinities in a few verbal roots and the numerals 2, 3, 4, and sometimes 5 between Bantu and the languages of the Gold Coast, Calabar, the Benue river, the lower Niger, and Northern Togoland.

In the region of the Cross River and Benue, as well as the lower Niger, eastern Niger delta, and Northern Togoland, simple prefixes are used to express the quality of the noun and the difference between singular and plural. However, these do not correspond to the prefixes of the Bantu type, even though they sometimes fall into "classes."
