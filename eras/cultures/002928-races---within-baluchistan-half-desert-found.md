# Races of Baluchistan

The region of Baluchistan is characterized by a diverse ethnographic composition, containing representatives of half the great races of Asia. The population is distributed across a sparsely populated tract that is larger than any Indian province with the exception of Bengal, Madras, and Burma.

## Nomadic and Desert Tribes
Within the Baluchistan half of the desert, scattered tribes of nomads known as Rekis (or "desert people") are found. Among these, the Mohamadani are the most numerous and are likely of Arab origin. The Mohamadani occupy the central mountain region. 

To the south of this region lie the Kharan and Mashkel deserts. These areas are inhabited by the Naushirwanis, a purely Persian race that entered Baluchistan during historic times, though the specific date of their arrival is uncertain. The Naushirwanis are described as a fine, manly race who are in many respects superior to their modern compatriots in Iran; they appear to be identical to the Tahukani or Tahuki found in Perso-Baluchistan.

## Arab and Persian Influence
Arab influence is strongly impressed upon the ethnography of the region. All Rind tribes claim extraction from Koraish and Arab origin. This is attributed to the early Arab occupation of Seistan and southern Baluchistan, and their subsequent spread through the Sind valley, where they remained until the 12th century.

An underlying Persian population, known as Tajik, is scattered throughout the entire country and is recognizable almost everywhere. This population is sometimes represented by a dominant local tribe, but more frequently exists as the bondsman and agricultural slave of the general community. Examples of this group include the Dehkans (or Dehwars) and the Durzadas (referred to by Herodotus as *Derusiaei*), who are found throughout Makran and are called Nakibs when serving as slaves.

## Coastal and Valley Populations
In the narrow valleys of the Kej and Rakshan tributaries, as well as around the sources of the Hingol, there are innumerable tribes. These groups consist of remnants of races that can be traced through records of recent immigration or recognized in the works of Herodotus. The coast is also inhabited by a fish-eating population.

## Regional Distribution
The broader geography of the region further divides its inhabitants. The northern district, characterized by the long ridges of the Suliman system, is the prehistoric home of the Pathan highlander. To the south of the railway line lies a square block of territory that serves primarily as the home of the Baluch and the Brahui.

## Fauna
Beyond human populations, the desert plains and open table-lands of Baluchistan are inhabited by the onager (*Equus onager*), a species of Asiatic wild ass. The onager is smaller than the chigetai or kiang, possesses a broader dorsal stripe bordered with white, and varies in color from greyish to sandy.
