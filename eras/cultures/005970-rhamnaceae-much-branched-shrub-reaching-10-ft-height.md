# Buckthorn

Buckthorn, known botanically as *Rhamnus cathartica* and belonging to the natural order Rhamnaceae, is a much-branched shrub that can reach 10 feet in height. The plant is characterized by blackish bark, spinous branchlets, and ovate leaves that are sharply serrated, measuring 1 to 2 inches in length. These leaves are arranged several together at the ends of the shoots.

## Botanical Characteristics
The flowers of *Rhamnus cathartica* are small, green, and regular, with parts arranged in fours. Male and female flowers are borne on separate plants. The resulting fruit is globose, black, and succulent, containing four stones. 

An allied species found in England is *Rhamnus Frangula*, also known as black alder or berry-bearing. It is distinguished from the common buckthorn by its bisexual flowers (with parts in fives), non-serrated leaves, and the absence of spiny branchlets.

## Distribution and Habitat
*Rhamnus cathartica* is native to Europe, north Asia, and north Africa. It has also become naturalized in certain parts of eastern North America. In England, it is found primarily in thickets and woods, occurring chiefly on chalk; it is not wild in Scotland and is considered rare in Ireland.

Other species of the *Rhamnus* genus include:
* **American Buckthorns:** *Rhamnus purshiana* (also called Cascara sagrada), found on the Pacific coast and used for producing cascara bark, and *Rhamnus Caroliniana* (alder-buckthorn).
* **Mediterranean Species:** *Rhamnus Alaternus*, which is grown in shrubberies in several varieties.
* **Other Species:** *R. infectoria*, *R. tinctoria*, and *R. davurica*.

Additionally, *Bumelia lycioides* (or *lanuginosa*) is popularly referred to as "southern buckthorn."

## Practical Uses
The buckthorn plant provides several materials for human use:
* **Medicinal:** The fruit possesses strong purgative properties. Similarly, the fruits of *Rhamnus Frangula* are purgative.
* **Dyes:** The bark of *Rhamnus cathartica* yields a yellow dye. The fruits of *Rhamnus Frangula* produce a green dye when they are unripe. Other species, specifically *R. tinctoria* and *R. davurica*, are used to produce the China green of commerce.
* **Industrial:** The wood of *Rhamnus Frangula* is soft and porous; known as black dogwood, it is utilized for the production of gunpowder.

## Related Species and Distinctions
While *Hippophae rhamnoides* is called sea-buckthorn, it is a willow-like shrub reaching 1 to 8 feet in height with narrow leaves that are silvery on the underside and orange-yellow globose fruits. It occurs on sandy seashores from Sussex to Kent and York, though it is not common.
