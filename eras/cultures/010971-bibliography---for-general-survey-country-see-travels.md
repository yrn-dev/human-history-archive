# Cartography and Regional Documentation

The process of documenting regions and creating chorographical maps—general maps of a country or region—relies on a variety of source materials and methodologies. These maps are typically either reduced from existing topographical maps or compiled from miscellaneous available sources.

## The Process of Map Compilation

When a cartographer reduces a topographical map, the primary task is to generalize information, select place names judiciously, and ensure the final product is not overcrowded with details. However, the task becomes significantly more difficult when no surveys are available. In such instances, the map must be compiled from diverse materials, including:
* Reconnaissance surveys of small districts.
* Route surveys and astronomical observations provided by travellers.
* Information gathered from native sources.

The compiler must evaluate the value of these sources, a process that requires personal knowledge of surveying and astronomical position determination. Proficiency in the languages of the travellers' accounts and native languages is considered almost indispensable. Furthermore, the compiler should independently examine explorers' accounts rather than relying solely on existing maps, as draughtsmen or explorers often fail to include all available information.

While latitudes from travellers' observations are generally reliable, longitudes are often accepted with caution. For example, Captain Speke placed the capital of Uganda at longitude 32° 44´ E., whereas more trustworthy observations determined the true longitude to be 32° 26´ E., an error of 18´. Similarly, a map illustrating Livingstone's "Last Journals" incorrectly showed the Luapula issuing from the Bangweulu in the north-west; native accounts of those who carried the explorer's remains to the coast indicated it actually leaves the lake on the south.

## Regional Documentation: Africa

Documentation of African regions often relies on the journals of European and local travellers. In Darfur, the first known European visitor was William George Browne, who stayed at Kobbe from 1793 to 1795. Other significant accounts include those of Sheik Mahommed-el-Tounsi, who travelled through Darfur in 1803 in search of his father, Omar (translated into French in 1845), and Gustav Nachtigal, who spent several months there in 1873. The region is further documented through the journeys of Slatin, Gordon, and others. Slatin Pasha's *Fire and Sword in the Sudan* provides information on the country's history and the Mahdi's overthrow of Egyptian authority.

In the Gold Coast and Ashanti regions, general surveys are found in R.A. Freeman's *Travels in Ashanti and Jaman* (1898) and C.P. Lucas's *Historical Geography of the British Colonies* (1900). Ethnological data is provided by Col. A.B. Ellis in *The Tshi-speaking Peoples of the Gold Coast* (1887). Early valuable works include T.E. Bowdich's *A Mission from Cape Coast Castle to Ashantee* (1819) and J. Dupuis's *Journal of a Residence in Ashantee* (1824). History is documented by Col. A.B. Ellis and C.C. Reindorf, a native pastor of the Basel mission.

## Regional Documentation: North America

Documentation of North American states frequently combines geological surveys with administrative and social records. 

In Kentucky, physical features and natural resources are recorded in the *Reports of the Kentucky Geological Survey*, U.S. Census reports, and the *Biennial Reports of the Bureau of Agriculture, Labor and Statistics*. Early descriptions include Gilbert Imlay's 1797 *A Topographical Description of the Western Territory of North America*, which reprints John Filson's 1784 work on "Kentucke." Social life in the mountain districts is detailed by William H. Haney (1906). Administrative history is tracked through the Constitution of 1891, the *Official Manual for the Use of the Courts*, and the *General Statutes of Kentucky*.

In Idaho, physical and economic resources are discussed in works by J.L. Onderdonk and Israel C. Russell, as well as U.S. Geological Survey reports regarding the Snake River Plains and mining districts like Silver City and De Lamar. Administration and history are primarily sourced from H.H. Bancroft and W.B. Hepburn.

In Louisiana, documentation is divided by specialization: the U.S. Biological Survey covers fauna and flora, while the U.S. Department of Agriculture provides climate and crop data. River navigation and improvements, particularly for the Mississippi, are extensively documented in the *Annual Reports* of the Chief of Engineers, U.S. Army.

## Regional Documentation: Europe

In England, documentation often centers on the *Domesday Book*. Key scholarly works include those by R. Kelham, H. Ellis, and F.W. Maitland. The *Victoria County History* provides a translation of the Domesday text, maps, and introductions for each county. Specifically for Kent, authorities include W. Lambarde's *Perambulation of Kent* (1576) and E. Hasted's *History and Topographical Survey of Kent*.
