# CONCERNING THE PROVINCE OF TENDUC, AND THE DESCENDANTS

## Geography and Administration
Tenduc is a province located toward the east, characterized by numerous towns and villages. Its chief city, also called Tenduc, served as the seat of government for Prester John during his rule over the Tartars. The province is currently held under the authority of the Great Kaan. 

The region is situated on the route eastward from Tangut toward Shangtu and Chagannor. Various historical and geographical analyses suggest its location in the vicinity of the great northern bend of the Hwang-Ho (also referred to as the Caramoran), specifically in a district known to Chinese authorities as Thianté or Thianté-Kiun. Other theories identify the city of Tenduc as Kuku-Khotan (known in Chinese as Kwei-hwa Ch’eng or Tsing-chau), a significant mart and center of Lamaitic Buddhism. Alternative views suggest the city may be Tou Ch’eng (or Toto Ch’eng), located near the village of Ho-k’ou on the Yellow River.

## The Lineage of Prester John
The province is ruled by King George, who is of the lineage of Prester John and is specifically the sixth in descent from him. While King George holds the land under the Great Kaan, he does not possess the entirety of the lands once held by Prester John. A standing custom exists whereby the kings of Prester John's lineage always marry either daughters of the Great Kaan or other princesses from his family. 

The city of Tenduc is noted as the place where Aung Khan’s sovereignty was once seated and where he suffered his final defeat; his descendants are said to still reside there in a reduced state.

## Demographics and Social Structure
The rule of the province is held by Christians, though the population includes a significant number of worshippers of Mahommet and Idolaters. In the areas extending seven days' journey eastward toward the provinces of Cathay, the inhabitants are primarily Mahommetans, mixed with Nestorian Christians and Idolaters.

A distinct class of people known as the Argons (translated into French as *Guasmul*) resides in the province. The Argons are described as being sprung from two different races: the Idolaters of Tenduc and the worshippers of Mahommet. They are characterized as being handsomer and more capable than other natives, which allows them to attain positions of authority and succeed as capital merchants.

## Economy and Natural Resources
The inhabitants of Tenduc sustain themselves through tillage, the keeping of cattle, trade, and handicraft. The province is known for several specific resources and industries:

*   **Minerals:** The stone used to produce Azure is found here, extracted from veins in the earth and noted for its fine quality. Additionally, the province contains a high-quality silver mine located in a mountain at a place called Ydifu.
*   **Textiles:** There is a major manufacture of fine camlets of various colors made from camel's hair. In the eastern regions of the province, inhabitants weave silk stuffs and fine cloths of gold known as *Naques* and *Nasich*.
*   **Trade and Military:** The city of Sindachu is a center for various crafts that provide equipment for the Emperor's troops. The region is also well-stocked with both bird and beast game.

## Regional Nomenclature and History
The area is identified by some as the country of Gog and Magog. However, local designations refer to it as Ung and Mungul. These names derive from two races of people who inhabited the province prior to the migration of the Tartars; *Ung* was the title of the local people, while *Mungul* was a name occasionally applied to the Tartars.
