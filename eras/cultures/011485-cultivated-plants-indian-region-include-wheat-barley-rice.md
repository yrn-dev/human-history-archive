# Cultivated Plants of the Indian Region

The agriculture of the Indian region is characterized by a combination of harvests from both temperate and tropical zones. This diversity is largely driven by the climatic variations found across the region, particularly in relation to the tropic.

## Cereals and Grains
The cultivated plants of the Indian region include a wide variety of cereals. These include wheat, barley, rice, and maize. Additionally, various millets are grown, specifically *Sorghum*, *Penicillaria*, *Panicum*, and *Eleusine*. 

The cultivation of these crops is heavily dependent on the season and geography. North of the tropic, the winter cold allows for the cultivation of nearly all vegetables and cereals found in Europe. In this area, wheat is typically sown in November and reaped in early April. During the summer, the heat and rain create a tropical climate suitable for raising rice and other tropical cereals, which are generally sown in early July and reaped in September or October.

In contrast, the climate in southern India—as well as other Asian regions and islands with similar conditions—does not have a sufficient difference between the summer and winter half-years to allow for the proper cultivation of barley or wheat. While other cereals may occasionally be grown in these areas at all seasons through the use of artificial irrigation, agricultural operations are generally limited to the rainy months when the necessary water supply is available.

## Legumes, Spices, and Industrial Crops
Beyond cereals, the Indian region produces many pulses, including beans and peas. The region is also known for the cultivation of mustard and rape, as well as ginger and turmeric. Pungent crops such as pepper and capsicum are grown, along with several members of the *Cucurbitaceae* family.

The region produces several industrial and stimulant plants, including:
*   Cotton, indigo, and sugar.
*   Coffee and tea.
*   Tobacco, *Sesamum*, poppy, *Crotolaria*, and *Cannabis*.

## Fruits and Palms
The flora of the Indian region includes a diverse array of fruits. These include oranges and lemons of many varieties, as well as pomegranate, mango, figs, peaches, vines, and plantains. 

The most common palms found in the region are *Cocos*, *Phoenix*, and *Borassus*. These palms are utilized to supply toddy and cocoa-nut.

## Regional Comparisons
The cultivated plants of Arabia are noted to be very similar to those of northern India. Both regions grow wheat, barley, the common *Sorghum*, cotton, indigo, lemons, and dates. However, coffee in Arabia is restricted specifically to the slopes of the western hills.
