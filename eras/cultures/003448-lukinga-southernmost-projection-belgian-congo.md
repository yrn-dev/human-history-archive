# Lukinga and the Southernmost Belgian Congo

## Linguistic Classification
Lukinga is located in the southernmost projection of the Belgian Congo. Linguistically, it is categorized within the Bisa (or Wisa) family of Bantu speech. This specific family includes the languages of Bausi and Iramba, as well as the dialects of Ilala and Lubisa, which are situated between the Luangwa river to the east and south, and the Chambezi river and Lake Bangweulu to the north. The Bisa family may also encompass some of the languages found along the course of the Upper Luapula river.

Broadly, the domain of Bantu speech is divided into four great sections. The region encompassing the southern part of the Belgian Congo, extending to the Angola-Congo coast and Damaraland, is classified as the South-West group. This linguistic family is noted for a striking homogeneity compared to the languages found north of the Bantu borderland.

## The Belgian Congo: Political History
The Belgian Congo was a colony in Equatorial Africa that occupied the majority of the Congo river basin. Its origins trace back to the personal interests of King Leopold II in the commercial development and exploration of the region. In 1876, the King presided over the Brussels Geographical Conference, which led to the creation of the *Association internationale africaine* to explore the Congo and combat slave-drivers. 

By 1884-1885, the International Association of the Congo was recognized by global powers as an independent state, known as the *Etat Independent du Congo* (Congo Free State). While the union between Belgium and this new state was initially declared to be purely personal, the state's headquarters were in Brussels and its officials became almost exclusively Belgian. In 1889, King Leopold announced his intention to bequeath the state to Belgium, and the Belgian government acquired the right of annexation in 1890. However, immediate annexation was delayed due to the king's reluctance and a similar disinclination among the Belgian public.

Following reports of misgovernment that sparked international agitation for reform in the United States and Great Britain, public opinion in Belgium shifted. Consequently, the Congo Free State was annexed to Belgium in 1908, officially becoming the Belgian Congo.

## Administration and Infrastructure
The administrative capital of the Belgian Congo was Boma (properly *Mboma*), a port located on the north bank of the Congo river, approximately 60 miles from its mouth. Boma had served as a station for European traders since the 16th and 17th centuries and functioned as an entrepot for lower Congo commerce and a mart for slaves, with trade managed primarily by Dutch, British, French, and Portuguese firms. In 1884, the local natives granted a protectorate to the International Association of the Congo.

Regarding infrastructure and connectivity, various railway projects were proposed to establish communication between South Africa and the Mediterranean. One Belgian project involved constructing a railway from Stanleyville at the Congo bend, passing over the Kilo gold-fields to Mahagi on Lake Albert. Other proposed routes involved mixed Belgian-British cooperation, utilizing existing lines to Kabalo on the Congo. Additionally, a railway from Lobito Bay in Portuguese West Africa was being constructed in 1909 to connect with the main line near the Congo frontier.

## Colonial Honors and Demographics
To recognize service within the Congo State, King Leopold II instituted several specific orders: the Order of the African Star (1888), the Congo Star (1889), and the Royal Order of the Lion (1891).

Demographically, the presence of Belgian nationals in the region was relatively small; in 1904, there were 1,505 Belgians residing in the Congo State.
