# BOLE

Bole, derived from the Greek word *bolos* meaning "a clod of earth," is a clay-like substance characterized by colors of yellow, brown, or red. Chemically, it consists essentially of hydrous aluminium silicate containing varying amounts of iron.

## Physical and Chemical Properties
Bole is distinguished from ordinary clay by its lack of plasticity. When placed in water, it tends to drop to pieces rather than remain cohesive, a behavior similar to that of fuller's earth. 

## Medicinal Use and Tradition
Historically, bole was held in high medicinal repute. The most famous variety was the Lemnian Earth (*gae Laemnia*), sourced from the Isle of Lemnos in the Greek Archipelago. The extraction of this earth was conducted with significant ceremony only once per year. After being mixed with the blood of goats, the substance was formed into small balls or cakes. These were then stamped by priests, leading to the name *Terra sigillata*, which translates to "sealed earth."

## Geological Occurrence and Formation
Bole is found in several regions across the world, often associated with volcanic activity:

*   **British Isles:** In the west of Scotland and the north of Ireland, large quantities of bole appear as red partings between successive lava flows of the Tertiary volcanic series. In these areas, it is believed to have formed from the decomposition of kindred rocks and basalt via meteoric agencies during periods of volcanic repose. In Antrim, bole is found in association with bauxite, pisolitic iron-ore, and lithomarge.
*   **India:** Bole occurs between the great sheets of the Deccan traps.
*   **Italy:** A similar substance is found interbedded with some of the doleritic lavas of Mount Etna.

## Related Substances
Bole shares certain physical characteristics with other earthy materials:

*   **Fuller's Earth:** Like bole, fuller's earth is a clay-like substance that is not markedly plastic and falls to pieces in water. While bole is red, brown, or yellow, fuller's earth is most often greenish-grey, olive-green, or greenish, though it may bleach or turn brown upon weathering. Fuller's earth is specifically valued for its ability to clarify oil or absorb grease from cloth.
*   **Marl:** Unlike the aluminium silicates found in bole, marls are calcareous clays or mixtures of argillaceous matter and carbonate of lime (typically 25-75%). Marls are found in connection with limestone or chalk formations and burn to a distinctive sulphur-yellow colour.
*   **Brick Clays:** True clay substances used for bricks consist chiefly of hydrated aluminium silicates. Depending on the oxide of iron content (usually 2% to 10%), these clays burn to colors ranging from light buff to chocolate, including salmon or red.
