# BOLAS

Bolas (the plural of *bola* or ball) are weapons used for the chase and for war by South American Indians. These weapons consist of balls made of stone which are attached to the ends of a rope. The rope is constructed from braided or twisted hemp or hide.

## Types and Construction
According to the descriptions provided by Charles Darwin in his *Voyage of the Beagle*, there are two primary types of bolas:

*   **Two-ball bolas:** This is the simplest variety, used primarily for the purpose of catching ostriches. It consists of two round stones covered in leather and joined by a thin, plaited thong measuring approximately 8 feet in length.
*   **Three-ball bolas:** This variety differs from the simpler version by having three balls united by thongs to a common center.

Additionally, there are *bolas perdidas* (meaning "lost"), which are stones that are either attached to a very short thong or have no thong at all.

## Usage and Mechanics
The three-ball variety is utilized by the Gaucho, a native of Spanish descent. The user holds the smallest of the three balls in their hand and whirls the remaining two around their head. Once the user takes aim, the balls are sent revolving through the air, similar to chain shot. Upon striking an object, the balls wind around it, cross each other, and become firmly hitched.

## Geographic Distribution and Comparisons
Bolas have been employed for centuries across the forest regions of the Rio Grande and the South American pampas. F. Ratzel, in *History of Mankind*, suggests that the bolas are a form of lasso. A similar weapon is used by the Eskimos for the purpose of killing birds.
