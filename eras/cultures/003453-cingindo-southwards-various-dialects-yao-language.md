# Yao Languages

The Yao genus of languages is an extensive group of Bantu speech located in East Africa. Its geographical distribution stretches from the area just behind the coast of the Lindi settlements in German East Africa, where the dialect *Ki-mwera* is spoken, south-westward across the Ruvuma river.

## Geographical Distribution and Dialects
The Yao language domain extends to the north-east shores of Lake Nyasa, characterized by the dialect *Ki-kese*. From there, it reaches back to the valley of the Lujenda-Ruvuma, where *Cingindo* is spoken. Further southwards, various dialects of the Yao language are found in the region east of the Shire river, situated between the Shire highlands, Mt. Mlanje, and the south-east corner of Lake Nyasa.

Since the middle of the 19th century, the Yao language has expanded its territory to the south of Lake Nyasa. Despite this expansion, colonies of Nyanja-speaking people still remain within the Yao domain.

## Regional Linguistic Context
The Yao domain is bounded to the east by the Makua genus, a well-marked group of languages. This boundary is defined to the north by the range of the Yao language in the Ruvuma valley and by the separate *Ki-makonde* group. The Makua genus, which occupies much of Portuguese East Africa outside the Lake Nyasa watershed, is divided into several dialects, including *I-medo*, *I-lomwe*, *I-tugulu*, and *Anguru*. In the Angoji coast-region, there are unnamed dialects of Makua as well as strong colonies of Swahili-speaking people. The southern portion of the Makua domain is occupied by the *Ci-cuambo* of the Quelimane district.

## Ethnic and Linguistic Characteristics
In the region of British Central Africa, the population is linguistically categorized as belonging entirely and exclusively to the Bantu family. The native population is described as being of a black or chocolate-coloured Negro type. While the Bantu races in some areas show signs of Bushman or Hottentot intermixture, and legends of unmixed Bushman tribes exist in mountain districts like Mount Mlanje, the linguistic landscape remains Bantu.

The Bantu dialects found in the plateau country between the south end of Lake Tanganyika, the north end of Lake Nyasa, and the river Luapula are of an archaic type. Among these, *Kibemba* (or *Kiwemba*) and the *Subiya* of southern Barotseland are noted for being close to the original form of the Bantu mother-language.
