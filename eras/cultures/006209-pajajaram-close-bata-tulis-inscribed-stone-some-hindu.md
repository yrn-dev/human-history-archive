# Pajajaram

Pajajaram was a historical entity in Java, Dutch East Indies, whose princess once established her capital at the location now known as Buitenzorg.

## Buitenzorg and Bogor
Buitenzorg, referred to by the native population as Bogor, is a hill station situated in the residency of Batavia on the island of Java. The location is positioned at the foot of the Salak volcano, approximately 860 feet above sea level, and is characterized by a cool and healthy climate. 

The district of Buitenzorg occupies the northern slopes of a range of hills that separate it from Preanger. The region is noted for its fertile soil, which supports the cultivation of various crops, including rice, sugar-cane, coffee, tea, cinchona, pepper, cloves, and nutmegs. Until 1866, Buitenzorg served as an assistant residency; it currently forms the southern part of the residency of Batavia and covers an area of 1,447 square miles.

## Historical and Cultural Remains
In the vicinity of the former capital of the princess of Pajajaram, there are Hindu remains located at a site called Bata Tulis, which translates to "inscribed stone."

## Modern Administration and Institutions
Buitenzorg serves as the usual residence for the governor-general of the Dutch East Indies. The palace of the governor-general was originally founded in 1744 by Governor-General van Imhoff, though it required rebuilding after an earthquake destroyed it in 1834. Additionally, the town is the seat for the department of mines and the general secretary of the state railway.

The area is highly regarded as a health resort and is home to botanical gardens that are considered among the finest in the world. Established in 1817, these gardens were initially part of the park attached to the governor-general's palace. The collection was expanded under J.S. Teysmann, who became *hortulanus* in 1830. By 1868, the gardens were recognized as a government institution with a director. Between 1868 and 1880, the site was expanded to include a school of agriculture, a museum, and a culture garden. Subsequent additions to the complex include a herbarium, a library, and laboratories for pharmacological, chemical, and botanical research.
