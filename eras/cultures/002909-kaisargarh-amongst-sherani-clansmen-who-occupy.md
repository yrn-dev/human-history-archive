# Kaisargarh

Kaisargarh is a peak and a geological massif located in the northern region of Baluchistan. It is situated within the central range of the Suliman hills, which serves as the dominant geographical feature of the area. The axis of this range lies slightly east of the 70° E. meridian and is geologically composed of one or more great folds of the Cretaceous series.

## Geography and Topography
Amongst the Sherani clansmen who occupy the region, the oblong block or "massif" formed by a group of peaks amidst neighboring ridges is known as "Kaisargarh." Generally, on the frontier, this area is referred to as "Takht-i-Suliman" (Solomon's throne), a name derived from a celebrated shrine of that name located near its southern abutment.

The massif of the Takht is a high tableland reaching approximately 8,000 feet above sea level. It is bounded by steep, rugged, and high parallel ridges on its eastern and western edges. The western ridge reaches its highest point in the north at the peak of Kaisargarh, which stands at 11,300 feet. The eastern ridge culminates in the south in a detached headland or block at 11,070 feet, where the "zirat" or shrine is located.

The tableland is composed of a massive cap of coral limestone, which Griesbach estimated to be between 4,000 and 5,000 feet thick. The plateau is characterized by a sprinkling of juniper on higher slopes and a fairly thick growth of "edible" pine, known as chilghosa. The area was first surveyed and ascended in 1883.

## Hydrology and Geology
The tableland is rent at both ends by gorges that descend through stupendous precipices. To the north, these lead to the channel of the "Gat" or Draband, and to the south, they lead to the Dhana. These channels direct mountain streams from the western slopes of the massif across the axis of the mountains and through minor ridges to the plains of the Indus.

From the summit of the Kaisargarh peak, the landscape of northern Baluchistan is visible. To the west, toward Afghanistan, there are parallel lines of broken, jagged ridges consisting of Cretaceous series folds overlaid by shales and coarse sandstones. Immediately west of Kaisargarh is the Shingarh Mountain, which is a geological repetition of the Kaisargarh ridge, featuring crags of coral limestone and a summit black with pines.

To the east, the view reveals numerous minor ridges and folds created during the upheaval of the Suliman Mountains at the end of a great volcanic epoch. On the outer edge facing the Indus plains lies the Siwaliks, a higher and more rugged ridge of hills. The Baluch Siwaliks provide evidence of a geological past where gigantic mammals, similar to those that inhabited the forests below the Himalaya, once roamed.

## Regional Context and Resources
The Sheranis are identified as one of the chief frontier tribes in the borderland of Baluch tribes and Pathans, specifically within the context of the District of Dera Ismail Khan. 

Regarding mineral wealth, the Sherani hills are noted for the presence of petroleum of excellent quality. This is part of a broader, though generally inconsiderable, mineral distribution in Baluchistan, which includes sulphur in the Koh-i-Sultan volcanoes and coal in the Tertiary beds along the Harnai route to Quetta.
