# Mbundu

The Mbundu represent a general classification of a language group located in Central Angola. This group is part of the broader Bantu family of speech, which is characterized by a striking homogeneity compared to the linguistic diversity found in Africa north of the Bantu borderland.

## Linguistic Classification and Composition
The Mbundu group is closely allied with the Kongo language group. In the south, the Kongo dialects melt imperceptibly into the Angola language, and it is this combined association that is styled as Mbundu. This group encompasses several languages of Central Angola, specifically:
* Ki-mbundu
* Mbamba
* Ki-sama
* Songo
* U-mbangala

Within the wider classification of Bantu speech, the Mbundu group falls into the section of languages of the South-West, which extends from the Angola-Congo coast and Damaraland to the southern portion of the Belgian Congo.

## Geographical Boundaries
The territory of the Mbundu language group is defined by several natural and geographic markers:
* **North:** The river Loje serves, to some extent, as the frontier between the Mbundu tongues and the Kongo languages.
* **East:** The boundary is likely the Kwango river. Beyond this river, the Lunda languages begin.
* **South:** The boundary of the group is approximately the 11th degree of south latitude.

## Related Language Groups
The Mbundu group exists alongside several other distinct Bantu families in the region:
* **Oci-herero group:** This group is very distinct from Ki-mbundu speech, although connecting forms exist between them. It includes languages such as Umbundu of the Bihe highlands of south Angola, Nano of the Benguela coast, and Oci-mbo, Ku-anyama, and Herero.
* **Kiboko family:** Also known as Kibokwe or Kioko, this family of eastern Angola shows affinities to both the Mbundu (group No. 28) and the Oci-herero (group No. 29) groups, as well as the languages of the Upper Zambezi. This family includes the Lubale of northern Barotseland and the sources of the river Zambezi, and potentially the Gangela of south-western Angola.

## Academic Study and Documentation
The study of the Mbundu languages has been documented in various linguistic works. Specifically, Dr. Heli Chatelain has authored a *Grammar of Kimbundu* as well as a work titled *The Folklore of Angola*. These contributions exist within a larger body of Bantu research, including the comparative studies of Carl Meinhof on the original structure of the Bantu mother-speech and the works of Father J. Torrend and Dr. W. I. Bleek.
