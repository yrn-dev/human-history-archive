# BUCKTHORN

Buckthorn, known botanically as *Rhamnus cathartica*, is a much-branched shrub belonging to the natural order Rhamnaceae. The plant can reach a height of 10 feet and is characterized by blackish bark and spinous branchlets. Its leaves are ovate and sharply serrated, measuring between 1 and 2 inches in length, and are arranged several together at the ends of the shoots.

## Botanical Characteristics
The flowers of *Rhamnus cathartica* are small, green, and regular, with their parts arranged in fours. These plants are dioecious, meaning the male and female flowers are borne on different plants. The resulting fruit is globose, black, and succulent, containing four stones.

## Distribution and Habitat
Buckthorn is native to Europe, north Africa, and north Asia. In England, it occurs primarily in thickets and woods, chiefly on chalk. While it is found in England, it is rare in Ireland and does not occur wild in Scotland. Additionally, the plant has become naturalized in certain parts of eastern North America.

## Practical Uses
The plant provides materials for several practical applications. The bark of the buckthorn is used to produce a yellow dye, and the fruit possesses strong purgative properties.

## Related Species and the Rhamnaceae Order
Several other species are allied to *Rhamnus cathartica* within the Rhamnaceae order:

*   ***Rhamnus Frangula***: Also common in England and known as black alder or berry-bearing. It is distinguished from *Rhamnus cathartica* by its bisexual flowers (with parts in fives), its non-serrated leaves, and the absence of spiny branchlets. Its fruits are purgative and produce a green dye when they are unripe. The wood of this species is soft and porous, referred to as black dogwood, and is utilized for gunpowder.
*   ***Zizyphus Lotus***: A bush native to south Europe. Its fruits are as large as sloes and contain a mealy substance used to create a fermented drink and bread. In ancient times, these fruits were a significant food source for the poor, leading to the term "lotophagi" or lotus-eaters.
*   **Other *Rhamnus* species**: Dyes are obtained from the bark and fruits of *R. infectoria*, *R. tinctoria*, and *R. davurica*. The latter two species are used to produce the China green of commerce.
*   ***Rhamnus Alaternus***: A Mediterranean species with several varieties grown in shrubberies.

## Other Buckthorns
There are several other plants referred to as buckthorns that differ botanically:

*   **Sea-buckthorn**: Known botanically as *Hippophae rhamnoides*, this is a willow-like shrub ranging from 1 to 8 feet in height. It features narrow leaves that are silvery on the underside and globose orange-yellow fruits measuring one-third of an inch in diameter. It is not common, but occurs on sandy seashores from Sussex to Kent and York.
*   **American Buckthorns**: These include *Rhamnus Caroliniana* (the alder-buckthorn) and *Rhamnus purshiana* (also called *Cascara sagrada*), which is found on the Pacific coast and produces cascara bark.
*   **Southern Buckthorn**: This is the popular name for *Bumelia lycioides* (or *lanuginosa*).
