# Buffaloes

Buffaloes are heavily built oxen characterized by broad muzzles, massive angulated horns, large ears, and long, tufted tails. Their skin is sparsely haired, and they possess 13 pairs of ribs, a feature that makes them resemble typical oxen.

## The Asiatic Buffalo
The typical Indian buffalo, *Bos (Bubalus) bubalis*, is primarily restricted to Ceylon and India, though local races are likely present in the Islands and the Malay Peninsula. This species has been introduced to other regions, including Italy, Egypt, and Asia Minor. 

Asiatic buffaloes are distinguished from African species by several physical markers:
*   **Head and Horns:** They possess a more narrow and elongated head, a wider separation of the horns, and larger horn size.
*   **Ears:** Their ears are less thickly fringed than those of African species.
*   **Hair:** The hair on the fore-part of the back is directed forwards.

Within the Indian buffalo species, specific local races exist in Assam. One race, *B. bubalis macrocercus*, is identified by immense horns that direct mainly outwards rather than curving in a circular upward form. Another race, *B. bubalis fulvus*, is distinguished by its tawny hair and hide, as opposed to the typical black.

## Behavior and Ecology of the Indian Buffalo
In the wild, Indian buffaloes inhabit grass-jungles near swamps where the grass can exceed 20 feet in height. In these environments, they create covered pathways that provide complete concealment. They are fond of wallowing in mud in marshes during the day. 

These animals are not shy and are known to cause significant damage to crops. They typically form herds of fifty or more individuals. During the autumn rutting season, a single male is followed by several females, forming smaller herds. The gestation period for the female is ten months, resulting in the birth of one or two calves.

The bull is described as being capable of overthrowing an elephant and is generally a match for the tiger. Tigers typically avoid combat with the buffalo unless driven by hunger.

## Domestication and Use
The buffalo is described as an aberrant species of cattle that has been kept in a state of domestication in Egypt and India from time immemorial. From Egypt, they were introduced into southern Europe. In India, drivers of tame buffalo herds are able to enter jungles frequented by tigers, as the cattle's massive horns can quickly dispatch any tiger they encounter. Buffalo fights, as well as fights between buffaloes and tigers, have been recognized.

In Bengal, it is noted that the domesticated breed may occasionally be refreshed through the introduction of wild blood.

## Comparison with African Buffaloes
The African buffalo, *Bos (Bubalus) caffer*, differs significantly from the Asiatic variety. A primary distinction is that all African buffaloes have the hair on their backs directed backwards.

The Cape buffalo's horns do not reach excessive lengths, but in old bulls, the base becomes so thickened and expanded that it forms a helmet-like mass protecting the forehead. Other African variations include:
*   **Eastern Africa (*B. caffer aequinoctialis*):** These buffaloes are brown rather than black and have smaller horns that do not meet in the middle line.
*   **West Africa (*B. nanus*):** Known as the red dwarf buffalo, this animal is approximately two-thirds the size of the southern variety and possesses relatively small, flattened horns that curve upward.

Unlike the Indian buffalo, the African species has never been domesticated, and no attempts appear to have been made to reduce it to service. Like its Indian ally, it is fond of water and mud; the latter protects the animal from gadflies once it hardens in the sun. African buffaloes also maintain a relationship with red-beaked rhinoceros birds (*Buphaga erythrorhynca*), which feed on parasitic ticks on the buffalo's hide and often warn the animal of a hunter's proximity.
