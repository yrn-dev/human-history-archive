# Circular Measure and Circular Forms

## Ballistic Applications of Circular Measure
In the study of ballistics, the circular measure, denoted as $i$ radians, is utilized to track the direction of motion of a shot. When the direction of motion changes through $\Delta i$ radians during a small finite interval of time ($\Delta t$), and $v$ represents the mean velocity during that interval, the change in inclination in degrees ($\Delta \delta$) is calculated using the formula $\Delta \delta = 180/\pi \Delta i = 180g/\pi \Delta t/v$.

For certain purposes, it is preferable to retain the circular measure $i$ radians because, when $i$ is small—as is the case in direct fire—it is undistinguishable from $\tan i$ and $\sin i$. These calculations are part of a broader set of functions, including $T, S, D, I,$ and $A$ (the altitude function), which are presented numerically in abridged ballistic tables. In these tables, velocity serves as the argument and proceeds by increments of 10 f/s.

## Circular Design in Incubation
Circular forms have been applied to the design of poultry incubators, such as the model invented by F. Schröder, manager of the British National Poultry Company. This incubator featured a circular form with four egg drawers, each occupying a quadrant of a circle and meeting at the center. 

The design incorporated several circular elements for ventilation and temperature control:
* **Ventilation:** A vertical chimney passed upwards from the center, opening from the inner corners of the egg drawers to act as a ventilator.
* **Humidity:** A tank of cold water sat beneath the drawers; vapor from this water diffused through perforated zinc floors to maintain humidity.
* **Heating:** A circular tank containing warm water was positioned above the egg drawers. The floor of this tank served as the roof for the drawers, while its own roof formed the floor of an upper circular chamber.
* **Rearing:** The upper circular chamber, containing sand to retain heat and provide a bed, was used to dry and rear hatched chicks.

The water in the warm-water tank was heated by an external boiler. Temperature was measured by a thermometer located in the warm-water tank, though this was later considered an incorrect position because the temperature of the tank could differ by several degrees from that of the egg drawers.

## Circular Forms in Art and Architecture
Circular patterns and structures appear across various historical and cultural contexts:

**Japanese Pottery:** In the Yamato tombs, pottery known as *iwaibe* (sacred utensils) was used for sepulture. These vessels were turned on a wheel and occasionally featured tazzas or other utensils pierced in circular, quadrilateral, or triangular patterns.

**Roman Architecture:** In the town of Calleva, archaeological findings include a circular temple situated between the south gate and the forum, alongside two smaller square temples.

**Ancient Babylonian Records:** Cadastral surveys for taxation in Babylonia, dating back to the age of Sargon of Akkad (3800 B.C.), utilized clay tablets that were circular in shape. Tablets from 2100 or 2300 B.C. include depictions of lower Babylonia encircled by the "salt water river," Oceanus.

## Circular Tools and Projections
The circular form is integral to various technical tools and mapping methods:

**Industrial Tools:** In modern carpentry, logs are converted into planks at saw-mills using machinery powered by gas, steam, or electricity to drive circular saws.

**Cartography:** The globular projection is a conventional representation of a hemisphere used for simplicity in drawing. In this projection, the intersection of the equator and central meridian (two equal straight lines at right angles) serves as the center of a circular boundary. The meridians and parallels are represented as arcs of circles. This projection is considered effective for conveying the general position and shape of major land masses, surpassing the stereographic projection commonly used in atlases.
