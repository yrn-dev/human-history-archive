# Jungermanniaceae Anacrogynae

The Jungermanniaceae Anacrogynae represent a primitive group of liverworts characterized by a specific organization of their sexual generation. In this group, the formation of archegonia does not utilize the apex of the branch, meaning the branch continues to grow after the archegonia are formed. Consequently, the archegonia and the subsequent sporogonia are positioned on the dorsal surface of the leafy plant or thallus.

## Diversity of Form in Britain
The range of form within the sexual plant of this group is illustrated by nine genera found in Britain. These include various thalloid and transitional forms:

*   **Pellia**: A thalloid form.
*   **Sphaerocarpus**: One of the simplest liverworts, occurring rarely in stubble fields. Its small thallus features antheridia and archegonia on the upper surface of distinct individuals, each surrounded by a tubular involucre. The sporogonium possesses a small foot, though the sterile cells among the spores do not develop into elaters. This genus lacks a provision for the opening of the capsule.
*   **Aneura**: In this genus, the thallus may exhibit a division of labour between assimilating, stem-like, and root-like branches. Sexual organs are located on short lateral branches.
*   **Metzgeria**: A relative of *Aneura* found on tree trunks and rocks. It possesses a narrow thallus where small sexual branches emerge from the lower surface of the midrib.
*   **Blasia pusilla**: Commonly found by streams and ditches, this species serves as a transition to foliose types. Its thallus features thin marginal lobes of limited growth, which are comparable to the leaves of other anacrogynous forms. The ventral surface contains concave scales and additional flat scales.

## Comparative Genera and Specialized Structures
Other genera within the Anacrogynous Jungermanniaceae exhibit distinct morphological traits:

*   **Riella**: Unlike all other liverworts in appearance, these plants grow rooted in mud in shallow water (none are British). They feature a usually erect thallus with two rows of large scales below and a broad wing-like outgrowth from the dorsal surface. Like *Sphaerocarpus*, the capsule of *Riella* has no provision for opening, and its sterile cells do not develop into elaters.
*   **Pallavicinia**: This genus and its relatives are characterized by broad wings made of one layer of cells and a definite midrib. The midrib contains a special water-conducting strand composed of elongated lignified cells with pitted walls.

In the genera *Aneura* and *Metzgeria*, the elaters are attached to a sterile group of cells that project into the upper end of the capsule; upon dehiscence, these elaters remain connected to the tips of the valves.

## Distinction from Acrogynous Jungermanniaceae
The Anacrogynous group is distinguished from the Acrogynous Jungermanniaceae by the placement of their sexual organs and the nature of their growth. While Anacrogynous forms allow the branch to continue growing after archegonia formation, in Acrogynous Jungermanniaceae, the archegonia occupy the ends of the branches or the main shoot, terminating the growth of the branch. 

Furthermore, while Anacrogynous forms include thalloid varieties, the Acrogynous group is throughout foliose. In the Acrogynous group, the capsule typically opens by splitting into four halves, and the antheridia are generally long-stalked and globular.
