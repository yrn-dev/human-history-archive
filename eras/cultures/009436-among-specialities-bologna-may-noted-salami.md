# Bologna

Bologna is a city and archiepiscopal see located in Emilia, Italy. Serving as the capital of the province of Bologna and the headquarters of the VI. army corps, the city is situated at the base of the Apennines on the edge of the Emilia plain, 180 ft. above sea-level. As of 1901, the town population was 102,122, with a commune population of 153,501.

## Urban Layout and Architecture
The city's origins are visible in its rectangular Roman center, which is oriented on the points of the compass with streets arranged at right angles. The outer city, which received fortifications in 1206, features streets that radiate from the outskirts of the Roman center rather than from its middle. While no Roman buildings remain above ground, several of the oldest churches, such as SS. Vitale ed Agricola, S. Giovanni in Monte, and S. Stefano, lie outside the Roman limits. S. Stefano is a complex of at least seven buildings; its earliest, the former cathedral of SS. Pietro e Paolo, was built around the middle of the 4th century using Roman debris. S. Sepolcro, a circular church from the 6th or 7th centuries, features brick ornamentation.

The city is characterized by arcaded streets, a feature maintained in modern additions and restorations of medieval buildings. Notable structures include:
* **The Leaning Towers:** The Torre Asinelli (1109) stands 320 ft. high and is 4 ft. out of the perpendicular. The Torre Garisenda (1110) is an unfinished tower 163 ft. high and 10 ft. out of the perpendicular.
* **Churches:** S. Petronio, the city's largest church and dedicated to the patron saint of Bologna, began construction in 1390 in the Gothic style. The church of S. Domenico contains the body of the saint who died there in 1221. The present cathedral, S. Pietro, was erected in 910 and is now largely baroque.
* **Palaces and Views:** The town contains numerous fine private palaces dating from the 13th century onwards. A view of the city can be seen from the Madonna di S. Luca, located 938 ft. high to the south-west.

## Education and Culture
The University of Bologna, founded in the 11th century, gained a European reputation for jurisprudence under the glossators, including Irnerius and Pepo (the first known teacher of Roman law at Bologna, circa 1076). Between the 12th and 15th centuries, student numbers ranged from three to five thousand, reportedly reaching nearly ten thousand in 1262, including Petrarch and Dante. Anatomy was taught there in the 14th century.

The university was an autonomous corporation without a fixed residence until 1520, when law professors were given apartments near the church of S. Petronio. In 1562, Cardinal Carlo Borromeo constructed a university building by order of Pius IV. This building has served as the communal library since 1838. The university later moved to the 16th-century Palazzo Poggi in 1803. After a decline in students between 1815 and 1848 due to government political persecutions, enrollment rose to 355 in 1859. It currently has four faculties and approximately 1,700 students, and has occasionally numbered women among its professors.

## Arts and Museums
The Museo Civico is one of Italy's most important museums, housing collections of antiquities from Bologna and the surrounding area. Its picture gallery features works by the Caracci, Guercino, Domenichino, and Guido Reni (the 16th and 17th-century Bolognese eclectics). Merit is also found in 15th-century works by Lorenzo Costa of Ferrara (1460-1535) and Francesco Francia (1450-1517). The collection's greatest treasure is Raphael's *S. Cecilia*, painted around 1515 for the church of S. Giovanni in Monte.

## Economy and Infrastructure
Bologna is a significant railway center, mirroring the role of the ancient Bononia as a meeting-point for roads. The main line from Milan divides here: one branch follows the ancient Via Aemilia from Piacenza toward Brindisi, Ancona, and Rimini; the other passes through the Apennines to Rome and Florence. Additional lines connect to Padua, Ferrara, Budrio, Portomaggiore, and S. Felice sul Panaro. Steam tramways provide transport to Malalbergo, Pieve di Cento, and Vignola.

The city is known for specific local specialities, including liqueurs, *tortellini* (a type of macaroni), and *salami* or *mortadella* (Bologna sausage).

## Notable Figures
Illustrious natives include Prospero Lambertini (Pope Benedict XIV) and Luigi Galvani (1737-1798), who discovered galvanism. Other figures associated with the city include the 17th-century anatomist Marcello Malpighi and the student Tasso, who was tried in 1564 for writing a satirical poem.
