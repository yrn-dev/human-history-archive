# Society of Antiquaries

The Society of Antiquaries and its associated regional branches are organizations dedicated to the study of antiquities, archaeology, and historical records. These societies produce various publications and maintain fellowships for naturalists, scholars, and historians.

## The London Society of Antiquaries
The London Society of Antiquaries is an institution to which scholars and naturalists have been elected as fellows. For example, Henry Baker (1698–1774), an English naturalist and founder of the Society of Arts, was elected a fellow of the Society of Antiquaries in 1740. 

The society is associated with the publication of the *Monumenta Vetusta*, a work that provides examples and illustrations of early costume. Other kindred works used for the study of early costume include *Archaeologia* and the journals of various Archaeological Societies. Additionally, the *Proceedings of the Society of Antiquaries* (specifically volume xviii) have been utilized as a source for information regarding villages in Roman Britain.

## The Society of Antiquaries of Scotland
The Society of Antiquaries of Scotland is a distinct organization with its own membership and publications. Its records and proceedings are significant sources for the study of Scottish excavations and prehistoric sites. Specifically, volumes xx through xl of the *Proceedings of the Society of Antiquaries of Scotland* contain data on Scottish excavations, including the work of J. Macdonald on Bar Hill. Furthermore, volumes 36, 37, and 38 (published between 1901 and 1903) include Dr. T. H. Bryce's records of explorations concerning the cairns of Arran and Bute.

The society has included notable figures as members. Dr. John Yule (d. 1827), a botanical tract author and *savant*, was one of the society's earliest corresponding members. In 1882, Yule (a different individual from the earlier Dr. John Yule) was named an Honorary Fellow of the Society of Antiquaries of Scotland, filling a vacancy created by the deaths of Dean Stanley and Thomas Carlyle.

## Other Antiquarian Societies
Beyond the primary societies in London and Scotland, other organizations exist to preserve and study the past. In Denmark, there is a Society of Northern Antiquaries, as well as a Museum of Northern Antiquities.

Various other archaeological and antiquarian records contribute to the broader field of study, including the *Cumberland Archaeological Society Transactions*, which contains reports of excavations by Professor F. Haverfield (1894–1904) and work by R.S. Ferguson on Hardknott.
