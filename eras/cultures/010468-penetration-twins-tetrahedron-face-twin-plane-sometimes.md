# Boracite

Boracite is a mineral noted for its optical anomalies. It was first observed in 1787 in the salt deposits at Luneburg in Hanover, where small crystals with sharply defined faces are found embedded in anhydrite and gypsum.

## Physical and Optical Properties
Boracite crystals vary in appearance from transparent to translucent and possess a vitreous lustre. While they are typically white or colourless, they are frequently tinged with green, yellow, or grey. The mineral has a specific gravity of 3.0 and a hardness of 7 on the Mohs scale.

A significant characteristic of boracite is its pyroelectricity, first observed by R.J. Hauy in 1791. When a cubic crystal of boracite is heated, it becomes electrified; four of its corners become positively electrified, while the four opposite corners become negatively electrified. In crystals where the rhombic dodecahedron predominates, the larger and bright tetrahedral faces (denoted as _s'_) are located at the antilogous poles, and the smaller, dull tetrahedral faces (_s_) are situated at the analogous poles, which become positively electrified upon heating.

## Crystallography and Form
The symmetry of boracite is the same as that of tetrahedrite and blende, appearing as cubic with inclined hemihedrism. The habit of the crystals varies depending on which of the following forms predominates:
* The cube
* The tetrahedron
* The rhombic dodecahedron

In some instances, penetration twins are observed, utilizing a tetrahedron face as the twin-plane.
