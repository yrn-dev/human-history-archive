# Marwats and the Pathan Races of the Bannu District

The Marwats are a Pathan race that inhabits the lower and more sandy portions of the Bannu valley. They are one of the principal tribes residing within the district of Bannu, located north of the Indus.

## Geography of the Bannu District
The district of Bannu consists of the Marwat and Bannu tahsils following the constitution of the North-West Frontier Province in 1901. It covers an area of 1,680 square miles. The valley of Bannu proper is shaped as an irregular oval, measuring approximately 60 miles from north to south and 40 miles from east to west, extending to the foot of the frontier hills.

The region is drained by the Indus and other streams, including the Gambila and the Kurram, the latter of which flows into the Indus. The Indus is navigable for native boats throughout its 76-mile course within the district, though it is not bridged anywhere in this area. Economic activity in the district includes the export of wheat and the quarrying of alum and salt, the latter of which is conducted on government account at Kalabagh.

## Tribal Composition and Population
As of 1901, the population of the Bannu district was 231,485, with the great majority being Mahommedans. The district is inhabited by several primary groups:
*   **Marwats:** A Pathan race located in the sandy, lower portions of the valley.
*   **Waziri Pathans:** Recent immigrants from the hills who are generally regarded as good cultivators and peaceable.
*   **Bannuchis:** Described as a mongrel Afghan tribe characterized by mean vices and bad physique.

On the border, the chief frontier tribes include the Dawaris, Battannis, and Waziris.

## Regional History and Governance
The inhabitants of the Bannu district have a history of stubborn resistance against their Sikh and Afghan predecessors. Following the annexation of the Punjab, the valley was administered by Herbert Edwardes. His thorough administration transformed the valley into a source of strength during the Mutiny. While the current inhabitants of the valley are peaceful, the area remains subject to incursions from the neighboring hills and the Waziri tribes of the Tochi valley.

## Broader Pathan Context
The Pathan element is predominant in the borderlands of the North-West Frontier Province. In the neighboring District of Dera Ismail Khan, the population is chiefly Baluch and Pathan Mahommedans. In the District of Bajour, the area is peopled by Pathan races of Afghan origin, almost exclusively Tarkani (Tarkalanri) Pathans—including the Ismailzai, Isazai, and Mamunds—along with some Utmauzais and Mohmands.

Other notable Pathan groups in the region include the Khattak tribe, who inhabit the eastern and south-eastern portions of Kohat and the south-eastern portion of the Peshawar district. The Khattaks are noted as excellent soldiers and loyal subjects of the British government.

Additionally, the Durani (or Durrani) are the dominant race of Afghans. The term "Bar Durani" is sometimes used to describe independent Pathan tribes inhabiting the Salt Range, the Indus valley, the range of Suliman, and the hill districts south of the Hindu Kush. This grouping includes the Shinwaris, Orakzais, Afridis, Mohmands, Tarkanis, Utman Khel, and Yusafzai, as well as the Pathan tribes of Bangash, Khattak, and the plains of Peshawar.

## Comparison with Baluch Races
The Pathan is contrasted with the Baluch race, with the geographical boundary between the two in the hills nearly corresponding to the northern limit of the Dera Ghazi Khan district. In terms of character, the Baluch is described as less fanatical, less bloodthirsty, less treacherous, and easier to control than the Pathan, due to a lack of blind allegiance to priests and a different tribal organization. Physically, the Baluch frame is more spare, wiry, and shorter than that of the Pathan.
