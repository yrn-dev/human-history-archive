# Celtiberians

## Early Geography and Tribal Composition
From the 5th century B.C., the earliest records regarding the geography of Spain describe the region as being occupied by a collection of tribes. These were primarily distinguished as Celts, Celtiberi, and Iberi. These groups lacked cohesion and were frequently engaged in warfare with one another, unless they were temporarily united to oppose a foreign enemy. The tribes were in a state of constant movement, with the ruder groups being pushed northwards by the progress of Mediterranean civilization.

## Language and Literacy
In the south, specifically in Baetica, the tribes possessed a literature, poems of ancient date, and written laws according to the accounts of Strabo and Trogus, though none of these works have survived. Evidence of ancient communication remains in the form of marks on pottery, legends on coins, inscriptions, and megalithic monuments. These records utilize alphabets that differ slightly and are associated with six geographical districts.

While these alphabets still await an interpreter, they indicate that a general language was once spoken throughout the whole of Spain and extended for a short distance onto the northern slope of the Pyrenees. The characters of these letters are clearly of Levant origin. However, the specific connections between these alphabets and the Basque language remain undetermined.

## Relationship with the Basques
There has been significant scholarly interest in the connection between the Basques and the ancient peoples of Spain. Classical scholars after the Renaissance noted that certain ancient toponyms transcribed by foreign Greek and Latin writers—who were ignorant of the local language—bear a strong resemblance to actual place-names in Basque. Examples include Iliberis (Iriberry) and Iluro (Oloron). W. von Humboldt first brought these observations to European attention in 1821.

While a majority of students have accepted the theory that a people speaking a language akin to Basque were extended throughout Spain, and possibly into Sardinia and Sicily, some competent Basque scholars deny this. Consequently, it cannot be said to be conclusively proved that there is a certain connection between the Basques and either the Iberians or the Celtiberians, whether in terms of language or race.

## Character and Military Traits
The Celtiberians, along with the Iberians, were noted by the Romans for possessing specific physical and mental attributes. Specifically, they were recognized for their power of endurance and tenacity. These same traits are observed in the Basque people, who are described as splendid marchers in their capacity as soldiers.
