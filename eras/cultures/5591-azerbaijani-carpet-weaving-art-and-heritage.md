# Azerbaijani Carpet Weaving: Art and Heritage

## Overview
Azerbaijani carpet weaving is not merely a craft but a profound visual language that encodes the history, spirituality, and social structures of the South Caucasus. For centuries, these textiles have served as a bridge between the nomadic traditions of the steppes and the sedentary urban cultures of the Silk Road. 

The art is characterized by a sophisticated use of geometric patterns, floral motifs, and a distinct color palette derived from natural dyes. Historically, Azerbaijani carpets are categorized into several primary schools: Guba, Shirvan, Baku, Ganja, Gazakh, Karabakh, and Tabriz. While some schools focused on the intricate, small-scale precision of city workshops, others emphasized the bold, monumental designs of tribal weaving.

A central point of historical discussion among scholars concerns the exact origins of specific motifs. While some historians argue that the "Buta" (almond shape) is an indigenous symbol of fire and eternity linked to Zoroastrianism, others suggest it evolved through cross-cultural exchanges with Persian and Indian textile traditions. Regardless of the origin, the Azerbaijani interpretation remains a cornerstone of the region's cultural identity.

## Key Facts
*   **Materiality:** Traditional carpets are woven from high-quality wool, though silk is frequently used in urban centers (like Baku) to achieve a higher luster and finer detail.
*   **The "Buta" Motif:** Perhaps the most iconic symbol, the Buta represents a stylized flame or a budding flower, symbolizing life, renewal, and the spiritual fire of the region.
*   **Regional Diversity:** 
    *   *Karabakh carpets* are known for their large floral compositions and vibrant reds.
    *   *Guba and Shirvan carpets* are characterized by tighter weaves and complex geometric patterns.
*   **Natural Dyes:** Historically, colors were extracted from nature: madder root for red, indigo for blue, and pomegranate peel for yellow.

## Did You Know?
*   **UNESCO Recognition:** In 2010, the art of Azerbaijani carpet weaving was officially recognized by UNESCO as a masterpiece of intangible heritage.
*   **The Carpet Museum:** Baku is home to the Azerbaijan National Carpet Museum, a building architecturally designed in the shape of a rolled-up carpet.
*   **Social Status:** In traditional Azerbaijani society, the quality and pattern of a carpet often indicated the social status and regional origin of the family that owned it.
