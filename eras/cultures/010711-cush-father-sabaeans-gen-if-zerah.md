# Cush

## Identification and Lineage
In historical and biblical contexts, the term "Cushite" is used to designate individuals from specific regions, most probably Arabians or Ethiopians. According to Genesis x. 7, Cush is identified as the "father" of the Sabaeans. Other archival records, such as those found in Genesis x. 6, also reference Cush.

## Military Conflict with King Asa
Historical accounts detail a significant military expedition led by Zerah, identified as the king of Ethiopia, against Asa, the king of Jerusalem. Asa is described as a ruler of excellent character who maintained a strong regard for God and focused his governance on the observation of laws and the purification of his kingdom from wickedness.

When Zerah launched his campaign, he commanded a massive army consisting of:
* 900,000 footmen
* 100,000 horsemen
* 300 chariots

The Cushite forces advanced as far as Mareshah, a city belonging to the tribe of Judah. King Asa met the invading army in a valley called Zephathah. Facing a vastly superior force, Asa besought God for victory, stating that he depended entirely on divine assistance to make the weaker party superior to the more numerous enemy.

## Scholarly Interpretations of Zerah
There is scholarly debate regarding the identity of Zerah. Some perspectives suggest that if Zerah is understood as a Sabaean or Ethiopian prince rather than a king, certain difficulties in the narrative are removed, as there is no known king Zerah of Ethiopia during that specific period. 

Furthermore, some analyses suggest that the identification of Zerah with Osorkon I or II is unlikely given the weakness of Asa. Instead, it is proposed that the invaders may have been "desert hordes" who frequently troubled Judah. If the battle took place at Mareshah, it is considered probable that these invaders were allied with Philistine towns, a situation similar to those encountered during the reigns of Jehoram and Ahaz.
