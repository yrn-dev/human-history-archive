# Rang and Ferdinand Denis, Paris, 1837

The publication by Sander Rang and Ferdinand Denis in Paris, 1837, titled *La Fondation de la regence d'Alger, histoire des Barberousse, chronique arabe du XVIème siecle*, is noted as a source providing a curious Moslem version of the story of the Barbarossas.

## Historical Context and Use
This work serves as a corrective or alternative perspective to other accounts of the Barbarossas, such as those found in Joseph von Hammer-Purgstall's *The History of the Ottoman Empire* (which was translated into French by J. J. Hellert between 1835 and 1843). While Hammer-Purgstall provides accounts of the Barbarossas, the archive indicates that his work requires correction by other authorities, including the 1837 publication by Rang and Denis.

Other related historical evidence regarding the region and the figures involved includes:
*   The *Histoire d'Alger* (1887) by H. D. de Grammont, who also contributed a paper to the *Revue Africaine* (No. 171) discussing the origin of the name.
*   Works by Ad. Jurien de la Graviere, specifically *Les Corsaires barbaresques et la marine de Soliman le Grand* (1887) and *Doria et Barberousse* (1886), which describe their campaigns using technical knowledge.
*   *The History of the Maritime Wars of the Turks* by Hajji Khalifa (translated by J. Mitchell in 1831), which is said to be based on evidence collected by order of the sultan Suleiman.
