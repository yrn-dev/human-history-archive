# Buchu and Related Botanical Species

## Buchu (Barosma)
Buchu, also known as buka leaves, is produced by several shrubby plants of the genus *Barosma* (natural order Rutaceae) native to the Cape of Good Hope. There are three primary species that yield buchu:

*   ***B. crenulata***: The principal species, characterized by oblong-ovate leaves with a smooth leathery texture. These leaves range from one to one and a half inches in length and feature crenulate or serrulate margins. Conspicuous oil-glands are located on the underside and the margins of the leaves.
*   ***B. serratifolia***: This species possesses linear-lanceolate leaves that are sharply serrulate.
*   ***B. betulina***: This species has cuneate-obovate leaves with denticulate margins.

In commerce, these leaves are a pale yellow-green color, possessing a slightly astringent bitter taste and a peculiar aromatic odour. The leaves contain a bitter principle and a dark yellow volatile oil. Within the oil-glands, a liquid hydro-carbon serves as the solvent for camphor, which deposits in a form of camphor when exposed to air.

The leaves of *Empleurum serratulum* are sometimes used as an adulterant or substitute for buchu; however, because they lack glands, they are considered a worthless substitute.

## Medicinal and Commercial Use of Buchu
The British Pharmacopoeia lists a tincture of buchu (administered in doses of a drachm) and an infusion (administered in doses of an ounce). The infusion is of very slight value as it contains very little of the volatile oil. While buchu was once valued for treating diseases of the urinary tract, its use has become practically obsolete following the arrival of modern synthetic products.
