# Camelidae

The Camelidae is a family of animals that includes several species found in South America, which share the structure and habits of the African camel. However, the South American members of this family are smaller, lack a hump, and inhabit steppes and mountainous regions rather than level sandy areas.

## South American Species
The Camelidae of the Andes and Patagonia are adapted to arid and semi-arid plateaus, possessing the ability to survive for long periods without food or drink. This group consists of four primary animals:

*   **Guanaco (_Auchenia huanaco_):** Considered the original type, the guanaco is the largest of the four species. It has the widest range, extending from Peru to Tierra del Fuego. Found only in a wild state, it is hunted for its skin, which is used to create robes or attractive rugs.
*   **Vicuna (_A. vicugna_):** Like the guanaco, the vicuna exists only in the wild. It is celebrated for its wool, which is woven by natives into other wearing apparel and costly, beautiful blanket cloaks known as *ponchos*.
*   **Llama (_A. llama_):** Domesticated long before the discovery of America, the llama serves as a pack animal in Peru and Bolivia. Its coarse wool is utilized by natives to make garments.
*   **Alpaca (_A. pacos_):** Also domesticated, the alpaca is highly prized for its fine wool, which serves as a staple export from Bolivia. Despite its value, the animal is difficult to rear, and the production of its wool cannot be largely increased.

The rearing of alpacas and llamas is a recognized industry in the highlands of Bolivia. This industry is managed entirely by the Indians, who are the only ones who seem to understand the peculiarities and habits of these animals. Currently, the numbers of vicuna and guanaco are rapidly diminishing due to slaughter.

## Other Camelids and Observations
Beyond the South American species, the archive notes the existence of dromedaries from Egypt, as well as Bactrian camels. Ancient sources, including Xenophon, Pliny, and Ælian, affirmed a natural antipathy between the horse and the camel. However, this claim is derided by "the Orientals" and is disproved by daily experience.

## Religious References
In the sacred text of the Qur'an, specifically in Chapter 81 (At-Takwir), the abandonment of camels is described as one of the events associated with the "overthrowing." The text mentions a time "when the camels are left untended" or "when the she-camels, ten months with young, are left untended."
