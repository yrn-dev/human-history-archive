# Ural Mountains and Other Low-Altitude Ranges

The archive contains various records regarding geographical elevations across Asia and Africa, detailing specific mountain ranges and plateaus where average altitudes remain relatively low, specifically within the 2000 to 3000 ft. range.

## The Ural Mountains
The Ural Mountains are characterized by a relatively low average altitude, which does not exceed 2000 or 3000 ft. While the average is low, the highest summits of the range do not exceed 6000 ft. The range is not uniform in height; for example, one of its passes is as low as 1400 ft. The southern half of the Ural range is significant for containing the chief mining districts of Russia.

## Other Low-Altitude Mountain Groups
Beyond the Urals, other specific mountain groups exhibit similar altitude ranges:
*   **Kordofan:** In the north-west of Kordofan, the mountain groups of Katul and Kaja reach altitudes between 2000 and 3000 ft. Additionally, some of the Nuba hills in the south-east part of Kordofan exceed 3000 ft. in height.
*   **Central Asia:** In the central depression of Asia—a region that was once a vast inland sea—the level between the Sarikol range and the mountains enclosing the sources of the Hwang-ho probably never exceeds 2000 ft. above sea level.

## Comparative Continental Elevations
The archive provides broader context for these low-altitude features by contrasting them with higher elevations and plateaus:

### Asia
Asia is divided into sections with varying altitudes. A northern section maintains an average altitude of less than 5000 ft. A central section of depression, where drainage is lost in swamps or *hamuns*, has an average level that probably does not exceed 2000 ft. above sea level. In contrast, the southern section includes highly elevated table-lands reaching 15,000 to 16,000 ft.

Other Asian features include the Great Khingan range, where the base of the western slope lies at 3000 to 3500 ft., while the crest rises to between 4800 and 6500 ft. The plains of Manchuria, to the east of this range, have an altitude of only 1500 to 2000 ft. In the Gobi desert, it appears likely that no part of the region is less than 2000 ft. above sea level, with routes across the desert between Kiakhta and the Chinese frontier remaining above 3000 ft.

The highest elevations in Asia are found in the Himalaya and its connected elevated areas. These mountains rise far above the line of perpetual snow, with peaks of 20,000 ft. being abundant and passes rarely falling below 15,000 or 16,000 ft.

### Siberia and India
Siberia features high plains ranging from 1500 to 2000 ft. high, which eventually drop to lowlands a few hundred feet above sea level. In British India, the great plain of northern India reaches a maximum height of about 1000 ft. The Indian peninsula is a table-land where the general level lies between a maximum of 3000 ft. and 1000 ft., although the ordinary altitude of its higher hills hardly exceeds 4000 ft.

### Africa
In certain African protectorates, the country consists largely of high plateau with an average altitude of 3500 ft. Low elevations are rare, though the Luangwa valley may be only about 900 ft. above sea level. High mountains in this region vary significantly, from Mount Chiradzulu at 5500 ft. to Mount Mlanje, which reaches 9683 ft.
