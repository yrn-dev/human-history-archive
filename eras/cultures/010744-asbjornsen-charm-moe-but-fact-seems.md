# Peter Christen Asbjornsen and Jorgen Engebretsen Moe

Peter Christen Asbjornsen (1812–1885) and Jorgen Engebretsen Moe (1813–1882) were collectors of Norwegian folklore. Their professional and personal lives were so closely integrated that they are rarely named separately in historical accounts.

## Early Life and Partnership
Peter Christen Asbjornsen was born on January 15, 1812, in Christiania. He was a member of an ancient family from the Gudbrandsdal, a lineage believed to have ended with him. Asbjornsen began collecting and recording legends and fairy stories in 1832 at the age of twenty, later expanding his collection by traveling on foot throughout Norway. He entered the university as a student in 1833 and eventually became a zoologist by profession. In this capacity, he conducted several investigating voyages along the Norwegian coasts, with a particular focus on the Hardanger fjord.

Jorgen Engebretsen Moe was born on April 22, 1813, at the Mo i Hole parsonage in Sigdal Ringerike. He first met Asbjornsen at the age of fourteen, initiating a close friendship that lasted until their deaths. In 1834, Asbjornsen discovered that Moe had independently begun searching for relics of national folklore. The two friends decided to work in concert, comparing their findings and collaborating on their research. Moe left Christiania University in 1839 to study theology and supported himself as a tutor in Christiania, using his holidays to collect stories while wandering through remote mountain districts.

## Major Works and Contributions
The collaboration between Asbjornsen and Moe resulted in a significant body of work. Between 1842 and 1843, they published the first installment of *Norwegian Popular Stories (Norske Folkeeventyr)*. This work was recognized across Europe as a valuable contribution to both literature and comparative mythology. A second volume followed in 1844, and they published a new collection in 1871. In 1859, Sir George Dasent translated many of the *Folkeeventyr* into English.

Aside from their joint efforts, Asbjornsen published a separate collection of Norwegian fairy tales, titled *huldreeventyr og folkesagn*, in 1845. Later in his life, Asbjornsen's interests expanded to environmental issues; in 1856, he drew attention to the deforestation of Norway and successfully induced the government to address the matter.
