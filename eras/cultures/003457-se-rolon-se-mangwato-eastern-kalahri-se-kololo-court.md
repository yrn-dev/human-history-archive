# Bechuana Language Group

The Bechuana language group is a well-marked collection of Bantu dialects characterized by its own distinct features. This group encompasses all Bantu dialects located within the Bechuanaland protectorate to the west of the Guai river.

## Geographic Distribution
The Bechuana dialects extend across a wide geographic area, covering significant portions of the north and west of the Transvaal. Their reach further extends over the entirety of Bechuanaland and the Orange River Colony. In a broader sense, Group No. 42, to which these dialects belong, stretches from the Orange river in the south to the Zambezi in the north. This group extends westward to the domain of the Oci-herero, though this expanse is interrupted by the presence of Bushmen and Hottentot populations.

## Dialects and Regional Usage
The group consists of several distinct dialects used across different regions:

*   **Se-rolon and Se-mangwato:** These dialects are associated with the Eastern Kalahri.
*   **Se-kololo:** This dialect serves as the court language of Barotseland.
*   **Se-suto:** This is the language utilized in Basutoland.
*   **Ci-venda and Se-pedi (or Peli):** These are identified as the principal dialects of the Transvaal.
*   **Se-[chi]lapi:** This is also listed as one of the dialects within the Bechuana group.

## Related Languages
The archive notes other linguistic groups in the region, such as the Ronga (Tonga) languages found in Portuguese South-East Africa, including the Lower Limpopo valley, Gazaland, and patches of the North Transvaal (Shi-gwamba) and Delagoa Bay. These Ronga languages are almost equally related to the Zulu and Nyanja groups, though the influence of Zulu is said to predominate.
