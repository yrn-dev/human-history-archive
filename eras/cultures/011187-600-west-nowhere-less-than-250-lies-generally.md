# Geographic Plateaus and Regional Surveys

## The Great Plateau of Asia
A significant plateau extends from the Mediterranean to the Indus, measuring approximately 2500 m. in length from east to west. Its breadth is upwards of 600 m. in the west and is never less than 250 m. This region generally maintains altitudes between 2000 ft. and 8000 ft. above sea level.

The plateau is divided into two distinct halves:
*   **Eastern Half:** Comprising Baluchistan, Afghanistan, and Persia, this area is poor and unproductive. It is characterized by a severe winter climate and extremely hot summers. Rainfall is very scanty, and running waters are rarely found except among the mountains that form the country's scarps. The population is sparse, frequently nomadic, and noted for being addicted to plunder, with limited progress in civilization and the arts.
*   **Western Half:** This portion falls within the Turkish empire. It possesses a less hot climate and greater natural productiveness, supporting a population that is more settled and generally more advanced.

## Regional Geography of Asia and the Indian Peninsula
Rainfall varies significantly across the Asian continent. In northern Asia, between the Lena and the Volga, rainfall generally ranges from 19 to 29 in., with increases seen in Japan, Siam, and Korea. Specific measurements include 22 in. at Tiflis, 7 or 8 in. on the Caspian, and 5 or 6 in. on the Sea of Aral. In Siberia, rainfall is 12 to 14 in. in the south-west, diminishing to 5 or 6 in. at Urga in northern Mongolia, while eastern Siberia receives about 15 to 20 in.

In China, rainfall is approximately 23 in. at Peking, increasing to 78 in. at Canton. Similar levels are found at Batavia in Java (78 in.) and Singapore (nearly 100 in.). The Burmese and Tenasserim coasts record falls of more than 200 in., with a minimum of 75 or 80 in. In the Indian peninsula, the west coast sees 75 to 100 in. at sea level and over 250 in. at certain mountain elevations. Conversely, the east coast receives far less, never rising to 50 in. and reducing to 25 or 30 in. toward the southern apex. The tract along the Indus is almost rainless until within 60 or 80 m. of the Himalaya.

## Persian Provinces and Cities
### Kerman
Kerman (ancient Karmania) is a province of Persia bounded by Khorasan and Yezd to the north, Seistan and Baluchistan to the east, Baluchistan and Fars to the south, and Fars to the west. It has an estimated area of 60,000 sq. m., with a maximum length of 400 m. (east to west) and a maximum breadth of 300 m. (north to south). 

The province consists of mountainous regions and deserts. The true desert of Kerman is located in the north and north-east, merging into the "Lut" desert. The southern region features fertile districts divided by longitudinal valleys (averaging no more than 12 m. in width) by mountain chains, including the Jamal Bariz and the Kuh Hazar (14,700 ft.). Snow on these peaks feeds the canals and springs that allow for cultivation in a region that is otherwise almost rainless in summer.

### Iconium
Iconium (modern Konia) is a city of Asia Minor located in a fertile plain. It is situated 6 m. from the Pisidian mountains to the west. To the east, a dead level plain extends for hundreds of miles. While streams from the Pisidian mountains create garden-like conditions to the south and south-west, much of the soil to the north and east remains uncultivated. Trees are found only in gardens near the city, and irrigation is required for productiveness.

## European Regional Surveys
### Kiev
Kiev is a government of south-western Russia with an area of 19,686 sq. m. It is a deeply trenched plateau with altitudes ranging from 600 to 800 ft., reaching 950 to 1050 ft. in the west. The land slopes north toward the Pripet marshes and falls abruptly to the east into the Dnieper valley (250 to 300 ft. above sea). The region is rich in minerals, including iron ore, marble, and various granites. The central and southern surfaces are covered in "black earth." As of 1906, the estimated population was 4,206,100.

### Kolguev
Kolguev is a roughly oval island off the north-west of Russia in Europe, located 50 m. from the mainland. It measures 54 m. in length and 39 m. in breadth, with a highest point of 250 ft. The surface consists largely of grass lands and peat-bogs. It is composed of clay or disintegrated sandstone with scattered granite. The island supports a few Samoyed hunters who rely on Arctic animals, such as foxes and bears, and birds like swans and geese.

## Other Geographic Notes
*   **The Baltic:** An unbroken ridge from Stockholm to Hango separates the Baltic basin from the Aland Haf. The Gulf of Finland runs eastwards for about 250 m., separating Esthonia from Finland. The total area of the Baltic, including the Kattegat and Skagerrak, is 166,397 sq. m.
*   **Greece Proper:** Located between the 36th and 40th parallels of north latitude, its greatest length is 250 English miles (from Cape Tænarus to Mount Olympus) and its greatest breadth is 180 miles.
*   **Wall of Media:** Xenophon records the length of this wall as 20 parasangs, which equals 600 stadia or 75 miles.
