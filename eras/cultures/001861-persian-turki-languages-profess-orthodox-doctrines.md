# Persian and Turki Languages and Orthodox Doctrines

## Linguistic Distribution in Central and Southern Asia
The Persian and Turki languages are prevalent across various regions of Asia, often intersecting with Arabic. West of the Indus River, dialects increasingly resemble Persian. This language meets Turki and Arabic west of the Tigris River, as well as along the Caspian and the Turkoman desert. In these tracts, a common set of letters is used for Persian, Arabic, and Turkish, which are written from right to left.

In northern India, the spoken languages are diverse and Sanskritic in their vocables, though they lack Sanskrit grammar. A notable exception is Urdu (also known as Hindostani), which is a mixed language used by the Mahommedan conquerors of northern India; it utilizes the Persian character for its writing.

## The Region of Badakshan
The population of Badakshan Proper consists of Arabs, Turks, and Tajiks. These groups speak the Turki and Persian languages. In contrast, the mountainous districts of the region are inhabited by Tajiks who speak distinct provincial dialects or languages that differ materially from one another. These include:
* **Shighnáni** (or Shighni), spoken in Roshán and Shignán.
* **Ishkáshami**, spoken in Ishkásham.
* **Wakhi**, spoken in Wakhán.
* **Sanglichì**, spoken in Zebák and Sanglich.
* **Minjáni**, spoken in Minján.

While the inhabitants of the mountainous tracts use these separate dialects, those living in the principal places often combine their native speech with a knowledge of Persian.

## Religious Affiliations and Orthodox Doctrines
In Badakshan Proper, the Tajiks, Turks, and Arabs profess the orthodox doctrines of the Mahommedan law, specifically as adopted by the Sunnite sect. Conversely, the Tajiks inhabiting the mountainous districts profess the Shi'ite (or Shiá) creed.

## Historical Turki and Persian Influences in India
Turki and Persian influences entered India through various invasions and empires. Between approximately 150 B.C. and A.D. 300, tribes of Turki (such as the Yue-chi) and Parthian origin invaded from the north. The Kushans, who founded a state encompassing Kashmir and northern India, were Buddhists. It is probable that the northern form of Buddhism, known as Mahayana, resulted from an amalgamation of Gotama's doctrines with Persian and Greek ideas.

Later, between 1200 and 1526, India experienced a Turki period consisting of several dynasties of mixed Iranian and Turki race. This was followed by the Mogul empire. While Islam spread rapidly near the Moslem capitals, Hindu civilization persisted in regions such as Vijayanagar (Mysore) and Rajputana.

## Documentation and Translation
Historical records regarding the Mogul and Turki periods include institutions written in the Turki or Mogul language. Some of these have been preserved through Persic versions. For example, a Persic version of such institutions was translated into English in 1783 by Mr. White and Major Davy, and later translated into French in 1787 by M. Langlès.
