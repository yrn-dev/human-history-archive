# Mostyn Hall and Related Antiquities

## Overview of Mostyn Hall
Mostyn Hall is the seat of one of the oldest families in Wales. Located near the town of Bagillt in Flintshire, North Wales, the hall dates back to the time of Henry VI. The site is notable for housing various manuscripts and antiquities, including materials concerning Welsh and old British history that were brought to the hall from Gloddaeth.

## Notable Artifacts and Collections
The collections at Mostyn Hall include a harp dated to 1568 and torques, referred to as *torchau*. 

The year 1568 is also associated with the Mostyn family through a session held at Caerwys. This session was conducted by the special command of Queen Elizabeth to confer regular degrees in vocal and instrumental music upon fifty-five minstrels. The prize for this event, a silver harp, was adjudged by the Mostyn family.

## Historical Associations
Mostyn Hall is linked to several figures of English and Welsh history:

*   **Henry VII:** It is said that Henry VII, while he was the earl of Richmond, was concealed at Mostyn Hall during the reign of Richard III. At that time, the lord of Mostyn was Richard ap Howel.
*   **Owen Gwynedd:** In the vicinity of the hall and the town of Bagillt lies Bryn Dychwelwch, known as the "Hill of Retreat." This location is so named because Owen Gwynedd effected a retreat there while being pursued by Henry II, who possessed superior numbers.

## Regional Context
The town of Bagillt, situated in the ancient parish of Holywell and approximately 14-1/2 miles from Chester, is located on the London & North Western railway. As of 1901, the town had a population of 2,637. Its primary importance is derived from its collieries and its works producing iron, lead, zinc, and alkali.
