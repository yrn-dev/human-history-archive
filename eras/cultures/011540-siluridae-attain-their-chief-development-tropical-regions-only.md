# Siluridae

The Siluridae are a family of fishes whose distribution and development vary significantly across the world's different climatic and geographical zones. They are most prominently developed in tropical regions, where they form a characteristic element of the freshwater fauna.

## Global Distribution and Zonal Characteristics

The distribution of Siluridae is categorized by three primary zones:

*   **The Equatorial Zone:** This zone is characterized by the development of Siluridae. It encompasses the entirety of Africa and Madagascar, and extends into Australia, likely following the southern coast of the continent. In Australia, tropical fishes of this region follow the Murray river far toward the south and potentially to its mouth. The boundary of this zone also extends to the western slope of the Andes in South America, bending southward to include the system of the Rio de la Plata.
*   **The Northern Zone (Holarctic Region):** This region is characterized by the Acipenseridae and contains few Siluridae. This zone is further divided into the Europaeo-Asiatic (Palaearctic) and North American (Nearctic) regions.
*   **The Southern Zone:** This zone is characterized by a scarcity of Siluridae and a complete absence of Cyprinidae.

## Regional Development and Species

In tropical regions, the Siluridae attain their chief development. In southern Asia, the fresh waters are chiefly inhabited by siluroids, along with carp, simple or spined eels, and climbing and walking fishes. 

The distribution of the genus *Silurus* is notably limited in certain areas; only one *Silurus* is found in Europe, and this same species extends into Africa and southern Asia.

## Ecological Context in Southern Asia

In the fresh waters of southern Asia, the Siluridae exist alongside other diverse aquatic life. While the *Salmonidae* are entirely absent from these waters, the *Cyprinidae* (carp) are largely represented. The *Cyprinidae* reach sizes in southern Asia unknown in Europe, such as a *Barbus* taken from the Tigris weighing 300 lb. 

Additionally, south-eastern Asia and Africa are home to walking or climbing fishes. These species possess an organization that allows them to breathe while out of the water, enabling them to survive in regions of rain and periodical drought that would be fatal to other fish species.
