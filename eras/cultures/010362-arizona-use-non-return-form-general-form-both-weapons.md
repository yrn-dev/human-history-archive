# Boomerang

The boomerang is a missile weapon utilized by the Australian aborigines and other peoples. The term is derived from a native name used by a single tribe in New South Wales; it was identified by Captain King in 1827 as "the Port Jackson term." Contrary to some beliefs, the weapon is not onomatopoeic, as it whistles in the air rather than making a "boom" sound. Additionally, it has been erroneously linked to the *womera*, or spear-thrower.

## Types and Distribution
There are two primary types of boomerangs: the return boomerang and the non-return (or war) boomerang. While both types are found throughout most parts of Australia, similar weapons appear in other regions:
* **North-East Africa:** A weapon closely resembling the boomerang survives here, and from this region, allied forms made of metal, such as throwing knives, have spread.
* **Ancient Egypt:** According to General Pitt-Rivers, the return form was used in ancient Egypt.
* **South India:** Among the Dravidians, there is a boomerang-shaped instrument capable of returning.
* **Arizona:** The Hopis (Moquis) utilize a non-return form of the weapon.

It remains uncertain if the instruments found in India and Egypt bear a genuine resemblance to the Australian return boomerang.

## Physical Characteristics and Dimensions
The general form of both the return and non-return boomerangs is the same. They are sickle-shaped and typically constructed from wood, although in India they may be made of steel or ivory. The proportions of the weapon are modeled such that the thickness is approximately 1/6th of the breadth, and the breadth is 1/12th of the length. The total length of these weapons varies from 6 inches to 3 or 4 feet.

## Technical Specifications of the Return Boomerang
The return boomerang is usually 2 to 3 feet in length and weighs approximately 8 oz. Its arms may be straight and set at an angle between 70 and 120 degrees, though in Australia they are always curved at an angle of 90 degrees or more. 

The weapon features a specific asymmetrical cross-section where the upper side is convex and the lower side is flat or nearly flat. The arms possess a "skew," meaning they are twisted 2 or 3 degrees from the plane running through the center of the weapon. Furthermore, the ends are raised to some extent above the plane of the weapon. Due to this construction, the return boomerang must be thrown with the right hand.

## The Non-Return Boomerang
The non-return boomerang is similar to the return version in almost every respect, with one primary difference: it possesses a skew in the opposite direction.
