# Objections to the Ciliary Classification System

The classification system based on the presence and nature of cilia in organisms faces several principal objections, primarily concerning the difficulty of preparation and the inconsistency of the biological markers used for categorization.

## Technical and Biological Limitations
One of the primary objections to this system is the extraordinary difficulty associated with obtaining satisfactory preparations that clearly show the cilia. Furthermore, it has been discovered that these motile organs are not formed on all substrata. In some instances, cilia are only developed during short periods of activity while the organism is young and vigorous, a factor that renders this specific character almost nugatory. 

An example of this instability is found in *B. megatherium* and *B. subtilis*. These organisms transition from a motile stage characterized by peritrichous cilia into a stage of filamentous growth within a few hours after growth commences, a process preceded by the casting of the cilia.

## Issues of Distribution and Genera
A second major objection concerns the distribution of species across the system's genera. The vast majority of described species—exceeding 1,000—are concentrated within only three genera: *Micrococcus* (approximately 400), *Bacillus* (approximately 200), and *Bactridium* (approximately 150). Consequently, only about a quarter of the forms are actually selected out by the other genera, limiting the system's utility in differentiation.

## Inconsistency of Ciliary Conditions
The system is further complicated by the fact that monotrichous and lophotrichous conditions are not constant, even during the motile stage of the organism. For instance, *Pseudomonas rosea* (Mig.) may possess one, two, or three cilia at either end. Under Fischer's classification, this organism would be distributed between *Bactrinium* and *Bactrillum* depending entirely on which state was observed at the time.
