# The Four-Handed Game

The four-handed game is a sport involving a shuttle and a net, where play is divided between an "in" (serving) side and an "out" (non-serving) side. The game is typically played to 15 aces, though it is occasionally played to 21 aces. In ladies' singles, the game is played to 11 aces. A rubber consists of the best of three games.

## Service and Positioning
The right to serve first in the first game of a rubber, as well as the choice of ends, is determined by tossing. The winner of the toss may choose either the first service or the ends; if they choose one, the opposing side chooses the other. Alternatively, the winner of the toss may request that the other side make the first choice.

In the four-handed game, the player who serves first must stand in his right-hand half court. The serve is delivered to the opponent standing in the opposite right-hand half court. During this process, the other players may stand anywhere on their respective sides of the net. Once the server's racket hits the shuttle, all players are permitted to stand anywhere on their side of the net.

The first service of every innings is always made from the right-hand half court. In the first innings, the side that starts the game possesses only one "hand" (serve). In all subsequent innings, every player on each side is granted a "hand," and partners serve consecutively.

## Scoring and Gameplay
Only the "in" side can score an ace. If the player who was served to returns the shuttle by hitting it into any part of the opponents' court before it touches the ground, the shuttle must be returned by a member of the "in" side, and then by a member of the "out" side, continuing in this manner until the shuttle ceases to be "in play" or a "fault" is committed.

The consequences of a "fault" depend on which side commits it:
* **Fault by the "in" side:** The server loses his "hand," and the player who was served to becomes the new server. No score accrues from this event.
* **Fault by the "out" side:** The "in" side scores an ace. Following an ace, the players on the "in" side change half courts. The server then serves from his left half court to the player in the opposite left half court who has not yet been served to.

While a side remains "in," service is made alternately from each half court into the diagonally opposite half court, with the change of half courts occurring whenever an ace is scored. Only the player who was served to is permitted to take the service.

## Rules of Play and "Lets"
A stroke is considered good if the shuttle falls on a line. In general play, a stroke is also good if the striker's racket follows the shuttle over the net. If the shuttle strikes the net during play but still passes over, the stroke remains good.

However, specific conditions result in a "let," meaning the stroke does not count and the server must serve again:
* If the shuttle strikes the net during service but the service is otherwise good. This applies even if the player served to has already struck the shuttle, as it is assumed the shuttle would have fallen into the proper half court.
* If the server misses the shuttle entirely while attempting to serve.

## Match Structure and End Changes
In a rubber, sides change ends at the start of the second game and again at the start of the third game, if necessary. In the third game, the sides change ends when the leading side reaches a specific score:
* 8 in a game of 15 aces.
* 6 in a game of 11 aces.
* Half the number of aces required to win in handicap games.

In matches decided by a single game of 21 aces, sides change ends when the leading side reaches 11 aces. The side that wins a game serves first in the following game. In the four-handed version, either player from the side that won the previous game may take the first service in the next game.
