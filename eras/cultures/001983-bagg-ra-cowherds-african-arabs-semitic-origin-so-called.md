# BAGGARA

The Baggara, whose name means "Cowherds," are African "Arabs" of Semitic origin. They are so named because they are breeders and owners of great numbers of cattle.

## Geography and Lifestyle
The Baggara occupy the region west of the White Nile, situated between the territory of the Shilluk and Dar Nuba, with their primary presence found in Kordofan. They are characterized as true nomad Arabs who have preserved most of their national characteristics, having intermarried very little with the Nuba.

As a purely pastoral people, the Baggara move from pasture to pasture based on the availability of food. In their daily operations, true tribesmen utilize oxen as pack and saddle animals. While many possess firearms, their customary weapons are the sword and lance; they do not carry shields. They are also noted as hunters of big game, including elephants, which they attack using spear and sword.

## Physical and Mental Characteristics
Descriptions of the Baggara's appearance vary. G. A. Schweinfurth describes them as the best-looking of the Nile nomads, noting their sinewy limbs, erect athletic bodies, fine heads, and overall physical beauty. Their skin colors range from deep black to a dark red-brown. While their features are regular and lack negro characteristics, there is little that appears Semitic in their physical appearance. In terms of mental power, they are described as being much superior to the indigenous races in their vicinity. They possess a passion for ornaments and fine clothes, often decorating themselves with rings and glass trinkets.

Conversely, other descriptions characterize them as having a sinister expression and "savage eyes" that speak of every crime and murder, asserting that courage is their only positive quality.

## History and Political Influence
The exact date of the Baggara's arrival in the Sudan is uncertain, though it appears they drifted up the Nile valley and dispossessed the original Nuba population. From the earliest times, they were engaged in the slave trade.

The Baggara played a pivotal role during the revolt against the Egyptians in 1882. They were among the first and most fervent supporters of the mahdi, serving as his real fighting force. The victories of the mahdi are attributed to the fanatical courage of the Baggara. Their decision to leave their own country to follow the mahdi to Khartum resulted in the fall of that city. Following the mahdi, his successor, the khalifa Abdullah, was himself a Baggara; during his rule, the tribe enjoyed the highest level of favor.

## Linguistic and Racial Context
The Baggara are identified as being of Semitic origin. Philologists categorize Semitic languages—including Arabic and Hebrew—as a group distinct from Aryan languages, possessing different root words and grammatical fundamentals. Semitic speakers are generally classed as Caucasians of the Mediterranean type. 

Some theories suggest a primordial connection between the Semitic and Hamitic language groups, with the possibility that Semitic languages arose as a specialized proto-Hamitic group. While the original Semitic speakers may have lived in north-east Africa or south Arabia, the Hamitic speakers are thought to have originated over a great area to the west.
