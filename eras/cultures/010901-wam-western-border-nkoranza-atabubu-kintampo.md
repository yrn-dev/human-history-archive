# Towns and Trade of Ashanti

## Urban Centers and Geography
In the region of Ashanti, there are few important towns beyond the capital, Kumasi, which has a population of approximately 6,000. Other notable settlements include Obuassi, located in the south-west, which serves as the center for the gold-mining industry. On the western border lies Wam, while Nkoranza, Atabubu, and Kintampo are situated in the north.

Kintampo is a town of significant size, located about 130 miles north-east of Kumasi. It serves as a meeting-place for traders arriving from the coast and from the Niger countries. While it was formerly one of the great ivory and slave marts of West Africa, it has transitioned into a depot for government stores and a center for the commerce of kola-nuts.

## Industry and Art
The Ashanti people demonstrate skill in several types of manufacture, with a particular proficiency in weaving cotton. They also show considerable skill in their works in gold and pottery. 

The artistic elaboration of their goldsmiths' work and silver-plate is evidenced by items found in 1874 within the king's palace at Kumasi. These valuable objects included masks made of beaten gold. Additionally, the influence of Moorish art is perceptible in these works.

## Religion and Beliefs
The great bulk of the population in Ashanti are spirit-worshippers. In general, the Ashanti show a repugnance to the doctrines of Islam, although a few Mahommedans reside there, most of whom are traders from other countries. Christianity has also gained some converts through the efforts of Wesleyan and Basel missionaries.
