# BALUCHISTAN

Baluchistan is a region extending from the Gomal river to the Arabian Sea, and from the borders of Afghanistan and Persia to those of Sind and the Punjab. In a wider sense, it is divided between British India and Persia.

## Persian Baluchistan
Persian Baluchistan is a province of Persia with an area of approximately 60,000 sq. m. It is situated along the northern shore of the Arabian Sea and is bounded to the west by Kerman, to the north by the central Persian desert and Seistan, and to the east by independent and British Baluchistan. Administratively, it forms a division of the province of Kerman.

The province is subdivided into twenty districts: Bampur, Serhad, Dizek, Jalk, Sib, Irafshan, Magas, Serbaz, Lashar, Champ, Fannuj, Bazman, Aptar, Daman, Aprandagan, Asfehgeh, Surmij, Meskutan, Pushteh, and Makran. Makran, known as the country of the Ichthyophagi, contains the subdistricts of Bahu, Dasht, Bint, Geh, Kasrkand, and Kucheh.

The total population of Baluchistan is under 200,000. Historically, the province remained practically independent until Persian troops occupied Bampur in 1849; Persian supremacy was not recognized in some of the extreme eastern districts until 1872.

## British Baluchistan
The portion of Baluchistan within the borders of British India is divided into two main divisions. British Baluchistan, which covers 9,403 sq. m., consists primarily of tracts ceded by Afghanistan under the treaty of Gandamak in 1879 and was formally declared part of British India in 1887. 

The second division consists of foreign territories under the superintendence of the chief commissioner as agent to the governor-general. This area is further split into:
* **Directly-administered districts:** These include the eastern portion of the Quetta tahsil, the Bolan Pass, and the whole of the Chagai and Zhob political agencies, totaling 36,401 sq. m. Some of these areas are held on lease from the khan of Kalat.
* **Native states:** Covering 79,382 sq. m., this includes Las Bela and the state of Kalat (which includes Kharan and Makran).
* **Tribal areas:** Covering 7,129 sq. m., these are in the possession of the Bugti and Marri tribes.

Direct British control extends over the northern boundary, the north-eastern corner, and the railway running from the Afghan-Baluch frontier at New Chaman through Quetta.

## Geography and Climate
The region is part of a great plateau extending from the Mediterranean to the Indus. The eastern half of this plateau, which includes Baluchistan, Afghanistan, and Persia, is characterized as poor and unproductive. The climate is extremely hot in summer and very severe in winter, with very scanty rainfall. Running waters are rarely found except among the mountains that form the scarps of the elevated country.

In Persian Baluchistan, the land consists of barren hills, mountains, and arid, waterless plains featuring stony deserts and moving sands. Only a small portion of the land is under cultivation. The principal rivers are the Bampur and Mashkid, which flow away from the sea and disappear into depressions known as *hamuns*. Rivers that flow into the sea are unimportant and typically dry for most of the year.

## Geology and Natural History
The mountain ranges are primarily composed of Tertiary and Cretaceous beds arranged in a series of folds running parallel to the ridges. These folds are part of a system stretching between Mount Ararat and Peshawar. In British Baluchistan, Jurassic rocks are present in the hills, and *Fusulina* limestone and Triassic beds are found along the south side of the upper Zhob. Most deposits are marine, except for the lower Tertiary and upper Cretaceous, which feature conglomerates and volcanic tuffs.

A line of recent volcanic cones stretches from eastern Persia into western Baluchistan; while these appear extinct in Baluchistan, the Koh-i-Tafdan beyond the Persian frontier still emits vapours. The cones are mostly andesitic. Mud "volcanoes" are found on the Makran coast, though their connection to true volcanic agencies is doubted.

The flora of the region is continuous with the desert flora of northern and eastern Africa. In the less elevated parts of Baluchistan, the vegetation is characterized by aromatic shrubs and rare trees, including *Celtis*, *Dodonaea*, *Pistacia*, poplars, and the date palm. Dry hills are covered by prickly forms of *Astragalus* and *Statice*. In spring, bulbous plants and herbaceous vegetation appear.

Zoologically, Baluchistan and Sind belong to the Ethiopian region (as defined by Dr. Sclater), which also encompasses Africa south of the Atlas, Arabia, Palestine, and southern Persia.
