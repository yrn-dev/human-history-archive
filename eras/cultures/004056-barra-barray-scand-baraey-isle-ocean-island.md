# BARRA

Barra, also known as Barray (derived from the Scandinavian *Baraey*, meaning "isle of the ocean"), is an island located in the outer Hebrides of Inverness-shire, Scotland. As of 1901, the island had a population of 2,362.

## Geography and Topography
The island measures 8 miles in length and varies in breadth from 2 to 4 miles. However, at a sandy isthmus located 2 miles below Scurrival Point, the breadth narrows to only a few hundred yards. The geological rock formation of the island is gneiss. Its highest elevation is Heaval, which reaches 1,260 feet, and the landscape features several small lochs.

The parish of Barra includes a variety of smaller islets and islands. To the northeast lie Frida, Gighay, Hellisay, and Flodda. To the southeast are Pabbay, Vatersay, Berneray, and Mingalay, the latter of which had a population of 135. Berneray contains Barra Head, the most southerly point of the outer Hebrides chain and the highest point of Berneray; a lighthouse is situated here, 680 feet above high water.

## Settlement and History
The chief village on the island is Castlebay, which is served by a steamer from Glasgow once a week. The village is named after the castle of Kishmul, a stronghold located on a rock in the bay. This castle once belonged to the McNeills of Barra, who are recognized as one of the oldest Highland clans.

The island contains various historical remains, including Druidical circles, Danish duns, and ancient chapels. Communication with South Uist, which lies approximately 5 miles to the northeast, is maintained via ferry.

## Economy and Land Use
The land on Barra is divided between 4,000 acres of arable land and 18,000 acres of meadow and hill pasture. The economy is heavily reliant on the sea; the fisheries for ling, herring, and cod are of importance. Additionally, the coasts are rich in shell-fish, and the island has always been particularly famous for its cockles.
