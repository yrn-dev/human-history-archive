# THE PROVINCE OF BADASHAN

## Overview and Governance
Badashan is described as a very great kingdom and province. The population of the region worships Mahommet and speaks a peculiar language. Governance in Badashan is hereditary, with the royalty claiming descent from King Alexander and the daughter of King Darius, who was the Lord of the vast Empire of Persia. Out of regard for Alexander the Great, the kings of this province refer to themselves in the Saracen tongue as Zulcarniain, a term meaning "Alexander."

## Geography and Neighboring Regions
The province is situated such that travelers arriving from the town of Casem must ride for three days without finding any habitation, food, or drink before reaching Badashan. To the south of Badashan, a ten-day journey leads to the Province of Pashai, inhabited by brown-complexioned Idolaters who are adept in diabolic arts and sorceries.

To the north-east, a twelve-day journey ascending a river leads out of Badashan. This route passes through land belonging to a brother of the Prince of Badashan, an area containing scattered habitations, villages, and towns inhabited by valiant Mahommetans. This path leads to Vokhan, a small province extending no more than three days' journey in any direction. The people of Vokhan also worship Mahommet, speak a peculiar language, and are gallant soldiers. They are liegemen to the Prince of Badashan and are led by a chief titled "None," which translates to "Count."

## Cities and Landmarks
Historical records and maps indicate various significant sites within or near the province:
*   **Faizabad:** Described by Wilford in the late 18th century as the new capital of Badakhshan, constructed near the site of the old capital.
*   **Badakhshan City:** Represented on Chinese maps as being located to the east of Faizabad.
*   **Jaúzgún:** According to tradition, the Lady Zobeidah (daughter of Al-Mansúr and wife of Ar-Rashid) spent her springs here and built a palace, the ruins of which remain visible.

## Regional Characteristics and Economy
The archive notes several distinct characteristics associated with the province and its surrounding highlands:
*   **Natural Resources:** The region is associated with azure mines, the Balas Ruby, and the presence of wild sheep and naked barley.
*   **Agriculture and Livestock:** The province is noted for its horses.
*   **Dress:** Historically, the ladies of Badakhshan were known for the great amplitude of their garments, specifically their trousers. While this "bombasticall dissimulation" is no longer practiced by the ladies of Badakhshan, similar styles are noted among Afghan ladies and in the Panjab.
*   **Conflict:** The country has suffered repeated devastation resulting from war.

## Historical Events
The province served as a transit point for military movements. Nogodar, a king described as a leader of "scoundrels" and the nephew of Chagatai (brother to the Great Kaan), led a body of 10,000 horsemen through Badashan. Nogodar and his unscrupulous forces traveled through Badashan and the provinces of Pashai-dir and Ariora-keshemur before entering India at the province of Dalivar.
