# Tajiks

The Tajiks are a population group found across several regions of Central Asia, appearing in various social, religious, and linguistic contexts. They are identified in some regions as Iranians and in others as agriculturists and tradespeople of mixed race.

## Distribution and Settlements
Tajiks are present in several key territories and urban centers:

*   **Darvaz:** In this small vassal state of Bokhara, situated in a mountainous region on the Panj, the population of approximately 35,000 consists primarily of Moslem Tajiks and the closely-related Galchas. The chief town of this area is Kala-i-khumb, located on the Panj at an altitude of 4,370 ft.
*   **Badakhshan:** In Badakhshan proper, Tajiks live alongside Turks and Arabs. However, in the more mountainous tracts of the region, the inhabitants are Tajiks.
*   **Urban Centers:** Tajiks are found in the town of Marghelan (or Marghilan), the administrative center of the province of Ferghana, where they reside alongside Sarts. In the town of Khojent (or Khojend), located in the province of Syr-darya on the left bank of the Syr-darya (Jaxartes), the majority of the inhabitants are Tajiks.
*   **Other Regions:** They are listed as part of the population of the oasis of Khiva, where they are described as tradespeople and agriculturists of mixed race. Additionally, they are categorized as Iranians, along with Kurds and Baluchis, in a province where the population is estimated at about a million.

## The Galchas
The Galchas are described as being closely related to the Tajiks. They are specifically referred to as "Highland Tajiks." They are located in the region between the headstreams of the Oxus and the Talass in Bokhara and Ferghana (Khokand), where they come into contact with the Sol (or "Left") section of the Kara-Kirghiz.

## Religion and Language
The religious and linguistic characteristics of the Tajiks vary by geography:

*   **Badakhshan:** In Badakhshan proper, Tajiks are Sunnis who follow the orthodox doctrines of the Mahomedan law and speak Persian. In contrast, the Tajiks inhabiting the mountainous districts profess the Shi'ite creed. These mountain populations speak distinct dialects specific to their districts; for example, the *Wakhi* is spoken in Wakhán, the *Ishkáshami* in Ishkásham, the *Shighnáni* in Shignán and Roshán, the *Minjáni* in Minján, and the *Sanglichì* in Sanglich and Zebák. In the principal towns of these mountainous areas, knowledge of Persian is combined with these local dialects.
*   **Other Areas:** In certain Iranian-populated provinces, the Tajiks, along with Kurds and Arabs, have largely conformed to the national Shiah faith.
