# Andros Island and the Abaco Islands

The names Andros and Abaco refer to distinct geographical locations found within the archive's records: the Abaco Islands and Andros Island as part of the Bahamian archipelago in the British West Indies, and the island of Andros as part of the Cyclades in Greece.

## The Bahamas: Andros and the Abaco Islands

In the British West Indies, the Bahamas is an archipelago consisting of an estimated 29 islands, 2387 rocks, and 661 cays. This group extends approximately 630 miles from Florida in the northwest to Haiti in the southeast. Within this archipelago, the Abaco Islands (comprising Little and Great Abaco) are located in the northwest, while Andros is situated further west.

### Geography and Physical Characteristics
Andros is often referred to as a single island, but it is actually divided into several by narrow straits. It has a length of 95 miles and a total area of 1600 square miles. A notable geological feature of Andros is the presence of an extremely fine white marl that resembles a chalky ooze. Furthermore, Andros is the only island in the entire Bahamian group where streams of running water can be found.

Great Abaco is 70 miles long with an area of 680 square miles. The surrounding seas of the archipelago are generally shallow, though the Tongue of Ocean is a depression that extends southward along the east side of Andros.

### Flora and Timber
Andros Island and the Abaco Islands are specially noted for their profusion of large timber. This valuable timber includes:
* Mahogany
* Mastic
* Lignum vitae
* Iron wood
* Bullet wood

Despite the abundance of these resources, the lack of roads and labor has made it impossible to turn much of this timber to useful account, although efforts have been made to work the timber in Abaco.

## Andros Island (Greece)

In the context of Greek geography, Andros is one of the islands in the Archipelago belonging to the group known as the Cyclades (islands encircling the sacred centre of Delos). Geologically, it is considered a continuation of the mountain chain of Eubœa, just as other Cyclades islands like Keôs and Kythnos are continuations of the chain reaching to Cape Sunium.

### Historical Accounts
Historical records indicate that Andros was once besieged by the Hellenes under the command of Themistocles. Themistocles demanded money from the islanders, claiming he brought with him two great deities: Persuasion and Necessity (also referred to as Compulsion). 

The Andrians refused the demand, responding that while Athens was prosperous because it was supplied with serviceable gods, the inhabitants of Andros were miserably poor. They claimed to be protected by two "unprofitable" or "unkind" deities that never left their island: Poverty and Helplessness. They argued that the power of Athens could not overcome their total inability to pay. 

Because Andros was found to be unproductive, the fleet eventually returned to Salamis. Themistocles later used Andros as a starting point to extort private sums of money from other islands, such as Paros and Carystus, without the knowledge of other commanders.

The archive also notes that the state or nobles of Venice eventually made conquests of several islands, including Andros.
