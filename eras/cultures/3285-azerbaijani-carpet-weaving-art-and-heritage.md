# Azerbaijani Carpet Weaving: Art and Heritage

## Overview
Azerbaijani carpet weaving is not merely a craft but a complex visual language that encodes the history, geography, and spiritual beliefs of the South Caucasus. For centuries, these textiles have served as a primary medium for artistic expression, blending nomadic traditions with the sophisticated urban aesthetics of the Silk Road. 

The art is categorized into several distinct regional schools—most notably Guba, Shirvan, Baku, Ganja, and Karabakh. Each school is defined by specific palettes and motifs; for instance, Guba carpets are renowned for their geometric precision and high knot density, while Karabakh carpets often feature larger, floral compositions reflecting the lush landscapes of the region. Historically, these carpets were woven using local wool and natural dyes derived from plants and insects, such as madder root for reds and indigo for blues.

A point of scholarly discussion among historians and ethnographers concerns the exact origins of specific motifs. While many patterns are uniquely Azerbaijani, some argue that the "cross-pollination" of the Silk Road makes it difficult to isolate a single point of origin for certain geometric symbols, which appear in various forms across Central Asia and Anatolia. However, the specific technical execution and the symbolic "alphabet" of Azerbaijani weaving remain distinct.

## Timeline of Development

| Era | Significance |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Ancient/Medieval** | Early development of weaving techniques; integration of Zoroastrian and later Islamic symbols. |
| **15th - 18th Century** | Golden age of regional schools; carpets become major export commodities via the Silk Road. |
| **19th Century** | Increased Russian influence; establishment of larger workshops and standardization of some patterns. |
| **20th Century** | Transition to state-sponsored cooperatives; formalization of the "Azerbaijani School" of weaving. |
| **2010** | Azerbaijani carpet art is inscribed on the UNESCO Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. |

## Key Facts
*   **Symbolism:** Motifs often represent elements of nature, mythology, or daily life. The "Buta" (almond-shaped leaf) is one of the most iconic symbols, representing fire, eternity, or a budding flower.
*   **Technique:** Most traditional Azerbaijani carpets utilize the symmetrical (Turkish) knot, though variations exist across different regional schools.
*   **Materials:** High-quality wool from local sheep is the primary material, though silk is frequently used in the more opulent Baku and Guba carpets to achieve a shimmering finish.

## Did You Know?
*   **UNESCO Recognition:** In 2010, the art of Azerbaijani carpet weaving was officially recognized by UNESCO as a masterpiece of intangible cultural heritage.
*   **The Carpet Museum:** Baku is home to the Azerbaijan National Carpet Museum, a building architecturally designed in the shape of a rolled-up carpet.
*   **Natural Pigments:** Traditional weavers used pomegranate skins to create yellow tones and walnut shells for deep browns, ensuring the colors remained vibrant for centuries.
