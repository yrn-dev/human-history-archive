# Luapula, Palms, and Raphia

## The Luapula River
The Luapula (also referred to as the upper Congo) is a river located in British Central Africa. It exits from the southern point of Lake Bangweulu, where it is approximately one mile wide at the outflow, though it quickly narrows to about 300 yards. The river flows south through a swamp for some 50 miles before turning west and subsequently north. 

The Luapula serves as a drainage mechanism for Lake Bangweulu, although this process is believed to be occurring very slowly. Geographically, the river's right or east bank falls within the limits of a territory encompassing approximately 250,000 square miles. This region includes various other bodies of water, such as Lake Mweru, which the Luapula reaches at its northern issue. Near the river's outflow lies Lake Kampolombo, which is separated from the Luapula by a narrow, forest-clad tongue of land known as Kapata, although various channels connect the river to the lake.

## The Borassus and Hyphaene Palms
Palms of the genus *Borassus* are common in the Indian region, where they are utilized to supply toddy. Specifically, the Palmyra palm (*Borassus flabelliformis*) is used in India for the extensive manufacture of "Cajan" baskets made from its fronds. This specific manufacturing process has also been established in Germany's Black Forest, where it has become a characteristic and important staple.

## The Phoenix Palm and Wild Date
The *Phoenix* genus includes several species of palms found across various regions:

### Phoenix dactylifera
Known as the date palm, this beautiful tree grows to heights of 60 to 80 feet. It features a stem marked with old leaf-scars and a crown of shining pinnate leaves. Because the trees are unisexual, artificial fertilization of female flowers is required for cultivation. The fruit is oblong and fleshy with a deeply furrowed hard seed. 

The date palm ranges from the Canary Islands through Northern Africa and south-east Asia to India. In Arabia, it is the primary source of national wealth and the staple food. In Northern Africa and the Sahara oases, the dates are pressed and pounded into cakes. While introduced to Europe's Mediterranean shores, the fruit does not ripen there; consequently, the plants are used primarily for the celebration of the Passover by the Israelites and for the Christian festival of Palm Sunday. Spanish missionaries also introduced the tree to Mexico and the dry districts of the south-western United States.

### Phoenix sylvestris and Phoenix farinifera
In the East Indies, *Phoenix sylvestris* (the toddy palm) is used to produce date sugar from its sap or toddy. This juice may also be drunk fresh or distilled into arrack. This species is closely allied to the date palm and was once thought to be the parent stock of all cultivated varieties. In the hill country of southern India, a small species called *Phoenix farinifera* is used to obtain date palm meal from its stem.

## Raphia
In the provided records, Raphia is identified as a city. It was located at the sea-side and was among the cities possessed by the Israelites that had previously belonged to the Phoenicians, Syrians, and Idumeans. At a later period, the city of Raphia was among those rebuilt under the orders of Gabinius.
