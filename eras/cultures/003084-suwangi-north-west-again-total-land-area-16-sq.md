# Suwangi and the Banda Islands

The Banda Islands are a group located in the Dutch East Indies within the Banda Sea, situated to the south of Ceram. Administratively, they belong to the residency of Amboyna. The archipelago consists of three chief islands and several smaller ones.

## Geography and Composition
The islands are characterized by a volcanic formation. Lontor (also known as Great Banda) is a sickle-shaped island that, together with Gunong Api and Banda Neira, forms part of a crater's circle, an arrangement comparable to Santorin in the Aegean Sea. 

The islands are distributed as follows:
* **Lontor**: The largest island in the group.
* **Banda Neira**: Located to the north of Lontor.
* **Gunong Api**: Situated west of Banda Neira.
* **Wai (or Ai)**: Located further west.
* **Run**: Positioned to the south-west of Wai.
* **Pisang**: Located north of Gunong Api.
* **Suwangi**: Located to the north-west.

The total land area of the group is approximately 16 sq. m.

## Volcanic Activity and Climate
Gunong Api, which translates to "Fire Mountain," is an active volcano reaching a height of 2,200 ft. Its earthquakes and eruptions have frequently caused destruction. A notable event occurred in 1852, where the damage was primarily caused by a huge sea wave.

While the general climate of the islands is hot, it is not considered unhealthy. However, Lontor was found to be too unhealthy to serve as the site for the principal settlement.

## Maritime Features and Settlements
The chief town is Banda, located on Neira. The settlement is commanded by two Dutch forts from the early 17th century: Belgica and Nassau.

A good harbour exists in the space between Gunong Api, Neira, and Lontor. This harbour features entrances on either side, allowing vessels to enter regardless of the monsoon. A third channel exists between Neira and Gunong Api, though it is only navigable for small vessels.

## Commerce and Agriculture
The primary articles of commerce for the Banda group are mace and nutmeg, with the nutmeg being indigenous to the area. Nutmegs are grown under the shade of other trees, typically the canari, to suit natural conditions. Annual production reaches nearly 1,500,000 lb of nutmegs and 350,000 lb of mace.

The history of the plantations, known as *perken*, involves several shifts in labor and ownership:
* The Dutch cleared off the native population.
* Plantations were subsequently worked by convicts and slaves until emancipation in 1860.
* Following emancipation, Chinese and Malay labourers were introduced.

Originally, the conquerors of the natives held the plantations while the government maintained a monopoly on produce at a fixed rate. This government monopoly was abolished in 1873. Additionally, jatti (or Jalti) wood is cultivated on the small island of Rosingen.
