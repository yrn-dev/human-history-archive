# Language Groups of Angola and Surrounding Regions

The linguistic landscape of Angola and its neighboring territories is characterized by several distinct language groups, including the Mbundu, the Oci-herero group, and the Kiboko family. These groups are defined by their geographical distribution and their varying degrees of affinity with one another.

## The Mbundu Group
The Mbundu group, also referred to as the Angola language, is closely allied with the Kongo language group. In the south, the dialects of the Kongo group melt imperceptibly into those of the Mbundu. This group encompasses the languages of Central Angola, specifically including Ki-mbundu, Songo, Ki-sama, Mbamba, and U-mbangala. 

The geographical boundaries of the Mbundu group are defined by several landmarks. To the north, the river Loje serves as a frontier between the Mbundu tongues and the Kongo languages. To the east, the boundary is likely the Kwango river, beyond which the Lunda languages begin. To the south, the group's boundary is approximately the 11th degree of south latitude.

## The Oci-herero Group
The Oci-herero group is described as being very distinct from Ki-mbundu speech, although there are connecting forms between them. This group comprises several languages distributed across south Angola and German South-West Africa:
* **Herero**: Spoken in Damaraland.
* **Umbundu**: Spoken in the Bihe highlands of south Angola.
* **Nano**: Spoken along the Benguela coast.
* **Si-ndonga, Ku-anyama, and Oci-mbo**: Spoken in the northern half of German South-West Africa and the southern regions of Portuguese Angola.

The Oci-herero languages likely extend inland to the Zambezi watershed, specifically reaching the Kubango and Kwito rivers. To the south, this group is confronted by Hottentot languages. Notably, an enclave of the Haukoin or Hill Damaras—a Negro race of unexplained affinities who apparently speak a Hottentot language—exists within the area of Herero speech.

## The Kiboko Family
The Kiboko family (also known as Kibokwe or Kioko) is located in eastern Angola. This language group exhibits affinities with the languages of the Upper Zambezi, as well as with the Oci-herero and Mbundu groups (Groups 29 and 28). 

The Kiboko family extends eastward into the south-western portion of the Belgian Congo. It includes the Lubale language, spoken at the sources of the river Zambezi and in northern Barotseland. Additionally, the Gangela of south-western Angola may possibly be included in this group.
