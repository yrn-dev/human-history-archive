# Guipuzcoa, Navarre, and the Arrondissement of Bayonne

The regions of Guipuzcoa, Navarre, and the arrondissement of Bayonne are integral parts of the Basque territories, inhabited by the Basque people. These people call themselves *Eskualdunak* ("those who possess the *Eskuara*") and refer to their homeland as Eskual-Herria.

## Geography and Administration
The Basque Provinces (*Provincias Vascongadas*) are a division of north-eastern Spain comprising Guipuzcoa, Biscay (or Vizcaya), and Alava. As of 1900, this area covered 2,739 square miles with a population of 603,596, making it the third most densely populated region in Spain. The territory is shaped like a triangle, bounded by the Bay of Biscay and France to the north, Navarre to the east, and the provinces of Logrono, Burgos, and Santander to the south and west. Among these, Guipuzcoa is the only province that is wholly Basque.

In France, the Basque region (the French Pays Basque) is located in the west of the Basses-Pyrenees department. This department was formed in 1790 from Bearn and three districts of Gascony: Soule, Labourd, and Basse-Navarre. The French Basque region is divided into the arrondissements of Bayonne and Mauleon. Bayonne serves as the principal port of the department and is one of its principal towns.

## History and Governance
The historical boundaries of the region may have originally corresponded to the dioceses of Calahorra, Bayonne, and Pamplona, which likely aligned with the ancient tribes of the Vascones, Varduli, Caristi, and Autrigones. 

The political history of the Spanish provinces involved shifting dependencies between Castile and Navarre. Guipuzcoa was definitively united to Castile in 1200. Alava joined the crown of Castile in 1332 via the confederation of Arriaga, and Biscay's definitive union with Castile occurred in 1370. Despite these unions, the provinces remained a *tierra apartada* (land apart), maintaining their own customs and laws known as *fueros*. These *fueros* were sworn to be maintained by Spanish kings; royal orders or decrees of the Cortes held no force in the provinces unless countersigned by the juntas. The provinces used a seal with the word *Iruracbat* ("The Three One") as a symbol of unity. 

Guipuzcoa demonstrated significant autonomy; in 1481, it alone proposed a treaty of friendship, free trade, and peace with England, which was signed in Westminster on March 9, 1482. This tradition of autonomy lasted until 1833, following the death of Ferdinand VII.

## Culture and Religion
The Basque language, *Eskuara*, is an isolated tongue in Europe, described as a polysynthetic, incorporating, and agglutinative system. While it bears grammatical similarities to Finnic and Magyar languages, it occupies an intermediate place between Ugrian or Ugro-Altaic languages and American languages.

The people are described as highly religious and conservative in their practices. They have maintained the primitive order of deaconesses and the earliest form of Christian marriage. Notable figures from these regions who influenced Europe include Francis Xavier of Navarre and Ignatius de Loyola of Guipuzcoa. 

Despite their piety, the Basques resisted being "priest-ridden." They opposed the authority of Spanish bishops, and in Guipuzcoa, bishops had no share in ecclesiastical patronage; instead, priests were elected by the people or chosen via competitive examination. In Navarre, the cortes once compelled the king to withdraw a 1757 order from Spanish bishops that had forbidden drama.

## Economy and Industry
Historically, both the Pays Basque and the Basque Provinces were major cider-producing regions, though wine-growing became more prevalent during the 19th century. 

In the Spanish provinces, iron mining and sea fisheries are the primary industries. In 1905, the Basque Provinces produced 5,302,344 tons of iron (the majority from Biscay), with over 5.8 million tons exported to England. Guipuzcoa is specifically noted for the swords of Mondragon and the small-arms factory at Eibar, where Senor Zuloaga revived the artistic inlaying of steel and iron with silver and gold in the 19th century.

In the French department of Basses-Pyrenees, the economy includes:
*   **Manufacturing:** Paper, chocolate, cord slippers, sashes, and woollen caps.
*   **Agriculture and Food:** "Bayonne hams" and other delicacies produced at Orthez.
*   **Resources:** Salt, building-stone, and quarry products.
*   **Trade:** Bayonne exports resinous products, minerals, salt, wine, timber, and mine-props, while importing wool, grain, phosphates, minerals, and coal.

## Notable Locations
*   **Fuenterrabia:** A town in Guipuzcoa on the French frontier and the left bank of the river Bidassoa. It features a walled fortress with a castle, town hall, and old church, contrasted by newer suburbs and a fishermen's quarter. It became a popular summer resort toward the end of the 19th century.
*   **Bayonne:** The principal port of its department and the birthplace of the painter Leon Joseph Florentin Bonnat (1833–), who became a professor at the Ecole des Beaux Arts and director in 1905.
*   **Vitoria:** The capital of Alava, said to have been founded in 581 by the Gothic king Leovigild.
