# Ki-mbundu, Mbamba, Ki-sama, Songo, and U-mbangala

The languages of Ki-mbundu, Mbamba, Ki-sama, Songo, and U-mbangala are members of a linguistic group generally styled as Mbundu. This group consists of the languages of Central Angola and is closely allied to the Kongo language group.

## Linguistic Classification and Relations
The Mbundu group is characterized by its relationship with the Kongo dialects, into which the Mbundu tongues melt imperceptibly in the south. The Kongo group itself comprises dialects found along the lower course of the Congo river, extending from its mouth to Stanley Pool, as well as the territory of the old kingdom of Congo. This territory lies north of the river Loje and south of the Congo river, stretching from the coast eastwards to the longitude of Stanley Pool and the watershed of the river Kwango.

While the Mbundu group is closely allied with the Kongo group, it is very distinct from the Oci-herero group, although connecting forms exist between the two. The Oci-herero group includes the Herero language of Damaraland, the Nano of the Benguela coast, the Umbundu of the Bihe highlands of south Angola, and the Oci-mbo, Ku-anyama, and Si-ndonga of the northern half of German South-West Africa and southern regions of Portuguese Angola.

Additionally, there is a language family in eastern Angola known as Kibokwe, Kiboko, or Kioko. This family appears to offer affinities to the Mbundu group (group No. 28), the Oci-herero group (group No. 29), and the languages of the Upper Zambezi. The Kibokwe family extends into the south-western portion of the Belgian Congo and includes the sources of the river Zambezi and the Lubale of northern Barotseland, and it may possibly include the Gangela of south-western Angola.

## Geographic Boundaries
The boundaries of the Mbundu linguistic genus are defined by specific geographic and coordinate markers:

*   **Eastern Boundary:** The boundary on the east is probably the Kwango river. Beyond this river, the Lunda languages begin.
*   **Northern Boundary:** The river Loje serves, to some extent, as the frontier separating the Mbundu tongues from the Kongo tongues.
*   **Southern Boundary:** The southern boundary of the group is approximately the 11th degree of south latitude.
