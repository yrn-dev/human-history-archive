# Summer Temperatures North of the Tropic of Cancer

## Factors Influencing High Summer Temperatures
The occurrence of very high summer temperatures in the regions located north of the tropic of Cancer is primarily explained by the increased length of the day associated with higher latitudes. This extended daylight duration more than compensates for the loss of heat that results from the sun's smaller mid-day altitude. 

For example, on June 21st, the difference in the heating power of the sun's rays at noon between latitude 20 deg. and latitude 45 deg. is only approximately 2%. However, the accumulated heat received throughout the day is about 11% greater in the higher latitude, where the day is lengthened to 15-1/2 hours, compared to the lower latitude, where the day lasts only 13-1/4 hours.

## Modifying Climatic Causes
While the length of the day provides the main outline for observed temperatures, periodical summer rains serve as a significant modifying cause across the tropical region. These rains tend to greatly arrest the increase of summer heat in the areas where they prevail, giving the heat altogether peculiar characteristics.

## Regional Variations in Asia
Temperature extremes vary significantly across different Asian territories:

*   **Central Asia:** The region comprising Mongolia, Turkestan, western Manchuria, and the area between the southern border of Siberia and the temperate alpine zone of north China and the Himalaya is characterized as an almost rainless region. This area experiences summers of intense heat and winters of extreme severity.
*   **South and East Asia:** High temperatures and heavy periodical summer rains are found in southern China, the Indo-Chinese peninsula, India, and the western part of the Malay Archipelago.
*   **The Plains:** In certain plains, the ordinary range of the thermometer extends from approximately 52 deg. F. in the coldest month to 103 deg. in the shade during the summer.
*   **Siberia and the Far East:** In the Maritime Province, the climate is harsh. At Nikolayevsk, summer temperatures as high as 94.6° F. are observed, though winter temperatures can drop to -41.5° F. In Okhotsk (59° 21´ N.), the summer average is 52.5° F., and at Ayan (56° 27´ N.), it is 50.5° F.

## Atmospheric Pressure and Monsoon Winds
The rise in temperature north of the equator during the hot half of the year leads to a great diminution of atmospheric pressure over Asia. This process begins to be strongly marked in April and May and reaches its maximum in July, shortly after the solstice. 

This pressure drop, occurring simultaneously with relatively higher pressure over the equator and regions to the south, results in the south-westerly winds known as the south-west monsoon. As temperatures fall north of the equator and barometric pressure is equalized within the tropics after the October equinox, the south-west winds subside. They are then succeeded by the north-east monsoon, which develops during winter months due to the relatively greater atmospheric pressure over Asia compared to the equatorial region.
