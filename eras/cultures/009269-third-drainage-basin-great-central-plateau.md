# The Central Plateau Drainage Basin

The drainage basin of the great central plateau, also referred to as the *alta-planicie*, is recognized as one of the most elevated lacustrine basins in the world. While the region once drained toward the east, it currently possesses no surface outlet.

## Hydrography and Watercourses
The basin's water system is centered around Lake Titicaca, which collects water from several short streams descending from neighboring heights. From Lake Titicaca, water discharges via the Desaguadero, a sluggish river that flows south for 184 miles. As the Desaguadero progresses, its depth gradually diminishes until it reaches Lake Pampa Aullaguas, also known as Lake Poopo. The Desaguadero is navigable for small craft and is fed by two or three small tributaries originating from the west.

Lake Pampa Aullaguas is served by two small streams and possesses a small outlet called the Lacahahuira. This outlet flows west for 60 miles, eventually emptying into the Cienegas de Coipasa, which are salt-swamps.

## Absorption and Evaporation
The drainage of this extensive district is characterized by a lack of external discharge. The water is believed to be wholly absorbed by evaporation and the dry soil of the desert. This pattern of absorption is also evident in the extreme south of the region, where the Rio Grande de Lipez is absorbed in the same manner.

## Regional Context in Bolivia
The river systems of Bolivia are naturally categorized into three distinct regions: the Amazon, La Plata, and the Central Plateau. While the Amazon system—which includes the Mamore and Beni rivers and covers more than half of the republic—drains toward the Madeira, the Central Plateau remains a distinct hydrographic entity. The Guapay (or Grande) river, which rises in the Cordillera Oriental east of Lake Pampa Aullaguas, serves as a primary stream that eventually flows east toward the Bolivian lowlands and joins the Mamore at the Junta de los Rios.
