# Chiquitos Region

The Chiquitos region is a geographical area located within Bolivia. Contrary to the popular conception of Bolivia as an exclusively rugged and mountainous country, three-fifths of the nation—including the Chiquitos region—consists of gently undulating forest regions, great swamps, flooded bottomlands, and low alluvial plains.

## Geography and Topography
The topography of the region is varied. Between the Andes and the Chiquitos sierras lie the Llanos de Chiquitos, an area characterized by a more diversified surface and a higher general elevation. To the north of this elevation, which served as the southern shore of the ancient Mojos Lake, are the llanos of Mojos and Guarayos. This extensive northern region is traversed by numerous tributaries and several major rivers, including the Beni, Mamore, Yacuma, Guapay, San Miguel, Guapore, and Madre de Dios.

The region continues to be influenced by its prehistoric geography; the area once covered by the Mojos Lake still contains large undrained sections, such as Lake Rojoagua (also known as Roguaguado). In the north-west, where the Madre de Dios and Beni rivers traverse the country, the lower slopes of the Andes are covered in heavy forests. This specific district is noted as one of the richest in Bolivia and is capable of sustaining a large population.

## Hydrography and Drainage
The Chiquitos region is part of a complex river system. The Guapay (or Grande) river, which rises in the Cordillera Oriental, flows east to the north extremity of the Sierra de Misiones before emerging upon the Bolivian lowlands. From there, it skirts the Llanos de Chiquitos in a magnificent curve before turning north-west to unite with the Mamore river at Junta de los Rios.

A significant challenge for the region is defective drainage. During the rainy season, streams possess a current too sluggish to effectively carry off water. This results in extensive swamps and low-lying plains, particularly in southern Santa Cruz and eastern Chuquisaca, which are subject to periodical inundations. Due to these conditions, these specific areas are of little value for pastoral or agricultural purposes. However, there are considerable areas within the region that remain above the flood levels and provide rich grazing lands.

The south-east drainage basin, which discharges into the Paraguay, extends from the Cordillera Oriental east to the Paraguay and from the Sierras de Chiquitos south to the Argentine frontier. This basin includes the Pilcomayo river, which flows through the sierra region to the Bolivian Chaco across nearly level country.

## Economy and Land Use
Despite the drainage issues in certain sectors, the region contains extensive open plains and rich agricultural districts. Cattle-raising has been successfully practiced in these open plains since the era of the Jesuit missions.

## Population and Society
The population of the Chiquitos and Mojos regions includes Indian tribes who were organized into industrial communities by the Jesuits during the 17th century. These groups are described as industrious and peaceable, serving as an important labouring element. 

Following the expulsion of the Jesuits, these tribes have been less prosperous, though they have remained in organized communities, preserving the religion and industries taught to them. The archive notes that these populations often exist in a state of peonage and suffer from profound ignorance. Additionally, they are noted for the excessive use of *chica*, a native beer produced from Indian corn.
