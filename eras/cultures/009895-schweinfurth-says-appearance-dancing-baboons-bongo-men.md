# The Bongo People

The Bongo, also known as the Dor or Deran, are a tribe of Nilotic negroes inhabiting the south-west portion of the Bahr-el-Ghazal province in the Anglo-Egyptian Sudan. They are believed to be related to the Zandeh tribes of the Welle district.

## Population and History
According to G.A. Schweinfurth, who lived among the Bongo for two years, the tribe numbered at least 300,000 before the arrival of slave-raiders around 1850. Their population was greatly reduced by these slave-raiders and subsequently by the dervishes. The Bongo previously lived in numerous peaceful and independent small communities. It was not until the Sudan government established effective control between 1904 and 1906 that the population was able to recuperate. Under this government, the Bongo have returned to managing their own affairs.

## Physical Characteristics and Appearance
The Bongo are described as a race of medium height and a tendency to be thick-set. Their complexion is red-brown, which Schweinfurth noted is similar to the soil of their residence, and they have black hair. Schweinfurth observed that their heads are nearly round, stating that, to his knowledge, no other African race possesses a higher cephalic index.

In later life, Bongo women incline toward steatopygia. Schweinfurth remarked that this deposit of fat, combined with the tail of bast worn by the women, gave them the appearance of "dancing baboons" as they walked.

## Dress and Ornamentation
Historically, Bongo men wore only a loin-cloth and dozens of iron rings on their arms, which were arranged to function as a type of armour. Women wore a girdle with an attached tuft of grass. In more recent times, both sexes have largely adopted cotton cloths for their dresses.

Tribal ornaments include plugs or nails passed through the lower lip. Women frequently wear a disk several inches in diameter in the lower lip, as well as a bit of straw or a ring in the upper lip, a ring in the septum, and straws in the *alae* of the nostrils.

## Housing and Lifestyle
The Bongo build well-constructed huts, some of which reach heights of 24 feet. Unlike other Negroes of the upper Nile, the Bongo are not great cattle-breeders; instead, they dedicate their time to agriculture. Their primary cultivated crops include durra, sesame, tobacco, and sorghum.

The Bongo diet is diverse, consisting of fungi, tubers, and fruits found in their environment. They eat almost every type of creature, including reptiles, insects, beasts, and birds, though they do not eat dogs. They do not despise any flesh, whether it is fresh or putrid, and they are known to drive the vulture from carcasses.
