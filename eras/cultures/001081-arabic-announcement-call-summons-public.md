# AZAN

The Azan is the Arabic term for "announcement," specifically referring to the call or summons to public prayers. This proclamation is performed twice daily in all Mahommedan countries by a crier known as the Muezzin.

## Performance and Location
The location of the Muezzin during the Azan depends on the size of the mosque. In larger mosques, the Muezzin takes his position in the minaret to deliver the call. In smaller mosques, he stands either at the side of the building or at the door.

## The Call and Responses
The translated text of the Azan consists of a series of specific phrases:
* "God is most great!" (repeated four times)
* "I testify there is no God but God!" (repeated twice)
* "I testify that Mahomet is the apostle of God!" (repeated twice)
* "Come to prayer!" (repeated twice)
* "Come to salvation!" (repeated twice)
* "God is most great!" (repeated twice)
* "There is no God but God!"

For the morning Azan, the phrase "Prayer is better than sleep!" is added twice.

Devout Moslems are required to provide a set response to each phrase. While most responses are simple repetitions of the Azan, there are specific formal answers for two calls. When the Muezzin cries "Come to prayer!", the listener responds, "I have no power nor strength but from God the most High and Great." To the call "Come to salvation!", the response is, "What God willeth will be: what He willeth not will not be."

## Social and Religious Observance
The recital of the Azan is to be listened to with the utmost reverence. This requirement extends to the general public: those walking in the streets must stand still, individuals at work must cease their labors, and those who are in bed must sit up.
