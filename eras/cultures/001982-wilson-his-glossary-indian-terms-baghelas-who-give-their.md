# Bagelkhand and the Baghelas

## Geography and Administration
Bagelkhand (also referred to as Baghelkhand) is a tract of country located in central India. It is occupied by a collection of native states and is organized as the Bagelkhand agency. This agency was established in March 1871; prior to this date, the region was under the Bundelkhand agency, with which Bagelkhand maintains historical and geographical connections.

The administration of the agency falls under the political superintendence of the governor-general's agent for central India. Direct jurisdiction is held by a political agent who also serves as the superintendent of the Rewa state. This official typically resides in either Rewa or Sutna. The agency comprises the Rewa state along with eleven estates and minor states, the most significant of which are Sohawal, Nagode, and Maihar.

## Demographics and Climate
The total area of Bagelkhand is 14,323 square miles. As of 1901, the population was recorded at 1,555,024. This figure represented an 11% decrease over the preceding decade, a decline attributed to the effects of famine. The region suffered from very deficient rainfall between 1895 and 1897, which led to a famine in 1897. Additionally, drought affected certain sections of the agency during 1899-1900.

## The Baghelas
The region derives its name from the Baghelas. According to Wilson, in his *Glossary of Indian Terms*, the Baghelas are a branch of the Sisodhyia Rajputs. This group migrated eastward and previously held rule in Gujarat.
