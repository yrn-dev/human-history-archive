# Lough Beg and the Bann Valley

## Geography of Lough Beg
Lough Beg is a body of water located in the north of Ireland. It is situated immediately below a larger lough, which is Lough Neagh.

## The River Bann
Lough Beg is formed by the Bann, which is the principal river in the north of Ireland. The river originates in the Mourne mountains in the south of County Down. From its source, the Bann flows in a north-westerly direction until it enters Lough Neagh. The river serves to drain Lough Neagh, flowing in a north-north-westerly direction toward an estuary located at Coleraine.

The river is categorized into two distinct sections based on its position relative to the larger lough: the portion above Lough Neagh is known as the Upper Bann, while the portion below is referred to as the Lower Bann.

## Valley and Drainage
The valley of the river Bann has a length of approximately 90 miles, a measurement that excludes the lesser windings of the river. 

The total drainage area for the river is about 2,300 square miles. This area includes other important feeders of Lough Neagh and extends westward to the borders of County Fermanagh. The drainage basin encompasses parts of several counties, specifically:
* Antrim
* Armagh
* Down
* Londonderry
* Monaghan
* Tyrone

## Economic and Navigational Characteristics
The river Bann is noted for having valuable fisheries for salmon. However, it is not considered to be of much importance regarding navigation.
