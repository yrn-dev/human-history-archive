# The Feast of the Ascension

The Feast of the Ascension is one of the oecumenical festivals of the Christian Church, ranking in solemnity alongside the festivals of Easter, Christmas, and Pentecost. It celebrates the ascension of Christ into heaven, which occurred forty days after the resurrection.

## Calendar and Observance
The festival is held forty days after Easter, or ten days before Whitsunday. It always falls on a Thursday, a day referred to as Holy Thursday or Ascension Day. In the sequence of principal church feasts depending on Easter, Ascension Day occurs 39 days after Easter and seven weeks before Pentecost or Whitsunday.

## History and Traditions
The festival is of great antiquity, though discoverable traces of it do not appear before the middle of the 4th century. Subsequent historical references suggest it was established early; St Augustine mentioned that it had been kept from time immemorial and was likely instituted by the apostles. Other early records include:
* **Antioch:** Chrysostom noted a celebration of the festival in the church of Romanesia.
* **Constantinople:** Socrates recorded that in the year 390, the people celebrated the feast in a suburb of the city as an "old custom."
* **Bethlehem:** The *Peregrinatio* of Etheria (c. A.D. 385) states the festival was held in the Church of the Nativity.

Historically, the festival was associated with professional pilgrimages to commemorate the passing of Christ and his apostles to the Mount of Olives. In the 7th century, Adamnan, abbot of Iona, described such a procession taking place at Jerusalem, where the feast was celebrated in the church on Mount Olivet. In the West during the middle ages, this tradition evolved into processions with banners and candles outside the church, serving as a symbol of Christ's triumphant entry into heaven.

## Observance in the Anglican and Lutheran Churches
In the Anglican Church, Ascension Day and its octave continue to be observed as a great festival. To mark this occasion, a special preface to the consecration prayer in the communion service is provided, similar to the provisions for Trinity Sunday, Whitsunday, Easter, and Christmas.

The celebration of the Feast of the Ascension was also retained in the Lutheran churches, as it is warranted by Holy Scripture.

## Artistic Representations
The Ascension has been a prominent subject in ecclesiastical art and architecture:
* **The Church of the Holy Apostles:** The Ascension was depicted in the central dome.
* **St Sophia, Salonica:** The Ascension is featured in the composition.
* **St Mark's, Venice:** The central dome repeats the composition found in St Sophia, Salonica.
