# Dendroceros

The genus *Dendroceros* is a member of the *Anthocerotales*, a small and very natural group of plants within the Bryophyta. This group, which also includes the genera *Anthoceros* and *Notothylas*, is noted for occupying an isolated position among the Bryophyta in many respects.

## Thallus Characteristics
The thallus of plants in this group, such as *Anthoceros*, is dark green and composed of parenchymatous cells. It features an ill-defined midrib. Within each assimilating cell, there is typically a single large chloroplast. 

The apical region of the thallus is governed by a single initial cell. This region is protected by mucilage, which is secreted by mucilage slits. These slits are described as small pit-like depressions located between the superficial cells on the lower surface of the thallus. Additionally, mucilage is often formed within the intercellular spaces of the thallus. These mucilage slits frequently serve as habitats for colonies of *Nostoc*, which cause the slits to become enlarged.

## Sexual Organs and Reproduction
The sexual organs of these plants are scattered across the upper surface of the thallus. The antheridia are globular and stalked; they are exceptional because they are formed endogenously and are situated in groups within special intercellular spaces. The cavity containing the antheridia is bounded by a superficial layer of cells that does not break down until the antheridia are nearly mature. In some instances, antheridia may develop on the surface of portions of the thallus that are shaded.

The archegonia have necks that hardly project above the general surface of the thallus. While differences in detail exist, their structure and development agree with other Hepaticae.

## The Sporogonium
Following fertilization, the young sporogonium is protected by a thick calyptra, which is derived from the thallus tissue surrounding the archegonium. The sporogonium consists of two primary parts: a large bulbous foot and a long capsule. The superficial cells of the foot grow out into processes. 

The capsule continues to grow for several months, driven by the activity of a zone of cells located between the capsule and the foot; this growth can allow the capsule to reach a length of an inch and a half. The wall of the capsule is several layers of cells thick. Because the underlying cells possess chlorophyll and the epidermis contains functional stomata, the capsule is capable of assimilation.

Internally, the capsule contains a columella, which is a strand of narrow elongated cells in the center. Between this columella and the capsule wall, spores are formed from the dome-shaped archesporium, mixed with elaters.
