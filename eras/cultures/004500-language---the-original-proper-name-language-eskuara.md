# Eskuara

Eskuara (also written as euskara or uskara) is the original and proper name of the language spoken by the Basques. The exact meaning of the word has not yet been ascertained, though it is thought to possibly correspond with the idea of "clearly speaking."

## The Basque People
The Basques inhabit Navarre and the three Basque Provinces of Guipuzcoa, Alava, and Biscay in Spain, as well as Mauleon and the arrondissement of Bayonne in France. The population of those considered pure Basques is approximately 600,000 within Europe. Additionally, there are perhaps 100,000 emigrants in the Americas, primarily located in the South American region of La Plata.

The term "Basques" is historically derived from *Vascones* (or *Wascones*), a term that also provided the name *Gascons* for a different race. The Basques refer to themselves as *Eskualdunak*, meaning "those who possess the Eskuara," and they call their country Eskual-Herria.

## Linguistic Characteristics
Eskuara is described as a highly interesting language that remains absolutely isolated from other European tongues. From a purely grammatical perspective, it recalls the Finnic and Magyar languages. It utilizes a polysynthetic, incorporating, and agglutinative system of speech. 

Within the general series of organized linguistic families, Eskuara would occupy an intermediate position between the Ugrian or Ugro-Altaic families on one side and the American languages on the other.
