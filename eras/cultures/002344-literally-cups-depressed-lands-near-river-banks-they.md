# Depressed Lands and River-Bank Hollows

In various geographical regions, depressed lands and hollows located near river-banks play significant roles in agriculture, ecology, and the natural movement of water. These features often result from the cyclical overflow of streams or the specific geological composition of the surrounding terrain.

## The Pats of the Arable Tract
In certain districts, the arable tract is characterized by a "dead-level" of rich fields. The soil in this region is lighter in color than that found in Behar or Bengal and is notably friable, often splitting into small cubes with rectangular cleavage. A distinctive feature of this landscape is the *pats*, which literally translates to "cups." These are depressed lands situated near river-banks. It is believed that these *pats* were originally marshes that became partially silted up due to the yearly overflow of streams. Consequently, these specific areas produce the finest crops.

The broader arable tract is largely treeless, though villages are encircled by tamarind, banyan, *pipal*, and fine mango trees, with bamboo forming green, shady lanes. The landscape is further dotted with screw-pines—a type of aloe with triple rows of hook-shaped thorns—as well as date-palms and palmyras.

## Riverine Depressions and the Nile
Along the Nile, the relationship between river swelling and land depressions is central to the local ecology. When the Nile begins to swell, water soaks through from the river, and the depressions by the side of the river and the hollow places of the land are the first to fill. 

These filled depressions immediately become populated with small fish. This occurs because, during the previous year when the Nile's waters retreated, fish laid their eggs in the mud. When the water returns to cover the land, these eggs produce the fish that fill the hollows.

## Regional Basins and Low-Lying Tracts
Different regions exhibit various forms of river-related depressions and basins:

*   **Azamgarh District:** This area is bounded by the rivers Ganges and Gogra. The land along the banks of the Gogra is a low-lying tract. The district's geography consists of parallel ridges with summits that are depressed into hollows where rivers flow. Between these ridges lie low-lying rice lands and numerous natural reservoirs.
*   **Assam:** This region is a series of fertile valleys centered around the Brahmaputra (meaning "Son of Brahma"). The river enters Assam through a series of rapids and waterfalls. In the Lakhimpur district, the southernmost branch enters through a gorge known as Parasuramkunda or Brahmakunda, a site of reverence for Hindus.
*   **Bangweulu:** In British Central Africa, Bangweulu is a shallow lake formed by the head streams of the Congo. It occupies the north-west part of a central basin within an extensive plateau. The surrounding land slopes gently toward this depression, and various streams drain into it, turning much of the area into a morass of papyrus and reeds.
*   **Amur and Usuri Regions:** In the southern part of a province near the Bureya or Little Khingan range, a wide and deep depression runs south-west toward the lowlands of the lower Sungari and the confluence of the Usuri and Amur. Additionally, Lake Kidzi occupies a depression that may have previously served as an outflow from the Amur to the sea.

## River-Bank Geography and Boundaries
River-banks and their associated marshlands have historically served as boundaries or sites for settlement. In ancient Greece, the Akarnanians occupied both banks of the river Achelôus in its lower course. The town of Œniadæ was specifically located on the overflowed and marshy land near the river's mouth. 

In other regions, such as Bundelkhand, the depression of river channels and the height of the banks make many streams unsuitable for irrigation. In these areas, irrigation is instead managed through *jhils* and tanks—artificial lakes created by placing embankments across the lower extremities of valleys to accumulate flowing water.
