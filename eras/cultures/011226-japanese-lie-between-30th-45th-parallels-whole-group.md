# Japan

## Geography and Physical Features
The islands of Japan, excluding Sakhalin (of which half is Japanese), are situated between the 30th and 45th parallels. The archipelago is traversed by a line of volcanic mountains, some of which remain active. The highest point in this mountain range reaches approximately 13,000 feet above sea level. The country is characterized as being generally fertile, well cultivated, and well watered.

In central Japan, seven provinces—Musashi, Sagami, Kai, Awa, Izu, Suruga, and Kazusa—are grouped in a shape resembling a Japanese fan (uchiwa), with Izu serving as the handle. To the east of this fan along the Pacific coast are the provinces of Hitachi and Shimosa. Inland from the circumference of the fan lie the provinces of Kotsuke and Shinano.

## Society and Civilization
The Japanese people are noted for their ancient civilization and a remarkable faculty for art. In the modern era, they have integrated Western methods and demonstrated a capacity for progress in the fields of commerce and war. These attributes have distinguished them among Eastern races as a great modern world-force.

## Political and Imperial History
The historical record of Japan includes a variety of administrative and imperial structures. The "bakufu" system was established under Yoritomo, with the "Shogun" serving as the general and head of this system. Over time, the role of the Shogun evolved; under the Hojo, the position became unimportant, and later, Imperial princes or the Fujiwara were appointed. During the period of the Ashikaga in the Northern Court, powers were transferred to the kwanryo. Under the Tokugawa, power shifted to the minister, who was separated from the ministerial council. Eventually, power was resigned to the Crown.

Notable figures in Japanese history include Prince Shotoku (Taishi, 572-621), who opposed the uji system, authored a "Constitution," and built a Buddhist temple. The archive also records Emperor Shomu (the 45th Emperor, 724-48) and Empress Shotoku (765-70), who returned to the throne as Koken and ordered the printing of amulets.

## Territorial Disputes and Relations with Russia
Japan has had significant territorial conflicts with Russia, particularly regarding the island of Saghalien (Sakhalin). In 1858, the Russian commander Muravieff demanded that the Strait of La Pérouse, which separates Yezo from Saghalien, be the Russo-Japanese frontier. Japan refused this proposal, which would have granted the entire island to Russia. Following Russian attempts to settle emigrants on the island, the Japanese Government proposed dividing the island or purchasing the Russian portion for approximately $2,000,000 (£400,000).

A final settlement was reached in 1875, in which Japan recognized Russia's title to all of Saghalien, and Russia recognized Japan's title to the Kuriles. The Kuriles had always been considered Japanese property. Thirty years later, Saghalien was the site of a Japanese invasion and was eventually divided between the two nations along the 50th parallel, fulfilling the original proposal of the Bakufu statesmen.

## Military Conflict
Japanese military capabilities were demonstrated in conflicts against Russian forces. In one engagement, Japanese infantry successfully cleared a strong, confident Russian army from inaccessible fortifications and superior artillery in a single day. Following this victory, the Japanese advanced rapidly and took possession of Dalny on May 30th. Dalny served as a strategic base for the subsequent attack on the fortress of Port Arthur.
