# BACILLEAE

The Bacilleae is a sub-family of the family Bacillaceae, which falls under the order Haplobacterinae. This classification describes organisms characterized by a unicellular vegetative body that may be isolated or connected in growth series that are filamentous or otherwise.

## Characteristics of the Family Bacillaceae
The Bacillaceae family is defined by vegetative cells that are straight and may be ovoid, ellipsoid, or cylindric (referred to as rodlets). A primary characteristic of this family is that the planes of division are always perpendicular to the long axis of the cell.

## The Sub-family Bacilleae
Within the Bacillaceae, the sub-family Bacilleae is distinguished by sporogenous rodlets that are cylindric and do not undergo alterations in shape. The members of this sub-family are categorized based on their motility and the nature of their cilia:

*   ***Bacillus* (Cohn):** These organisms are non-motile.
*   ***Bactrinium* (Fischer):** These are motile and possess a single polar flagellum, a condition known as monotrichous.
*   ***Bactrillum* (Fischer):** These are motile and feature a terminal tuft of cilia, described as lophotrichous.
*   ***Bactridium* (Fischer):** These are motile and have cilia distributed all over the surface, a condition termed peritrichous.

## Morphology and Growth
In the broader context of rodlets, these are elongated cells that can be biscuit-shaped, fusiform, or cylindrical. Cylindrical forms are further distinguished by length: those that are only three or four times as long as they are broad are termed *Bacterium*, while longer forms are termed *Bacillus*. 

Growth and division processes in *Bacillus* have been observed in hanging drops. For example, a single rodlet can grow and divide into eight rodlets over the course of several hours. In some instances, such as *Bacillus anthracis*, rodlets may grow into long filaments resembling *leptothrix* that later become septate and develop spores within their segments.

## Spore Development
The development of spores in *Bacillus megaterium* involves several successive stages. This process includes the formation of rodlets that may be segmented; for instance, a rodlet segmented into four may contain one ripe spore in each segment. Following the development of these spores, germination occurs in successive stages after periods of drying.

## Comparison with Related Sub-families
The Bacilleae are distinguished from other sub-families within the Bacillaceae by the shape of their sporogenous rodlets. While Bacilleae rodlets remain cylindric, those in the sub-family Clostridieae (such as *Clostridium*) are spindle-shaped, and those in the sub-family Plectridieae (such as *Plectridium*) are drumstick-shaped. Both Clostridieae and Plectridieae are peritrichous and motile.
