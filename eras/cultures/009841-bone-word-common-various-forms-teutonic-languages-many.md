# BONE

## Definition and Etymology
Bone is the hard tissue that serves as the framework for the animal skeleton. The term is a word common to Teutonic languages in various forms. In many of these languages, the word is specifically confined to the shank of the leg, as seen in the German word *Bein*.

## Anatomical Properties and Variations
While bone constitutes the skeletal framework, other animal projections referred to as "horns" vary in their composition. According to Sir R. Owen, the substances forming these projections are distinct from one another and differ from teeth. 

The horns of deer are composed of bone and are processes of the frontal bone. Similarly, the horns of the giraffe are independent bones, known as "epiphyses," which are covered by hairy skin. In the case of antelopes, sheep, and oxen, the horns are "apophyses" of the frontal bone, covered by a sheath of true horny material and the corium. The prong-horned antelope possesses a combination of these traits, with bony processes at the basis covered by hairy skin, while the rest of the extent is covered by horny sheaths.

In contrast, true horny matter—such as that found in the horns of the rhinoceros—is a modified form of epidermic tissue consisting of the albuminoid "keratin." This material is also found in the nails, claws, and hoofs of animals, as well as in the scales of the pangolin, the carapace of armadilloes and tortoises, birds' feathers, and porcupine quills.

## Medical Considerations
The study of bone includes the treatment of specific diseases and injuries. Two primary areas of focus within this field are fractures and ostitis, the latter being the inflammation of bone.

## Henry Bone
Henry Bone (1755–1834) was an English enamel painter born in Truro. He initially worked for London jewellers on small enamel designs before gaining public recognition as an artist. His work attracted the attention of the Royal Academy, which admitted him as an associate in 1800 and elevated him to the status of academician in 1811.

Between 1800 and 1831, Bone produced many beautiful miniature pieces of a larger size than had previously been attempted in England. His most admired works include eighty-five portraits from the time of Queen Elizabeth, ranging in size from 5 by 4 inches to 13 by 8 inches. Following his death, these works were sold at a public sale. One of his notable pieces, a painting of Bacchus and Ariadne after Titian executed on a plate, sold for 2200 guineas.
