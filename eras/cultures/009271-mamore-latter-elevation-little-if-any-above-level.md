# Mamore

The Mamore is a principal river of Bolivia and a major component of the Amazon river system. It serves as one of the two primary rivers in this system, alongside the Beni, which together form a drainage basin covering more than one-half of the republic.

## Course and Geography
The Mamore rises on the north-east slopes of the Sierra de Cochabamba, slightly south of the 17th parallel. Its upper section is known as the Chimore. From its source, the river follows a serpentine course in a northerly direction, primarily situated between the 65th and 66th meridians. The river has a total length of approximately 600 m.

The geography of the river is characterized by a level plain for three-fourths of its length, extending from Chimore—which is 925 ft. above sea level—to the rapids located near its mouth. This extensive portion of the river is navigable. The Mamore eventually reaches a confluence with the Beni at 10 deg. 20' S. lat., where the two rivers unite to form the Madeira.

## Tributaries
The Mamore is fed by several large Bolivian tributaries, all of which originate on the north-east flanks of the Andes. These include the Chapare, Secure, and Manique (also known as Apere), as well as the Yacuma, which drains a region of swamps and lakes north of the Sierra Chamaya.

The Guapay (or Grande) is the principal Bolivian tributary of the Mamore. Rising in the Cordillera Oriental east of Lake Pampa Aullaguas, the Guapay flows east to the north extremity of the Sierra de Misiones before emerging onto the Bolivian lowlands. It follows a magnificent curve around the south-east extremity of the Sierra de Cochabamba and skirts the Llanos de Chiquitos. The Guapay is larger and longer than the Mamore above their confluence and is considered the main stream. It has a tortuous, non-navigable course of over 700 m. and joins the Mamore at Junta de los Rios (15 deg. 20' S. lat. and 64 deg. 40' W. long.). The Guapay's own principal tributaries are the Yapacani, the Mizque, and the Piray (or Sara).

## Regional Hydrography and Environment
The Mamore traverses the llanos of Mojos and Guarayos, a region that was once covered by the great Mojos Lake. This area is characterized by low alluvial plains, flooded bottomlands, and great swamps. Because of defective drainage and sluggish currents, the region is subject to periodical inundations during the summer months (November through March or May), often rendering traffic possible only by boat.

Within the great lacustrine basin between the Beni and the Mamore, there are several lagoons and lakes. Notably, the Roguaguado lagoon and marshes cover a large area of territory near the Mamore. The elevation of the Roguaguado is little, if any, above the level of the Mamore, which appears to drain the region. The area of this lagoon is estimated at approximately 580 sq. m.

## Commerce and Navigation
The Mamore is a critical link for Bolivian trade, as the country lacks direct maritime ports. The Bolivian port of entry for trade via the Amazon is Villa Bella, located at the confluence of the Mamore and the Beni. However, this port is situated above the falls of the Madeira and is also shut off from navigable rivers further upstream by the falls of the Mamore and the Beni. Consequently, reaching Villa Bella from the lower river requires a long and costly portage. It is noted that a railway to be built by Brazil may remedy this situation and promote the development of north-eastern Bolivia by providing a better outlet.
