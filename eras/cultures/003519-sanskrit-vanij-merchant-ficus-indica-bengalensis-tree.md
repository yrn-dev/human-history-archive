# Banyan

The banyan, also referred to as the banian, is a tree of the fig genus known scientifically as *Ficus Indica* or *Ficus Bengalensis*. 

## Etymology and Terminology
The term "banyan" or "banian" is an Arab corruption of the Sanskrit word *vanij*, which translates to "merchant." This name was originally applied by Europeans to a specific tree located on the Persian Gulf, beneath which a group of Hindu merchants had constructed a pagoda. 

In the context of Calcutta, the word was previously used to describe a head clerk or native broker within a private house or business, a role now typically referred to as a sircar. A related term, *bunya*—a corruption of a word common in Bengal—is generally used to describe a native grain-dealer. Additionally, early writers occasionally used the term generically to refer to all Hindus in western India. In Anglo-Indian usage, "banyan" also referred to an undershirt, referencing the body garment worn by Hindus, particularly the Banyans.

## Botanical and Geographical Distribution
The banyan is recognized as a well-known Indian tree. It is identified in some texts as the *Tholaḳ* (or *Thoulk*), a tree found in Arabia Felix. While some scholars, such as Pauthier, attempted to link the *Tholaḳ* to the Chinar (Plane-tree) via the title *Ficus Vasta* given by Forskal, other analysis suggests that the *Tholaḳ* is actually the Indian banyan or a closely allied species. Arab botanists have also referred to it as *Delb* (or *Dulb*), though this application of the name has been described by De Sacy as erroneous.
