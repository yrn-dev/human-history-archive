# Hydrographic Basins of Asia

The hydrographic structure of Asia can be analyzed through three primary divisions, categorized by their drainage patterns and the regions they embrace. These divisions consist of the northern division, the southern division, and the interior or inland basins.

## The Northern Division
The northern division of Asia encompasses a total area of approximately 6,394,500 sq. m. This division is composed of three distinct drainage areas:
*   **Arctic river basins:** The largest portion of this division, covering 4,367,000 sq. m.
*   **Aralo-Caspian basin:** Covering 1,759,000 sq. m.
*   **Mediterranean:** Covering 268,500 sq. m.

The Ob river basin is the largest river basin in all of Asia, with an extent that exceeds 1,000,000 sq. m.

## The Southern Division
The southern division is nearly equal in extent to the northern division, totaling 6,514,000 sq. m. This area is divided between two major drainage systems:
*   **Pacific drainage:** Covering 3,641,000 sq. m.
*   **Indian Ocean:** Covering 2,873,000 sq. m.

Significant river basins within the eastern and southern regions include the Amur (776,000 sq. m.), the Yang-tsze-kiang (685,000 sq. m.), the Ganges (409,500 sq. m.), and the Indus (370,000 sq. m.).

## Interior and Inland Basins
The interior or inland basins cover approximately 3,141,500 sq. m., which is about half the size of the other two divisions. These basins include several specific regions:
*   The Gobi depression.
*   The Tibetan plateau.
*   The Iranian (or Perso-Afghan) uplands.
*   The Syro-Arabian inland basin.
*   The basin of Asia Minor.
*   Lacustrine regions located south of the Arctic watershed.

In Central Asia, the Tarim Basin (roughly Eastern Turkestan) is located between the Kuen Lun and Thien Shan mountain systems. This basin is characterized by rivers that end in intermittent lakes and swamps rather than reaching the sea. While currently less fertile, this basin was more fertile in the past.

## Specific Hydrographic Features
### Lake Baikal
Hydrographic surveys conducted by Drizhenko between 1897 and 1902 reveal that Lake Baikal is divided into two basins by the peninsula of Svyatoi Nos and the hilly island of Olkhon. The lake is characterized by steep slopes, with the 100-fathom and 250-fathom lines running close to the shores. 

The deepest section is found in the south-east at the foot of the Khamar-daban border-ridge. Here, an elongated trough 66 m. long reaches a maximum depth of 880 fathoms (approximately 5,280 ft. below ocean level). In the northern basin, most of the area exceeds 400 fathoms in depth, with maximum soundings of 491, 485, and 476 fathoms. The Selenga river enters from the south-east, contributing alluvial deposits from a drainage area of 173,500 sq. m. and creating a wide delta that reduces the lake's width to 20 m.

### The Caspian Sea
Triangulation data from 1840 indicated the level of the Caspian Sea was 84 ft. below the level of the Black Sea, while later triangulation from 1860-1870 placed it at 89 ft. below.
