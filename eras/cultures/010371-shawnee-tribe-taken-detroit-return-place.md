# Daniel Boone and the Shawnee

## Capture and Adoption
Daniel Boone (1734–1820) was an American pioneer and backwoodsman of English descent. After spending years hunting and trapping in the Kentucky region and leading the party of settlers who founded Boonesborough in 1775, Boone encountered conflict with roving bands of Indians. On February 7, 1778, Boone and the party he was leading were captured by a band of Shawnees. Following his capture, he was adopted into the Shawnee tribe.

## Journey to Detroit and Escape
During his time with the Shawnees, Boone was taken to Detroit. It was during the return journey from Detroit that he managed to escape his captors. Following his escape, Boone undertook a perilous journey of 160 miles, which he completed within four days. This journey allowed him to reach Boonesborough in time to provide warning regarding a formidable attack planned by the Shawnees.

## Defense of Boonesborough
The attack Boone warned against took place from September 8 to September 17. Boone played a conspicuous part in repelling this assault. His military experiences in the region continued later in his life, including his participation in the "Battle of Blue Licks" in 1782.

## The Shawnee in Regional Treaties
The Shawnee tribe was one of several groups involved in significant territorial and political agreements in the Ohio region. On August 3, 1795, the Shawnees, along with the Wyandots, Delawares, Ottawas, Chippewas, Pottawatomies, Miamis, Weeas, Kickapoos, Piankashas, Kaskaskias, and the Eel-river tribe, entered into the treaty of Greenville. Under this treaty, the Indians agreed to a cessation of hostilities and ceded a considerable portion of Ohio, as well as small tracts in Michigan, Illinois, and Indiana—including the site of Detroit—to the United States. In exchange, the United States agreed to pay $20,000 worth of goods immediately and a permanent annuity of goods valued at $9,500.

Later, in July 1814, a second treaty was negotiated at Greenville by Lewis Cass and General W. H. Harrison. In this agreement, the Shawnees, Miamis, Delawares, Wyandots, and (Ohio) Senecas agreed to provide aid to the United States in its war against Great Britain.

## Military Conflicts at Detroit
Detroit remained a focal point of conflict involving Indian forces. In August 1812, Sir Isaac Brock, a British soldier and administrator, defended Upper Canada against an American invasion. On August 16, 1812, Brock commanded approximately 730 men and 600 Indians led by their chief, Tecumseh. This force compelled General William Hull and his American force of 2,500 men to surrender at Detroit.
