# Formosa

Formosa is an island situated near the coast of China, positioned under the northern tropic. Its geography is characterized by a high range of mountains that reach elevations of nearly 13,000 ft. The island is divided into two distinct regions: the western side consists of open and well-cultivated plains occupied by an immigrant Chinese population, while the eastern side is mountainous and inhabited by independent indigenous tribes who are in a less advanced state.

## Zoological Zones and Fauna

Formosa occupies a unique position regarding zoological boundaries, particularly in its freshwater fish populations. It is situated where the equatorial zone and the northern zone overlap. While other parts of its fauna exhibit the characteristics of the equatorial zone, the island has also received characteristic Japanese freshwater fishes.

Specifically, several Japanese Cyprinids and a Salmonid flourish on the island. This presence of Salmonids is notable as the family is entirely absent from the waters of southern Asia, though they exist in rivers flowing into the northern Pacific and the Arctic Ocean, extending perhaps as far as Formosa.

## Regional Geography and Proximity

Formosa is located in the North Pacific region. To its northeast lies the Luchu Archipelago (also known as Riukiu, Loo-choo, or Liukiu), a long chain of Japanese islands. The southern extremity of this archipelago, specifically the Sakishima-retto group, reaches a point 73 m. from the northeast coast of Formosa.
