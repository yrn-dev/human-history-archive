# Baluchistan

Baluchistan is a country located within the borders of British India, extending from the Gomal river to the Arabian Sea, and from the borders of Afghanistan and Persia to those of Sind and the Punjab. It is characterized as a sparsely populated tract, covering a larger area than any Indian province with the exception of Bengal, Madras, and Burma.

## Political Administration
The territory is divided into two primary divisions. British Baluchistan, which is a portion of British India under a chief commissioner, covers 9,403 sq. m. This area consists mainly of tracts formally declared part of British India in 1887 and ceded by Afghanistan under the 1879 treaty of Gandamak. 

The second division comprises foreign territories under the superintendence of the same officer acting as agent to the governor-general, totaling 79,382 sq. m. This includes the native states of Las Bela and Kalat (which encompasses Kharan and Makran). Additionally, there are tribal areas possessed by the Bugti and Marri tribes covering 7,129 sq. m. Directly administered districts within this second class include the eastern portion of the Quetta tahsil, the Bolan Pass, and the whole of the Chagai and Zhob political agencies, totaling 36,401 sq. m.

## Geography and Topography
The region is physically divided into two distinct parts by a railway line that runs from the Indus to Quetta. 

North of the railway lie rough highlands embracing the central ranges of the Suliman system. This northern district has an average breadth of 150 m., narrowing to less than 100 m. at the Gomal and expanding to over 200 m. at the railway line. The climate here is clear and bracing, with alpine vegetation and no great temperature extremes.

South of the railway is a square block of territory measuring roughly 300 m. by 300 m. This area includes the Kalat plateau, known as Jalawan. The fortress of Kalat is situated approximately 6,800 ft. above sea level on the western edge of a cultivated plain. The region is surrounded by significant peaks, including Murdar to the south, Chahiltan (Chiltan) to the south-west, Takatu to the north, and Khalifat to the east of Quetta. These heights range from 10,500 to 11,500 ft. Quetta itself sits on an open plain 5,500 ft. above the sea.

## Hydrography
Baluchistan's drainage is divided into several systems:

*   **Northern Drainage:** In the northern Pathan district, water either runs north toward the Gomal or gathers in lateral valleys before bursting eastwards through the limestone axis of the hills toward the Indus.
*   **The Kalat Hub:** Kalat serves as a center from which the Mulla, the Bolan, and southern Lora affluents radiate. The Mulla carries floods from the Kalat highlands into the Gandava basin. The Lora drains the Pishin valley to the north; these two systems unite in Shorawak before disappearing into swamps and deserts west of Nushki.
*   **Southern and South-Western Drainage:** South of the Mulla sources, a separate system exists. In Southern Baluchistan, drainage generally trends south and west. Streams such as the Dasht and Hingol (ancient *Tomerus*) drain Makran, while the Purali (ancient *Arabus*) drains the flats of Las Bela, and the Hab river forms the boundary west of Karachi. These flow into the Arabian Sea. Other streams curve westwards to disappear into inland swamps, such as those of Mashkel and Lora.

## Demographics and Natural Resources
The region is primarily the home of the Baluch and the Brahui. In the northern highlands, the Pathan highlander is the dominant inhabitant, with the Mando Khel of Zhob and the Dumars and Kakars of Pishin being prominent tribal divisions. However, the Rind confederation of tribes—including the Bozdars and the Marri and Bugti sections—occupy the twisted flexures at the end of the Suliman system; these groups are of Arabic origin, and no Pathans reside in their specific area.

The mineral wealth of the region is considered inconsiderable. Poor quality coal has been worked in Tertiary beds along the Harnai route to Quetta. Petroleum of excellent quality has been found in the Sherani hills, and a viscid form of mineral oil exists at Khattan in the Marri country. Sulphur is worked on a small scale in the Koh-i-Sultan, the largest volcano in western Baluchistan. Zoologically, the region belongs to the Ethiopian region, as defined by Dr. Sclater.

## Borders
The boundary between Baluchistan and Afghanistan is precisely demarcated. From Domandi, it follows the Kundar stream for 40 m. south-west, then diverges northwards before striking the Kadanai river. It follows the western edge of the Toba plateau (reaching 8,000 ft.) near New Chaman, then descends to the plains and follows hill ranges southwards to Nushki. The eastern boundary of the northern section is the "red line" at the foot of the frontier hills, which marks the border of British India.
