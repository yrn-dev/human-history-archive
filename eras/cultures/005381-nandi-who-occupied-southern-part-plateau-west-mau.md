# Nandi

The Nandi are a Nilotic tribe located in the north-west region of the protectorate in British East Africa. They occupied the southern portion of the plateau situated to the west of the Mau escarpment.

## Geography and Geology
The Nandi country is located north of the equator, where the Mau plateau reaches a height of 9,000 ft. The Nandi region is somewhat lower than this peak, characterized as being partly forested and well watered. Geologically, the area is associated with Archaean formations, specifically consisting of schists and quartzites.

The surrounding geography is defined by the Mau Escarpment, a wall of cliffs marked between the equator and 1° S. Opposite Lake Naivasha, this escarpment exceeds 8,000 ft. in height and features a crest covered by a vast forest. To the south of these woods, the landscape becomes more open, and the plateau descends into open country that drains toward the Dogilani plains.

## Relations with British Administration
The Nandi were identified as one of the most troublesome tribes in their dealings with the British. Their activities included repeated raids against neighbors who were less warlike, as well as the commission of wholesale thefts from telegraph and railway lines.

In response to these actions, an expedition was launched against the Nandi in September 1905. This campaign resulted in the tribe's submission by November of that year. Following this submission, the Nandi were removed into a reserve in early 1906.

## Labor and Social Dynamics
Following their relocation, the majority of the Nandi natives showed an aversion to taking service under white farmers, as they were unaccustomed to regular work. This reluctance contributed to an inadequate labor supply, which served as an early source of trouble for settlers. Furthermore, the enforcement of labor regulations led to considerable friction between the administration and the colonists during the period of 1907-1908.
