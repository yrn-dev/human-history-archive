# Sophus Bugge

Sophus Bugge (1833–1907) was a Norwegian philologist who made extensive contributions to the study of folklore, Runic inscriptions, and various ancient languages.

## Life and Academic Career
Born on January 5, 1833, at Laurvik, Norway, Bugge received his education in Berlin, Copenhagen, and Christiania. In 1866, he was appointed as a professor of Old Norse and comparative philology at Christiania University. Throughout his career, he authored a vast number of books focusing on folklore and philology. His academic interests extended beyond Scandinavian studies to include the study of the Etruscan, Umbrian, Oscan, Romance, and Celtic languages. He also spent time collecting traditions and folk-songs from Norway.

## Major Works and Theories
Bugge's principal scholarly achievement was a critical edition of the elder Edda, titled *Norroen Fornkvoedi*, which was published in Christiania in 1867. In his analysis of these texts, Bugge maintained that the earlier sagas and the songs of the *Edda* were largely based on Latin and Christian traditions that had been imported into Scandinavian literature via England.

His bibliography includes several specialized works on Norse literature and linguistics:
* *Gamle Norske Folkeviser* (1858), a collection of Old Norse folk-songs.
* *Bidrag til den aeldste skaldedigtnings historie*, published in Christiania in 1894.
* *Helge-digtene i den Aeldre Edda* (Copenhagen, 1896), which was later translated into English as *The Home of the Eddic Poems* in 1899.
* *Norsk Sagafortaelling op Sagaskrivning i Island* (Christiania, 1901).

In addition to these titles, Bugge wrote various books regarding Runic inscriptions.

## Death
Sophus Bugge died on July 8, 1907.
