# Borneo

## Geography and Topography
Borneo is the largest and most western island of a northern group of islands that extends between 110 deg. and 150 deg. E., reaching as far as Papua or New Guinea. The island's surface is characterized as rough and mountainous, with the majority of the land covered in forest. The exception to this forested landscape is found near the coast, where alluvial lands have attracted settlers from various surrounding countries.

The elevation of the island varies significantly. While ordinary elevations typically do not exceed 4,000 or 5,000 ft., the highest mountain on the island rises to nearly 14,000 ft. In terms of natural characteristics and general condition, Borneo is noted to resemble Celebes.

## Population and Demographics
The population of Borneo is described as small, rude, and uncivilized. The archive's records on the inhabitants of the region include the Dyaks. In terms of administrative and regional population figures, the combined population of North Borneo, Sarawak, and mixed brown populations is listed at 700,000.

## Political Status
