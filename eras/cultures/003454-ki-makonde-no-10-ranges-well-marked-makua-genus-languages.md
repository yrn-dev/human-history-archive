# Ki-makonde

The Ki-makonde language, also referred to as Makonde or Mabiha, is an archaic tongue spoken in the lower Ruvuma region and along the coast between Ibo and Lindi. Within the classification of regional languages, it is identified as group No. 10. Due to its characteristics, it is suggested that this group might conceivably be attached to the Swahili branch.

## Geographic Context and Neighboring Languages

The domain of Ki-makonde is situated in a region bounded by several other linguistic groups. To its east and south lies the well-marked Makua genus. The Makua genus occupies a significant portion of Portuguese East Africa, excluding the watershed of Lake Nyasa. The Makua language is likely divided into several dialects, including Anguru, I-lomwe, I-medo, and I-tugulu. Additionally, there are unnamed dialects located in the Angoji coast-region, an area characterized by the settlement of strong colonies of Swahili-speaking people. The southern reaches of the Makua domain are occupied by the Ci-cuambo of the Quelimane district.

To the west and north of the Makua genus, and bordering the Ki-makonde group, is the extensive Yao genus of languages. The Yao languages stretch from the north-east shores of Lake Nyasa (Ki-kese) south-westward across the Ruvuma river to the Lindi settlements in German East Africa (Ki-mwera). From there, the genus extends back to the valley of the Lujenda-Ruvuma (Cingindo) and southwards into various dialects reaching the region east of the Shire river—between the Shire highlands, Mt. Mlanje, and the south-east corner of Lake Nyasa. While the Yao language has expanded into territory south of Lake Nyasa since the middle of the 19th century, colonies of Nyanja-speaking people still remain within its domain.

## Related Linguistic Groups in East Africa

Beyond the immediate vicinity of the Ki-makonde and Makua regions, other distinct language groups occupy the inland territories of German East Africa. One such group, described as Kaguru-Sagala-Kami, is located between the domain of the Nyamwezi to the west and the Swahili coast dialects to the east. This group, which includes Irangi and Kigogo, is bounded to the south by the Ruaha river and to the north by the non-Bantu languages of the Taturu, Mbugu, and Masai.

Further west, the Nyamwezi group encompasses all dialects of the Nyamwezi country, extending north to the Victoria Nyanza and south to the Upper Ruaha river. This genus reaches south-west toward Lake Rukwa and was previously spoken in the southern part of the Belgian Congo, where it was imported by traders who established themselves as chiefs.

In the Comoro Islands, located between Madagascar and the East African coast, the dialects are known as Shi-ngazija or Hi-nzua (Anzuani), both of which maintain a close relationship with Swahili.
