# Siberia: Geography and Topography

## Regional Overview
Siberia is the portion of Asia located between the Arctic Ocean to the north and a mountainous belt that bounds Turkestan, Mongolia, and Manchuria. The region is characterized by an immense high and broken plateau that extends from the south-west toward the north-east. As this plateau advances in a north-easterly direction, it loses both its width and its altitude.

## Topographical Features
The Siberian plateau is fringed on both sides by high border ridges. To the north-west, these ridges subside into a stretch of high plains. These plains reach elevations of 1500 to 2000 ft. high, before finally dropping to lowlands that sit a few hundred feet above sea-level.

The region is also home to the Ural Mountains. The average altitude of this range does not exceed 2000 or 3000 ft., with the highest summits reaching no more than 6000 ft. Within this range, one of the passes is as low as 1400 ft. The chief mining districts of Russia are located in the southern half of the Ural range.

## Climate and Environment
Siberia experiences very great extremes of both cold and heat. While rainfall is not heavy, it is sufficient to support vegetation compatible with the local temperature conditions. The surface of the land is frequently peaty or swampy. Vegetation varies by terrain: pine forests commonly clothe the mountain-sides, while the plains are covered with shrubs or grasses.

## Hydrography
Siberia is traversed by the Lena, Yenisei, and Ob rivers, which are ranked among the largest rivers in the world.

## Population and Settlement
The population of Siberia is very scanty. Cultivated tracts are restricted to the more settled districts and are comparatively small in extent. The towns found within the region are entirely Russian. The indigenous races are nomadic Mongols who are peaceful in character but exist in a very backward state of civilization.
