# Commination and Omissions in Historical Records

## The Commination Service
In the context of the reformed Churches, the Anglican Church is the only one that observes a specific day with a special service known as the Commination service. The defining characteristic of this service is the solemn reading of "the general sentences of God's cursing against sinners." These sentences are compiled from other places of Scripture as well as the twenty-seventh chapter of Deuteronomy. The lections used for this day are identical to those used in the Roman Church, specifically Matthew vi. 16, &c., and Joel ii. 12, &c.

## Variations in the American Prayer Book
The office of Commination is omitted in the American Prayer Book, though it is not entirely absent. The three concluding prayers are retained. These specific prayers are derived from anthems and prayers that were sung or said during the distribution and blessing of the ashes, following the Sarum Missal.

## The Ceremonial of Ashes
The use of ashes as a ceremonial practice was not prohibited in England during the Reformation. On February 26, 1538, Henry VIII issued a proclamation enjoining the ceremonial of the ashes, and it was enjoined again in 1550 under the reign of Edward VI. Despite these official mandates, the practice had fallen into complete disuse by the start of the 17th century.
