# The Zambezi Region

The Zambezi river and its surrounding basin serve as a primary geographical marker for the administration of territories, the distribution of linguistic groups, and the location of mineral resources in south-central Africa.

## Barotseland
Barotseland is defined as the country where the authority of the paramount Barotse chief is acknowledged. This territory encompasses the lands of the Barotse proper as well as fifteen subject and contiguous tribes. Geographically, the region extends from the Kafue river in the east to the Kwito river in the west, and from the Zambezi and Linyante (or Kwando) river in the south to the Congo-Zambezi watershed in the north.

The administration of Barotseland is divided into three distinct groups:
*   **Central Provinces:** These are administered directly by the paramount chief from the capital, Lialui (located on the Zambezi), as well as by the Mokwai from Nalolo and two chiefs of the blood from Sesheke.
*   **Outlying Provinces:** In areas lacking a central local government system, Barotse chiefs manage districts under the direction of the paramount chief.
*   **Subject Tribes:** Local chiefs in these areas are permitted to keep their positions provided they pay annual tribute and perform personal homage at Lialui when summoned.

## Linguistic Classifications of the Zambezi Basin
The Zambezi river basin is a significant boundary and hub for various Bantu speech groups. Broadly, the domain of Bantu speech is divided into four great sections, one of which includes the languages of the East Coast and the Great Lakes down to and including the Zambezi basin.

### The Nyanja Group
The Nyanja group is perhaps the most extensive of the cognate languages in the Bantu field. Its domain includes:
*   The valley of the Shire down to its junction with the Zambezi.
*   Lands on both banks of the Zambezi, extending to its delta.
*   The Central Zambezi and the river Luangwa (via the Senga language).
*   Regions south of the Central Zambezi as far west as the Victoria Falls, extending eastwards over Mashonaland to the sea-coast.

Specific dialects within this group include *Ci-sena* or *Ci-nyungwe* of Tete and Sena (Zambezi), *Ci-mazaro* of the Lower Zambezi, and *Ci-podzo* in the Zambezi delta.

### Barotseland and Related Languages
The languages of Barotseland include *Si-luyi*, the most well-known form, and the *Mabunda* of Western Barotseland. Other closely related dialects include those of the *A-kwamashi*, *Ma-bukushu*, *A-mbwe*, and *Ambwela*. Additionally, *Se-kololo* serves as the court language of Barotseland.

### Subiya-Tonga-Ila and Other Groups
The *Subiya-Tonga-Ila* group consists of two subsections. The *Tonga* subgroup includes the *Ba-ila* (*Mashukulumbwe*), the *Ba-tetela*, and all of Central Zambezia. *Ci-subiya* is the dominant language of South-West Zambezia, found along a portion of the Zambezi south of Barotseland and in the lands between the Zambezi and the Chobe-Linyante river. *Subiya* is noted as one of the most archaic Bantu languages.

Other linguistic groups defined by the Zambezi watershed include:
*   **The Oci-herero group:** These languages, including *Herero*, *Umbundu*, and *Si-ndonga*, extend inland to the Zambezi watershed and the Kubango and Kwito rivers.
*   **The Kiboko/Kibokwe family:** This group includes the *Lubale* of northern Barotseland and the sources of the Zambezi.
*   **The Bechuana group:** This group stretches from the Orange river in the south to the Zambezi in the north.
*   **The Rua-Luba-Lunda-Marungu group:** This group is bounded on the south by the Zambezi watershed.

## Regional Trade and Communication
Swahili, an archaic Bantu dialect influenced by Arabic, serves as the *lingua franca* of eastern Africa. Its reach as a commercial language extends from the White Nile down to the Zambezi.

## Mineral Resources
The regions surrounding the Zambezi and the Nyasa-Zambezi waterparting are rich in various minerals:
*   **Precious Metals and Minerals:** Gold has been located in the hills along the Nyasa-Zambezi waterparting and the Shiré Highlands. Silver (silver-lead, galena) and lead are also found in the Nyasa-Zambezi waterparting hills.
*   **Industrial Minerals:** Iron ore and mica are found almost universally. Graphite is present in the western basin of Lake Nyasa, and limestone is found in the Shiré basin.
*   **Other Resources:** Copper (pure ore and pyrites) is found in the hills of North Eastern and North Western Rhodesia. Malachite is located in North Western Rhodesia and south-west Angoniland. Coal is found in the north and west Nyasa districts and potentially along the Zambezi-Nyasa waterparting. There is also the possibility of petroleum along the Nyasa-Zambezi waterparting.
