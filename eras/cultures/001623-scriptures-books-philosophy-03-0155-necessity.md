# Scriptures and Philosophy

The relationship between sacred scriptures and philosophical study involves the pursuit of wisdom, the necessity of linguistic precision, and the intersection of divine revelation with human reason.

## Philosophy and Divine Wisdom
In certain philosophical frameworks, all true wisdom is considered to be contained within the Scriptures, whether implicitly or explicitly. From this perspective, the ultimate purpose of philosophy is to transition from an imperfect understanding of created things toward a knowledge of the Creator. While ancient philosophers lacked access to the Scriptures, their significant intellectual achievements are attributed to direct illumination from God.

Other systems blend philosophy, tradition, and Scripture into unified systems to describe the nature of futurity. Additionally, academic roles have historically integrated these fields, such as the position of professor of Moral Philosophy and Rhetoric, and the production of texts focusing on the scriptural idea of man and moral science.

## Teachings of the Qur'an on Scripture
The Qur'an presents a vision of life centered on belief in Allah, His messengers, and the books revealed to them.

### Commands and Belief
Believers are commanded to believe in the scripture revealed to the Prophet and the scriptures revealed to those before him. To deny Allah, His angels, His Books, His messengers, and the Day of Judgment (or Last Day) is described as wandering far astray or straying into a remote error. Those who fluctuate in their faith—believing, then rejecting faith, then believing again, and increasing in disbelief—are told that Allah will not forgive or guide them.

### Relations with the People of the Book
The text refers to "People of the Book" (or "followers of the Book"). Believers are instructed to argue with them in a way that is better, except with those who act unjustly, and to acknowledge that their Allah and the Allah of the People of the Book is One. 

The Qur'an describes the following regarding the People of the Book:
* **Revelation:** A Book was revealed to the Prophet so that the People of the Book and some pagan Arabs might believe in it.
* **The Messenger:** The Messenger came to the People of the Book to make clear much of what they had concealed in their Book, bringing a "new light" and a "plain Scripture" to guide those seeking Allah's pleasure toward paths of peace, safety, and a straight path.
* **Conduct:** The text notes that a party of the People of the Book desire to lead others astray, though they only lead themselves astray. They are questioned as to why they reject the signs of Allah despite being witnesses to them, or why they knowingly conceal the truth and confound it with falsehood. Some are described as distorting the Scripture with their tongues to make it appear as if their words are from Allah when they are not.

### Specific Revelations
The Qur'an states that a Book was given to Musa (Moses) as a guide, a mercy, and an explanation of all things in detail, intended to lead people to believe in the meeting with their Lord. The current revealed Book is described as a blessing; followers are urged to be righteous and ward off evil to receive mercy. This prevents the claim that the Book was only revealed to two previous parties, leaving others unaware of what was learned through assiduous study.

## Drama and Scripture
In the study of literature, dramatic elements are identified in two books of the Hebrew scripture: the *Book of Ruth* and the *Book of Job*. The *Book of Job* specifically served as an influential source for Goethe's *Faust* and the author of *Everyman*. In contrast, drama is noted as being entirely wanting or only an occasional, exotic growth among other Semitic peoples.
