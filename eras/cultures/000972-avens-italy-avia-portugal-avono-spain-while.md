# The Celtic Root "Avon" and Related River Names

## Etymology and Linguistic Origins
The term "Avon" serves as the name for several rivers in England and other regions. The word is of Celtic origin, appearing frequently in Welsh as *afon*, in Manx as *aon*, and in Gaelic as *abhuinn* (pronounced *avain*). Linguistically, this root is radically identical to the Latin *aqua* and *amnis*, as well as the Sanskrit *ap*, all of which mean water.

## Distribution in Europe
The Celtic root for water appears, often in a disguised form, in a vast number of river names across the Celtic areas of Europe. In the British Islands, this is evidenced by names such as *Evan*, *Aune*, *Anne*, *Ive*, *Auney*, and *Inney*. In France and Brittany, the root appears in forms such as *Aff*, *Aven*, *Avon*, and *Aune*.

The influence of this root extends into southern Europe, where it is found in the following river names:
*   **Italy:** *Avenza* and *Avens*
*   **Portugal:** *Avia*
*   **Spain:** *Avono*

Additionally, the terminal syllable of many Latinized names for French rivers—including the *Garumna*, the *Matrona*, and the *Sequana*—appears to have originally been derived from this same word. Outside of Europe, the root is visible in a clearer shape in names such as *Doab* and *Punjab*.

## Geographic and Cartographic Context
The regions containing these rivers have been subject to various surveying efforts. In Italy, *Tavulette rilevata* based on surveys from 1862–1890 are being published on scales of 1:25,000 or 1:50,000, alongside a *Carta del regno d'Italia* at 1:100,000. Portugal completed a *Charta chorographica* (1:100,000) since 1856. In Spain, a plane-table survey (1:20,000) has been in progress since 1870, though as of 1910, only 150 of the 1,078 sheets of the 1:50,000 scale map had been issued by the *depósito de la guerra*.
