# Nyasa, Ki-kese, and the Lujenda-Ruvuma Valley

The region surrounding Lake Nyasa and the valley of the Lujenda-Ruvuma is characterized by a complex intersection of linguistic groups, diverse physical geography, and significant mineral deposits. This area spans boundaries between British Central Africa, German East Africa, and Portuguese East Africa.

## Linguistic Distribution
The region is home to several Bantu language groups. The Yao genus of languages is extensive, stretching from the Lindi settlements in German East Africa (Ki-mwera) south-westward across the Ruvuma river to the north-east shores of Lake Nyasa, where the Ki-kese dialect is spoken. From there, the language extends back to the valley of the Lujenda-Ruvuma (Cingindo) and southwards to the south-east corner of Lake Nyasa and the region east of the Shire river, situated between the Shire highlands, Lake Nyasa, and Mt. Mlanje. While the Yao language has expanded into territory south of Lake Nyasa since the middle of the 19th century, colonies of Nyanja-speaking people still remain within its domain.

The Nyanja group is perhaps the most extensive group of cognate languages in the Bantu field. It is primarily associated with the west and east shores of the southern half of Lake Nyasa and covers the Shire valley down to its junction with the Zambezi. The Nyanja domain further extends to the lands on both banks of the Zambezi, including its delta. West of Lake Nyasa, the Senga language extends the Nyanja domain to the Central Zambezi and the river Luangwa. 

Among these, the Kese and Nkonde dialects of the north and north-east coasts of Lake Nyasa are noted as being among the most archaic dialects of Bantu speech.

## Physical Geography and Hydrography
Lake Nyasa is a prominent feature of the region, measuring approximately 350 miles in length with a breadth varying between 15 and 40 miles, situated at an elevation of 1,700 feet above sea level. The surrounding territory is largely high plateau, averaging 3,500 feet in altitude. Notable peaks include Mount Mlanje in the extreme south-eastern corner, which reaches 9,683 feet and features several craters resulting from a volcanic outburst of basalt through the Archean plateau. Other high points include Mounts Chongone and Dedza (7,000 feet), and the Konde Mountains and Nyika Plateau, which likely exceed 8,000 feet.

The hydrography of the region is dominated by the Zambezi basin. The Lujenda river is of particular note; while the salt lake Chilwa currently has no outlet, it is believed that in past ages it emptied into the Lujenda river and subsequently into the Indian Ocean. The Ruvuma river, which forms the southern boundary between German and Portuguese East Africa, originates from the mountains and high plateau east of Nyasa. Except for its highest reaches near the lake, the Ruvuma is not fordable.

## Mineral Resources
The region around Lake Nyasa and the Zambezi waterparting is rich in various minerals:
*   **Precious Metals:** Gold is found in the hills along the Nyasa-Zambezi waterparting, the Shire Highlands, and the mountainous region west of Lake Nyasa. Silver (silver-lead/galena) and lead are also present in the Nyasa-Zambezi waterparting hills.
*   **Industrial Minerals:** Graphite is located in the western basin of Lake Nyasa, while copper (pure ore and pyrites) is found in the west Nyasa region. Limestone is present in the Shire basin, and malachite is found in North Western Rhodesia and south-west Angoniland.
*   **Fuel and Others:** Coal occurs in the west and north Nyasa districts, particularly in the Rukuru valley's Karroo sandstones. There is also the possibility of petroleum along the Nyasa-Zambezi waterparting. Mica and iron ore are described as being almost universally present.

## Human Geography and Settlements
The indigenous population is of the Negro type, belonging exclusively to the Bantu family linguistically. While there is no general trace of Hamitic intermixture, the physique of the native Awankonde at the north end of Lake Nyasa recalls that of the Nilotic negro. In some areas, there are signs of Bushman or Hottentot intermixture, and legends of unmixed Bushman tribes exist specifically around Mount Mlanje. Arabs from Zanzibar began settling in the country at the start of the 19th century.

European settlements in the region include:
*   **Zomba:** The government capital of the protectorate.
*   **Blantyre:** Founded in 1876 by the Church of Scotland mission in the Shire Highlands.
*   **Other sites:** Fort Johnston (near the Shire river outlet from Lake Nyasa), Likoma (an island off the east coast), Karonga (north-west coast), and Kotakota and Bandawe (west coast).
