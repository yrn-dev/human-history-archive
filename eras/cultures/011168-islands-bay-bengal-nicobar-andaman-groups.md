# The Andaman and Nicobar Islands

The Andaman and Nicobar islands are groups of islands located within the Bay of Bengal, a triangular portion of the Indian Ocean situated between Burma and India. Among the numerous islands found in the bay—which also include the Mergui group and the islands of Cheduba and others off the Burmese coast—the Andaman and Nicobar groups are noted as being the only ones worth notice.

## Geography and Geology
The islands are positioned along a line that connects Cape Negrais, the south-western extremity of Burma, with the northern end of Sumatra. It is possible that these islands owe their existence to volcanic agencies that extend from Sumatra across this region of the Indian Ocean. 

In terms of maritime depth, a zone 50 miles wide extends from the Coromandel coast and the island of Ceylon to the head of the bay, and then continues southwards through a strip that embraces the Nicobar and Andaman islands. This zone is bounded by the 100-fathom line of the sea bottom, with the 500-fathom limit lying approximately 50 miles beyond.

## Climate and Natural Phenomena
The Andaman and Nicobar islands are associated with the origin of revolving storms, or cyclones, which occur frequently in the Indian Ocean during the hot months from June to October, and more rarely in November. These cyclones appear to originate over the Andaman and Nicobar islands and are commonly propagated in a north-westward direction. They often strike the east coast of the Indian peninsula at various points before advancing with an easterly tendency over land, frequently passing with extreme violence across the delta of the Ganges. These storms are believed to be originated by the meeting of south-west monsoon currents with adverse currents from the north.

## Flora and Fauna
In the study of avifauna, the Andaman and Nicobar islands are classified as belonging to the Indian (or Cisgangetic) province. While the Nicobar islands contain a Megapode—indicating an influence from the Australian realm—neither the Nicobars nor the Andamans possess cockatoos, as those birds remain strictly on the other side of Wallace's line.

Regarding entomology, the insects of these regions belong to a broader group encompassing all of southern Asia, including the Malayan Islands, China, and India south of the Himalaya. In some instances, the species of insects are the same on opposite sides of the Bay of Bengal.

## Human Population
The Andaman Islands are inhabited by races categorized as Negroid, specifically referred to as Negritos due to the small size of some individuals. These people are closely akin to the negroes of South Africa and exhibit characteristic African physical traits, including broad flat noses, projecting lips, dark skins, and woolly but scanty body hair and beards. The Negritos are thought to be derived from a mixture of the Australoid type and the true Negro.

## Political History
The Andaman Islands were annexed in 1858.
