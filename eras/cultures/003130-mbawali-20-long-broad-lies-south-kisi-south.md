# Bangweulu

## Geography and Location
Bangweulu is a shallow lake situated in British Central Africa, formed by the head streams of the Congo. It is located between 10deg 38' and 11deg 31' S. and is cut by 30deg E. The lake occupies the north-west portion of a central basin within an extensive plateau, sitting approximately 3700 ft. above sea level. The surrounding land slopes gently toward the depression from the north, east, and south.

While the term Bangweulu is sometimes used to describe the entire depression, it is properly confined to the area of clear water. The banks of the lake are clearly defined only on its western and south-west sides. The greatest extent of open water measures approximately 60 m. from north to south and 40 m. from east to west.

## Lake Features and Islands
The water surface of the lake is diminished by several islands and long, narrow sandbanks. These sandbanks almost separate the main body of water from Chifunawuli, which is the western pan of the lake. 

Among the islands, Kirui (also known as Chiru) is the largest and is located on the east side of the lake, close to the swamp. Kisi (also known as Chishi) is a small island in a central position, located just south of 11deg S. Further south of Kisi lies Mbawali, an island measuring 20 m. long by 3 m. broad.

## Hydrology and Surrounding Waters
A considerable number of streams drain into the depression, turning much of the area into a morass of papyrus and reeds. To the south of Bangweulu, this swamp extends to 12deg 10' S. 

The Chambezi, the most remote head stream of the Congo, flows into the east side of this swamp. The Chambezi mingles its waters with those of the Luapula within the swamp without ever entering the lake itself. The Luapula leaves Bangweulu at its southernmost point; at this outflow, the river is about a mile wide, though it soon narrows to approximately 300 yards. From there, the Luapula flows south through the swamp for some 50 m. before turning west and then north.

Near the outflow of the Luapula and to its west lies Lake Kampolombo, which measures 20 m. long and 8 m. broad at its southern end. A narrow tongue of land covered in forest, called Kapata, intervenes between Lake Kampolombo and the Luapula, while a sandy track separates Kampolombo from Bangweulu. Despite these land barriers, various channels connect the river to the lake.

## Environmental Changes
The swamp is gradually being solidified by the flood waters of other streams and the Chambezi, which deposit large quantities of alluvium. Additionally, it is believed that the Luapula is very slowly draining Bangweulu.
